Not theory, not paperwork—real people.

Clean transition.

## **TRANSFER**

Their souls were escorted directly to the Express.

Six in total.

Six tickets were already waiting.

No explanation on how they were issued—just there, aligned with each individual like it had already been decided long before anyone signed anything.

Boarding was smooth.

No resistance. No confusion.

The environment itself seemed to… guide them.

The train stood still, quiet, glowing faintly against everything around it. Windows tinted in a soft azure tone, just transparent enough to see through.

Inside, they took their seats.

Outside, Director Lucious stood with several High Command Administrators—

and Candleheart.

She insisted on coming.

No one stopped her.

---

## **DEPARTURE**

As the train prepared to leave, those outside did the only thing they could.

They waved.

Simple gesture.

Through the glass, the employees waved back.

No fear.

If anything—relief.

Then the train departed.

No sound. No smoke. No motion you could track.

It was just… gone.

## **POST-DEPARTURE INTERACTION**

After departure, the Administrators were instructed to return.

Only two remained:

- Director Lucious
- Candleheart

Archangel Rose approached.

Her attention didn’t go to Lucious first.

It went to Candleheart.

She looked at her—really looked.

A roaming spirit.  
Half candle, half something soft and physical.  
Still here, when she shouldn’t be.

Rose spoke first:

> “So, we have someone who couldn’t let go, huh?”

Candleheart didn’t deny it.

She just answered, a bit shy—

> “I know what *that* feeling is… I just want to help others to not fall in the same fate as mine.”

Director Lucious followed up, tone steady:

> “We’re investigating your… incident. We’ll find whoever’s responsible.”

No promises beyond that.

Just intent.

## **OBSERVATION**

Rose’s reaction shifted.

Not concern.

Not warning.

Something closer to… interest.

Genuine fascination.

A soul that stayed behind—not out of failure, not out of error—

but by choice.

To help others.

That’s not standard.

No doubt.
