# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZFAUNA-LOG-122**

### **PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE ADDENDUM — MART SUNBRINGER**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – MEDICAL / RESIDENTIAL

---

## **SUMMARY**

Following continued observation, Psychological Services has issued a correction to Mart Sunbringer's behavioral profile.

Several staff members had previously assumed Mart remained close to others because he was afraid of solitude.

This assessment is **incorrect**.

Mart does **not** fear being alone.

In many cases...

He actively prefers it.

---

## **REVISED BEHAVIORAL PROFILE**

Mart regularly seeks out quiet locations throughout the Blacksite.

Commonly observed locations include:

- The library after closing hours.
    
- Observation decks overlooking the snow.
    
- Rooftops.
    
- Whitefall Lake.
    
- Maintenance corridors with little foot traffic.
    

He has been observed remaining alone for several hours without signs of distress.

He often spends this time reading, thinking, or simply watching the snowfall.

Medical staff note that these periods appear restorative rather than isolating.

---

## **PRIMARY FEAR**

Interviews and long-term observation indicate Mart's greatest fear is **not solitude**.

It is abandonment.

More specifically...

The belief that he is no longer wanted.

Psychologists describe this as a fear of becoming emotionally unnecessary rather than physically isolated.

When Mart believes someone genuinely wants him nearby, he is calm.

When he believes he is in the way...

He quietly leaves.

Usually without saying anything.

---

## **SOCIAL OVERSTIMULATION**

Mart also exhibits discomfort when surrounded by large groups for extended periods.

Symptoms include:

- Becoming noticeably quieter.
    
- Avoiding eye contact.
    
- Withdrawing toward the edge of the room.
    
- Choosing not to speak unless directly addressed.
    
- Looking for an opportunity to excuse himself.
    

This behavior should not be interpreted as rudeness or hostility.

Rather, Mart appears to become emotionally overwhelmed when too many people focus their attention on him at once.

Given the opportunity, he will politely leave and seek a quieter environment.

After spending time alone, he generally returns on his own.

---

## **CARE RECOMMENDATIONS**

Personnel are advised to:

- Respect Mart's need for quiet time.
    
- Avoid interpreting solitude as emotional withdrawal.
    
- Reassure him that his presence is welcome without placing pressure on him to stay.
    
- Avoid drawing unnecessary attention to him in large social gatherings.
    

Psychological Services emphasize that companionship and personal space are **not** mutually exclusive needs.

Mart benefits from both.

---

Mart has never asked her to leave.

## **CURRENT STATUS**

Mart continues to recover emotionally.

His preference for solitude is considered a normal aspect of his personality and **not** a symptom requiring correction.

Personnel are reminded that giving Mart quiet space is an act of support, not neglect.

---

### **Closing Observation**

One evening, Mart quietly slipped away from a lively gathering in the residential commons.

No one stopped him.

About twenty minutes later, Taiga found him sitting on an observation balcony, watching fresh snow settle across the mountains.

She didn't ask why he left.

She simply sat beside him.

For several minutes, neither sibling spoke.

Finally, Mart broke the silence.

> "...Thanks for finding me."

Taiga smiled gently.

> "...I wasn't looking because you left."

She leaned lightly against his shoulder.

> "...I came because I wanted to sit with my brother."

Mart looked out at the falling snow.

The faint worry in his expression eased.

Not because he was no longer alone.

But because, even after choosing a quiet place...

Someone had quietly reminded him he still belonged there.
