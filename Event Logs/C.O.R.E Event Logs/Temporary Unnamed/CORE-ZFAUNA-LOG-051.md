**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – MEDICAL / BIOLOGICAL

## **STATUS UPDATE**

Medical Services has documented several new physiological developments regarding Taiga following her most recent comprehensive examination.

The observations include increased sensitivity to thermal injury, an unexplained episode of epistaxis (nosebleed), and gradual changes to her natural pigmentation.

At present, none of the changes appear immediately life-threatening. Investigation remains ongoing.

---

## **I. THERMAL SENSITIVITY**

Despite possessing cryogenic anomalous abilities and an unusually high resistance to cold, Taiga has demonstrated an unexpectedly poor tolerance to heat.

Medical testing indicates she is considerably more susceptible to burns than the average human.

Documented examples include:

- Minor contact burns from cookware that would normally cause only brief discomfort.
    
- Increased skin irritation after prolonged exposure to heaters.
    
- Reduced tolerance to open flames.
    
- Accelerated blister formation following thermal contact.
    

Researchers theorize that Taiga's body has become highly specialized for extremely low temperatures, making it less capable of tolerating elevated temperatures.

As a precaution, personnel are encouraged to assist Taiga when handling hot equipment, boiling liquids, or open flames.

---

## **II. UNEXPLAINED NOSEBLEED**

During breakfast on **[REDACTED]**, Taiga unexpectedly developed a mild nosebleed.

There was:

- No evidence of trauma.
    
- No signs of infection.
    
- No abnormal blood pressure.
    
- No detectable anomalous activity.
    

The bleeding stopped within several minutes after routine treatment.

A small adhesive bandage was applied across the bridge of her nose at Medical's recommendation to protect the irritated area while it healed.

Subsequent examinations have not yet identified a definitive cause.

Medical continues to monitor for recurrence.

---

## **III. PIGMENTATION CHANGES**

Routine examinations have confirmed additional changes to Taiga's appearance.

Several new black leopard-like stripes have gradually appeared along both legs and upper thighs.

The markings match the pigmentation already present across her arms, face, and hair.

The changes occurred gradually over several weeks and caused no pain or discomfort.

Researchers believe they represent another stage of Taiga's natural maturation rather than illness.

No treatment is required.
