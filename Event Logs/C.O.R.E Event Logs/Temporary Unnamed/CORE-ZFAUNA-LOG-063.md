# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZFAUNA-LOG-063**

### **PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION — EMPATHETIC DISTRESS RESPONSE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – MEDICAL / PSYCHOLOGICAL

---

## **STATUS UPDATE**

Medical Services has formally confirmed a recurring behavioral trait in Taiga.

Taiga exhibits an unusually strong empathic distress response toward injured individuals.

In simple terms:

**She hates seeing other people get hurt.**

The severity of her emotional response appears largely independent of who the injured person is.

Whether the individual is:

- A roommate.
    
- A security officer.
    
- A scientist.
    
- A maintenance worker.
    
- Or someone she has only just met.
    

Her reaction remains remarkably consistent.

---

## **OBSERVED BEHAVIOR**

When witnessing another person sustain an injury, Taiga commonly exhibits one or more of the following:

- Immediate concern for the injured individual.
    
- Attempts to assist or call Medical personnel.
    
- Visible anxiety.
    
- Trembling.
    
- Crying if the injury appears severe.
    
- Difficulty calming herself until she knows the person is safe.
    

Medical believes this behavior stems from both her naturally compassionate personality and the trauma associated with witnessing the violent death of her mother.

---

## **NOTABLE INCIDENT**

During a routine maintenance inspection, an engineer accidentally cut his hand on a sheet of metal.

The injury was minor.

Before the engineer even had time to react—

Taiga had already reached him.

"...Are you okay?"

"...Does it hurt?"

"...Medical!"

The engineer smiled awkwardly.

> "...It's just a small cut."

Taiga wasn't convinced.

She remained nearby until Medical applied a bandage.

Only after the physician confirmed everything was fine did she finally relax.

---

## **MAJOR INJURIES**

Medical has observed that Taiga's emotional response increases dramatically when injuries are serious.

During one emergency response involving an injured security officer, witnesses reported that Taiga became visibly shaken despite not being injured herself.

After the officer was transported to Medical, Taiga quietly remained outside the treatment room.

She refused to leave until someone informed her the operation had been successful.

When the physician finally stepped outside and said,

> "He's going to recover."

Taiga immediately began crying.

Not from fear.

From relief.

---

## **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Medical concludes that Taiga's emotional distress when witnessing injuries is genuine and involuntary.

Personnel should avoid dismissing her concern with phrases such as:

> "It's nothing."

or

> "Don't worry about it."

Instead, clear and calm reassurance has been shown to help her recover more quickly.

Providing accurate updates on an injured person's condition significantly reduces her anxiety.

## **CURRENT STATUS**

Medical considers Taiga's compassion to be one of her defining personality traits.

However, her tendency to absorb the emotional weight of others' injuries can become psychologically exhausting, particularly when combined with her existing PTSD.

Personnel are encouraged to reassure Taiga when injuries occur and, where appropriate, allow her to assist in simple, safe ways, as this has been shown to reduce her anxiety.

### **Closing Observation**

During a routine training exercise, a recruit stumbled and scraped his knee.

Before the instructors could react, Taiga was already beside him with a bandage.

"...Does it hurt?"

The recruit laughed softly.

> "...Only my pride."

Taiga blinked.

"...Your... pride?"

The recruit smiled.

> "...I'll be okay."

After the bandage was in place, Taiga gave a small nod.

"...Good."

As she walked away, one of the instructors quietly remarked,

> "...If you get hurt around Taiga..."

He smiled.

> "...The injury won't be the only thing you'll have to deal with."

The other instructor chuckled.

> "...You'll also have a very worried snow leopard making sure you're alright."
