# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZFAUNA-LOG-052**

### **DIETARY & PHYSIOLOGICAL OBSERVATION — THERMAL TOLERANCE PARADOX**

**Classification:** LEVEL 2 – MEDICAL / NUTRITION

---

## **STATUS UPDATE**

Medical and Nutrition Services have documented an unusual contradiction in Taiga's physiology.

Despite demonstrating an abnormally high susceptibility to burns and other heat-related injuries, Taiga exhibits no measurable sensitivity to capsaicin—the chemical compound responsible for the "spicy" sensation in peppers.

This phenomenon has been designated the **Thermal Tolerance Paradox**.

---

## **I. LOW HEAT TOLERANCE**

As previously documented, Taiga is unusually vulnerable to physical heat.

Medical personnel believe her cryogenic physiology is still developing, making her body poorly suited to withstand elevated temperatures.

Examples include:

- Hot drinks must be allowed to cool before consumption.
    
- Heated cookware requires insulated gloves.
    
- Open flames are avoided whenever practical.
    
- Prolonged exposure to high temperatures causes discomfort significantly sooner than expected.
    

Medical notes that Taiga's young age may also contribute to this reduced tolerance.

---

## **II. DIETARY OBSERVATIONS**

The above findings make one discovery particularly confusing.

Taiga eats nearly every type of vegetable offered to her.

Her favorites include:

- Lettuce.
    
- Cabbage.
    
- Carrots.
    
- Cucumbers.
    
- Tomatoes.
    
- Bell peppers.
    

More surprisingly...

She also willingly eats:

- Jalapeños.
    
- Bird's eye chilies.
    
- Habaneros.
    
- Ghost peppers.
    

And, most notably—

**Carolina Reapers.**

---

## **III. CONTROLLED TEST**

To verify the observation, Nutrition Services conducted a supervised tasting session.

Taiga was offered a single Carolina Reaper under medical supervision.

She thanked the staff.

Ate it whole.

Chewed thoughtfully.

Swallowed.

Then politely asked,

> "...Do you have another one?"

The observing physician stared silently for several seconds.

> "...You're... not in pain?"

Taiga tilted her head.

> "...Was I supposed to be?"

---

## **IV. COMPARISON**

To confirm the peppers were authentic, one researcher sampled a very small piece of the same Carolina Reaper.

Medical intervention was required shortly afterward.

Testing confirmed the peppers were entirely ordinary.

Only Taiga appeared unaffected.

No elevated heart rate.

No tearing.

No coughing.

No discomfort.

No observable physiological response whatsoever.

---

## **MEDICAL HYPOTHESIS**

Researchers currently believe Taiga's resistance applies specifically to capsaicin rather than heat itself.

Although capsaicin produces the sensation of burning, it does not physically increase temperature.

Accordingly:

- Actual heat still causes burns.
    
- Spicy food does not.
    

Further study is ongoing.

## **CURRENT STATUS**

Medical concludes that Taiga's inability to tolerate physical heat does **not** extend to capsaicin-containing foods.

She remains the only documented Blacksite resident capable of consuming Carolina Reapers as though they were ordinary vegetables, displaying no observable adverse reactions.

Personnel are reminded that this resistance is unique to Taiga and should not be considered representative of normal human physiology.

---

### **Closing Observation**

Lunch in the cafeteria had become something of a spectacle.

Taiga quietly assembled a salad.

Lettuce.

Tomatoes.

Carrots.

Cucumbers.

Then, without hesitation, she added two whole Carolina Reapers.

She sat down beside Aster and happily ate her meal.

Across the room, several security officers stared in disbelief.

One finally muttered,

> "...She can't drink hot chocolate until it cools down..."

He watched Taiga bite into another Carolina Reaper without even blinking.

> "...But _that_ is somehow fine."

No one in Medical had an explanation.

Taiga simply smiled and continued eating her lunch.
