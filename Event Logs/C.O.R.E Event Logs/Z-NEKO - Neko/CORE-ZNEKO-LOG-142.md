# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZNEKO-LOG-142**

### **FIVE THINGS**

**Classification:** INTERNAL – PERSONNEL WELFARE / LEVEL 5 CONFIDENTIAL

---

## **I. DISCOVERY**

At 16:42 Blacksite Time, following the successful repair of a ruptured primary coolant pipe in Reactor Maintenance Corridor C-11, personnel discovered a small notebook resting on top of a closed toolbox.

The repair itself had been flawless.

Estimated completion time:

**47 minutes.**

Expected completion time:

**3 hours.**

The notebook appeared to have been forgotten accidentally.

Its cover simply read:

> **N**

Maintenance attempted to locate Neko immediately.

He had already left.

No cameras recorded where he went after leaving the corridor.

---

## **II. THE NOTEBOOK**

Most pages contained ordinary engineering notes.

Pressure calculations.

Pipe schematics.

Coolant flow corrections.

Small mechanical sketches.

Nothing unusual.

Until the final page.

There...

In noticeably smaller handwriting than the rest...

Five short sentences had been written.

No title.

No explanation.

No date.

---

> **Things I learned.**

---

> **1. Don't expect anything.**

---

> **2. Don't interrupt people.**

---

> **3. Everyone is busy.**

---

> **4. Work comes first.**

---

The fifth sentence had been written more slowly.

The pen had pressed harder into the paper.

---

> **5. I slow people down.**

---

Nothing else.

No signature.

No crossed-out words.

No corrections.

Just...

Five sentences.

---

## **III. MAINTENANCE**

The notebook quietly passed from one engineer to another.

No one spoke for nearly a minute.

Finally—

---

### Technician

> "...No."

---

Another engineer looked away.

---

### Engineer

> "...He actually believes this."

---

The room fell silent again.

Because nobody could honestly say he didn't.

---

## **IV. LUCY**

The notebook reached Lucy shortly afterward.

She recognized the handwriting instantly.

She only made it to the second sentence before her vision blurred.

By the fifth...

She quietly closed the notebook.

She couldn't continue reading.

---

### Lucy

> "...No..."

---

She shook her head repeatedly.

---

### Lucy

> "...No..."

---

A maintenance engineer placed a hand on her shoulder.

She barely noticed.

---

### Lucy

> "...That's not what we taught him..."

---

Her voice broke.

---

### Lucy

> "...That's what he learned."

---

No one corrected her.

---

## **V. MISS NEKO**

Miss Neko sat alone in Engineering.

The notebook rested on the desk between herself and Niko.

Neither of them had spoken for several minutes.

Eventually—

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Lil bro writes down things he wants to remember."

---

Niko slowly looked at the page again.

---

### Niko

> "...These aren't reminders."

---

Miss Neko nodded.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...They're conclusions."

---

She gently touched the final sentence.

---

> **I slow people down.**

---

She stared at it for a very long time.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...That's the one that scares me."

---

### Niko

> "...Because if someone believes that..."

---

Miss Neko quietly finished the sentence.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...They stop asking people to stay."

---

## **VI. STAR**

Star read the page once.

Then again.

Then a third time.

She frowned.

---

### Star

> "...That's wrong."

---

Nobody answered.

---

### Star

> "...It's all wrong."

---

She looked around the room.

---

### Star

> "...Right?"

---

Silence.

Not because anyone agreed with the notebook.

But because they were all asking themselves the same question.

If it was wrong...

How had Neko come to believe it?

---

## **VII. NIGHTSHADE**

Nightshade took the notebook.

Read all five lines.

Closed it immediately.

---

### Nightshade

> "...Idiot."

---

No anger.

Only frustration.

---

### Nightshade

> "...You kept every promise."

---

She looked toward the hallway.

---

### Nightshade

> "...Except the one where you were supposed to let us help."

---

She placed the notebook back onto the table.

More carefully than anyone expected.

---

## **VIII. DIRECTOR LUCIOUS**

The notebook was delivered personally.

Lucious read the page in complete silence.

He did not immediately return it.

Instead...

He opened Neko's personnel file.

Performance reviews.

Engineering commendations.

Medical recovery reports.

Maintenance recommendations.

Letters from coworkers.

Every page described someone dependable.

Helpful.

Kind.

Brilliant.

Nowhere...

Nowhere in thousands of pages...

Was there any indication that he was a burden.

Lucious quietly closed the folder.

Then looked back at the notebook.

---

### Director Lucious

> "...Somewhere..."

---

A long pause.

---

### Director Lucious

> "...We failed to show him what every document here already says."

---

Nobody disagreed.

---

## **IX. THE INVESTIGATION**

The internal investigation remained active.

Security had now expanded its review to include scheduling systems, work allocation patterns, emergency dispatch procedures, and departmental communication delays.

The statistical anomalies persisted.

Every apparent interruption still had a legitimate operational explanation.

No falsified records.

No unauthorized access.

No identifiable individual directing events.

The evidence did not support assigning responsibility to a person.

Yet the overall pattern remained difficult to ignore.

The investigation therefore continued.

---

## **X. FINAL OBSERVATION**

Medical archived the notebook as a personnel welfare document rather than an engineering record.

The page contained no threats.

No indication of self-harm.

No hostility.

Only five beliefs that appeared to guide Neko's recent behavior.

The final entry received particular attention.

> **"I slow people down."**

Behavioral specialists noted that this belief, if deeply held, could explain many of his recent actions:

- Working alone whenever possible.
    
- Avoiding conversations unless spoken to.
    
- Taking breaks in isolated locations.
    
- Finishing repairs before others arrived.
    
- Declining opportunities for companionship without being asked.
    
- Accepting discomfort without complaint.
    

A final handwritten remark was added to the case file by Director Lucious.

Unlike his usual reports, it was not an order.

It was a single observation.

> **"Machines fail because of broken parts. People rarely do. Sometimes they fail because they become convinced that everyone would work better without them. We will not let that belief become the final thing Neko learns."**
