## **I. LUCY**

Lucy had replayed the conversation hundreds of times.

Every night.

Every break.

Every quiet moment.

She remembered exactly where they were standing.

Exactly how Neko looked at the pipe instead of her.

Exactly how long the silence lasted.

Exactly how softly he had said—

> **"...Oh."**

It was only one word.

Yet it echoed louder than anything else she had ever heard.

---

She had meant—

> _"You don't have to worry about us every second."_

What Neko heard was—

> _"You're easier to deal with when you don't need anyone."_

Those weren't the same sentence.

But intentions no longer mattered.

The damage had already been done.

---

One evening, Miss Neko quietly found Lucy sitting alone in Maintenance.

A mug of untouched coffee sat beside her.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...You haven't gone home."

---

Lucy didn't look up.

---

### Lucy

> "...I had one chance."

---

Silence.

---

### Lucy

> "...One."

---

She stared at the maintenance report without reading it.

---

### Lucy

> "...If I had just stopped talking five seconds earlier..."

---

Her voice trembled.

---

### Lucy

> "...He would've smiled."

---

Miss Neko pulled up a chair.

She didn't interrupt.

Lucy continued.

---

### Lucy

> "...He actually believed me."

---

A long silence filled the workshop.

---

### Lucy

> "...The worst part..."

---

She closed her eyes.

---

### Lucy

> "...Is that he thanked me."

---

Miss Neko quietly reached over and rested a hand on Lucy's shoulder.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Lil bro forgives people much faster than he forgives himself."

---

Lucy nodded weakly.

---

### Lucy

> "...That's exactly what scares me."

## **III. IT KEPT HAPPENING**

Another day.

Someone intentionally misaligned a conveyor sensor.

Neko arrived.

Security suddenly requested Maintenance assistance.

Everyone left.

---

Another day.

A ventilation motor mysteriously "needed inspection."

Neko arrived.

A fire suppression alarm activated elsewhere.

Everyone ran.

---

Another day.

Someone deliberately loosened a pipe coupling.

Neko appeared.

Research requested emergency coolant support.

The hallway emptied.

Again.

---

Eventually...

The jokes stopped.

Nobody laughed anymore.

---

### Senior Maintenance Engineer

> "...How many times has this happened?"

---

Someone counted.

---

**Twenty-three.**

---

Twenty-three separate attempts.

Twenty-three interruptions.

Twenty-three coincidences.

---

Nobody spoke.

## **VII. WHILE THIS WAS HAPPENING...**

Neko knew nothing about the meeting.

Nothing about the investigation.

Nothing about the timelines.

He was quietly repairing an air filtration unit on the opposite side of the Blacksite.

Alone.

He finished.

Wrote another small note.

> **"Filter replaced."**

Then picked up his toolbox.

And walked toward the dim maintenance alcove for his break.

No one was there.

## **VIII. FINAL OBSERVATION**

Security officially designated the recurring scheduling anomalies as an **active internal investigation**.

At present:

- No individual has been identified.
    
- No evidence of sabotage directed specifically at Neko has been confirmed.
    
- The repeated overlap of legitimate work orders and attempted interactions remains unexplained.
    

Separately, Maintenance informally agreed to discontinue deliberately creating repair requests. While intended as a harmless way to spend time with Neko, intentionally introducing equipment faults—even minor ones—posed unnecessary safety risks and violated Blacksite maintenance standards.

The investigation therefore shifted away from speculation and toward verifiable facts.

One note from the Security Chief was attached to the case file:

> **"Coincidences happen. Twenty-three similar coincidences deserve to be examined carefully, but they are not proof by themselves. We will follow the evidence wherever it leads."**

The case remained open.

And somewhere else in the Blacksite, under a dim light that no one had yet replaced, Neko quietly finished another day of work without realizing that nearly the entire facility had begun asking the same question:

**"Why does every chance to reach him keep slipping away?"**
