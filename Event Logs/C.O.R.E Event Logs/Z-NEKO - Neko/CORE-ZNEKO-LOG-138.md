# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZNEKO-LOG-138**

### **THE QUIET CORNER**

**Classification:** INTERNAL – PERSONNEL WELFARE / CONFIDENTIAL

---

## **I. OBSERVATION**

Within several days of the events documented in **CORE-ZNEKO-LOG-137**, personnel across multiple divisions independently reported the same behavioral change.

Neko had not become hostile.

He had not become uncooperative.

His work quality remained exemplary.

He simply... withdrew.

Completely.

---

## **II. ROUTINE**

His daily schedule became almost mechanical.

Arrive before most personnel.

Collect assignments.

Complete every repair.

Submit documentation.

Leave.

Conversations became increasingly rare.

If someone greeted him—

---

### Engineer

> "Morning, Neko."

---

### Neko

> "Morning."

---

Nothing more.

No small jokes.

No quiet sarcasm.

No asking how someone's project was going.

No requests for sandwiches.

No curious questions about what everyone else was building.

The hallway would return to silence within seconds.

---

## **III. IMPOSSIBLE TO FIND**

It wasn't that Neko was hiding.

He simply worked where nobody else happened to be.

Maintenance would arrive at a leaking pipe.

The pipe would already be repaired.

Research would request Engineering assistance.

The equipment would already be recalibrated.

On the workbench, there would only be a small note.

> **"Finished."**

No signature.

No explanation.

By the time someone looked around...

He was already somewhere else.

---

## **IV. BREAKS**

There was one change everyone eventually noticed.

When Neko became bored...

He no longer designed anything.

No Glacier Spray.

No experimental tools.

No impossible engineering concepts scribbled onto napkins.

Instead...

He disappeared for ten or fifteen minutes.

Eventually, an intern discovered where he went.

A rarely used maintenance alcove.

One dim ceiling light.

A corner between two coolant manifolds.

Neko would simply sit there.

Back against the wall.

Knees drawn up slightly.

Glasses still on.

Looking at nothing in particular.

Just listening to the quiet hum of the facility.

---

The intern hesitated before speaking.

---

### Intern

> "...Neko?"

---

Neko slowly looked up.

---

### Neko

> "...Hi."

---

### Intern

> "...What are you doing?"

---

A long pause.

---

### Neko

> "...Taking a break."

---

His voice wasn't sad.

It wasn't cheerful either.

It was simply... tired.

---

## **V. MISS NEKO**

Miss Neko eventually found him there.

She didn't call his name.

She simply walked over and sat beside him on the floor.

Several minutes passed.

Neither spoke.

Finally—

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Lil bro."

---

### Neko

> "...Hm?"

---

### Miss Neko

> "...It's dark in here."

---

Neko looked toward the small overhead light.

---

### Neko

> "...It's quiet."

---

Another long silence.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Everyone's worried."

---

Neko gave a tiny nod.

---

### Neko

> "...I know."

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Then why won't you talk to us?"

---

He thought for a long time before answering.

---

### Neko

> "...Because everyone has work."

---

She shook her head.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...That's not the reason."

---

Neko finally looked at her.

His blue eyes were calm.

Almost empty.

---

### Neko

> "...If people have to stop what they're doing just to talk to me..."

---

He looked back at the floor.

---

### Neko

> "...Then I'd rather let them finish."

---

Miss Neko felt her chest tighten.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...You think spending time with you is interrupting us."

---

Neko didn't answer.

He didn't need to.

---

## **VI. LUCY**

Lucy learned where Neko spent his breaks.

The next afternoon, she walked toward the maintenance alcove.

She stopped before turning the corner.

She could hear him.

For a brief moment...

She smiled.

Finally.

He was talking.

Then she realized...

He wasn't talking to another person.

He was quietly reading through an old engineering notebook.

Correcting calculations out loud.

Explaining them to himself.

The same way he used to explain them to Star.

Or Niko.

Or Miss Neko.

Now...

There was only silence answering him.

Lucy couldn't bring herself to step into the room.

She quietly walked away.

---

## **VII. NIGHTSHADE**

Nightshade found the alcove a few days later.

She leaned against the doorway.

---

### Nightshade

> "...You picked a terrible hiding place."

---

Neko looked over.

---

### Neko

> "...Wasn't hiding."

---

### Nightshade

> "...Liar."

---

No response.

---

She folded her arms.

---

### Nightshade

> "...You're making everyone worry."

---

Neko lowered his head slightly.

---

### Neko

> "...Sorry."

---

### Nightshade

> "...Stop apologizing."

---

Silence.

---

She sighed.

---

### Nightshade

> "...I don't know what to say."

---

Neko managed the faintest smile.

---

### Neko

> "...That's okay."

---

Nightshade stayed for another minute.

Then her communicator sounded.

Another assignment.

She looked at the device.

Then at Neko.

---

### Nightshade

> "...I..."

---

She wanted to stay.

Duty called first.

Again.

---

### Nightshade

> "...I'll see you later."

---

### Neko

> "...Okay."

---

He watched her leave.

Then looked back at the floor.

---

## **VIII. DIRECTOR LUCIOUS**

Department reports continued arriving.

Every one of them praised Neko's performance.

No missed deadlines.

No incomplete repairs.

No safety violations.

No disciplinary concerns.

Lucious set the reports aside.

---

### Director Lucious

> "...Performance is excellent."

---

He looked toward the window overlooking the Blacksite.

---

### Director Lucious

> "...Personnel welfare is not."

---

## **IX. FINAL OBSERVATION**

Medical Division noted that Neko's emotional withdrawal had progressed despite continued physical recovery.

Notably:

- He no longer sought out companionship.
    
- He no longer occupied himself by inventing new devices when idle.
    
- He spent breaks alone in quiet, dimly lit areas of the Blacksite.
    
- Conversations with other personnel had become exceedingly rare.
    

One observation from Maintenance was included in the report:

> **"Before, if Neko got bored, we'd worry he'd build something impossible."**

> **"Now, when he gets bored... he just sits by himself."**

The report concluded with a recommendation that no one should attempt to force conversations or demand that Neko "cheer up." Instead, personnel were encouraged to be consistently present when they could, recognizing that rebuilding trust and connection would likely take time.

As one final note, an engineer who passed the maintenance alcove late that evening wrote only a single sentence:

> **"The corner isn't dark because the light is broken. It's dark because that's where he feels he doesn't get in anyone's way."**
