# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZNEKO-LOG-122**

### **THE WEIGHT OF GUILT**

### **Classification:** INTERNAL – PERSONNEL WELFARE / OMEGA PRIORITY

---

## **I. THE SILENCE**

The Blacksite had endured disasters before.

Containment failures.

Reality fractures.

Hostile incursions.

None of them had made the halls feel as quiet as they did now.

Neko remained in his unexplained state.

Alive.

His heart still beat.

His body still breathed.

But he had not opened his eyes for days.

The weight of uncertainty settled over every division.

People still worked.

But conversations became shorter.

Laughter became rare.

---

## **II. THE ENGINEER**

Among those struggling most was the maintenance engineer from the gas leak incident.

The one who had, in desperation, forced ice-cold lemonade into Neko's mouth so his anomalous sneeze would seal the ruptured gas pipe and prevent a catastrophic explosion.

The Blacksite had been saved.

Neko had never blamed her.

In fact, after recovering from the immediate headache that incident caused, he had told her personally:

> "You made the best decision you could with what you knew."

She remembered those words.

Every day.

Yet she couldn't stop asking herself the same question.

_"What if I hadn't?"_

---

## **III. NO BLAME**

An internal investigation had already reached its conclusion weeks earlier.

There was no misconduct.

No negligence.

The emergency had required immediate action.

Multiple senior officers signed the report.

Director Lucious signed it.

Medical signed it.

Miss Neko signed it.

Even Neko himself, while he was still awake, had insisted the report include one additional sentence.

> **"No one is to blame for making a difficult decision during an emergency."**

The case had been closed.

But guilt doesn't always listen to reports.

---

## **IV. SHE DISAPPEARED**

One evening, she failed to report for her shift.

At first, no one thought much of it.

Someone assumed she'd overslept.

Another thought she'd swapped shifts.

After several unanswered calls, Security began looking for her.

They found her quarters empty.

A note rested neatly on her desk.

Unopened.

Security immediately sealed the room and contacted Director Lucious.

---

## **V. THE NOTE**

The note was addressed simply:

> **"To everyone."**

It wasn't angry.

It didn't accuse anyone.

It didn't ask for blame.

It spoke only of how deeply she regretted the possibility that her decision had harmed someone she respected.

She ended by repeating Neko's own words back to him.

> **"You said you didn't blame me. I wish I could believe you as easily as you believed me."**

The room remained silent as the note was read.

---

## **VI. TRAGEDY**

Despite the efforts to find her in time...

She had taken her own life.

The loss sent another wave of grief through the Blacksite.

Medical personnel who had fought to save countless lives stood speechless.

Maintenance halted work for several minutes.

Even veteran officers who had faced impossible situations found themselves unable to speak.

---

## **VII. MISS NEKO**

Miss Neko stood quietly in the empty workshop where the engineer had spent so many nights repairing equipment.

She rested one hand on the workbench.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...You didn't deserve this."

---

She looked at the unfinished repair lying on the bench.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Lil bro forgave you before you ever asked."

---

Her voice broke.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...I wish you could've forgiven yourself."

---

## **VIII. DIRECTOR LUCIOUS**

Director Lucious addressed the senior staff later that day.

He stood in the briefing room for a long moment before speaking.

---

### Director Lucious

> "We have now lost one of our own."

---

He looked around the room.

---

### Director Lucious

> "Not because anyone here blamed her."

---

Another pause.

---

### Director Lucious

> "But because guilt can become its own burden."

---

He closed the report.

---

### Director Lucious

> "From this point forward, every critical incident will include mandatory psychological follow-up for everyone involved."

---

No one objected.

---

## **IX. THE ROOM**

That evening...

Miss Neko sat beside Neko's bed.

As always.

She gently held his hand.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...Lil bro..."

---

She looked at his sleeping face.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...She believed she'd failed you."

---

The heart monitor answered with its steady rhythm.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...You would've told her the same thing again, wouldn't you?"

---

Silence.

---

### Miss Neko

> "...That it wasn't her fault."

---

She rested her forehead against the bed.

For the first time since Neko had stopped waking...

She cried without trying to hide it.

---

## **X. AFTERMATH**

In the weeks that followed, the Blacksite honored the engineer's years of dedicated service.

Leadership also expanded mental health support, peer check-ins, and confidential counseling for personnel involved in traumatic incidents, recognizing that emotional injuries could be as serious as physical ones.

Among the staff, one lesson was repeated quietly:

No one should have to carry unbearable guilt alone.

And if someone begins to believe they do, they should never have to face it in silence.
