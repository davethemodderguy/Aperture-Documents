# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-Z321-LOG-008**

### **RESEARCH ADDENDUM — Z-321'S SENSORY PERCEPTION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 4 – RESEARCH / MEDICAL

---

## **SUMMARY**

While Z-321 ("Endmin") remains under medical observation for the temporary fever and motion sickness documented in **CORE-Z321-LOG-007**, Research Division conducted several **non-invasive** observations to better understand how she perceives the world.

No equipment was attached to Endmin, and no modifications to her protective mask were made, in accordance with Lucy's standing instructions.

The results indicate that Endmin does **not** "see" through ordinary vision.

Instead, she perceives her surroundings through an entirely different sensory framework.

---

## **CURRENT UNDERSTANDING**

Endmin describes her perception as:

> "...It's not really seeing."

> "...It's just..."

She searched for the right words.

> "...Everything tells me where it is."

Research Division currently theorizes that Endmin detects extraordinarily delicate vibrations and echoes propagating through the surrounding air.

These disturbances are far too subtle for ordinary human perception.

From these signals, her brain constructs a coherent representation of the environment.

This representation differs fundamentally from conventional sight.

---

## **CHARACTERISTICS OF HER PERCEPTION**

Testing indicates that Endmin can distinguish:

- The outline of nearby objects.
    
- Relative distance and depth.
    
- Surface contours.
    
- Minute changes in elevation.
    
- Movement.
    
- Fine texture.
    

One particularly surprising observation involved printed material.

Researchers placed several closed books in front of her.

Without opening them, Endmin accurately determined which one had embossed lettering on the cover.

When given an ordinary printed page, she slowly traced a finger near—not directly over—the surface.

She then began reading aloud.

A researcher asked,

> "...Can you actually see the letters?"

Endmin shook her head.

> "...Not like you do."

She smiled.

> "...The ink is just... there."

> "...I can tell it's raised."

Although ordinary printing is only microscopically elevated, Endmin's perception appears sensitive enough to detect these subtle differences.

---

## **DIGITAL DISPLAYS**

Researchers then presented Endmin with a tablet computer.

To the surprise of everyone present, she read its contents without difficulty.

When asked how this was possible despite the absence of raised ink, Endmin simply shrugged.

> "...Screens vibrate differently."

No further explanation could be provided.

Current research has not yet identified the precise mechanism involved.

Until additional evidence is available, the phenomenon remains classified as anomalous.

---

## **SPATIAL ORIENTATION**

Another unexpected discovery concerns orientation itself.

During testing, Endmin occasionally referred to directions that did not correspond to conventional up, down, left, or right.

When questioned, she explained:

> "...Your up..."

She pointed toward the ceiling.

> "...Isn't always my up."

Researchers initially assumed this was metaphorical.

Subsequent navigation exercises suggested otherwise.

Endmin appears to organize her environment according to an internal spatial framework generated by her anomalous perception.

Although it rarely affects her daily movement, it occasionally results in descriptions that seem unusual to those with ordinary eyesight.

Despite these differences, her navigation remains exceptionally accurate.

---

## **MEDICAL UPDATE**

Endmin remains in the infirmary.

Current condition:

- Mild fever persists.
    
- Motion sickness continues to improve slowly.
    
- Balance remains slightly impaired.
    
- Eye condition remains stable.
    
- Protective mask remains in continuous use.
    

Medical Division continues to recommend rest and minimal stimulation until symptoms fully resolve.

## **ROOM N-07 VISITS**

### Mart

Mart visited during the afternoon carrying another book.

He hesitated before placing it on the bedside table.

> **Mart:** "...You probably finished the last one."

Endmin smiled weakly.

> "...I did."

Mart blinked.

> "...Already?"

She nodded.

> "...Couldn't sleep."

Mart quietly sat beside her for a while.

Neither spoke.

---

### Taiga

Taiga arrived later carrying a tray with soup.

She looked at Endmin carefully.

> "...Still dizzy?"

Endmin nodded once.

Taiga gently pushed the tray closer.

> "...Then you're eating first."

Endmin laughed softly.

> "...Yes, Big Sis."

---

### Astral

Astral adjusted Endmin's blanket before checking the small **":3"** sticky note still attached to her mask.

It had begun peeling at one corner.

Astral carefully pressed it flat again.

Endmin noticed.

She smiled.

> "...Thank you."

---

## **DIRECTOR'S COMMENT**

After reviewing the report, Director Lucious added the following note:

> "Do not describe Z-321 as 'seeing normally.'"

A second sentence followed.

> "She perceives the world differently."

> "That difference is not a deficiency."

---

## **CURRENT STATUS**

**Z-321 – Endmin**

- Fever: Improving.
    
- Motion sickness: Improving.
    
- Sensory perception: Stable.
    
- Mask: Required.
    
- Research cooperation: Voluntary.
    

Research Division concludes that Endmin's perception is not a substitute for ordinary vision, but a unique sensory modality with capabilities—and limitations—distinct from human eyesight.

---

### **Closing Observation**

That evening, one of the nurses accidentally dropped a handwritten note onto the floor.

Before anyone could pick it up, Endmin glanced in its direction.

> "...You dropped your shopping list."

The nurse looked surprised.

> "...You can read it from there?"

Endmin smiled.

> "...Not exactly."

She tilted her head slightly.

> "...The paper told me enough."

No one in the room could explain what she meant.

Endmin simply returned to sipping her soup, the little **":3"** on her mask still slightly crooked.
