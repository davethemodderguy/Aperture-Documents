# 1. ARCHIVIST SUMMARY

This disclosure occurred several days after Neri's six-hour historical interview.

No interview had been scheduled.

No personnel approached Neri for additional information.

No questions were prepared.

Neri simply approached an Archivist in the SAFEHOUSE kitchen while they were organizing resident records.

She was eating cake.

She stood there quietly for several seconds before saying:

> **NERI:** “I don't know. I just felt so…”

She stopped.

The Archivist waited.

Neri eventually continued.

The resulting conversation provided additional information regarding her childhood that had **not** been disclosed during the original six-hour interview.

The Archivist has specifically requested that this material be handled differently from ordinary intelligence.

This is not a testimony to be repeatedly extracted.

It is something Neri chose to give us.

# 3. THE HOUSE

Neri described her childhood home in considerably more detail than previously recorded.

According to her account, the household was not constantly violent.

This distinction is important.

The abuse was unpredictable.

Some days were relatively quiet.

Other days were not.

Neri learned to recognize changes in mood and behavior among family members.

She learned when to stay silent.

She learned when not to ask for things.

She learned that being noticed could be dangerous.

She also learned that being **unnoticed** could be dangerous.

This produced what C.O.R.E. Behavioral Analysis describes as a persistent contradiction:

> **Be useful enough to remain.**  
> **Be quiet enough not to cause problems.**  
> **Do not become expensive.**  
> **Do not become inconvenient.**

Neri eventually stopped believing she had a right to ask for anything.

---

# 4. HER SISTER

Neri provided additional information concerning her younger sister.

The two reportedly spent significant amounts of time together when adults were absent or uninterested.

Neri taught her things.

Her sister followed her around.

They shared food.

They played together.

And, according to Neri, her sister liked sweets.

This appears to be one of the strongest remaining positive memories from Neri's childhood.

When asked what her sister's favorite sweet was, Neri initially laughed.

> **NERI:** “She liked everything.”

The Archivist asked:

> **ARCHIVIST:** “Like you?”

Neri smiled.

> **NERI:** “Yeah.”

---

# 5. THE LAST DAY

Neri eventually described the day her sister was killed.

The account remains incomplete.

C.O.R.E. intentionally does not record every detail here.

The essential information is sufficient.

Her family had decided that her sister was no longer worth the effort required to care for her.

Neri witnessed the decision.

She witnessed what followed.

She was unable to stop it.

Her sister died in front of her.

Neri repeatedly returned to one detail:

> **“She was scared.”**

And another:

> **“I couldn't do anything.”**

---

# 6. THE SWEET

During the disclosure, Neri recalled that her sister had wanted something sweet that day.

The exact item could not be established.

Possibilities mentioned included cake or candy.

Neri could not remember which.

She remembered that she had promised to share.

She never got the chance.

This may be relevant to Neri's current behavior.

She frequently gives sweets to people she cares about.

She frequently becomes excited when somebody brings cake into the SAFEHOUSE.

She frequently asks others if they want some.

This may not be because she simply enjoys eating sweets.

It may be because **sharing them with someone she loves is an old behavior that survived everything else.**

# 8. SUBSEQUENT FRIENDSHIPS

Neri described how she behaved after leaving her family.

She sought people who were lonely.

She recognized loneliness quickly.

She would talk to people who were ignored by others.

She shared food.

She listened.

She became friends with people who needed someone.

Unfortunately, several of these people were already suffering from severe depression and other serious difficulties.

Multiple friends subsequently died from self-harm.

Neri began interpreting every loss as another personal failure.

Her reasoning became increasingly distorted:

> **“If I had stayed.”**

> **“If I had noticed.”**

> **“If I had helped more.”**

> **“If I was stronger.”**

None of these conclusions are supported by evidence.

They nevertheless became part of Neri's internal worldview.

---

# 9. THE PATTERN

C.O.R.E. Behavioral Analysis now identifies a consistent psychological progression:

### Childhood

**Family abandonment**  
→ “People leave when I'm useless.”

### Sister's death

**Unpreventable loss**  
→ “I should have protected her.”

### Later friendships

**Repeated bereavement**  
→ “I failed to protect them.”

### SPAWN captivity

**Physical helplessness**  
→ “I couldn't protect myself.”

### SAFEHOUSE

**New friendships**  
→ “I have people again.”

This creates the foundation for her present-day protective behavior.

Neri does not simply fear losing friends.

She appears to believe that **failing to protect them makes her responsible for their loss.**

# 10. CURRENT SAFEHOUSE BEHAVIOR

This information provides additional context for several otherwise unusual behaviors.

Neri:

- Becomes highly protective of friends.
    
- Reacts disproportionately to perceived threats.
    
- Forms strong attachments.
    
- Becomes distressed when friends disappear for extended periods.
    
- Prefers staying near people she trusts.
    
- Walks with Alice frequently.
    
- Calls Bao “Mom.”
    
- Is exceptionally affectionate toward people who consistently treat her kindly.
    
- Becomes extremely hostile toward people who hurt her friends.
    
- Has an unusually strong preference for sweets and fish.
    
- Frequently shares food with friends.
    

The last behavior may be particularly important.

Neri doesn't merely **receive** sweets.

She gives them away.

# 12. ANALYST CONCLUSION

The current hypothesis concerning Neri's affinity for sweets remains unconfirmed.

It may be:

- A childhood association.
    
- A memory of her sister.
    
- An association with sharing.
    
- A symbol of affection.
    
- A coping mechanism.
    
- A simple preference that became reinforced by positive experiences.
    

It may also be all of these simultaneously.

C.O.R.E. will not attempt to determine which.

Neri does not need to explain why cake makes her happy.

She is allowed to simply enjoy cake.
