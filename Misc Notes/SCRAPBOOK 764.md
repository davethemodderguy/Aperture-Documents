# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — RECURRENT SYNCOPE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD SAFETY  
**Status:** MEDICAL EMERGENCY / CONTINUOUS OBSERVATION

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice has begun fainting frequently.

There is no consistent warning.

She can be sitting quietly one moment—

and unconscious the next.

The frequency has increased alongside her prolonged refusal of food and water.

Medical personnel have determined that this cannot be treated as something Alice will simply "get over."

She is five years old.

She is already physically fragile.

And she has almost no ability to communicate when something is wrong.

---

# **INCIDENT 01**

Alice was sitting at the kitchen table with Corin.

She was awake.

Quiet.

Looking at a cup of water.

Corin was talking to her.

Alice blinked.

Her head lowered.

Then her entire body went limp.

Corin caught her before she struck the floor.

> "Alice!"

No response.

She regained consciousness shortly afterward.

Her first action was to look at Corin.

Her second was to gesture that she was okay.

She was not.

---

# **INCIDENT 02**

Alice was walking through the SAFEHOUSE.

She suddenly stopped.

Her ears drooped.

She swayed.

Belle caught her.

Alice remained unconscious for several seconds.

When she woke, Belle was crying.

Alice looked confused.

She reached up and gently wiped Belle's tears.

She did not seem to understand why Belle was upset.

---

# **INCIDENT 03**

Alice fainted while sitting.

There was no fall.

No injury.

She simply leaned sideways and became unresponsive.

Neko immediately checked her.

She eventually woke.

He asked:

> "Alice, can you tell me when you're feeling dizzy?"

Alice pointed toward her mouth.

Then shook her head.

She couldn't.

---

# **INCIDENT 04**

Alice fainted while standing beside Corin.

This time, Corin noticed the warning.

Alice's eyes lost focus.

Her knees weakened.

Corin caught her.

She was unconscious before she reached the floor.

---

# **THE PATTERN**

The incidents began occurring more frequently.

While walking.

While sitting.

While being carried.

While eating.

While simply standing still.

Sometimes several times within the same day.

There was no reliable pattern.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

After regaining consciousness, Alice almost always does the same thing.

She looks around.

Confirms that everyone is there.

Then gestures:

**I'm okay.**

She never asks what happened.

She never complains.

She never seems frightened.

---

# **CORIN'S RESPONSE**

Corin has stopped accepting the gesture.

When Alice gives her the usual sign that she is okay, Corin simply answers:

> "I know you think you're okay."

> "You're still staying with me."

Alice doesn't resist.

---

# **THE FOOD PROBLEM**

Alice's continued refusal of nourishment is now considered an immediate contributing concern.

Her body is already under significant strain.

Every episode of unconsciousness increases the risk of injury.

The fact that Alice may faint without warning makes ordinary movement dangerous.

---

# **THE FIVE-YEAR-OLD PROBLEM**

Alice does not understand her own physical limitations.

She may stand up because she wants to go somewhere.

She may walk because someone else is walking.

She may follow someone despite feeling weak.

She may continue moving until her body simply stops cooperating.

She doesn't reliably understand:

> **"I need to sit down before I fall."**

---

# **NEW SAFETY RULE**

Alice is no longer permitted to wander through the Blacksite alone.

Not temporarily.

Not "just for a minute."

Not because the destination is nearby.

Not because someone can see her from a distance.

She requires active supervision.

If she is walking:

**Someone walks with her.**

If she is sitting:

**Someone remains nearby.**

If she is sleeping:

**Someone remains responsible for monitoring her.**

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE**

The residents have quietly adapted.

Someone is always with Alice.

Corin spends the most time with her.

Belle frequently sits beside her.

Star stays nearby whenever possible.

Orphie checks on her constantly.

Others rotate in.

Nobody complains.

Nobody considers it an inconvenience.

---

# **WHEN ALICE FAINTS**

The response has become almost automatic.

Someone catches her.

Someone checks her.

Someone calls medical personnel.

Someone stays beside her.

And when Alice eventually opens her eyes—

there is always somebody there.

---

# **THE MOST CONCERNING DETAIL**

Alice appears to have become accustomed to waking up surrounded by worried faces.

She doesn't ask why.

She doesn't ask what happened.

She doesn't seem embarrassed.

She simply looks around.

Then quietly settles back against whoever is holding her.

As though this is normal.

It isn't.

---

# **CORIN'S NOTE**

Corin wrote the following in the SAFEHOUSE medical log:

> "She keeps telling us she's okay."

> "She isn't."

> "And I don't think Alice understands the difference anymore."

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

**Fainting frequency:** Increasing.

**Warning signs:** Inconsistent.

**Communication:** Nonverbal.

**Nutrition:** Refused.

**Hydration:** Refused.

**Risk of injury:** Severe.

**Supervision:** Continuous.

**Medical monitoring:** Continuous.

**Subject cooperation:** Generally passive.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice has always been quiet.

Now that quietness has become frightening.

There is no dramatic warning when she collapses.

No cry.

No call for help.

Sometimes she simply closes her eyes.

And someone has to catch her.

Because Alice will not tell anyone when she is about to fall.

She won't say:

> "I'm dizzy."

She won't say:

> "I feel sick."

She won't say:

> "Please help me."

She may not even understand that she needs help.

So the SAFEHOUSE has made the decision for her.

**Someone will always be there to catch her.**
