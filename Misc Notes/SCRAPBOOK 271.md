# **KRAMPUS COMPLIANCE AUTHORITY**

## **CAFETERIA INCIDENT LOG**

### **SUBJECT: DIALYN COOKED SOMETHING... GOOD?**

**Classification:** Internal Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

On ██/██/20██, Dialyn volunteered to prepare dinner for a small group consisting of herself, Corin Lockwood, and Corey Lockwood.

Given Dialyn's well-documented inability to perceive saltiness—and her usual tendency to substitute chili for seasoning—the kitchen staff initially prepared for another memorable culinary incident.

None occurred.

---

# **THE MEAL**

Dialyn prepared a simple chicken and potato stew.

Ingredients were intentionally modest.

- Chicken
    
- Potatoes
    
- Carrots
    
- Onion
    
- Homemade stock
    
- Salt
    

No herbs.

No pepper.

No chili.

No spices whatsoever.

When asked why she wasn't adding anything else, Dialyn simply replied:

> "...I wanted it to taste warm."

---

# **COOKING METHOD**

Personnel observing the preparation noted something unusual.

Dialyn measured absolutely nothing.

She did not use measuring cups.

She did not weigh ingredients.

She did not time additions with a clock.

Instead, she cooked entirely by instinct.

Whenever someone asked how much of an ingredient she had added, the answer was always the same.

> "...Enough."

Most concerning to observers was the salt.

Unable to taste it herself, Dialyn simply looked into the pot, considered it for several seconds, and added what she believed was the correct amount.

Medical staff quietly prepared for the worst.

---

# **RESULT**

Against all expectations...

The stew was excellent.

Independent tastings by Cafeteria Services later confirmed that the sodium concentration was almost exactly what would be expected in a professionally prepared home-style stew.

How Dialyn estimated the amount remains unknown.

She herself could not explain it.

> "...It just felt right."

---

# **COREY'S RESPONSE**

Corey accepted the bowl politely.

She took one spoonful.

Then another.

She became completely silent.

Corin initially worried something was wrong.

Instead, Corey quietly began to cry.

When asked if the food was bad, she shook her head.

> "...No."

She smiled through tears.

> "...It tastes..."

A long pause.

> "...Almost exactly like my mom's."

The room fell silent.

For the first time in many weeks, Corey finished an entire meal without needing encouragement.

Before anyone could offer seconds, she had already reached for the pot herself.

---

# **CORIN'S OBSERVATION**

Corin watched Corey eat.

She smiled.

Not because of the stew.

Because it was the first genuine smile she had seen from Corey in a long time.

Corin later remarked:

> "...I missed seeing her enjoy something."

---

# **PERSONNEL COMMENTS**

**Dialyn**

When everyone complimented the meal, Dialyn looked genuinely confused.

> "...Really?"

She scratched the back of her head.

> "...I was kind of guessing."

---

**Bao**

After tasting the stew, Bao quietly laughed.

> "...Maybe don't let her season spicy food..."

> "...But she can definitely cook."

---

**Belle Merryweather**

> "...I'm updating the kitchen rules."

> "Dialyn is now allowed to cook."

A brief pause.

> "...Under supervision."

---

**Endmin Merryweather**

> "...One successful statistical anomaly does not invalidate previous reports."

---

**Star**

Star enthusiastically asked for the recipe.

Dialyn hesitated.

> "...I don't have one."

---

**Nightshade**

> "...Acceptable."

She finished her bowl.

Then accepted another.

---

**Neko**

Neko attempted to document the recipe.

After thirty minutes of questioning, his notes consisted of:

- "Enough potatoes."
    
- "Enough chicken."
    
- "Enough salt."
    

He quietly closed the notebook.

> "...I dislike instinct."

---

**Miss Neko**

> "...Some people cook with measurements."

She looked at Dialyn.

> "...Some people cook with memory."

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL FOLLOW-UP**

Over the following week, Psychological Services documented a small but measurable improvement in Corey's condition.

Observations included:

- Increased participation in conversations.
    
- More consistent eating habits.
    
- Reduced isolation.
    
- Several spontaneous smiles.
    
- One documented laugh.
    

The improvement was modest.

But it was real.

When asked what had changed, Corey thought for a long time before answering.

> "...For a little while..."

She smiled softly.

> "...I got to go home again."

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

The chicken and potato stew has since become an unofficial comfort meal at SAFEHOUSE.

Dialyn still refuses to write down a recipe.

She insists she doesn't have one.

She simply cooks until it "feels right."

Nobody has been able to replicate it exactly.

Corey doesn't mind.

She says the important part was never the ingredients.

It was the feeling the meal brought back.

For the first time in a long while...

That feeling wasn't grief.

It was home.

**End of Cafeteria Incident Log**
