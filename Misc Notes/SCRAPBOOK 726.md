### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / MEDICAL & PERSONNEL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — LOST VOICE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **NONVERBAL / CAUSE UNKNOWN**

---

# **THE MORNING**

Alice woke up normally.

Corin was nearby.

Orphie was sitting across the room.

Alice opened her eyes and looked around.

> **"Morning..."**

The word was barely audible.

Corin looked over.

> **"Good morning, Alice."**

Alice smiled.

She tried again.

> **"Corin..."**

Her voice was quieter.

Corin immediately noticed.

> **"Alice?"**

Alice frowned.

She tried to speak again.

The sound became smaller.

Weaker.

Almost a whisper.

> **"...hungry."**

Corin moved closer.

> **"Can you talk?"**

Alice nodded.

She tried to answer.

Nothing.

She swallowed.

Tried again.

A tiny sound escaped.

Then her voice weakened further.

---

# **LOSS OF VOCAL FUNCTION**

Over the following minutes, Alice's voice progressively deteriorated.

Her speech became:

**Quiet.**

Then:

**Barely audible.**

Then:

**A breath.**

Eventually, even attempting to vocalize became difficult.

Alice opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

She tried again.

Her throat produced only a faint rasp.

Then she stopped.

Her neck muscles appeared to give out from the effort.

Alice looked frightened.

Corin immediately held her.

> **"Don't force it."**

Alice nodded.

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

The cause remains unknown.

No obvious structural damage was identified.

No infection has been confirmed.

No external injury preceded the event.

Alice simply lost the ability to produce speech.

The medical team currently considers the condition **indeterminate**.

Recovery may occur.

There is currently no reliable estimate of when.

Alice is therefore not to be pressured into speaking.

---

# **COMMUNICATION**

Fortunately, Alice still has other ways to communicate.

She can:

- Write
    
- Draw
    
- Gesture
    
- Nod
    
- Shake her head
    
- Point
    
- Use simple signs
    

Her handwriting has improved considerably.

Her drawings have improved even more.

---

# **THE DRAWINGS**

Nobody expected Alice to become particularly skilled at art.

Her previous drawings were usually simple.

Childlike.

Often consisting of rough shapes and incomplete figures.

That has changed.

Alice now draws with remarkable precision.

Her lines are delicate.

Her proportions are unusually consistent.

Her use of shading is sophisticated for her age.

She can convey emotions through tiny changes in expression.

Most importantly:

**She can communicate things she cannot say.**

---

# **CORIN'S DISCOVERY**

Corin gave Alice a notebook.

She wrote:

> **"You can use this whenever you want."**

Alice nodded.

A few minutes later, she returned the notebook.

On the page was a drawing.

It depicted three figures sitting together beneath a tree.

Corin recognized herself.

Orphie was beside her.

And Alice was between them.

Above the three figures, Alice had drawn a small sun.

Underneath it, she had written:

> **"Together."**

Corin didn't say anything.

She simply hugged her.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has begun keeping a small stack of blank notebooks within easy reach of Alice.

No matter where Alice goes, she has something to write on.

Alice occasionally uses the notebooks for ordinary things.

**"Soup."**

**"Sleep."**

**"Cold."**

**"Scooter."**

But sometimes she writes much longer messages.

Her grammar remains imperfect.

Her meaning does not.

---

# **STAR**

Star has adapted quickly.

She now carries a small writing board when spending time with Alice.

Star asks questions.

Alice writes answers.

Sometimes Star asks:

> **"Do you want to go outside?"**

Alice writes:

> **"Yes."**

Star:

> **"Treehouse?"**

Alice:

> **"Yes."**

Star:

> **"Scooter?"**

Alice draws a very enthusiastic picture of the scooter.

Star laughs.

---

# **ALICE'S FIRST DAY WITHOUT HER VOICE**

By evening, Alice had stopped trying to speak.

Not because she had given up.

Because she understood that forcing it hurt.

She sat in bed with her notebook.

Corin asked:

> **"Are you scared?"**

Alice thought.

Then wrote:

> **"Little."**

Corin nodded.

> **"We'll wait."**

Alice looked at her.

She wrote another sentence.

> **"What if no voice?"**

Corin's expression softened.

> **"Then we'll listen another way."**

Alice stared at the sentence for several seconds.

Then drew a small heart beside it.

---

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice woke up with a voice.

By the end of the morning, she had none.

Nobody knows when it will return.

Perhaps tomorrow.

Perhaps months from now.

Perhaps longer.

For now, Alice has her notebooks.

Her pencils.

Her drawings.

Her hands.

Her expressions.

And the people around her who already know how to listen.

Her voice may have disappeared.

**Alice did not.**

And whenever she cannot say something—

she can draw it.

She can write it.

She can show them.

Someone will always be there to understand.
