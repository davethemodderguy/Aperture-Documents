# **HEAVEN'S BLACKSITE**

## **CONFIDENTIAL PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION**

### **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – ACUTE TRAUMA RESPONSE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 – Medical Director & Executive Personnel Only

---

## **OBSERVATION**

Corin Lockwood has increasingly isolated herself during periods of severe emotional distress.

When overwhelmed, she habitually locks her office door, silences incoming communications, and withdraws from colleagues.

This behavior appears motivated by shame rather than anger.

During a counseling session, she explained:

> "...I don't want anyone to see me like this."

When asked what she meant, she quietly replied:

> "...They're used to seeing someone who has everything together."

> "...Not... this."

---

## **INCIDENT REVIEW**

A welfare check conducted after Corin failed to answer routine communications found her office locked from the inside.

After receiving permission from Director Lucious under the executive wellness protocol, medical personnel entered.

Corin was seated on the floor beside her desk.

Financial reports were scattered nearby.

She had been crying for a prolonged period.

She looked up only briefly before lowering her head again.

No words were exchanged for several minutes.

The responding clinician chose not to question her immediately, allowing her time to regain her composure.

---

## **PATIENT STATEMENT**

Later, during a voluntary counseling session, Corin described what had been on her mind.

> "...Sometimes I imagine what my life would have been."

She paused.

> "...Growing up with my family."

> "...Going to school."

> "...Complaining about homework."

A faint, sad smile appeared.

> "...Normal things."

The smile disappeared.

> "...Then I remember that someone decided I wasn't allowed to have that life."

She looked at her hands.

> "...And I grieve for it."

The attending psychologist noted that Corin was expressing grief for opportunities and experiences she believes were taken from her, a common response among survivors of prolonged childhood abuse.

---

## **CLINICAL ASSESSMENT**

Corin continues to experience profound grief related to the loss of her childhood and the life she imagined she would have lived.

She frequently compares her present circumstances to that imagined future, particularly during periods of exhaustion or isolation.

Her treatment focuses on acknowledging those losses without allowing them to define the entirety of her identity.

Counselors continue to encourage Corin to build new memories alongside the painful ones rather than expecting the painful memories to disappear.

## **DIRECTOR LUCIOUS' ANNOTATION**

Director Lucious reviewed the report before issuing an amendment to the executive wellness protocol.

> "Privacy is to be respected."

> "Isolation during a crisis is not."

He continued:

> "If Corin closes her door because she needs quiet, allow her quiet."

> "If she closes her door because she believes no one should see her suffering, remind her—gently—that she does not have to earn the right to be cared for."

---

## **STATUS**

Corin remains engaged in ongoing medical and psychological treatment.

Her colleagues have been instructed to continue offering steady, compassionate support while respecting her autonomy and dignity.

The goal is not to erase the grief she carries, but to ensure she is never left to carry it entirely alone.
