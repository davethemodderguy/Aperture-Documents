## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — FIFTEEN-WEEK ABSENCE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 6  
**Species:** Full-Body Rabbit Thiren  
**Status:** **MISSING — CRITICAL MEDICAL EMERGENCY**

---

# **INCIDENT STATUS**

## **15 WEEKS**

Alice has been missing for fifteen weeks.

She has not returned to SAFEHOUSE.

No verified sighting has been recorded.

No confirmed communication has been received.

No confirmed location has been established.

The search has remained active for the entire duration.

This is no longer classified as a routine missing-person incident.

It is now classified as a:

# **CRITICAL VULNERABLE-PERSONNEL DISAPPEARANCE**

---

# **MEDICAL STATUS**

Alice's medical condition prior to disappearance was already extremely poor.

Known concerns included:

- Severe physical weakness.
    
- Persistent fatigue.
    
- Recent illness.
    
- Previous concussion.
    
- Previous head injury and existing scalp scar.
    
- Poor nutritional intake.
    
- Difficulty communicating physical needs.
    
- Persistent emotional withdrawal.
    
- Severe feelings of worthlessness.
    
- Increasing refusal of assistance.
    

Of particular concern:

## **Alice had stopped reliably eating and drinking.**

Personnel had already documented instances in which she refused:

- Food.
    
- Water.
    
- Medicine.
    
- Nutritional supplements.
    

Her refusal appeared connected to her belief that requiring care made other people tired.

---

# **CURRENT RISK ASSESSMENT**

Because Alice was already medically vulnerable when she disappeared, C.O.R.E. cannot assume that she has remained physically stable.

If Alice has continued refusing food and water during her absence, the medical consequences could be severe.

Personnel are therefore instructed to treat **every possible sighting as a medical emergency.**

Alice does not need to appear visibly injured for intervention to be justified.

Extreme weakness alone is sufficient.

---

# **SEARCH EXPANSION**

The search has been expanded beyond the original SAFEHOUSE area.

All available resources have been assigned where operationally possible.

Search teams are examining:

- Residential sectors.
    
- Medical sectors.
    
- Cafeterias.
    
- Storage facilities.
    
- Maintenance corridors.
    
- Abandoned rooms.
    
- Service tunnels.
    
- Exterior structures.
    
- Perimeter areas.
    
- Sheltered outdoor locations.
    
- Areas exposed to rain or snow.
    
- Locations previously associated with Alice.
    
- Locations where a small child could remain unnoticed.
    

Every accessible enclosed space is being physically verified.

---

# **THE PROBLEM**

Alice has always been unusually difficult to find.

Not because she is exceptionally skilled at hiding.

Because she is quiet.

She does not shout.

She does not demand attention.

She does not knock loudly on doors.

She does not call for people when she is frightened.

She has previously remained in dangerous locations without making enough noise to be noticed.

This means a fifteen-week absence cannot be interpreted as evidence that Alice is safe somewhere.

It may instead mean that she has been somewhere no one thought to look.

---

# **PERSONNEL BEHAVIOR**

Search teams have been instructed to listen for extremely faint sounds.

A small voice.

Movement.

Footsteps.

Breathing.

Anything.

Personnel have also been instructed to look beneath furniture, behind equipment, inside unused rooms, and within sheltered areas rather than relying exclusively on normal sightlines.

Alice's size and tendency to remain motionless make visual searches particularly difficult.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle continues participating in the search.

Her behavior has changed substantially over the fifteen-week period.

She continues carrying a small carrot whenever she searches.

When asked why, Belle answered:

> "If Alice is hungry, she'll need something."

She has never been able to explain what she intends to do if Alice does not come.

She simply keeps carrying it.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has maintained a near-continuous involvement in the search.

Her primary concern remains the same:

> **"Alice won't ask for help."**

Corin has repeatedly reminded search teams:

> "Don't wait for her to call."

> "She probably thinks she's bothering us."

> "If she's somewhere scared, she might just stay there."

This remains the central assumption of the search.

---

# **THE WHITE BEANIE**

Alice's soft white beanie remains inside SAFEHOUSE.

She stopped wearing it before disappearing.

The item has not been moved.

It remains exactly where it was stored.

Personnel have not treated it as evidence of intentional abandonment.

Alice left behind other belongings as well.

Her scooter remains unused.

Her gifts remain untouched.

Her journal remains preserved.

None of these objects provide evidence that Alice intended to permanently leave.

---

# **C.O.R.E. DIRECTIVE**

The following instructions remain in effect:

### **DO NOT ASSUME ALICE IS SAFE.**

### **DO NOT ASSUME ALICE LEFT VOLUNTARILY.**

### **DO NOT ASSUME ALICE WILL ASK FOR HELP.**

### **DO NOT ASSUME SILENCE MEANS SHE IS NOT THERE.**

### **DO NOT STOP SEARCHING BECAUSE NO EVIDENCE IS FOUND.**

Every unverified possibility remains open.

---

# **IF ALICE IS FOUND**

Personnel are instructed to approach slowly.

Do not surround her.

Do not shout.

Do not immediately ask:

> "Where have you been?"

Do not ask:

> "Why didn't you come back?"

Do not tell her how many people have been worried.

Do not make her feel guilty for the search.

The first words should be simple:

> **"Alice, we found you."**

Then:

> **"You're safe."**

Then:

> **"We're taking you home."**

Medical personnel are to assume that Alice requires immediate assessment regardless of whether she claims to be fine.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Fifteen weeks.

Alice has been missing for fifteen weeks.

Somewhere there may be a six-year-old rabbit thiren who is sick.

Weak.

Hungry.

Thirsty.

And still believing that asking someone to help her would make them tired.

That possibility is unacceptable.

Alice spent much of her life trying to make herself smaller.

She stopped riding her scooter.

Stopped accepting gifts.

Stopped sitting with people.

Stopped asking for food.

Stopped asking for water.

Stopped asking for help.

Eventually, she disappeared.

The Blacksite has spent fifteen weeks looking for her.

Not because she owes anyone an explanation.

Not because she is an important anomaly.

Not because she is a responsibility that needs to be recovered.

Because she is a child who is missing.

And someone should have remembered her long before fifteen weeks passed.

The search remains active.

The doors remain checked.

The corridors remain searched.

The exterior remains monitored.

And somewhere in SAFEHOUSE, untouched and waiting, sits the little white beanie Alice stopped wearing.

C.O.R.E. has issued one final instruction to every department:

> **If you see Alice, stop what you are doing.**

> **Find her.**

> **Stay with her.**

> **Get her medical attention.**

> **Bring her home.**
