# SAFEHOUSE ARCHIVAL DISCOVERY

## SUBJECT: NERI

### ARTIFACT RECOVERED FROM CHEMISTRY TEXTBOOK

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL  
**Artifact:** Hand-drawn illustration  
**Location Found:** Neri's chemistry book  
**Date Recovered:** Two months after disappearance  
**Artist:** Confirmed Neri

---

# 1. DISCOVERY

The drawing was found between two pages of Neri's chemistry textbook.

It was not placed there carefully.

It appeared to have been drawn directly on a blank section of paper and then left inside the book.

There are no annotations surrounding it.

No chemical equations.

No notes.

No explanation.

Just a drawing.

---

# 2. THE DRAWING

The image depicts two figures.

One is Neri.

The other is Bao.

They are standing beside each other.

Bao is drawn noticeably taller.

Neri is drawn wearing her oversized coat.

Her sleeves cover her hands.

Bao appears to have one arm around Neri.

The drawing is simplistic.

Almost childlike.

There are no elaborate details.

No depiction of wings.

No anomalous effects.

No weapons.

No laboratory equipment.

No indication that either figure is anything unusual.

Just:

**Neri and Bao.**

Together.

There is, however, one deliberate alteration.

Neri's eyes have been crossed out.

Not erased.

Not omitted.

**Crossed out.**

Several dark lines have been drawn over both eyes.

Bao's eyes remain untouched.

---

# 3. INITIAL INTERPRETATION

The significance of the crossed-out eyes is unknown.

However, it is notable that the rest of Neri's face remains intact.

She did not cross out her mouth.

She did not cross out her nose.

She did not erase her entire face.

Only the eyes.

This may indicate that Neri did not want the drawn version of herself to **see**.

Or be **seen**.

The latter interpretation is considered particularly consistent with her recent behavioral pattern.

---

# 4. THE CONTRAST

Bao is drawn normally.

Neri is drawn beside her.

Bao's face is complete.

Neri's is not.

This creates an unusual visual distinction.

Bao is allowed to look outward.

Neri is not.

The drawing therefore appears almost like a representation of the relationship as Neri understood it:

**Bao could see.**

**Neri could not.**

---

# 5. WHAT MAKES THE DRAWING IMPORTANT

There are no words saying:

> "I love you."

No caption.

No explanation.

No farewell.

No indication that the drawing was intended for anyone.

Yet the choice of subject is significant.

Neri had hundreds of things she could have drawn.

She chose Bao.

Not Guiding Light.

Not Alice.

Not Jane.

Not Star.

Not herself alone.

**Bao.**

And despite everything Neri had recently begun believing about herself, she still drew herself standing beside her.

---

# 6. THE CROSSING-OUT

A member of the archival team initially suggested that Neri may simply have disliked how she drew her eyes.

This explanation was rejected after examination.

The eyes had been crossed out repeatedly.

The lines are heavier than the rest of the drawing.

The action appears deliberate.

Neri spent considerably more effort obscuring her eyes than drawing them.

There is no equivalent alteration anywhere else in the image.

---

# 7. BAO'S RESPONSE

Bao was shown the drawing.

She remained silent for several seconds.

She recognized herself immediately.

Then she noticed Neri's face.

Bao reportedly touched the page very carefully.

> **BAO:**  
> "...You drew us together."

No response.

There could not be one.

Neri was not there.

Bao looked at the crossed-out eyes.

> **BAO:**  
> "Why did you hide them?"

She did not receive an answer.

---

# 8. AN ARCHIVIST'S NOTE

The drawing has been preserved in its original condition.

No attempt has been made to remove the markings over Neri's eyes.

The Archivist responsible for cataloguing it added the following:

> _"I don't think she crossed them out because she hated how they looked."_
> 
> _"I think she didn't want herself looking back."_
> 
> _"Maybe because she didn't want to see herself."_
> 
> _"Maybe because she didn't want anyone else to see her."_
> 
> _"I don't know."_
> 
> _"I wish we could ask her."_

---

# 9. THE BOOK

The drawing was found in the same chemistry book Neri used almost every day.

The same book she carried to the cafeteria.

The same book she used to occupy her mind.

The same book that remained behind when she disappeared.

It is now kept with the rest of Neri's recovered belongings.

The page containing the drawing has not been removed.

The book remains open to it.

---

# 10. FINAL OBSERVATION

For months, Neri had been trying to disappear.

She stopped eating sweets.

She stopped reading.

She stopped visiting people.

She stopped talking.

She stopped allowing herself to be noticed.

Then she disappeared completely.

And now, among the few things she left behind, there is a picture of herself standing beside the woman who called her daughter.

Neri drew them together.

But she crossed out her own eyes.

Perhaps she couldn't imagine herself being seen.

Perhaps she couldn't imagine herself looking back.

Or perhaps, somewhere inside that little drawing, Neri had already decided that she did not deserve to look at the person standing beside her.

There is no way to know.

The drawing remains.

Two figures.

Bao.

Neri.

Together.

And one pair of eyes deliberately erased from existence.

**END ARCHIVAL ENTRY**
