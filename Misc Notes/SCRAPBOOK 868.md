# SAFEHOUSE CRITICAL WELFARE REPORT

**Classification:** EXTREME RESIDENT WELFARE CONCERN  
**Subject:** Alice  
**Age:** Minor  
**Status:** MISSING — APPROXIMATELY 20 WEEKS  
**Last Confirmed Contact:** Neri  
**Secondary Subject:** Neri  
**Current Search Status:** NO TRACE  
**Known Food/Water Access During Absence:** NONE CONFIRMED  
**Psychological Impact on Neri:** SEVERE

---

# 1. Current Situation

Alice has now been missing for approximately **twenty weeks.**

No confirmed sightings have been obtained.

No confirmed communication has been received.

No reliable physical trace has been discovered.

No evidence has been recovered demonstrating that Alice has had access to food or water during the entirety of her disappearance.

Search operations have consequently escalated from a conventional missing-resident investigation into a critical welfare operation.

Neri is aware of the duration.

She is no longer indifferent.

She is crying.

---

# 2. Neri's Realization

The change occurred after Neri independently discovered historical incident records concerning Alice.

Neri had previously stolen Neko's keycard and accessed archived incident logs.

The access was unauthorized.

However, the information she found fundamentally changed her understanding of Alice.

The records documented multiple previous occasions in which Alice had:

- left the SAFEHOUSE without authorization,
    
- remained away for extended periods,
    
- injured herself,
    
- refused or lacked adequate food and water,
    
- isolated herself,
    
- and eventually returned or been recovered.
    

These incidents had occurred repeatedly.

Neri read them.

Then she understood.

---

# 3. The Misunderstanding

Neri had previously believed that Alice's statement about hating Corin or Orphie meant:

> **“Alice hates her family.”**

She had interpreted it through her own childhood.

To Neri, family hatred was inseparable from abandonment.

She therefore believed Alice was describing something fundamentally incompatible with the kind of family Neri wished she had possessed.

Neri became heartbroken.

She left.

She did not realize Alice had been trying to communicate something entirely different.

Alice had been trying to tell her that relationships could become complicated without ending.

That people could hurt one another, become angry, even hate one another for a time—

and still care.

That people around Neri were trying their best.

That Neri was not alone.

Neri did not understand.

---

# 4. What Neri Learned From the Logs

The historical records introduced an additional fact Neri had never known.

Alice had done this before.

Not once.

Multiple times.

Alice had repeatedly disappeared when struggling.

She had previously placed herself in situations where she could become severely malnourished, dehydrated, or injured.

The current disappearance therefore cannot be understood solely through the conversation with Neri.

Alice had an established pattern of running away and isolating herself.

The conversation did not create that pattern.

However, Neri now believes she may have contributed to Alice's decision to leave.

Whether this belief is objectively justified remains unknown.

---

# 5. Neri's Interpretation

Neri reportedly remained silent for an extended period after reading the records.

She eventually began crying.

According to the observing Archivist, Neri repeatedly whispered:

> “She was trying to tell me.”

Then:

> “She was trying to help me.”

And eventually:

> “I didn't understand.”

Neri reportedly became increasingly distressed as she connected the information.

She remembered the conversation.

She remembered Alice's words.

She remembered becoming upset.

She remembered walking away.

Then she looked at the date of Alice's disappearance.

Twenty weeks.

---

# 6. The Question Neri Could Not Answer

Neri reportedly asked:

> “Why didn't she come back?”

No answer was available.

She then asked:

> “Did she think I hated her?”

Again, no answer was available.

Finally:

> “Did I make her leave?”

Personnel did not provide an answer.

There is currently insufficient evidence to establish that Neri caused Alice's disappearance.

Alice had disappeared before.

Alice had struggled before.

Alice had run away before.

Alice had harmed herself before.

The conversation may have affected her.

It may not have.

Nobody currently knows.

---

# 7. Psychological Consequences

The realization has severely affected Neri.

Her previous emotional position was:

> **Alice isn't my problem.**

Her current position appears closer to:

> **I abandoned someone who was trying to tell me she wasn't alone.**

This is particularly dangerous given Neri's existing psychological profile.

Neri already carries substantial guilt surrounding people she believes she failed to protect.

Her greatest fear is not simply death.

It is **helplessness.**

She fears watching someone disappear while being unable to stop it.

She now believes she may have participated in such a disappearance through misunderstanding.

That belief must be handled carefully.

---

# 8. Neri's Grief

Neri has begun crying when Alice is mentioned.

She has also been observed staring at locations where she and Alice previously spent time together.

The scooter routes Alice commonly used have become particularly difficult for her to pass.

One Archivist reported seeing Neri standing beside an empty section of hallway for several minutes.

When asked what she was doing, she answered:

> “Waiting.”

When asked for whom:

> “I don't know.”

---

# 9. Responsibility

### Alice's disappearance is not Neri's fault.

This must be explicitly recorded.

Alice had an established history of leaving.

Alice had an established history of self-destructive behavior.

Alice had her own psychological struggles.

Neri did not know the full history.

Neri did not understand Alice's meaning.

Neri did not intentionally abandon Alice.

At the same time, Neri's feelings should not be dismissed with:

> “You did nothing wrong.”

That statement would be emotionally ineffective.

Neri believes something happened between them that mattered.

The appropriate response is therefore not to erase her regret.

It is to help her understand that:

> **Regret does not equal responsibility for another person's entire life.**

---

# 10. Alice's Condition

Alice's historical records indicate repeated episodes of running away, self-injury, starvation, dehydration, and isolation.

The records contain a diagnosis described within SAFEHOUSE documentation as **“Bipolar Personality Disorder.”**

**Medical clarification:** The terminology in older records is inconsistent with modern psychiatric diagnostic terminology and should not be treated as a current clinical diagnosis without reassessment.

Regardless of terminology, the behavioral pattern documented across the records is genuine and concerning.

Alice has repeatedly entered periods in which she isolates herself and neglects basic survival needs.

The twenty-week disappearance therefore represents an exceptionally severe escalation.

---

# 11. Search Directive

Alice remains a missing child.

The search is ongoing.

All SAFEHOUSE personnel are instructed to report:

- sightings,
    
- communications,
    
- physical traces,
    
- discarded belongings,
    
- unusual environmental disturbances,
    
- evidence of shelter,
    
- evidence of food or water access,
    
- and any other potentially relevant information.
    

Neri is **not** to be assigned to the search as a formal operative.

Her emotional involvement is too significant.

If she independently wishes to help, her participation must be evaluated by welfare staff.

She must not be permitted to turn the search into a form of self-punishment.

---

# 12. Neri's Current Understanding

Neri now understands something she previously could not.

Alice was not saying:

> **“My family doesn't love me.”**

Alice was saying:

> **“Even when people have complicated feelings, they can still love each other.”**

And beneath that was an even more important message:

> **“You have people here.”**

Neri finally understands it.

Twenty weeks too late.

---

# 13. Final Psychological Note

Neri asked one final question after reviewing the records:

> “If I understood her... would she still be here?”

The Archivist could not answer.

That answer must remain:

> **Unknown.**

Nobody knows whether understanding Alice would have changed anything.

Nobody knows why Alice left.

Nobody knows where she is.

Nobody knows whether she will return.

And nobody knows whether Neri's conversation with her had any meaningful influence on the disappearance.

What is known is that Neri finally understood what Alice had been trying to tell her.

And that realization came after Alice had already been gone for twenty weeks.

---

## FINAL ENTRY

Neri spent her childhood wishing she had a family that would keep trying.

Alice had one.

Alice was trying to show Neri what that looked like.

Neri mistook it for rejection.

Now Alice is missing.

Neri cannot undo the conversation.

She cannot go back and hear Alice again.

She cannot make herself understand sooner.

All she can do now is hope that Alice is still somewhere out there.

For the first time since Alice disappeared, Neri has stopped saying:

> “I don't care.”

She now says:

> **“I should have listened.”**
