# **KRAMPUS COMPLIANCE AUTHORITY**

## **FAMILY PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION**

### **SUBJECTS: THE LOCKWOOD SIBLINGS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 5 – Medical & Executive Personnel Only

---

## **OBSERVATION**

A comparative evaluation of the three Lockwood siblings revealed a striking difference in autobiographical memory.

Each sibling adapted to childhood trauma differently.

---

## **ARCTURUS LOCKWOOD**

Arcturus remembers the important events of his childhood.

He remembers the house.

The neighborhood.

Learning to ride horses.

His parents.

His younger sisters.

However, many ordinary childhood details have faded with time.

He often jokes that age has simply caught up with him.

> "...I barely remember what I had for breakfast yesterday."

His memories are broad rather than precise.

He remembers feelings more than conversations.

Faces more than exact words.

---

## **NORMA LOCKWOOD**

Norma's memory follows a similar pattern.

She vividly remembers engineering projects, formulas, and technical knowledge.

Yet many small childhood moments have become indistinct over the years.

She occasionally asks Arcturus questions like:

> "...Did Mom used to make soup on Sundays?"

Or...

> "...Was Corin afraid of thunderstorms, or was that me?"

She laughs whenever Arcturus cannot remember either.

---

## **CORIN LOCKWOOD**

Corin is different.

Her autobiographical memory is unusually detailed.

She can recall conversations from years ago.

The arrangement of furniture.

The weather on significant days.

Minor accounting errors from months earlier.

Names of people she met only once.

Medical staff have repeatedly described her memory as exceptional.

Ironically...

The person she struggled most to remember was her own mother.

---

## **THE MISSING MEMORIES**

For years, Corin remembered fragments.

The feeling of warm hands.

The scent of tea.

A lullaby with forgotten lyrics.

The sensation of someone brushing hair away from her face.

She knew those memories belonged to someone important.

She simply could not remember who.

During one counseling session, Corin quietly admitted:

> "...I could remember the laboratory more clearly than my own mother."

She lowered her head.

> "...That frightened me."

The realization became one of the greatest sources of her emotional distress.

Not because she believed she had stopped loving her mother.

Because she feared she had forgotten the person she loved most.

---

## **THE ETHER RESONANCE**

Counselors believe this explains why the maternal Ether resonance became such an emotionally significant presence.

Whether the phenomenon originates from Ether, Corin's subconscious, or another unknown cause remains undetermined.

What is clear is that it consistently evoked feelings of safety associated with the memories Corin had struggled to access.

Rather than replacing her mother, it appeared to help Corin reconnect with emotions that trauma had obscured.

---

## **SIBLING CONVERSATION**

One evening, Arcturus and Norma were sorting through old storage boxes.

Most contained childhood keepsakes.

Norma laughed quietly.

> "...I don't remember half of this."

Arcturus picked up a faded toy.

> "...Neither do I."

Corin looked at them.

> "...You both forgot?"

Arcturus nodded.

> "...That's normal."

Norma smiled.

> "...People forget little things."

Corin looked down.

> "...I forgot Mom."

The room became silent.

Norma immediately walked over and hugged her.

> "...No."

She rested her chin gently on Corin's shoulder.

> "...You forgot details."

> "...You never forgot loving her."

Arcturus quietly agreed.

> "...Those aren't the same thing."

---

## **CLINICAL ASSESSMENT**

Psychologists note that Corin often interprets ordinary memory gaps as personal failures.

In reality, memory naturally changes over time, especially after severe trauma.

The emotional significance Corin attaches to forgetting specific details is therefore understood as a reflection of grief rather than evidence that her bond with her mother has diminished.

---

## **DIRECTOR LUCIOUS' ANNOTATION**

Director Lucious added a brief observation after reviewing the report.

> "No memory remains perfectly intact forever."

> "The measure of love is not how many details survive."

> "It is how deeply the person continues to shape the life that follows."

He closed the file.

> "By that measure..."

> "...Corin has never forgotten her mother."

**End of Family Psychological Observation**
