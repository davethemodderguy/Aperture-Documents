# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Blacksite doors are **not commercial smart locks**.

Despite the Blacksite's widespread use of modern consumer electronics, door access is intentionally isolated from personal electronic devices.

Personnel cannot open Blacksite doors using:

- smartphones;
    
- tablets;
    
- laptops;
    
- smartwatches;
    
- Bluetooth devices;
    
- mobile applications;
    
- PIN codes;
    
- or other personal electronic authentication methods.
    

There is no "unlock door" function in the Blacksite mobile environment.

This is intentional.

---

# 2. APPROVED ACCESS METHODS

Blacksite doors have two reliable methods of authorized access:

### 1. SECURITY CENTER PCs

Authorized personnel at designated Security Center workstations can remotely operate Blacksite doors through the dedicated physical-security infrastructure.

The Security Center PCs are directly integrated with the Blacksite's access-control system.

They are **not ordinary personal computers**.

They are physically secured systems with dedicated authentication requirements and access privileges.

A Security Center operator can therefore authorize a door remotely.

A normal employee sitting at a Dell laptop cannot.

---

### 2. KEYCARDS

Authorized personnel may use their assigned Blacksite keycard at compatible access points.

The keycard system remains the primary day-to-day method of physical access.

Permissions are determined by the access-control infrastructure.

A keycard may therefore:

- open some doors;
    
- fail to open others;
    
- provide access only during authorized periods;
    
- or be completely rejected depending on the holder's permissions.

# 4. WHY PERSONAL DEVICES CANNOT OPEN DOORS

The Blacksite's computing environment is designed to isolate digital convenience from physical security.

A phone may communicate with the Blacksite.

A laptop may communicate with the Blacksite.

A tablet may communicate with the Blacksite.

None of these devices are trusted as physical access credentials.

This creates an intentional separation:

> **Digital access ≠ Physical access.**

Someone having permission to access a Blacksite database does not automatically give that person the ability to open a secured door.

Likewise, someone possessing a Blacksite phone does not possess a digital key to the building.

---

# 5. NO PIN CODES

Blacksite doors do not use PIN pads.

Personnel cannot stand outside a door and enter a numerical code.

This was deliberately rejected during the original access-control design.

The reason is straightforward:

> **Numbers can be observed.**

Keycards can be revoked.

Security Center permissions can be changed.

PINs can be memorized, written down, shared, photographed, overheard, or otherwise compromised.

The Blacksite therefore does not rely on them.

---

# 6. NO "SMARTPHONE UNLOCK"

Personnel occasionally attempt to apply modern consumer expectations to Blacksite doors.

This usually occurs with newly assigned personnel.

Common questions include:

> "Can I add the door to my phone?"

No.

> "Can I use NFC?"

No.

> "Can I use Samsung Wallet?"

No.

> "Can I pair it over Bluetooth?"

No.

> "Can I use the Blacksite app?"

There is no Blacksite door-unlock app.

> "Can IT give me a PIN?"

No.

The answer is consistently:

> **Use your keycard.**

---

# 7. RELATIONSHIP TO BLACKSITE COMPUTING

This does **not** mean Blacksite devices are technologically primitive.

The opposite is true.

Blacksite phones and laptops support ordinary modern computing.

Personnel can:

- browse the Internet;
    
- watch YouTube;
    
- communicate;
    
- work remotely;
    
- customize their devices;
    
- use productivity applications;
    
- access authorized internal systems;
    
- and perform normal computer tasks.
    

The restriction exists specifically because physical security is treated differently.

The Blacksite can provide someone with a Samsung phone capable of accessing classified information while simultaneously refusing to let that same phone open a cafeteria door.

This is intentional architecture.

---

# 8. SECURITY CENTER

The Security Center is the primary control point for centralized physical access.

Security Center PCs possess dedicated interfaces for:

- door monitoring;
    
- access authorization;
    
- security alerts;
    
- lockdown procedures;
    
- access-log review;
    
- and physical-security administration.
    

These workstations are separate from ordinary employee computers.

A C.O.R.E. engineer cannot simply sit down at their personal Dell laptop and gain the same capabilities.

Likewise, possessing administrative privileges over an ordinary Blacksite computer does not automatically provide physical door-control authority.

---

# 9. KEYCARD LOSS

If a keycard is lost, personnel are required to report it.

The card can be revoked through the physical-security system.

A replacement can then be issued.

Personnel are **not** instructed to use their phone as a temporary replacement.

There is no digital substitute.

If a person does not have an authorized keycard, they must use an authorized access procedure.

---

# 10. EMERGENCY ACCESS

Emergency access remains under the control of the Blacksite's physical-security infrastructure.

In circumstances where normal access procedures become unavailable, authorized Security Center personnel may operate doors through the dedicated security system.

This remains independent from consumer electronics.

A dead phone does not affect the Blacksite's ability to operate its doors.

A dead laptop does not affect the Blacksite's ability to operate its doors.

A person forgetting their phone does not prevent them from entering a secured area.

They simply need their keycard.

---

# 11. SAFEHOUSE IMPLICATIONS

SAFEHOUSE residents are subject to the same basic physical-security architecture.

Their ability to use phones, laptops, tablets, or other consumer technology does not grant them digital control over doors.

This distinction is particularly important for anomalous residents.

Even residents capable of extremely unusual technological or anomalous behavior do not receive automatic phone-based access to Blacksite doors.

A resident can be capable of manipulating reality and still need a keycard to enter a secured hallway.

C.O.R.E. considers this acceptable.
