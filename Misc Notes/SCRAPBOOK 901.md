# SAFEHOUSE WELFARE RECORD

## NERI — THE CAFETERIA

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL — WELFARE  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Cafeteria  
**Status:** Ongoing  
**Behavior:** Stable / Persistent

---

## 1. Current Routine

Neri has returned to her usual seat in the cafeteria.

Usually alone.

Usually with cake.

Usually quiet.

She sits near the wall, pulls her oversized coat around herself, places the plate in her lap, and eats.

Nobody bothers her.

This has gradually become an understood SAFEHOUSE tradition.

Not a rule.

Not a medical restriction.

Not an order from management.

Just something everyone learned.

**If Neri is sitting in the cafeteria eating cake alone, leave her alone.**

---

## 2. Why

There was initially concern that Neri's solitary behavior represented another period of deterioration.

Medical staff monitored her closely.

Psychological staff checked on her.

Friends occasionally asked whether she wanted company.

Eventually, however, it became apparent that Neri was not necessarily asking to be rescued from the silence.

She was mourning.

And she had been mourning for a very long time.

---

## 3. The Sister

Neri once had a younger sister.

She loved her.

Her sister liked sweets.

Cake.

Candy.

The small things that children liked.

Neri remembers loving her.

She remembers being unable to protect her.

She remembers watching her sister being **stabbed to death directly in front of her**.

Neri was there.

She saw it happen.

She could not stop it.

Nobody came to save her sister.

Nobody came to save Neri.

That moment did not end when the event ended.

It remained with her.

---

## 4. The Cake

Neri's relationship with cake is therefore more complicated than simple comfort.

She genuinely likes cake.

She likes the taste.

She likes being able to eat something sweet simply because she wants it.

She likes cakes made by people she trusts.

But sometimes she sits alone with a slice of cake and remembers someone who is no longer there to eat it.

She does not necessarily cry.

She does not necessarily talk.

She simply eats.

Sometimes she quietly says:

> **“You used to like these.”**

And then continues eating.

No one interrupts.

---

## 5. Permanent Mourning

Neri's grief is not considered an acute episode anymore.

It has become part of her life.

She does not spend every waking moment visibly crying.

She can laugh.

She can play.

She can bother Neko.

She can walk around with Alice.

She can enjoy sweets.

She can call Bao “Mom.”

She can have genuinely good days.

None of that means she stopped mourning her sister.

Neri can be happy and still miss her.

She can laugh and still remember.

She can eat cake and still grieve.

The two things are not mutually exclusive.

---

## 6. Staff Understanding

A previous assumption was that if Neri appeared happy, her grief had temporarily disappeared.

This is no longer considered accurate.

Neri does not need to be visibly suffering for her loss to remain meaningful.

Likewise, staff should not interpret her sitting alone as an automatic request for intervention.

Her solitude has become a **protected personal routine**.

Unless there is an immediate medical concern, personnel should allow her to sit.

If she wants someone, she knows where to find them.

And if she doesn't—

Someone is still nearby.

---

## 7. The Unspoken Rule

The cafeteria has developed an unofficial protocol.

Neri sits down.

Cake arrives.

Neri eats.

Everyone leaves her alone.

Nobody asks:

> “Are you okay?”

Nobody asks:

> “What are you thinking about?”

Nobody asks:

> “Are you sad?”

Nobody asks her to explain herself.

Someone may quietly leave a fresh drink beside her.

Someone may make sure she has eaten something besides cake.

Someone may check from a distance that she has not fainted.

But otherwise:

**Leave Neri be.**

---

## 8. A Normal Evening

At approximately 21:40, Neri was observed sitting in her usual location.

A slice of cake rested on the plate.

She took a bite.

She looked toward the empty chair across from her.

She stared at it for several seconds.

Then quietly said:

> **“I wish you were here.”**

She took another bite.

No one approached.

No one needed to.

After several minutes, Neri smiled faintly at something remembered.

She continued eating.

The cafeteria remained quiet.

---

## 9. Final Assessment

Neri's mourning is not something SAFEHOUSE intends to cure.

There is no requirement that she “move on.”

There is no deadline by which she is expected to stop remembering.

Her sister was a real person.

Neri loved her.

She witnessed her death.

That loss is permanent.

What SAFEHOUSE can provide is something Neri did not have when it happened:

**A place where she is allowed to remember without being alone.**

So the tradition continues.

Neri sits in the cafeteria.

Neri eats her cake.

Neri mourns.

And everyone else quietly lets her.

Because sometimes caring for someone means talking to them.

Sometimes it means holding their hand.

And sometimes it means sitting in the next room and making sure they know that, if they finally decide they don't want to be alone anymore—

**someone will still be there.**
