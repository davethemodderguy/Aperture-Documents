# 1. TRIAL ANNOUNCEMENT

Neri has officially announced the first trial for the **Sleight of Hand** authorization program.

The prize:

> **SOH-002**

The rules are considerably simpler than the Night Stalker competitions.

They are also considerably more terrifying.

Neri will provide each participant with a piece of confidential intelligence.

The participant's objective:

> **Keep the intelligence secret.**

Their opponent:

> **Jane Doe.**

Jane has been explicitly instructed to extract the information by interrogation.

She has been given permission to use her full professional skillset.

She has also been told:

> **“Don't hold back.”**

Jane reportedly smiled.

# 3. THE INTEL

The information supplied by Neri is individually selected for each participant.

The intelligence is not inherently dangerous.

Its value lies in the participant's ability to protect it under pressure.

Participants are prohibited from intentionally destroying, concealing, altering, or physically damaging the intelligence itself.

The only acceptable defense is:

> **Do not surrender it.**

---

# 4. JANE DOE — ROLE

Jane Doe has been formally designated:

> **INTERROGATOR**

Her instructions are unusually broad.

She may use:

- psychological pressure;
    
- questioning;
    
- misdirection;
    
- intimidation;
    
- silence;
    
- observation;
    
- conversational manipulation;
    
- deception;
    
- patience;
    
- strategic repetition;
    
- controlled confrontation;
    
- and any other non-physical interrogation technique approved by C.O.R.E.
    

Physical coercion is prohibited.

Jane's professional expertise is considered sufficient without it.

The interrogation room was deliberately ordinary.

One table.

Two chairs.

One observation window.

No weapons.

No restraints.

No visible equipment.

Jane entered first.

She sat down.

The participant entered.

Jane smiled.

> **JANE:**  
> “Sit.”

The participant sat.

Jane placed her hands on the table.

> **JANE:**  
> “You have something I want.”

The participant said nothing.

Jane nodded.

> **JANE:**  
> “Good.”

The interrogation began.

# 8. FAILURE CONDITION

Participants are not required to withstand an arbitrary amount of psychological distress.

They may withdraw at any time.

However:

> **Withdrawal = Failure.**

The participant must voluntarily continue until Jane herself declares defeat.

There is no timer.

There is no fixed number of questions.

There is no minimum endurance requirement.

There is only one condition for victory:

> **Jane gives up.**

---

# 9. WHY THERE ARE NO WINNERS

Several participants have already completed extended interrogation sessions.

None have succeeded.

Some surrendered.

Some yielded partial information.

Some attempted deception.

Some became visibly frightened.

One participant simply stared at Jane for approximately twelve minutes before saying:

> **“I don't like you.”**

Jane replied:

> **“That's okay.”**

The participant surrendered six minutes later.

**FAILED.**

# 11. JANE'S WARNING

Before each interrogation, Jane provides the same warning:

> **JANE:**  
> “You can leave whenever you want.”

Participants sometimes interpret this as kindness.

It is not.

Jane immediately follows with:

> **“But if you leave, you lose.”**

She then waits.

The participant always has the choice.

That choice is part of the trial.

---

# 12. THE PSYCHOLOGICAL TEST

The trial is not actually testing whether participants can withstand intimidation.

It is testing whether they can maintain control over themselves while someone else attempts to take control of the conversation.

Jane is exceptionally skilled at identifying:

- hesitation;
    
- insecurity;
    
- contradiction;
    
- changes in speech;
    
- emotional responses;
    
- defensive behavior;
    
- attempts to redirect;
    
- and moments when a participant begins considering surrender.
    

She does not need to know the answer immediately.

She only needs to keep asking questions.

# 14. JANE'S POSITION

Jane has made one thing clear.

She is not going to deliberately hurt anyone.

She is, however, going to make them want to talk.

Her objective is not simply:

> **“Tell me the intel.”**

Her objective is:

> **“Make you decide to tell me.”**

That distinction is what makes the trial unusually difficult.
