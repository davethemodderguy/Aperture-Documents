# SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL OBSERVATION

## NERI'S PROXIMITY TO JANE DOE

**Subject:** Neri  
**Observer:** Jane Doe  
**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL  
**Primary Concern:** Self-expendability and failure to report acute medical symptoms

---

## 1. A NEW ROUTINE

Following their conversations, Neri began spending considerably more time around Jane.

There was no formal reason.

Jane simply happened to be someone who was usually walking around the Blacksite.

Neri liked walking around.

Therefore:

**Jane walks around.**

**Neri walks around.**

Sometimes they talk.

Sometimes Neri carries her chemistry book.

Sometimes Jane eats.

Sometimes Neri eats.

Sometimes neither of them says anything.

It is, by SAFEHOUSE standards, completely ordinary.

Jane doesn't mind.

In fact, she seems to enjoy having Neri around.

There is one problem.

Jane occasionally receives work assignments from HR.

And when Jane is working, Neri's behavior changes.

---

# 2. NERI'S LOGIC

Neri has developed a very consistent rule:

> **If Jane is working, don't bother Jane.**

This applies even when Neri herself is experiencing symptoms.

From Neri's perspective, Jane's work is important.

Her own condition is not.

The reasoning appears to be:

> _Jane is doing something important._

> _I'm not._

> _If I interrupt her, I'm making her job harder._

> _I can wait._

The final assumption is the dangerous one.

Neri frequently assumes that she can simply wait for symptoms to pass.

Her body does not necessarily provide her with that opportunity.

---

# 3. JANE GETS ASSIGNED WORK

Jane was eventually assigned an intelligence task by HR.

Nothing unusual.

She was sitting at a workstation reviewing information when Neri wandered into the room.

Neri sat nearby.

She opened her chemistry book.

Jane continued working.

> **Jane:** "You okay?"

> **Neri:** "Mm-hm."

Jane returned to her work.

For the next several minutes, everything appeared normal.

Neri read.

Jane worked.

The room was quiet.

Then Neri stopped turning pages.

Her head slowly settled into her hands.

Her eyes remained open.

Jane did not immediately notice.

She was focused on the assignment.

Neri didn't say anything.

---

# 4. NERI'S DECISION

Neri knew something was wrong.

Her vision had started becoming blurry.

Her breathing felt increasingly difficult.

Her heart hurt.

She recognized the beginning of the episode.

And she made a decision.

She wouldn't interrupt Jane.

Instead, she remained completely still.

Jane continued working.

Neri's internal reasoning was simple:

> _She's busy._

> _Don't bother her._

> _I'll wait._

She didn't call medical.

She didn't call Bao.

She didn't tell Jane.

She didn't even move toward the door.

She simply waited.

---

# 5. JANE NOTICES

Jane eventually looked away from the monitor.

Something felt wrong.

Neri hadn't turned a page in several minutes.

> **Jane:** "Neri?"

No response.

Jane looked over.

Same posture.

Head resting against her hands.

Eyes open.

Jane immediately stood.

> **Jane:** "…No."

She moved toward Neri.

> **Jane:** "Neri."

Nothing.

Jane checked her.

Another episode.

Jane immediately contacted medical staff and Bao.

---

# 6. AFTER STABILIZATION

Neri eventually recovered.

Jane did not return to her assignment.

She sat beside Neri.

Her expression was unusually serious.

> **Jane:** "Why didn't you tell me?"

Neri looked down.

> **Neri:** "You were working."

> **Jane:** "So?"

> **Neri:** "I didn't want to bother you."

Jane stared at her.

> **Jane:** "You were having an anemic episode."

> **Neri:** "I know."

> **Jane:** "You couldn't breathe."

Neri nodded.

> **Jane:** "Your vision was going."

Another nod.

> **Jane:** "And you decided that my paperwork was more important?"

Neri hesitated.

> **Neri:** "It was your job."

Jane's expression softened slightly.

Then became serious again.

> **Jane:** "And you're my friend."

Neri didn't respond.

> **Jane:** "My job can wait."

> **Neri:** "But HR—"

> **Jane:** "HR can survive."

Neri looked uncertain.

> **Jane:** "You can't necessarily."

That silenced her.

---

# 7. THE EXPENDABLE PROBLEM

Jane finally asked the question she had been avoiding.

> **Jane:** "Do you think you're expendable?"

Neri didn't answer.

Jane waited.

Eventually:

> **Neri:** "A little."

Jane stared.

> **Jane:** "A little?"

Neri shrugged.

> **Neri:** "I'm not important."

Jane immediately recognized the statement.

It wasn't self-pity.

Neri genuinely believed it.

> **Jane:** "Who told you that?"

> **Neri:** "Nobody."

> **Jane:** "Then where did you get it?"

Neri looked down.

> **Neri:** "I just know."

Jane leaned back.

There was nothing she could extract from that answer.

It wasn't information.

It was a belief.

And beliefs aren't removed by interrogation.

---

# 8. JANE'S NEW RULE

Jane established a personal rule after that incident.

If Neri is nearby while Jane is working, Jane periodically checks on her.

Not dramatically.

Not like a medical inspection.

Just:

> **Jane:** "You okay?"

And Neri usually answers:

> **Neri:** "Yeah."

Jane then watches her for a moment.

If Neri is genuinely fine:

Jane returns to work.

If Neri becomes unusually still:

Jane stops.

If Neri's eyes remain open but she stops responding:

Jane stops immediately.

The difference is important.

Jane no longer relies exclusively on Neri's answer.

Not because she thinks Neri is lying.

Because **Neri doesn't consider withholding her own medical emergency to be lying.**

She considers it being polite.

---

# 9. NERI'S UNDERSTANDING OF "BOTHERING"

Jane eventually realized something else.

Neri's definition of _bothering someone_ is extremely broad.

Asking for food?

Maybe bothering.

Asking someone to walk with her?

Maybe bothering.

Interrupting someone?

Definitely bothering.

Asking someone to stop working because she can't breathe?

Still potentially bothering.

Jane finds this absurd.

> **Jane:** "Neri, if you are dying, you are allowed to interrupt me."

> **Neri:** "Even if you're busy?"

> **Jane:** "Especially if I'm busy."

> **Neri:** "Why?"

Jane thought about it.

> **Jane:** "Because being busy isn't more important than keeping you alive."

Neri stared at her.

> **Neri:** "Oh."

Jane pointed at her.

> **Jane:** "And don't say 'oh' like I just explained gravity."

---

# 10. THE PARTICULAR FEAR JANE HAS

Jane herself has become somewhat anxious about being assigned work while Neri is nearby.

Not because she dislikes Neri's company.

Quite the opposite.

The problem is that Jane knows exactly what Neri will do.

Neri will sit quietly.

Neri will read.

Neri will wait.

And if she begins experiencing anemic symptoms, she may decide:

> _Jane is working._

> _Don't interrupt._

Jane knows that thought could become catastrophic.

So Jane has begun telling Neri beforehand:

> **"If I get assigned something, you still bother me."**

Neri:

> **"Okay."**

> **"I mean it."**

> **"Okay."**

> **"Even if I'm talking to someone."**

> **"Okay."**

> **"Even if I'm writing something."**

> **"Okay."**

> **"Even if HR is standing directly behind me."**

Neri finally smiles.

> **Neri:** "Okay."

Jane points at her.

> **Jane:** "Good."

---

# 11. JANE'S ASSESSMENT

Jane does not believe Neri is suicidal.

That distinction is important.

Neri does not appear to actively desire death.

She enjoys cake.

She enjoys chemistry.

She enjoys walking around the Blacksite.

She enjoys spending time with friends.

She searches desperately for people she cares about when they disappear.

She wants the people around her to survive.

The problem is that she applies a completely different standard to herself.

Neri's implicit hierarchy appears to be:

**Everyone else:**

> _Must survive._

**Neri:**

> _If possible._

That is the behavior Jane considers dangerous.

---

# 12. FINAL OBSERVATION

Jane's conclusion is blunt:

> **"Neri doesn't need someone to convince her that she wants to live."**

> **"She clearly does."**

> **"She needs someone to convince her that she's included in the list of people worth keeping alive."**

Until that belief changes, Jane intends to keep watching.

Not constantly.

Not intrusively.

Just enough.

Because Jane now knows that if Neri is sitting quietly beside her while she works, an open pair of eyes does not necessarily mean Neri is awake.

And silence does not necessarily mean Neri is fine.

Sometimes silence means:

> **"Jane is busy."**

And sometimes, that sentence could be the difference between Neri getting help and nobody realizing something is wrong.

**END OF OBSERVATION**
