## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At approximately 20:14, Neri approached Jane Doe in the cafeteria and handed her a small piece of paper.

The paper contained only four words:

> **“Am I useful”**

Jane initially interpreted the question as a request for reassurance regarding Neri's value to SAFEHOUSE.

She attempted to explain that Neri's worth was not dependent upon what she could provide to others.

However, several of Jane's chosen words were poorly received.

Neri interpreted Jane's explanation as confirmation that she was, in fact, **not particularly useful**, but was being kept because the residents cared about her.

Jane recognized the misunderstanding only after Neri's behavior changed.

The exchange is considered another example of the danger surrounding Neri's established belief that **usefulness determines whether she deserves to remain with others.**

## INVESTIGATION

The interaction demonstrates a significant communication hazard.

Jane's intended message was:

> **“Your usefulness has nothing to do with whether you belong here.”**

Neri appears to have received:

> **“You aren't particularly useful, but we keep you anyway because we care about you.”**

The distinction is obvious to an individual without Neri's established history.

To Neri, it is considerably less obvious.

Her internal framework remains approximately:

**Useful → Needed → Kept**

**Not useful → Unnecessary → Eventually discarded**

Therefore, removing “usefulness” from the equation does not automatically reassure her.

It creates a new and frightening question:

> **“If I don't have to be useful, what makes me worth keeping?”**

Jane's final statement — **“You don't have to earn us”** — appeared to affect Neri more strongly than the preceding explanations.

Neri's response:

> **“I DON'T KNOW HOW TO DO THAT”**

is considered significant.

It indicates that the difficulty may no longer be convincing Neri that other residents care about her.

The difficulty is understanding how to accept care that she has not earned through usefulness.

## FOLLOW-UP

Jane did not pursue Neri after she left.

This was deliberate.

She did not demand that Neri return, apologize, explain herself, or continue the conversation.

Later that evening, Jane placed a small note on the cafeteria table where Neri normally sits.

The note contained:

> **You don't have to prove anything to me.**
> 
> **You don't have to be useful.**
> 
> **You don't even have to talk to me.**
> 
> **You're still my Cute Seal.**
> 
> **— Jane**

No response was recorded.

Approximately thirty minutes later, the note was found folded neatly beside Neri's coffee.

It had not been discarded.

---

## C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jane's statement was not intentionally harmful.

However, this incident demonstrates that reassurance based around **“you don't need to be useful”** can paradoxically reinforce Neri's insecurity if the distinction between _“not required to be useful”_ and _“not useful”_ is not made carefully.

Future personnel should avoid framing Neri's value as either:

- useful,
    
- useless,
    
- needed,
    
- unnecessary,
    
- productive,
    
- unproductive,
    
- valuable because of what she provides.
    

The preferred framing is simpler:

> **“You are here because you belong here.”**

If Neri asks:

> **“Am I useful?”**

the recommended response is not:

> “You don't need to be useful.”

It should instead begin with:

> **“You are Neri. You are one of us. You don't have to earn your place here.”**

---

### FINAL NOTE

Neri's question was not really about productivity.

She was asking Jane a much more frightening question:

> **“If I stop being useful, will you still keep me?”**

Jane answered yes.

Neri simply did not yet know how to believe her.
