### **ANOMALOUS PHYSIOLOGY / ENVIRONMENTAL RESPONSE REPORT**

# **CORIN — WING WATER SATURATION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Phenomenon:** **Extreme Water Absorption**  
**Status:** Confirmed

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin's feathers possess an unusually high capacity for absorbing and retaining water.

Under normal conditions, this is harmless.

During heavy rain, however, the effect becomes increasingly problematic.

As the feathers become saturated, the wings gain substantial effective weight.

For Corin, this transforms her wings from a source of mobility into a significant physical burden.

---

# **FEATHER ABSORPTION**

The feathers readily absorb water rather than allowing it to simply run off their surface.

This produces several effects:

- Increased feather mass.
    
- Increased wing weight.
    
- Reduced maneuverability.
    
- Increased strain on the wing joints.
    
- Difficulty maintaining the wings in a folded position.
    
- Significant discomfort.
    

The wings can become so saturated that Corin has difficulty moving them normally.

---

# **PHYSICAL EFFECT**

Corin's wings are already large and structurally substantial.

When saturated, their additional weight places considerable strain on her back.

She has described the sensation as:

> **"Like someone tied weights to my spine."**

During particularly heavy rainfall, she has also described a persistent aching sensation across the wing roots.

The pain does not originate from the feathers themselves.

It comes from the additional load being supported by the muscles and structures connecting the wings to her body.

---

# **FLIGHT RESTRICTION**

Corin should not attempt prolonged flight with heavily saturated wings.

The additional weight significantly reduces her flight efficiency.

Attempting to take off while soaked requires substantially more effort.

Once airborne, maneuverability is reduced.

For this reason:

> **RAIN + FLIGHT = STRONGLY DISCOURAGED**

Corin has learned this herself.

---

# **CORIN'S EXPERIENCE**

After being caught in an unexpected downpour, Corin returned to the treehouse with her wings completely soaked.

She could barely keep them lifted.

Orphie immediately noticed.

> **Orphie:**  
> "Are they hurting?"

Corin nodded.

> **"They're so heavy."**

She lowered herself onto the floor.

> **"I hate this."**

Orphie helped her dry the feathers.

As the water was removed, Corin gradually regained normal movement.

---

# **WHY SNOW IS DIFFERENT**

Snow does not produce the same effect.

Snow accumulates **on** the feathers rather than being absorbed into them as efficiently as liquid water.

This allows Corin to simply shake her wings.

The snow falls away.

The resulting effect is one of the reasons Corin enjoys snowy weather.

Rain, meanwhile, penetrates deeply into the feather structure.

She cannot simply shake it off.

---

# **WATER REMOVAL**

Corin can remove some excess water by vigorously shaking her wings.

However, this is only partially effective when the feathers are heavily saturated.

The preferred method is allowing the wings to dry naturally in a warm, sheltered environment.

Controlled airflow is also effective.

High heat is discouraged due to the sensitivity of the Ether-integrated feather structures.

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECT**

The physical burden contributes significantly to Corin's dislike of rain.

Her wings represent freedom to her.

They allow her to fly wherever she wants.

When soaked, that freedom is temporarily removed.

The same wings that normally carry her become something she has to physically support.

This is particularly frustrating for Corin.

She once described the sensation as:

> **"My wings are supposed to let me go anywhere."**

Then:

> **"When they're wet, they just drag me down."**

---

# **CURRENT PRECAUTIONS**

During severe rain, Corin is advised to:

- Remain indoors when possible.
    
- Avoid prolonged flight.
    
- Seek shelter immediately if caught outdoors.
    
- Dry the wings thoroughly after exposure.
    
- Avoid folding heavily saturated wings for extended periods.
    
- Allow the wing roots to rest if discomfort develops.
    

Corin generally follows these precautions.

Mostly because she really does not want to experience it again.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin loves her wings.

They gave her freedom.

They let her fly to the Blacksite.

They let her carry Orphie.

They gave her a reason to look toward the sky again.

But water reveals an unfortunate weakness.

Her feathers absorb it extremely well.

Too well.

A dry wing carries Corin through the air.

A soaked wing becomes a collection of heavy weights attached to her back.

Snow makes her smile.

Rain makes her fold her wings, find shelter, and wait for them to dry.
