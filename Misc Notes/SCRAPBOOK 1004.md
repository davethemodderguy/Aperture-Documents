# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the discovery of Neri's birthday card, C.O.R.E. has identified a significant behavioral change.

Neri has established a new internal rule:

> **Do not bother anyone.**

Unlike her previous tendency to suppress symptoms, this is now appearing to function as a conscious personal directive.

The rule applies regardless of severity.

Including medical emergencies.

Including anemic shock.

Including situations in which Neri may be unable to safely recover without assistance.

Neri has apparently concluded that requesting assistance imposes an unacceptable burden upon other residents.

This belief is incorrect.

Its consequences may nevertheless be severe.

---

# 2. THE RULE

Neri's apparent reasoning can be summarized as follows:

> **People are busy.**

Therefore:

> **Interrupting them is bothering them.**

Therefore:

> **My problems are less important than whatever they are doing.**

Therefore:

> **I should handle them myself.**

And, most dangerously:

> **If I cannot handle them myself, I should wait.**

This is not a medical protocol.

It is a psychological survival rule.

---

# 3. WHY THE BIRTHDAY MATTERS

The birthday incident appears to have reinforced an existing belief.

Neri had wanted nothing from anyone.

She did not ask for a celebration.

She did not tell anyone it was her birthday.

She did not interrupt anyone's work.

She made her own cake.

She wrote her own birthday card.

She stayed alone.

From Neri's perspective, this may have been interpreted as successful behavior.

She wanted to avoid becoming a burden.

She succeeded.

Nobody had to stop working for her.

Nobody had to dedicate time to her.

Nobody had to rearrange their schedules.

Nobody had to celebrate her.

The fact that this outcome caused significant emotional harm does not appear to have altered her conclusion.

It may have strengthened it.

---

# 4. MEDICAL IMPLICATION

This behavior is particularly dangerous because Neri's medical condition does not reliably provide her with enough warning to seek assistance.

Her altered blood physiology can result in severe anemia without significant preceding activity.

As red blood cells are destroyed by her own white blood cells, Neri can deteriorate rapidly.

Bao's blood temporarily suppresses the abnormal immune response and provides replacement blood cells.

Therefore, seeking assistance is not an optional convenience.

It can be medically necessary.

Neri appears to understand this intellectually.

She does not appear to believe that her own medical needs justify interrupting someone.

This distinction is critical.

---

Several behaviors have subsequently been documented.

- avoids eye contact when approached;
    
- refuses to interrupt conversations;

- attempts to continue whatever activity she was performing;

When asked whether she was feeling sick, she again shook her head.

She subsequently remained alone.

No verbal explanation was provided.

Her inability to speak complicates assessment further.

---

# 6. THE MOST CONCERNING DEVELOPMENT

The concern is not that Neri believes she should be independent.

Independence is not inherently dangerous.

The concern is that Neri appears to have equated **asking for help** with **causing harm to others.**

She may therefore prioritize avoiding inconvenience over preserving her own life.

A hypothetical internal exchange may resemble:

> _I'm getting dizzy._

> _I should tell Bao._

> _Bao is busy._

> _I can wait._

> _I'm getting worse._

> _Jane is working._

> _I shouldn't bother her._

> _I might faint._

> _Someone will notice eventually._

This final assumption is particularly dangerous.

# 10. RESIDENTIAL WELFARE DIRECTIVE

Personnel are instructed to avoid framing assistance as an inconvenience.

Do not say:

> "Why didn't you tell me earlier?"

Do not say:

> "You should have interrupted me."

Do not say:

> "You made everyone worry."

Do not use guilt as an incentive for future disclosure.

These statements may unintentionally reinforce Neri's existing belief that her medical needs create problems for others.

Instead, personnel should communicate:

> **"You are allowed to interrupt me."**

> **"You are not bothering me."**

> **"You don't have to wait until it gets worse."**

> **"You can come to me even if I'm busy."**

> **"You don't have to apologize for being sick."**

---

# 11. SPECIAL CONCERN: ANEMIC SHOCK

If Neri displays symptoms consistent with an acute medical episode, personnel should not wait for her to request assistance.

Her current behavioral rule makes voluntary disclosure unreliable.

A lack of a request for help must **not** be interpreted as a lack of need.

In particular:

**Neri saying nothing does not mean Neri is fine.**

**Neri shaking her head does not necessarily mean she is fine.**

**Neri walking away does not necessarily mean she wants to be alone.**

**Neri apologizing does not mean the situation is resolved.**

Her silence may instead mean:

> _I'm trying not to bother you._
