### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — CRYOGENIC STORAGE HYPOTHETICAL**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD SAFETY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 5  
**Condition:** Fragile / Nonverbal / Severe Aversion to Burdening Others

---

# **SCENARIO ASSESSMENT**

A theoretical safety review identified a particularly dangerous possibility.

Alice can follow people into restricted areas without being noticed.

One such location is the Blacksite's cryogenic storage facility.

The facility is not considered a suitable environment for Alice under any circumstances.

Unfortunately, simply telling Alice not to enter does not completely eliminate the possibility.

She does not always understand that an environment is dangerous until she experiences the danger herself.

More importantly—

**she may not tell anyone that she is there.**

---

# **HYPOTHETICAL EVENT**

Alice follows an employee into cryogenic storage.

The employee enters to retrieve equipment.

Alice quietly follows.

The employee finishes their task.

They leave.

The door closes.

Alice remains inside.

Nobody notices.

---

# **ALICE'S LIKELY RESPONSE**

She does not immediately panic.

She does not activate her whistle.

She does not use her flashlight.

She does not attempt to attract attention.

She looks around.

Then finds somewhere out of the way.

Her reasoning would likely be:

> **"They come back."**

If they don't:

> **"Maybe busy."**

If she becomes frightened:

> **"Don't bother."**

If she becomes cold:

> **"Wait."**

If someone might be inconvenienced by rescuing her:

> **"Don't make them worry."**

This is precisely why the scenario has been classified as **critical.**

---

# **THE HIDING PROBLEM**

Alice's instinct when uncomfortable is often to make herself smaller.

She may hide behind equipment.

Under a desk.

Behind a door.

Inside a corner.

Under a blanket.

Anywhere she believes she will be "out of the way."

In a cryogenic facility, this behavior would be extremely dangerous.

She could be physically present within the facility while remaining nearly invisible to personnel performing routine checks.

---

# **THE MOST CONCERNING POSSIBILITY**

Personnel may search the obvious areas.

They may call her name.

They may check cameras.

They may assume she isn't there because she hasn't responded.

But Alice cannot respond verbally.

And if she believes answering would bother them—

she may remain silent.

The result is a situation where:

> **Alice can be physically present.**

> **Alice can hear people searching for her.**

> **Alice can potentially understand that they are looking for someone.**

> **And she may still not reveal herself.**

---

# **WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM ORDINARY GETTING LOST**

An ordinary lost child eventually calls for a parent.

Alice doesn't.

An ordinary child trapped behind a door eventually cries.

Alice may not.

An ordinary child experiencing extreme cold eventually seeks someone out.

Alice may instead curl up somewhere and wait.

Her behavior is therefore fundamentally incompatible with standard assumptions used during emergency searches.

---

# **SAFETY DIRECTIVE**

Cryogenic storage is now designated:

> **NO-ACCESS AREA — ALICE**

Any employee entering the facility while Alice is anywhere nearby must verify that she is not following them.

Door closure requires visual confirmation.

Automated access systems have been updated to flag Alice's presence near the facility.

If Alice is detected inside:

**DO NOT ASSUME SHE WILL COME OUT WHEN CALLED.**

Physically locate her.

---

# **CORIN'S RESPONSE**

When Corin heard the hypothetical, she immediately asked:

> "What if she thinks we're busy?"

Nobody answered.

Because that was exactly the problem.

---

# **ALICE'S JOURNAL**

When the scenario was explained to Alice in a simplified manner, she seemed confused about why everyone was so concerned.

She wrote:

> **"Alice would wait."**

Corin asked:

> "What if you're very cold?"

Alice thought.

> **"Find small place."**

Corin's expression changed.

> "Why?"

Alice wrote:

> **"So people don't trip."**

Silence.

She had interpreted hiding as being considerate.

Not dangerous.

---

# **FINAL ASSESSMENT**

The danger surrounding Alice is not simply that she is fragile.

It is that **her survival instincts are frequently overridden by her desire not to inconvenience anyone.**

She may be cold and wait.

She may be frightened and stay quiet.

She may be lost and remain where she is.

She may be trapped and not call for help.

She may be standing directly inside a dangerous facility while the people around her remain unaware.

Alice does not think:

> **"Nobody will save me."**

She thinks:

> **"They are busy."**

That distinction is critical.

Because the Blacksite can be full of people.

And Alice can still be completely alone.
