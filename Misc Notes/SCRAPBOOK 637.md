### **CORIN / PERSONAL ARCHIVE ANALYSIS**

# **THE NOTEBOOK — A COMPLETE RECORD OF CORIN**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Document:** Personal Medical / Psychological / Autobiographical Notebook  
**Estimated Duration:** **From Corin's return to life — Present**  
**Status:** **CRITICAL PERSONAL RECORD**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

The notebook recovered from the Treehouse is considerably more significant than initially believed.

It is not simply a diary.

It is not merely a collection of drawings.

It is not solely a record of Corin's thoughts during Orphie's disappearance.

It is a **long-term self-documentation project**.

Hundreds of pages record Corin's observations of herself from the moment she became alive again to the present day.

She documented nearly everything she could observe.

Her body.

Her abilities.

Her physical changes.

Her emotions.

Her fears.

Her relationships.

Her failures.

Her recovery.

Her pain.

Her happiness.

And eventually, her deterioration.

---

# **THE BEGINNING**

The earliest pages were written shortly after Corin became alive.

The writing is noticeably different from the later material.

Messy.

Curious.

Excited.

Several pages contain drawings of herself from different angles.

One of the earliest entries reads:

> **"I'm alive."**

Underneath:

> **"I don't know how."**

Then:

> **"But I'm here."**

The page contains an unusually large number of exclamation marks.

---

# **EARLY PHYSIOLOGICAL OBSERVATIONS**

Corin began documenting her body almost immediately.

She recorded:

- Heart rate.
    
- Breathing.
    
- Sleep.
    
- Appetite.
    
- Wing movement.
    
- Feather growth.
    
- Regeneration.
    
- Blood behavior.
    
- Physical strength.
    
- Changes in vision.
    
- Changes in her senses.
    
- Her ability to fly.
    
- Her ability to transform.
    

Some observations are extremely mundane.

> **"My wings are itchy today."**

> **"I think I grew another feather."**

> **"My left foot feels weird."**

Others are considerably more important.

> **"I have two hearts."**

> **"My blood isn't normal."**

> **"I heal faster when I'm happy."**

---

# **THE TWO HEARTS**

Corin appears to have discovered the presence of her second heart herself.

A diagram shows her torso with two hearts drawn separately.

An arrow points toward each.

Beside the drawing:

> **"WHY ARE THERE TWO?"**

Several pages later:

> **"Okay. Apparently both work."**

Eventually she began monitoring their behavior.

She noticed that emotional stress appeared to influence her physical condition.

---

# **THE WINGS**

Hundreds of sketches document Corin's wings.

She drew them:

- Fully extended.
    
- Folded.
    
- Damaged.
    
- Healing.
    
- Wet.
    
- Covered in feathers.
    
- Missing feathers.
    
- Armored.
    
- In flight.
    

Some pages contain technical drawings.

Others are simply:

> **"Pretty."**

---

# **THE FEATHERS**

Corin spent an extraordinary amount of time studying her feathers.

She documented their:

- Structure.
    
- Growth.
    
- Durability.
    
- Edibility.
    
- Combustibility.
    
- Kinetic properties.
    
- Lockpicking capability.
    
- Projectile capability.
    
- Interaction with her abilities.
    
- Interaction with Nekonium armor.
    

One page contains a simple list:

> **"Feather uses:"**

> **"Light."**

> **"Fire."**

> **"Boom."**

> **"Throw."**

> **"Lockpick."**

> **"Apparently food."**

Someone later added beneath it:

> **"Do not eat burning ones."**

The handwriting is not Corin's.

---

# **THE ILLNESS**

The notebook contains extensive documentation from Corin's mercury poisoning.

Unlike the later emotional entries, these pages are highly clinical.

Corin records her symptoms.

Pain.

Weakness.

Organ discomfort.

Regeneration.

Attempts to remain hydrated.

She describes the pain as:

> **"My heart and organs being constantly stabbed."**

She also repeatedly reassures herself:

> **"I'm not dying."**

Eventually:

> **"I got it out."**

The following pages document her extremely slow recovery.

---

# **THE MINIATURE CAT FORM**

Corin also documented her small cat form.

Several pages contain drawings of:

- Her tiny wings.
    
- Cat ears.
    
- Cat fangs.
    
- Loafing.
    
- Sleeping in jars.
    
- Sitting inside containers.
    
- Softly nibbing people.
    

One entry:

> **"I'm small."**

Another:

> **"Very small."**

Then:

> **"Jar."**

The following page is a drawing of Corin inside a jar.

---

# **THE NORMAL LIFE**

The middle section of the notebook is significantly happier.

Corin records:

- Cooking with Belle.
    
- Flying with the SAFEHOUSE residents.
    
- Playing with others.
    
- Her outfits.
    
- Her cat form.
    
- Flying.
    
- Her feathers.
    
- Her family.
    
- Quiet moments in the Treehouse.
    

There are numerous drawings of the SAFEHOUSE.

Some pages simply document ordinary days.

> **"Nothing happened today."**

> **"It was nice."**

These pages are among the most heavily decorated.

---

# **THE WING INCIDENT**

The pressure cooker accident receives an unusually large section.

Corin documents the injury to her left wing.

The pain.

The missing feathers.

The bandages.

The regeneration.

Her frustration at being unable to fly.

One page contains a drawing of the injured wing.

Beneath it:

> **"I hate looking at it."**

Another:

> **"Flying keeps my head quiet."**

Later:

> **"I want to fly again."**

Eventually:

> **"I can fly."**

The handwriting becomes noticeably happier.

---

# **THE ARMOR**

Corin documented the creation of her Nekonium wing armor.

She was apparently fascinated by its ability to:

- Block bullets.
    
- Store kinetic energy.
    
- Redistribute force.
    
- Transfer stored kinetic energy into her feathers.
    

One entry reads:

> **"Neko made me indestructible wings."**

Then:

> **"This is probably a terrible idea."**

Several pages later:

> **"It was a terrible idea."**

The railgun incident is documented extensively.

---

# **THE FEATHER THAT DESTROYED FIVE WALLS**

Corin documented the feather that absorbed the railgun's impact and subsequently released the stored kinetic energy.

She drew the feather.

Then five walls.

Then five alarm symbols.

Underneath:

> **"Oops."**

The feather eventually became part of the Blacksite museum collection.

Corin later wrote:

> **"They put my feather in a museum."**

Then:

> **"That's kind of cool."**

---

# **THE CHANGE**

The notebook eventually reaches the period following Orphie's disappearance.

The tone changes.

The cheerful drawings become less frequent.

The pages become increasingly sparse.

Corin begins recording herself more clinically.

She monitors:

- Appetite.
    
- Sleep.
    
- Movement.
    
- Emotional state.
    
- Feather brightness.
    
- Social interaction.
    
- Hope.
    

Her writing becomes an attempt to understand why she is deteriorating.

---

# **THE BODY AND THE MIND**

Corin eventually makes a connection between her physical and psychological states.

She writes:

> **"My body listens to me."**

Then:

> **"That's probably bad."**

Later:

> **"I think my body runs on hope."**

This becomes one of the central theories of the notebook.

---

# **THE BLACK EYES**

The Black Eye drawings become increasingly common.

They appear beside entries concerning:

- Self-hatred.
    
- Helplessness.
    
- Abandonment.
    
- Depression.
    
- Self-harm.
    
- Worthlessness.
    
- Isolation.
    

The later pages are sometimes almost entirely covered by them.

---

# **THE BLUE INK**

Blue ink appears frequently during discussions of Guiding Light.

Initially, the references are hopeful.

Later, they become angry.

Eventually:

> **"I hate you."**

followed by:

> **"Why didn't you find me?"**

Then:

> **"Don't guide me anymore."**

The blue writing increasingly becomes overwritten with blackened blood.

---

# **THE BLOOD**

Corin's own blood appears throughout some of the darkest sections.

Environmental interaction causes it to become black and toxic.

Corin appears to have intentionally used it as writing material.

The pages containing it are predominantly associated with severe emotional distress.

---

# **THE FINAL SECTIONS**

The final pages are significantly different from the beginning.

There is almost no curiosity.

Almost no excitement.

Very few drawings of flowers.

Very little humor.

Mostly:

> **Observations.**

> **Questions.**

> **Silence.**

And eyes.

---

# **THE COMPLETE RECORD**

The notebook therefore represents something extraordinary.

Corin did not begin documenting herself because she expected something terrible to happen.

She began because she was alive.

She wanted to understand herself.

She wanted to know:

> **What am I?**

Over hundreds of pages, that question gradually became:

> **Why am I like this?**

Then:

> **What's wrong with me?**

And finally:

> **Is there anything left of me?**

---

# **C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT**

The notebook should be considered one of the most comprehensive records of Corin's existence.

It contains years of information that no conventional medical examination could reproduce.

More importantly, it documents Corin's internal state continuously.

From:

**Life**

→ **Curiosity**

→ **Discovery**

→ **Connection**

→ **Pain**

→ **Recovery**

→ **Happiness**

→ **Loss**

→ **Hope**

→ **Abandonment**

→ **Self-abandonment**

The notebook is not simply a record of what happened to Corin.

It is a record of **who Corin believed herself to be at each stage of her life.**

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

The first page says:

> **"I'm alive."**

The later pages ask:

> **"Why am I like this?"**

Then:

> **"What's wrong with me?"**

And eventually:

> **"I don't need me either."**

Hundreds of pages separate those statements.

Hundreds of pages of Corin learning how her body works.

Hundreds of pages of her documenting her wings.

Her hearts.

Her feathers.

Her blood.

Her abilities.

Her friends.

Her family.

Her happiness.

Her pain.

Her recovery.

Her fears.

Her hope.

And then, slowly, her loss of it.

The notebook began as a way for Corin to understand **why she was alive.**

It eventually became a record of what happened when she stopped believing she had a reason to remain so.
