# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Neri's anemia is **not reliably associated with exertion, injury, poor nutrition, or blood loss.**

It can occur at any time.

Neri may spend an entire day doing absolutely nothing strenuous, eating cake, drinking something sweet, talking with friends, and otherwise appearing perfectly healthy.

Her blood will still gradually deteriorate.

The current understanding is that Neri's altered physiology causes the blood circulating within her body to progressively **disintegrate or become nonfunctional** over time.

The process appears to be continuous.

The rate varies.

The trigger does not appear to be external.

Eventually, her circulating blood reaches a critical threshold.

At that point, her cardiovascular system can no longer sustain itself.

Her heart stops.

As Neri's effective blood supply decreases, her skin can become progressively pale.

The change may be subtle initially.

Later it becomes unmistakable.

Personnel familiar with Neri have therefore been instructed to take sudden pallor seriously even if:

- She has been resting all day.
    
- She has eaten adequately.
    
- She has not been injured.
    
- She claims to feel fine.
    
- She has spent the previous several hours eating cake.
    

Especially the last one.

# 6. WHY SUGAR HELPS

Sugar does not replace the blood that Neri is losing functionally.

However, readily available energy can temporarily assist her body while the underlying problem is being addressed.

This can help during early-stage weakness or energy depletion.

Therefore:

> **Cake can help.**

But:

> **Cake cannot restart a stopped heart.**

This distinction is now prominently displayed in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria.

It does not permanently cure the underlying condition.

It simply gives Neri's body enough functional supply to continue operating.

Eventually, the process of blood degradation resumes.
