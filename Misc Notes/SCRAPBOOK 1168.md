**SUBJECT:** Neri — Temporary Lower-Limb Paralysis

---

# 1. MEDICAL UPDATE

Further examination has identified an additional consequence of Neri's recent impact.

Neri is experiencing **temporary paralysis from the legs downward.**

Her lower limbs currently demonstrate severely reduced voluntary movement.

This is believed to be a consequence of the trauma sustained during the previous incident.

The condition is currently considered **temporary**.

However, the duration of recovery cannot yet be predicted with certainty.

---

# 2. CURRENT FUNCTION

Neri retains normal movement in her:

- head;
    
- arms;
    
- hands;
    
- upper body.
    

Her lower body is different.

She cannot currently:

- stand;
    
- walk;
    
- climb;
    
- support her own weight with her legs;
    
- or voluntarily reposition herself using her lower limbs.
    

Her seal tail also remains largely unresponsive.

When asked whether she can feel her legs, Neri indicated that sensation is inconsistent.

At times she describes them as:

> **“Nothing.”**

At other times:

> **“Tingling.”**

She has not reported complete loss of sensation at all times.

---

# 3. IMPACT ON MOBILITY

The previous mobility guidance is therefore temporarily superseded.

Neri is not merely too weak to walk.

She is **physically unable to walk at present.**

She must be moved by other means.

Current approved transportation includes:

- being carried by trusted personnel;
    
- wheelchair or equivalent mobility equipment where appropriate;
    
- carefully assisted repositioning;
    
- or remaining stationary until assistance is available.
    

Neri should not be encouraged to attempt walking independently.

---

# 4. NERI'S REACTION

Neri appears more frustrated by the loss of mobility than frightened by it.

She repeatedly looks down at her legs.

She attempts to move them.

Nothing happens.

After several attempts, she stops.

She then looks toward whoever is nearby.

> **Neri:**  
> “...No.”

Bao understood immediately.

> **BAO:**  
> “They aren't moving?”

Neri nodded.

---

# 5. CARDIAC PAIN

The temporary paralysis is occurring alongside her existing severe pain.

The cardiac-region pain remains the dominant complaint.

This creates an unusual situation.

Neri is simultaneously:

- unable to walk;
    
- experiencing significant chest/heart-region pain;
    
- emotionally distressed;
    
- and dependent on others for transportation.
    

Medical personnel are therefore monitoring her closely.

---

# 6. TRANSPORTATION

Neri has begun accepting transportation without attempting to compensate for it.

When Jane asked:

> **JANE:**  
> “Want me to carry you?”

Neri nodded immediately.

Jane carefully picked her up.

Neri's legs remained completely still.

Jane noticed.

She did not comment.

She simply adjusted her hold to ensure Neri was comfortable.

After several seconds:

> **Neri:**  
> “Thank you.”

Jane quietly replied:

> **“You're welcome.”**

---

# 7. BAO'S INSTRUCTIONS

Bao has issued the following guidance to SAFEHOUSE personnel:

> **“Do not make her try to walk.”**

> **“Do not test whether her legs work.”**

> **“Do not keep asking if she can move them.”**

> **“If she needs to go somewhere, carry her or use appropriate equipment.”**

> **“Let her recover.”**

This guidance has been distributed to MSAC.

---

# 8. PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECT

The loss of mobility has created an additional source of vulnerability.

Neri already dislikes being dependent on other people.

She has historically interpreted needing assistance as evidence that she is becoming a burden.

Medical personnel have therefore observed her watching people who carry her with unusual attention.

She appears to be checking whether they are becoming annoyed.

This has been addressed by simply continuing normal behavior.

No dramatic reassurance is necessary.

No one needs to make her apologize.

She needs transportation.

So she receives transportation.

# 11. PROGNOSIS

Medical personnel currently consider the paralysis **temporary**.

Recovery is expected to occur gradually.

However, C.O.R.E. will not provide Neri with an artificial recovery deadline.

She does not need to be told:

> “You'll be walking tomorrow.”

She does not need to be told:

> “You'll be fine in a week.”

Until her neurological function improves, the only accurate statement is:

> **“Your legs aren't working right now. We're going to keep you safe while they recover.”**

Neri accepted this explanation.

She did not appear reassured.

But she stopped trying to force her legs to move.
