# SAFEHOUSE MEDICAL INCIDENT

## NERI — MULTISYSTEM PAIN DURING ACUTE ANEMIC EPISODE

**Classification:** MEDICAL — CRITICAL  
**Resident:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Incident Type:** Acute spontaneous anemia / severe pain presentation  
**Status:** Survived — continued monitoring required  
**Emergency Contact:** Bao

---

## 1. INCIDENT SUMMARY

Neri experienced another spontaneous anemic episode.

Unlike several previous episodes, this event was accompanied by **visibly severe pain**.

Neri was conscious during portions of the episode and demonstrated significant distress.

She reported pain affecting multiple areas of her body, including:

- the chest/heart region,
    
- bones,
    
- skin,
    
- back,
    
- hands,
    
- and legs.
    

The pain appeared to occur in multiple areas rather than remaining localized to a single site.

When asked to describe the sensation, Neri gave an answer that immediately concerned the medical staff:

> **“It's like what my mom did to me when I get bad grades.”**

---

# 2. NERI'S DESCRIPTION

Neri does not possess the medical vocabulary necessary to describe the underlying physiological process.

She therefore explains her symptoms through comparison to experiences she already understands.

During this episode, she repeatedly indicated that:

> **“It hurts.”**

When asked where:

> **“My heart...”**

followed by references to pain in her bones, skin, back, hands, and legs.

Her description was not consistent with ordinary dizziness or weakness alone.

This was visibly painful.

---

# 3. CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Neri's underlying blood abnormality has historically presented primarily through:

- blurred vision,
    
- weakness,
    
- breathing difficulty,
    
- pallor,
    
- loss of consciousness,
    
- and cardiovascular instability.
    

The emergence of pronounced, widespread pain during an episode represents a significant change in presentation.

The medical team is therefore treating this episode as evidence that the progression of Neri's condition may involve **increasing systemic physiological distress**.

The exact mechanism remains under investigation.

---

# 4. CARDIAC PAIN

The most concerning complaint was repeated pain around Neri's heart.

Neri did not consistently distinguish between individual sensations.

Her descriptions included variations of:

> **“It hurts here.”**

while indicating her chest.

She appeared frightened by the sensation.

This is particularly concerning given Neri's known cardiovascular fragility.

Her heart has previously approached complete failure during severe episodes.

Consequently, chest pain during an active anemic event cannot be dismissed as a simple symptom of discomfort.

---

# 5. PAIN IN BONES AND SKIN

Neri additionally reported pain throughout areas of her body that would not normally be expected to be described as a single localized injury.

She described her:

- bones hurting,
    
- skin hurting,
    
- back hurting,
    
- hands hurting,
    
- legs hurting.
    

The pain appeared to be diffuse.

Neri repeatedly moved or held different parts of her body as though attempting to find a position that hurt less.

No single external injury adequately explained the presentation.

---

# 6. THE CHILDHOOD ASSOCIATION

The statement:

> **“It's like what my mom did to me when I get bad grades.”**

was particularly significant.

Neri was not making a metaphorical comparison to an abstract concept.

She was referencing a real childhood experience of punishment and pain.

This means that the physical sensations produced by the episode were sufficiently severe to trigger a direct association with one of her traumatic memories.

The statement was subsequently documented without asking Neri to provide additional details.

**No further questioning was considered appropriate during the acute episode.**

---

# 7. PSYCHOLOGICAL RESPONSE

The pain appeared to produce a combination of:

- fear,
    
- confusion,
    
- distress,
    
- and emotional regression.
    

Neri became noticeably more childlike.

Her vocabulary became simpler.

She sought reassurance from people she trusted.

She was less interested in understanding what was happening and more concerned with making the pain stop.

This is an important distinction.

When healthy and stable, Neri can discuss extremely complicated chemistry.

During severe medical distress, she is simply a ten-year-old child who hurts.

---

# 8. BAO

Bao remained the primary person Neri sought during the incident.

Neri's relationship with Bao is particularly significant because Bao has repeatedly been responsible for stabilizing her during previous critical episodes.

When Neri recognizes Bao, she tends to seek physical proximity.

During this episode, the pattern was again evident.

Neri did not ask for a scientific explanation.

She wanted her mother.

---

# 9. WHY THIS EPISODE IS DIFFERENT

Previous incidents have established that Neri can become critically anemic without obvious external warning.

The cafeteria incident demonstrated that she can even become unconscious while appearing to simply read a book.

This episode adds another concern:

> **Her episodes may increasingly involve severe pain before loss of consciousness.**

This potentially gives staff another warning sign.

However, personnel must not assume that pain will always occur.

Neri can still experience a severe episode without obvious distress.

---

# 10. MEDICAL RESPONSE

During an acute episode, the priority remains stabilization.

Personnel should:

1. Keep Neri safe and prevent injury from collapse.
    
2. Contact medical personnel.
    
3. **Call Bao immediately.**
    
4. Administer compatible blood through the established emergency procedure.
    
5. Monitor cardiovascular status.
    
6. Continue medical observation after apparent stabilization.
    

Pain should be taken seriously.

However, pain relief must **never replace treatment of the underlying circulatory problem.**

---

# 11. POST-EPISODE CARE

Once stabilized, Neri should be allowed to remain with trusted people.

She may become clingy.

She may cry.

She may want Bao nearby.

She may not want to discuss what happened.

All of these responses should be respected.

Staff should not immediately interrogate her about the episode.

She has already experienced it.

She does not need to prove that it hurt.

---

# 12. PSYCHOLOGICAL NOTE

Neri's statement demonstrates something important about her trauma.

Her mind does not neatly separate:

**past pain**

from

**present pain.**

When something hurts badly enough, the past can become immediately present again.

A medical emergency can therefore become psychologically traumatic in addition to being physically dangerous.

This is another reason Neri requires compassionate care rather than purely clinical management.

---

# 13. C.O.R.E. WARNING

Personnel are reminded:

Neri is extraordinarily capable.

She can fight.

She can run.

She can manipulate matter.

She understands chemistry at an exceptional level.

None of those capabilities make her immune to pain.

None make her immune to fear.

None make her immune to trauma.

And none make severe chest pain during an anemic episode something she should be expected to simply endure.

---

# 14. FINAL OBSERVATION

Neri has spent years learning to tolerate pain.

That should never be mistaken for an inability to feel it.

When she says:

> **“It hurts.”**

believe her.

When she compares the pain to something from her childhood:

> **“It's like what my mom did to me when I get bad grades.”**

do not dismiss it as childish exaggeration.

It is a ten-year-old describing an experience that she finds almost impossible to put into words.

She has already endured enough pain.

SAFEHOUSE's responsibility is not to teach her how to endure more.

It is to make sure she doesn't have to.

**END MEDICAL INCIDENT**
