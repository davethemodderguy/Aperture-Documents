### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — PROGRESSIVE DECLINE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / DETERIORATING**

---

# **CURRENT CONDITION**

Alice's condition has deteriorated since her recovery.

Despite having access to food and water, she increasingly refuses both.

She is becoming weaker by the day.

Her body is attempting to maintain itself while simultaneously undergoing normal childhood growth.

This places an enormous demand on an already depleted system.

Alice is still a growing five-year-old.

Her body requires:

- Calories
    
- Protein
    
- Water
    
- Vitamins
    
- Minerals
    
- Rest
    
- Medical support
    

She is receiving very little of these voluntarily.

---

# **NUTRITION**

Alice's refusal to eat is now considered one of the most immediate threats to her survival.

She may stare at food for extended periods without touching it.

Sometimes she takes a few bites.

Sometimes she simply pushes it away.

When asked why, her answers vary.

> **"Not hungry."**

> **"Alice fine."**

> **"Someone else eat."**

Occasionally:

> **"Alice don't need."**

Medical personnel consider the last statement particularly concerning.

---

# **HYDRATION**

Her water intake is similarly inadequate.

Alice has previously survived by eating snow.

This behavior remains deeply ingrained.

If allowed outside, she may attempt to eat snow instead of drinking properly prepared water.

She does not appear to understand how dangerous this can become when combined with her existing condition.

---

# **THE CARROT PROBLEM**

There is exactly one food Alice consistently recognizes as something she likes.

**Carrots.**

Even this memory is incomplete.

When asked:

> **"What's your favorite food?"**

Alice will sometimes answer:

> **"Carrots."**

When asked why:

> **"Because rabbits eat carrots."**

She does not remember when she first learned this.

She does not remember anyone introducing carrots to her.

She simply knows:

> **Rabbit → Carrot → Good.**

This has become one of the few stable fragments of her childhood identity.

SAFEHOUSE personnel have therefore ensured that carrots remain available.

However, Alice cannot be sustained on carrots alone.

---

# **BIRTHDAY**

Alice does not have a reliably remembered birthday.

She does not know the exact date she was born.

She occasionally gives different answers when asked.

On one occasion:

> **"Birthday... snow?"**

Another time:

> **"Maybe summer."**

Another:

> **"Don't know."**

There is no verified date in the surviving records that Alice herself recognizes.

C.O.R.E. has therefore suspended attempts to determine her birthday through questioning.

---

# **FRAGMENTED MEMORY**

Alice's childhood memories are extremely fragmented.

Most memories exist only as disconnected pieces.

She remembers sensations more reliably than events.

For example:

**Snow.**

**Flowers.**

**Pain.**

**Hunger.**

**Running.**

**A voice she can no longer identify.**

**Someone loving her.**

**Carrots.**

**"Mom is dead."**

**"Mom loved Alice."**

These fragments do not form a coherent timeline.

Alice cannot reliably explain where she lived, what happened to her, or how she survived before Corin found her.

---

# **A GROWING BODY**

The medical department has emphasized one fact repeatedly:

> **Alice is still growing.**

Her age makes her current nutritional deprivation especially dangerous.

A healthy five-year-old is undergoing rapid physical development.

Alice's body is attempting to do the same while possessing:

- Very low muscle mass
    
- Fragile bones
    
- Severely reduced stamina
    
- A compromised immune system
    
- Significant nutritional deficiencies
    
- Persistent physiological abnormalities
    

Her body cannot simply pause childhood development.

It continues demanding resources.

Alice simply isn't providing enough.

---

# **WEAKNESS**

The decline is becoming visible.

Alice walks less.

She rests more.

She becomes tired after extremely short periods of activity.

Her movements have become slower.

She spends increasingly long periods sitting beside Corin.

Sometimes she falls asleep while sitting.

Sometimes she is too tired to climb onto someone's back without assistance.

Her body is conserving what little energy remains.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has become increasingly worried.

She keeps trying different foods.

Small portions.

Warm meals.

Soft foods.

Sweet foods.

Carrots.

Nothing consistently works.

Corin eventually stopped asking:

> **"Are you hungry?"**

because Alice would always answer:

> **"No."**

Instead, she simply sits beside her and offers food without pressure.

Sometimes Alice accepts.

Sometimes she doesn't.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle continues bringing Alice carrots.

Usually too many.

> **"I brought you some!"**

Alice looks at them.

> **"...Carrot."**

> **"Yep."**

Sometimes Alice eats one.

Belle treats that as a victory.

She never forces another.

---

# **MEDICAL DIRECTIVE**

Alice must not be pressured into eating through shame.

Personnel are forbidden from telling her:

> "You're making everyone worried."

> "You're wasting food."

> "You need to eat because we're taking care of you."

> "Look how skinny you are."

> "You're going to make yourself sick."

These statements risk reinforcing Alice's existing belief that her existence causes problems.

Food must remain associated with:

**Safety.**

**Comfort.**

**Care.**

Not guilt.

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice doesn't remember her birthday.

She doesn't remember her mother's face.

She doesn't remember her mother's name.

She doesn't remember most of her childhood.

She remembers almost nothing in a straight line.

She remembers fragments.

Snow.

Flowers.

A grave.

A mother who loved her.

Carrots.

And now she is five years old, still growing, while becoming weaker every day.

The most stable memory she has left about herself may simply be:

> **"Alice is rabbit."**

And rabbits like carrots.

So SAFEHOUSE keeps carrots available.

Not because carrots can fix Alice.

They cannot.

But because sometimes, when almost everything else from childhood has disappeared...

having one thing you can still remember liking matters.
