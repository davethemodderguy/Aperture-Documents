# SAFEHOUSE RESIDENT STATUS REPORT

**Subject:** Neri  
**Classification:** Resident Welfare / Prolonged Absence  
**Status:** ONGOING  
**Medical Note:** Anemic episodes decreasing in frequency  
**Behavioral Note:** Severe reduction in social presence

---

# 1. CURRENT STATUS

Neri is still a SAFEHOUSE resident.

She has not formally withdrawn from the community.

She has not requested relocation.

She has not stated that she intends to leave.

She simply...

**isn't around anymore.**

Personnel who previously encountered Neri multiple times throughout an ordinary day may now go several days without seeing her.

There is no consistent schedule to her appearances.

She does not appear to be avoiding one particular person.

She appears to be avoiding **people in general.**

---

# 2. CAFETERIA OBSERVATION

The cafeteria was one of the most reliable places to find Neri.

For months, the kitchen staff had quietly accounted for her eating habits by preparing additional desserts.

Extra cake portions.

Additional pastries.

Extra pudding.

Additional sweet snacks.

Not because Neri demanded them.

Not because she was being rewarded.

Simply because:

**Neri would usually eat them.**

There was an established expectation that an extra portion would disappear.

Recently, that stopped happening.

At first, cafeteria staff continued preparing the additional portions.

They waited.

Neri did not arrive.

The desserts remained untouched.

The next day, the same thing happened.

Then again.

And again.

Eventually, the cafeteria returned to its normal production quantities.

One staff member reportedly summarized the decision as:

> **"We kept making her cake because she kept coming."**

> **"She stopped coming."**

> **"So we stopped making extra."**

No disciplinary significance is attached to this decision.

It is simply a reflection of the change in Neri's routine.

---

# 3. THE EMPTY CHAIR

Neri's usual cafeteria seat remains identifiable.

Personnel have occasionally found themselves looking toward it automatically.

There is usually nobody there.

This has become an unusual psychological marker for SAFEHOUSE staff.

The seat is not officially reserved.

Nobody has declared it "Neri's seat."

Yet people still hesitate before sitting there.

One cafeteria worker stated:

> "I know it's just a chair."

> "But it feels wrong."

---

# 4. REDUCTION IN MEDICAL EPISODES

Interestingly, Neri's documented anemic episodes have also decreased.

This initially appeared encouraging.

It is not being treated as evidence that her underlying condition has improved.

Neri's blood degradation remains present.

Her cardiovascular abnormalities remain present.

Her compatibility dependence on Bao remains present.

Her medical risk has not disappeared.

Instead, the most likely explanation is behavioral.

Neri is simply doing considerably less.

She is:

- Walking less.
    
- Participating less.
    
- Fighting less.
    
- Experimenting less.
    
- Spending less time around others.
    
- Spending less time in communal areas.
    
- Exposing herself to fewer situations that previously coincided with physical or psychological stress.
    

In other words:

**Her body is being asked to do less.**

Consequently, there are fewer opportunities for a crisis to become apparent.

---

# 5. MEDICAL WARNING

Medical staff have specifically rejected the interpretation:

> **"Fewer episodes = Neri is getting better."**

The correct interpretation is closer to:

> **"Fewer episodes = Neri is doing less."**

This distinction is critical.

Her underlying condition remains.

If anything, her prolonged inactivity makes monitoring more difficult.

Previously, Neri's movements naturally brought her into contact with people.

Someone would see her in the cafeteria.

Someone would see her walking with Alice.

Someone would hear her arguing with Neko.

Someone would notice her sitting somewhere reading.

Someone would eventually notice if she looked pale.

Now there are significantly fewer opportunities for those observations.

---

# 6. THE STRANGE CONTRADICTION

SAFEHOUSE has therefore reached an unusual situation.

The number of medical emergencies is going down.

The number of social interactions is also going down.

The number of people seeing Neri is going down.

The number of times she asks for help is going down.

On paper, several of these statistics look positive.

In practice, the behavioral department is considerably less optimistic.

A resident experiencing fewer medical crises because they are living normally is one thing.

A resident experiencing fewer medical crises because they have almost stopped participating in life is something else.

---

# 7. NERI'S CURRENT ROUTINE

Her actual routine remains poorly understood.

Security records suggest that Neri spends substantial amounts of time in low-traffic areas.

She may remain there for extended periods.

She sometimes reads.

She sometimes eats.

She sometimes sleeps.

She sometimes does nothing observable.

She is not consistently accompanied.

She does not appear to be actively searching for anyone.

She does not appear to be actively asking anyone to find her.

This has made locating her difficult without deliberately searching.

---

# 8. HER FRIENDS HAVE NOT DISAPPEARED

This point has been repeatedly emphasized to staff.

Bao is still here.

Frost is still here.

Alice is still here.

Neko is still here.

Everyone Neri cares about has continued their ordinary lives.

They have not abandoned her.

They have not stopped caring.

But Neri's behavior makes it increasingly difficult for those relationships to function normally.

A friendship requires some degree of presence.

Neri is increasingly absent.

---

# 9. BAO'S OBSERVATION

Bao reportedly noticed the change before most staff.

She did not initially describe Neri as "depressed," "unstable," or "withdrawn."

She simply said:

> **"I don't see my daughter very much anymore."**

That statement was subsequently added to the welfare assessment.

It remains one of the simplest descriptions of the problem.

---

# 10. CAFETERIA STAFF NOTE

A cafeteria employee was asked whether the return to normal dessert portions meant that the cafeteria had "given up" on Neri.

Their response:

> "No."

> "We're just not wasting food."

After a pause:

> "If she comes back tomorrow and asks for cake, we'll make her one."

This remains the standing cafeteria policy.

No special stockpile is required.

No one is expected to wait for her.

But if Neri walks through the cafeteria again, she will be welcomed normally.

---

# 11. MEDICAL AND BEHAVIORAL DIRECTIVE

Personnel are instructed not to manufacture reasons to interact with Neri merely because her absence is concerning.

Likewise, personnel should not interpret her absence as proof that she wants to be left completely alone.

The appropriate response remains:

**Availability without pressure.**

If Neri appears:

> Talk to her if she wants to talk.

If she wants food:

> Give her food.

If she wants company:

> Stay.

If she wants to leave:

> Let her leave.

If she asks for help:

> Help her.

If she disappears from communal areas again:

> Keep an appropriate welfare awareness.

---

# 12. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Neri's presence within SAFEHOUSE has become increasingly rare.

The cafeteria no longer prepares unusually large dessert portions because the person those portions were intended for is no longer reliably there to eat them.

Her anemic episodes have decreased.

Her activity has decreased.

Her social interactions have decreased.

Her visibility has decreased.

None of these facts should be interpreted independently.

The most accurate summary is:

> **Neri is doing less.**

And because she is doing less, fewer things are happening around her.

There are fewer fights.

Fewer experiments.

Fewer conversations.

Fewer medical crises.

Fewer cakes disappearing from the cafeteria.

Fewer people asking:

> **"Where's Neri?"**

Because, increasingly, nobody knows.

---

### ADDENDUM — CAFETERIA

Several weeks after the return to normal dessert portions, a cafeteria worker noticed a small plate of cake missing from the counter.

No request had been logged.

No one had signed for it.

Security footage showed Neri entering the cafeteria alone.

She took one slice.

She sat at her usual table.

She ate it quietly.

She remained there for approximately nine minutes.

Then she left.

The worker who discovered the missing slice reportedly smiled.

They did not increase the dessert production again.

They simply wrote on the kitchen board:

> **"Neri came by today."**

Nothing else was added.

The note remained on the board for the rest of the shift.
