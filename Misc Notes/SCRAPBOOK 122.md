# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION REPORT**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – SOCIAL AVOIDANCE RESPONSE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 7 – Medical & Executive Personnel Only

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Although Corin Lockwood's Ether-sustained physiology has become a normal part of daily life within Project SAFEHOUSE, it can be deeply unsettling to personnel encountering her for the first time.

Most individuals have never met someone with Corin's unique condition.

Unexpected reactions are common.

Unfortunately, every startled expression reinforces Corin's already fragile self-image.

---

# **FIRST ENCOUNTERS**

Personnel unfamiliar with Corin often react instinctively.

Some become visibly nervous after noticing her lack of body heat.

Others are unsettled when thermal scanners fail to register a normal heat signature.

Several have stepped away in alarm before realizing who she was.

One researcher later admitted:

> "...My brain knew she was talking to me."

> "...My instincts kept telling me something was terribly wrong."

After receiving a proper briefing, the same researcher later apologized to Corin.

Corin smiled politely.

She accepted the apology.

But the memory remained with her.

---

# **CUMULATIVE EFFECT**

Medical Services observed that the incidents themselves are rarely severe.

The accumulation is.

Every frightened glance.

Every hesitant handshake.

Every person instinctively backing away.

Every whispered question.

None of them intend to hurt Corin.

Together...

They slowly convince her that she frightens people simply by existing.

---

# **RECORDED CONVERSATION**

Corin sat quietly in her office after another introduction to newly assigned staff.

Lumi entered carrying two cups of tea.

Corin spoke first.

> "...Another one ran."

Lumi placed the tea on the desk.

She waited.

Corin continued.

> "...They always apologize afterwards."

> "...But they still run first."

She stared at her hands.

> "...How many people have to be scared of me before it's my fault?"

Lumi answered immediately.

> "None."

Corin shook her head.

> "...If enough people react the same way..."

> "...maybe I'm the problem."

---

# **LUMI'S RESPONSE**

Lumi sat beside her daughter.

> "They're reacting to something they don't understand."

Corin looked away.

> "...They're reacting to me."

Lumi gently corrected her.

> "No."

> "They're reacting to the injuries someone else left behind."

A long silence followed.

> "Those are not the same thing."

Corin didn't argue.

But she wasn't ready to believe it.

---

# **SAFEHOUSE RESPONSE**

Without discussing it, the residents gradually changed how they introduced Corin to newcomers.

Rather than focusing on her appearance, they introduced her as a colleague.

An accountant.

A brilliant mathematician.

Someone who somehow approved Norma's impossible ammunition budgets.

Only afterward did they explain her Ether-dependent condition.

The result was noticeable.

People who met Corin as a person first were far less likely to define her by her appearance.

---

# **BELLE'S COMMENT**

When Corin apologized after another uncomfortable introduction, Belle simply wrapped her in a hug.

> "You know what I notice first?"

Corin quietly asked,

> "...What?"

Belle smiled.

> "That you're always worrying about everyone else."

> "I think that's a much bigger part of you than being cold."

Corin didn't answer.

But she hugged Belle back a little tighter.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin continues to struggle with feelings of guilt when strangers react fearfully to her unusual Ether-sustained physiology.

Medical staff emphasize that these reactions are understandable responses to an unfamiliar situation, not judgments of Corin's character.

Even so, Corin often internalizes them.

Her recovery therefore continues to focus not only on managing trauma, but also on helping her separate **what happened to her** from **who she is**.

**End of Psychological Observation Report**
