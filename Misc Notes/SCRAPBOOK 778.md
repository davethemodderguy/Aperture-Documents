### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — PSYCHIATRIC PROFILE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — PERSONNEL CARE / MEDICAL  
**Age:** 5  
**Status:** Under Continuous Care  
**Diagnosis:** **Bipolar Disorder — Under Clinical Management**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice has been diagnosed with a bipolar disorder characterized by significant fluctuations in mood, energy, activity, and sleep.

Her condition is complicated by her existing physical weakness, history of extreme sleep deprivation, memory problems, and anomalous physiology.

Medical personnel emphasize that Alice's diagnosis does **not** make her unpredictable or dangerous.

She remains Alice.

Her personality, friendships, kindness, and preferences continue to exist independently of her condition.

---

# **MOOD EPISODES**

Alice's mood changes can range considerably.

During depressive periods, she may become:

- Extremely quiet.
    
- Withdrawn.
    
- Less interested in activities.
    
- More fatigued than usual.
    
- Less communicative.
    
- More likely to isolate herself.
    
- More likely to believe she is bothering others.
    

During elevated periods, Alice may become noticeably more energetic.

She may:

- Talk considerably more.
    
- Become unusually enthusiastic.
    
- Ride her scooter around more frequently.
    
- Start many activities.
    
- Become more curious.
    
- Seek out more people.
    
- Speak faster than her normal quiet pace.
    
- Have difficulty settling down.
    

These changes are monitored carefully.

---

# **SLEEP**

Sleep is particularly important for Alice.

She already has a history of severe sleep deprivation.

Insufficient sleep can significantly worsen mood instability and physical exhaustion.

Personnel therefore treat Alice's sleep schedule as a serious medical concern.

If Alice has been awake for an unusually long period, someone is expected to intervene before exhaustion becomes severe.

---

# **ALICE'S COMMUNICATION**

Her restored ability to speak has made monitoring considerably easier.

Alice can now sometimes say:

> "Alice feels sad."

> "Alice is tired."

> "Alice is too happy."

> "Alice can't sleep."

These statements are taken seriously.

She is never told that she is "just being emotional."

---

# **IMPORTANT DISTINCTION**

Not every change in Alice's mood represents an episode.

Alice is five.

She can become excited.

She can cry.

She can become annoyed.

She can laugh at something silly.

She can suddenly want to play.

These are normal childhood behaviors.

Medical personnel therefore evaluate **patterns, duration, severity, and functional impact**, rather than labeling every emotion as a symptom.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has become particularly attentive to Alice's mood.

She doesn't constantly ask:

> "Are you having an episode?"

Instead, she asks normal questions.

> "How are you feeling?"

> "Want to sit with me?"

> "Do you want to play?"

> "Are you tired?"

Alice can answer honestly without feeling like she is being examined.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle remains one of Alice's most consistent sources of positive social interaction.

During difficult periods, Belle doesn't attempt to "fix" Alice.

She simply stays nearby.

Sometimes they sit together.

Sometimes Belle talks.

Sometimes Alice talks.

Sometimes neither says anything.

Alice knows Belle will remain her friend regardless of her mood.

---

# **SAFEHOUSE GUIDANCE**

Personnel are instructed:

**Do not shame Alice for mood changes.**

**Do not deliberately provoke elevated or depressed states.**

**Do not dismiss significant behavioral changes as attention-seeking.**

**Do not isolate Alice as punishment.**

**Do not assume every quiet period is a depressive episode.**

**Do not assume every energetic period is mania.**

When uncertain:

> **Ask. Observe. Notify medical personnel.**

---

# **ANOMALOUS ABILITY CONSIDERATION**

Alice's anomalous right eye remains covered.

Her ability to command or hypnotize others is not considered a symptom of her psychiatric condition.

The two are treated as entirely separate phenomena.

Alice's voluntary decision to keep the eye covered remains respected.

---

# **PERSONALITY**

Despite the diagnosis, Alice remains:

- Gentle.
    
- Affectionate.
    
- Curious.
    
- Social.
    
- Extremely fond of her friends.
    
- Particularly attached to Belle and Corin.
    
- Quiet by nature.
    
- Protective of others.
    
- Sometimes unintentionally silly.
    

Her condition does not erase any of these traits.

---

# **CORIN'S NOTE**

> "Alice isn't her diagnosis."

> "She's a five-year-old bunny girl who happens to have something she has to live with."

> "We're going to help her live with it."

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice's mood may change.

Her energy may change.

Her sleep may change.

Some days she may talk endlessly.

Other days she may barely speak.

Some days she may ride her scooter around the SAFEHOUSE looking for everyone.

Other days she may simply curl up beside someone she trusts.

None of those changes make her less Alice.

The purpose of the SAFEHOUSE is not to force her into behaving the same every day.

It is to make sure that, regardless of what kind of day Alice is having—

**she is safe, loved, and never left to manage it alone.**
