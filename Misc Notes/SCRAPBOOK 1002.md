# 1. SUMMARY

Neri has resumed drawing.

At first, this was considered positive.

Drawing had historically been one of Neri's quieter forms of self-expression, particularly when she did not wish to speak.

Recent drawings, however, have become increasingly disturbing.

The majority contain imagery associated with:

- death;
    
- isolation;
    
- abandonment;
    
- physical injury;
    
- empty rooms;
    
- figures without faces;
    
- figures being ignored by larger groups;
    
- distorted representations of Neri herself;
    
- medical imagery;
    
- darkened or crossed-out eyes;
    
- figures lying motionless;
    
- doors separating individuals;
    
- empty chairs;
    
- broken communication devices;
    
- and repeated depictions of someone reaching toward another person without being noticed.
    

The drawings vary considerably in artistic quality.

The emotional themes do not.

---

# 2. RECURRING IMAGE

One image has appeared repeatedly.

A small figure is drawn sitting alone in a large room.

Around the figure are numerous people.

The people are talking to one another.

None are looking toward the small figure.

The small figure has no mouth.

In several versions, Neri draws a speech bubble above the figure.

It is empty.

In other versions, the speech bubble contains:

> **"..."**

The figure is sometimes drawn with tears.

In some versions, there are no tears.

The figure simply sits there.

---

# 3. THE DOOR

Another recurring subject is a door.

Neri has drawn herself on both sides of it.

In one version:

She is outside.

The door is closed.

A keycard is drawn beside her.

She is unable to reach it.

In another:

She is inside.

The door is locked.

Several people are drawn on the opposite side.

They cannot hear her.

In a third:

The door is open.

Everyone walks past.

Neri remains standing in the doorway.

Nobody notices her.

---

# 4. THE CAFETERIA DRAWINGS

Several drawings appear to depict the cafeteria where Neri experienced her medical emergency.

One shows Neri sitting at a table reading.

The drawing is almost entirely normal.

The next page depicts the same scene.

The room is empty.

Neri is lying on the floor.

Her book remains open beside her.

The following drawing contains a single figure standing over her.

The figure is labelled:

> **JANE**

Neri has drawn Jane screaming for help.

No other people are present.

---

# 5. THE JANE DRAWINGS

Jane appears frequently in Neri's drawings.

This is notable given Neri's current deliberate avoidance of Jane.

In one drawing, Jane is holding Neri's hand.

Neri is drawn with her eyes closed.

Jane's face has been heavily erased.

In another, Jane is standing outside a closed door.

Neri is on the other side.

Jane's hand is pressed against the door.

Neri has drawn herself walking away.

In another:

Jane is sitting alone.

Neri is visible far in the background.

Jane is looking directly toward her.

Neri has drawn herself with no face.

---

# 6. THE MOST DISTURBING DRAWING

One drawing was sufficiently concerning that it was removed from general circulation and placed under restricted psychological review.

The page contains almost no color.

Neri has drawn herself lying motionless on the cafeteria floor.

Her body is extremely small compared with the room.

Her book is beside her.

A clock hangs above the scene.

The clock hands point toward **09:00**.

Near the doorway is Jane.

Jane is drawn kneeling beside Neri.

Above Jane is a speech bubble:

> **"Please wake up."**

There is no speech bubble above Neri.

Instead, Neri has drawn a small empty circle beside herself.

At the bottom of the page, written in small handwriting:

> **"Too late."**

---

# 7. DRAWING AFTER RESUSCITATION

A later drawing is significantly different.

Neri has drawn Bao holding her.

Bao is considerably larger.

Neri is curled against her.

There are two other figures in the background.

One is labelled:

> **JANE**

The other:

> **ALICE**

Both are crying.

Neri has drawn herself without a mouth.

Bao has one arm around her.

The other hand is holding a small container.

The drawing is captioned:

> **"Still here."**

This is currently considered the least concerning drawing produced during the observation period.

---

# 8. INTERPRETATION

C.O.R.E. does **not** consider the drawings sufficient evidence to conclude that Neri intends to harm herself or others.

The imagery appears more consistent with emotional processing.

Neri currently has limited avenues for communication.

She cannot speak.

She rarely approaches others.

She actively avoids people.

She is physically exhausted.

She has recently experienced a near-fatal medical event.

Her drawings may therefore be expressing thoughts that she is unwilling or unable to communicate directly.

Particular attention should be paid to the recurring themes rather than individual violent images.

The strongest themes are:

**"Nobody notices me."**

**"I cannot call for help."**

**"People will be hurt if they care about me."**

**"Someone will eventually find me too late."**

---

# 9. RESPONSE PROTOCOL

Personnel are instructed **not to confiscate Neri's drawing materials.**

Drawing appears to be one of the few activities through which Neri continues expressing herself.

Personnel should also avoid interrogating her about disturbing drawings.

Do not demand:

> "What does this mean?"

Do not assume:

> "This means you want to die."

Do not tell her:

> "Stop drawing things like that."

Instead, if Neri voluntarily shows a drawing, personnel may ask simple, non-leading questions such as:

> "Do you want me to sit with you?"

or:

> "Do you want to tell me about it?"

If Neri does not respond, the interaction should not be forced.

---

# 10. JANE DOE

Jane was shown several of the drawings.

She remained silent for an extended period.

She eventually pointed at the drawing depicting herself beside Neri's unconscious body.

**JANE:**  
"She thinks I'm going to find her too late."

No response was given.

Jane looked at the drawing again.

**JANE:**  
"That's what she thinks."

Pause.

**JANE:**  
"That's what she's afraid of."

Jane did not request the drawing be removed.

She asked whether she could keep a copy.

---

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Neri's drawings have become darker.

Considerably darker.

But the violence is not the most important feature.

The loneliness is.

The most repeated image is not Neri being hurt.

It is Neri being hurt **while everyone else fails to notice.**

The most repeated object is not a weapon.

It is a door.

The most repeated absence is not blood.

It is a voice.

And the person who appears most frequently is not an enemy.

It is Jane.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Neri cannot speak.

So she draws.

And what she draws is increasingly difficult to look at.

A little girl alone in a cafeteria.

A little girl behind a locked door.

A little girl falling down stairs.

A little girl reaching for somebody who cannot hear her.

A little girl lying on the floor while the clock keeps moving.

And, again and again, people arriving too late.

Perhaps the most unsettling part is that Neri does not draw herself screaming.

She draws herself **silent.**

Because that is the part she actually remembers.

— **END OBSERVATION**
