## Executive Summary

The incident involving Alice and Plushie Neri was initially documented as an accidental collision.

That description is technically correct.

It is insufficient.

The event was considerably more distressing than the initial report conveyed, primarily because of what it represented to Neri.

Alice was carrying Neri when another resident asked her to throw a clipboard.

Alice reached for the nearest object.

She picked up Neri.

Without realizing her mistake, Alice threw her with the same casual motion she would have used to throw a ball.

Neri was projected through the corridor and struck the wall **face-first**.

She then dropped to the floor and remained there.

There was no permanent damage.

There was, however, substantial pain.

More importantly, Neri did not attempt to protect herself at any point.

She had effectively surrendered control of her own safety.

---

# I. EVENT RECONSTRUCTION

### 14:20:17

Alice was holding Plushie Neri against her body.

Neri was completely passive.

This was consistent with her recent behavior.

She was not asleep.

She was conscious and aware of her surroundings.

She simply no longer moved voluntarily unless someone else moved her.

---

### 14:20:19

A nearby resident called to Alice:

> **"Alice, throw me that clipboard."**

The clipboard was resting on a nearby surface.

Alice looked toward it.

She reached down.

Neri was still in her hands.

There was no conscious decision to throw Neri.

Alice's attention had simply shifted toward the requested object.

She picked up the nearest item.

It happened to be Neri.

---

### 14:20:20

Alice performed a throwing motion.

Neri was released.

There was no preparation.

No warning.

No opportunity for Neri to reposition herself.

She was thrown essentially like a baseball.

Because of the distance and force involved, Neri did not merely fall.

She traveled through the air.

---

### 14:20:21

Neri struck the wall.

**Face-first.**

The impact produced a loud, unmistakable collision.

Her body folded against the wall before dropping directly to the floor.

She landed without attempting to roll, brace, or protect herself.

For several seconds, she did not move.

---

# II. THE SILENCE

This was the part that affected Alice most severely.

There was no immediate cry.

No angry complaint.

No:

> "Ow!"

No attempt to get up.

No attempt to crawl away.

No attempt to seek help.

Neri simply lay on the floor.

Alice stared at her.

The resident who had requested the clipboard stared at her.

Nobody spoke.

Then Alice realized what she had done.

> **Alice:** "...Neri?"

Neri's body moved slightly.

One small paw shifted against the floor.

Alice rushed toward her.

---

# III. NERI'S CONDITION

Alice picked Neri up.

Neri's usual plush softness was still present.

But she was trembling.

Her glasses had been displaced.

Her face was pressed against Alice's clothing as Alice held her.

Neri finally spoke.

Her voice was extremely quiet.

> **Neri:** "Ow."

Alice froze.

A few seconds passed.

Neri's breathing remained uneven.

Then:

> **Neri:** "It hurts."

Alice's expression immediately changed.

The realization that she had genuinely hurt Neri became unavoidable.

She had not tossed a harmless plush toy.

She had thrown a living person.

---

# IV. MEDICAL RECONSTRUCTION

Medical examination confirmed significant impact trauma and acute pain.

Fortunately, the plush state substantially reduced the likelihood of permanent structural injury.

No permanent damage was identified.

No lasting neurological impairment was identified.

No permanent organ damage was identified.

Neri recovered physically.

However, medical personnel noted that the impact had been dangerously close to exceeding the protective limits of her plush physiology.

### C.O.R.E. Medical Simulation

Had the projection been substantially harder, the force transferred through Neri's body could have exceeded the protective capacity of the plush state.

At sufficient impact energy, her internal organs could have suffered catastrophic trauma.

Such an event could have resulted in **immediate fatal injury**.

This is not being recorded as a hypothetical justification for fear.

It is being recorded because the distinction between:

> **"Neri was hurt."**

and:

> **"Neri could have died."**

is operationally significant.

The outcome was fortunate.

It was not guaranteed.

---

# V. ALICE'S REALIZATION

Alice did not immediately understand the severity.

She knew she had thrown Neri.

She did not initially understand that the throw could have killed her.

When medical personnel explained the potential consequences, Alice became completely silent.

She looked at Neri.

Neri was lying still.

Alice asked:

> **"She could've died?"**

The answer was yes.

Alice began crying.

> **"I didn't mean to."**

Nobody accused her of meaning to.

That did not make the realization disappear.

She had made a mistake.

A very serious one.

---

# VI. NERI'S RESPONSE

Alice eventually approached Neri.

She was afraid to touch her.

> **Alice:** "Can I hold you?"

Neri looked at her.

There was a long silence.

Then Neri nodded.

Alice carefully picked her up.

Neri did not resist.

Alice held her against her chest.

> **Alice:** "I'm sorry."

Neri remained quiet.

> **Alice:** "I'm really sorry."

Neri eventually whispered:

> **Neri:** "I know."

Alice started crying harder.

> **Alice:** "I didn't know."

Neri closed her eyes.

> **Neri:** "I know."

There was no anger in her voice.

That somehow made the situation worse.

---

# VII. THE MOST DISTURBING DETAIL

Neri could have protected herself.

Her plush form allows her to move.

She could have twisted.

She could have rolled.

She could have moved her head.

She could have attempted to land differently.

She did none of those things.

She did not even attempt to resist being thrown.

This was not because she was physically incapable.

She simply did not consider protecting herself important enough to act.

The outdoor incident appears to have fundamentally altered her self-preservation response.

The washing-machine incident demonstrated that she would allow danger to happen.

The throwing incident demonstrated something worse:

**She may not even react when danger happens suddenly.**

---

# VIII. POST-INCIDENT OBSERVATION

While recovering, Neri remained in Alice's arms.

Alice eventually asked:

> **"Were you scared?"**

Neri was silent.

Alice waited.

Eventually:

> **Neri:** "Yes."

This was one of the few times Neri had directly acknowledged fear since the outdoor incident.

Alice hugged her more carefully.

> **Alice:** "I'm sorry."

Neri did not say:

> "It's okay."

She did not minimize the event.

She simply remained there.

That distinction was considered important.

---

# IX. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

This incident should not be categorized merely as:

> **"Alice accidentally threw Neri."**

The physical accident was simple.

The underlying issue is not.

Neri currently believes that she is a liability.

She believes people are forced to accommodate her.

She has therefore stopped behaving as though her own safety is something she is responsible for protecting.

The result is dangerous.

A normal person accidentally thrown toward a wall would instinctively attempt to protect themselves.

Neri did not.

She trusted—or perhaps simply surrendered—to whatever happened next.

---

# X. DIRECTIVE

Personnel are reminded:

**Plushie Neri is a living resident.**

She is not an ordinary plush toy.

She is not an object.

She is not disposable.

She is not indestructible.

She is not safe to throw.

And most importantly:

> **Her failure to resist does not mean she is consenting to what is happening to her.**

If Neri is in danger, personnel must intervene.

If she does not move, do not assume she is comfortable.

If she does not object, do not assume she agrees.

If she allows herself to be carried, do not assume she wants to go wherever she is being carried.

---

## Final Note

The wall was undamaged.

The clipboard was undamaged.

Neri eventually recovered.

Alice eventually stopped crying.

The incident left no permanent physical injury.

But the event exposed something considerably more serious.

Neri had reached a point where being thrown across a room was not followed by:

> **"Stop."**

It was not followed by:

> **"Help me."**

It was not followed by:

> **"That hurt."**

It was followed by silence.

And only after someone picked her up did she finally say:

> **"Ow."**

C.O.R.E. considers that response more important than the impact itself.

Neri is still capable of recognizing that something hurts.

She is still capable of saying so.

The current objective is ensuring she learns that she is allowed to do so **before** the next wall.
