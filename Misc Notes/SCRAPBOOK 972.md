# SAFEHOUSE BEHAVIORAL REPORT

## SUBJECT: NERI

### PHENOMENON: “THE BACKGROUND EFFECT”

**Classification:** Psychological / Social / Medical  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Status:** SAFEHOUSE Resident  
**Current Concern:** Progressive social withdrawal and refusal of acknowledgement

---

# 1. SUMMARY

Neri has begun to **fade into the background**.

Not literally.

She remains physically present.

She can still be seen.

She can still be heard.

She can still interact with people.

But increasingly, she is behaving as though she does not expect anyone to notice her.

And, disturbingly, people have begun to stop noticing her.

The process is subtle.

There is no single moment where Neri disappeared from SAFEHOUSE life.

Instead:

She stopped joining people.

Then stopped asking.

Then stopped responding to invitations.

Then stopped expecting anyone to look for her.

And eventually, people began walking past her without realizing she was there.

---

# 2. INCIDENT — THE DOOR

Neri was attempting to reach Neko's laboratory.

She was unfamiliar with the route and decided to follow a Blacksite employee who appeared to be heading in the same direction.

Neri walked several steps behind them.

The employee entered a heavy security door.

Neri followed.

The employee did not notice her.

The door swung back.

It struck Neri directly in the face.

She was knocked to the ground.

A nosebleed developed shortly afterward.

The employee reportedly did not realize what had happened until another worker pointed it out.

> **Worker:** “Uh... did that door just hit someone?”

> **Second Worker:** “...Neri?”

Neri reportedly got back up without complaint.

She did not blame the employee.

She did not ask for help.

She simply continued toward Neko's laboratory.

---

# 3. INCIDENT — THE SNOW

The second incident was considerably more serious.

Bao had requested several plant samples.

Neri volunteered to collect them.

She went outside carrying a heavy box containing the samples.

After completing the collection, Neri attempted to return inside.

A guard entered the facility ahead of her.

The guard closed the door.

The door locked automatically.

Neri was left outside.

The guard did not notice her.

More importantly:

**Neri had left her keycard inside.**

She could not reopen the door.

Rather than calling someone, knocking repeatedly, or returning to another entrance, Neri sat beside the box.

It was snowing.

She waited.

---

# 4. FOUR HOURS

Approximately four hours passed.

Inside the Blacksite, Bao began looking for the plant samples.

The samples had not arrived.

Then Bao realized something else.

Neri was missing.

A search began.

Bao eventually went outside.

She found the box.

It was covered in thick snow.

And beside it—

**Neri.**

Neri was also covered in snow.

She had fallen asleep.

She had apparently decided that waiting was preferable to bothering someone.

---

# 5. MEDICAL RESPONSE

Neri was brought inside and assessed.

Her condition was concerning, but she survived the exposure.

When asked why she had not contacted anyone, Neri reportedly answered:

> “I didn't have my keycard.”

Bao asked:

> “Why didn't you call me?”

Neri paused.

> “You were busy.”

That answer was more concerning than the snow.

---

# 6. INCIDENT — JANE

Jane Doe later invited Neri to her office.

The invitation was casual.

No emergency.

No interrogation.

No medical appointment.

Just an invitation to spend time together.

Neri declined.

Jane asked why.

Neri's answer was brief.

> “I'm okay.”

Jane knew that phrase.

It did not necessarily mean Neri was okay.

It often meant:

> **“I don't want to be a problem.”**

Jane did not force her.

But the refusal remained in her mind.

---

# 7. INCIDENT — ALICE

Alice later invited Neri to the greenhouse.

Bao would be there.

It was supposed to be a relaxed afternoon.

Plants.

Greenhouse warmth.

Bao.

Alice.

Neri.

Neri declined.

Alice's usual cheerful expression disappeared almost instantly.

She did not argue.

She did not demand an explanation.

She simply sat on her scooter.

Then rode away.

The transition was immediate.

One moment:

**Alice was smiling.**

The next:

**she wasn't.**

---

# 8. BEHAVIORAL PATTERN

The incidents initially appeared unrelated.

They are not.

They demonstrate the same underlying behavior.

### Neri is increasingly refusing acknowledgement.

She does not want attention.

She does not seek company.

She does not ask for assistance.

She does not accept invitations easily.

She does not complain when people overlook her.

She does not appear angry when someone forgets her.

Instead—

**she accepts it.**

That may be the most worrying part.

---

# 9. THE BACKGROUND EFFECT

A SAFEHOUSE resident previously described Neri as:

> “The kid who is always somewhere.”

That description has become increasingly accurate.

Neri is present.

At a table.

In a hallway.

Outside a laboratory.

Near the cafeteria.

Reading.

Walking.

Carrying something.

Waiting.

But she rarely inserts herself into anyone else's attention.

She behaves like an environmental detail.

Something people see without consciously registering.

A chair.

A bookshelf.

A plant.

A child reading alone.

This has begun creating a feedback loop.

**Neri expects to be overlooked.**

↓

**She avoids drawing attention to herself.**

↓

**People become less likely to notice her.**

↓

**Neri interprets this as confirmation that nobody needs her.**

↓

**She withdraws further.**

---

# 10. THIS IS NOT TRUE INVISIBILITY

Medical and anomalous personnel have found no evidence that Neri is physically disappearing.

The phenomenon is behavioral and social.

However, the distinction provides little comfort.

A person does not need to become physically invisible to become functionally invisible.

Neri can stand directly behind someone and follow them through a door without being noticed.

She can sit alone in a cafeteria for hours.

She can be left outside in freezing weather.

She can decline invitations without explanation.

And unless somebody deliberately checks—

she can disappear from the social environment entirely.

---

# 11. JANE DOE'S ASSESSMENT

Jane was particularly disturbed by the pattern.

Neri's withdrawal is not presenting as anger.

It is not rebellion.

It is not hatred.

Neri isn't saying:

> “Leave me alone.”

She is behaving as though:

> **“You probably don't want me there anyway.”**

Jane recognizes the distinction.

It is consistent with the belief she has been fighting since Neri's earlier medical incidents:

> **“I'm not useful.”**

> **“I'm a burden.”**

> **“I'm not anyone's.”**

The difference now is that Neri is no longer merely thinking these things.

She is beginning to organize her behavior around them.

---

# 12. ALICE'S RESPONSE

Alice's reaction to the greenhouse invitation has been noted.

She did not become angry.

She became sad.

Alice had wanted Neri there.

Neri said no.

Alice accepted the answer.

Then she left.

This may be the first visible indication that Neri's withdrawal is beginning to affect the people around her.

Her absence is not neutral.

People notice when she doesn't come.

They just don't always know how to make her understand that.

---

# 13. MEDICAL CONCERN

The Background Effect is particularly dangerous because Neri already suffers from unpredictable anemia.

A socially withdrawn Neri is a medically vulnerable Neri.

If she is alone when an episode occurs:

Nobody may notice.

If she becomes trapped somewhere:

She may wait instead of asking for help.

If she becomes injured:

She may decide it isn't important.

If someone invites her somewhere:

She may decline because she believes she would be inconvenient.

The medical team therefore considers her social withdrawal a **secondary safety risk**.

Not because Neri is deliberately harming herself.

But because she increasingly behaves as though her wellbeing is somebody else's problem.

---

# 14. CURRENT ASSESSMENT

Neri is still present.

She still reads.

She still studies chemistry.

She still eats cake.

She still has friends.

She still has people who love her.

But she is slowly stepping away from all of them.

And the SAFEHOUSE is beginning to adapt around her absence.

That is precisely what must not happen.

Because once everyone becomes accustomed to Neri being somewhere in the background—

someone may eventually realize:

**she hasn't been seen all day.**

Then:

**she hasn't been seen for two days.**

Then someone asks:

> “When was the last time anyone actually talked to her?”

And nobody knows.

---

# 15. RECOMMENDATION

Do not respond to Neri's withdrawal by demanding that she socialize.

Do not force her into activities.

Do not repeatedly ask:

> “Why are you being like this?”

Do not make her feel guilty for declining invitations.

Instead:

**Keep inviting her.**

Keep talking to her.

Sit beside her.

Leave a piece of cake at her table.

Ask about her chemistry.

Ask about her book.

Walk with her.

Let her decline.

Then ask again another day.

The objective is not to force Neri back into the foreground.

It is to demonstrate that she was **never background to begin with.**

---

# 16. FINAL NOTE

Neri is not disappearing.

She is making herself smaller.

And the more successful she becomes at doing so—

the easier it becomes for everyone else to accidentally let her.
