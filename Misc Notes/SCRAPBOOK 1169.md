# 1. PROGRAM STATUS

Neri has fully recovered from the previous medical incident.

Follow-up examination confirms that the injuries sustained while she was in Plush Form **did not carry over after transformation back into her normal body.**

No residual paralysis has been identified.

No persistent cardiac injury from the impact has been identified.

No lasting physical impairment has been identified.

Neri has therefore resumed normal activity.

And, apparently, resumed the Hell Fire Trials.

The remaining Hell Fire units are no longer considered permanently suspended.

The next trial has been authorized.

It is substantially less straightforward than the previous one.

---

# 2. TRIAL II

## **THE JANE DOE RESIDENCY**

The objective is simple:

> **Live with Jane Doe for 34 consecutive hours.**

Participants will be placed into an isolated residential environment with Jane.

They will remain there for the entire duration.

There is no combat objective.

There is no conventional elimination round.

There is no requirement to defeat Jane.

Instead, participants must simply:

> **Survive living with her.**

---

# 3. PARTICIPANT GROUPING

Participants enter the trial in groups of:

> **FIVE.**

Each group receives its own trial period.

No individual may enter alone.

No group may exceed five participants.

At the conclusion of each 34-hour period, C.O.R.E. personnel evaluate the group.

If nobody qualifies:

> **No winner.**

The next group is scheduled.

---

# 4. TRIAL LOCATION

The participants will not remain inside the ordinary SAFEHOUSE.

They will be housed within the **unused fifth floor of the SAFEHOUSE pocket-dimension closet.**

The fifth floor has historically remained unused.

It contains:

- residential rooms;
    
- communal areas;
    
- bathrooms;
    
- a kitchen;
    
- dining space;
    
- recreational areas;
    
- storage;
    
- and basic residential infrastructure.
    

It was selected specifically because it is large enough to accommodate five participants and Jane without forcing them into an artificially cramped environment.

The floor is otherwise empty.

Until now.

---

# 5. JANE DOE

Jane has been formally designated the trial's primary hazard.

She is authorized to employ her established psychological and intelligence-gathering capabilities against participants.

This includes:

- intimidation;
    
- manipulation;
    
- interrogation;
    
- deception;
    
- psychological pressure;
    
- social engineering;
    
- surveillance;
    
- strategic misinformation;
    
- and other approved methods of mental disruption.
    

More unusually:

> **Jane has been authorized to shred a participant's mentality.**

This phrase appears directly in the trial authorization.

C.O.R.E. interpretation:

Jane is permitted to psychologically dismantle a participant's composure, confidence, assumptions, group cohesion, and resistance.

It does **not** authorize permanent neurological injury, physical mutilation, or irreversible destruction of a participant's mind.

The distinction was specifically reviewed.

Jane herself summarized the authorization differently:

> **JANE:**  
> “I'm allowed to make them question every decision they've made for the last twenty-four hours.”

Pause.

> **JANE:**  
> “Apparently that's considered shredding their mentality.”

C.O.R.E. accepted the description.

---

# 6. THE 34-HOUR REQUIREMENT

The timer begins when the group enters the fifth floor.

It does not stop for:

- sleep;
    
- meals;
    
- arguments;
    
- psychological breakdowns;
    
- boredom;
    
- Jane disappearing;
    
- Jane returning;
    
- participants becoming convinced they are being watched;
    
- or participants deciding they have made a terrible mistake.
    

The only exception is an actual medical emergency.

Otherwise:

> **34 HOURS.**

---

# 7. THE FIFTH FLOOR

The fifth floor has been prepared as a normal residential environment.

There are no intentionally hidden torture devices.

There is no artificial obstacle course.

There is no combat arena.

There is simply a large, mostly ordinary living space.

Which is precisely the problem.

Jane is not being locked in a cage with the participants.

She is living with them.

She can:

- cook;
    
- sit in the lounge;
    
- watch television;
    
- make coffee;
    
- enter communal areas;
    
- knock on someone's door;
    
- ask questions;
    
- start conversations;
    
- refuse to answer questions;
    
- disappear into another room;
    
- and generally behave like a resident.
    

Participants are expected to determine when Jane is being sincere.

They will not always be correct.

---

# 8. PARTICIPANT EXPECTATIONS

Participants have been explicitly informed:

> **Jane is allowed to lie.**

She may tell the truth.

She may tell a partial truth.

She may tell an obviously ridiculous lie.

She may tell the truth in such a convincing manner that participants assume it is a lie.

She may lie in such a convincing manner that participants assume it is the truth.

There is no rule requiring her to maintain consistency.

---

# 9. GROUP DYNAMICS

Groups of five were selected deliberately.

Jane's specialty is not simply individual psychological manipulation.

She is exceptionally effective at exploiting relationships.

Five participants therefore create additional variables.

Jane may attempt to convince:

- Participant A that Participant B is lying;
    
- Participant B that Participant C is secretly cooperating;
    
- Participant C that Participant D has already failed;
    
- Participant D that Participant E cannot be trusted;
    
- Participant E that everyone else is conspiring against them.
    

None of these claims are guaranteed to be true.

Some may be.

---

# 10. SLEEP

Participants are allowed to sleep.

Jane is not required to let them sleep peacefully.

She may:

- wake them;
    
- ask questions;
    
- leave notes;
    
- create harmless distractions;
    
- sit quietly nearby;
    
- or simply stare at someone until they wake up.
    

Physical assault remains subject to standard trial restrictions.

Psychological warfare does not.

---

# 11. FOOD

Food is available throughout the trial.

Jane may cook.

Participants may cook.

Participants may eat together.

Participants may choose not to eat what Jane prepared.

This is considered a personal decision.

Jane has already requested permission to make cheeseburgers.

Permission was granted.

---

> **“No.”**
