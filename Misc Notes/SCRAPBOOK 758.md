### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — THE LAST SKETCHBOOK**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD SAFETY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 5  
**Condition:** Fragile / Nonverbal / Socially Withdrawn

---

# **OBSERVATION**

Alice's sketchbook was once one of the most important objects she owned.

It contained drawings of everyone.

Corin.

Orphie.

Belle.

Neko.

Miss Neko.

Star.

Nightshade.

Endmin.

Lumi.

Taiga.

Mart.

Bao.

Frost.

Astral.

Her mother.

Even people she couldn't properly remember anymore.

The drawings were not technically perfect.

They didn't need to be.

They were Alice's way of keeping people in her mind.

When she couldn't speak—

she drew.

When she was afraid she would forget someone—

she wrote about them.

When she wanted to express something she couldn't explain—

she drew it.

The sketchbook was, in many ways, Alice's voice.

---

# **THE DAMAGE**

The sketchbook was ruined during the rain incident.

The pages had absorbed the water.

Ink had spread.

Pencil markings had dissolved into gray smears.

Pages had stuck together.

Several drawings became completely unrecognizable.

Faces disappeared.

Names blurred.

Some pages tore while being separated.

The drawings could not be restored.

---

# **ALICE'S REACTION**

Alice did not cry.

She did not become angry.

She did not blame anyone.

She simply sat down.

She held the sketchbook in both hands.

And stared at it.

For a very long time.

---

# **THE FIRST DAY**

Corin placed a new sketchbook beside her.

Alice looked at it.

She did not touch it.

Corin opened it to the first page.

> "You can draw again."

Alice shook her head.

Corin waited.

Alice looked back at the ruined sketchbook.

Then closed it.

---

# **THE SECOND DAY**

Alice carried the ruined sketchbook around with her.

She didn't open it.

She didn't draw.

She simply kept it close.

When someone offered to make copies of her surviving drawings, Alice shook her head.

When someone offered to give her a new notebook, she shook her head again.

---

# **THE THIRD DAY**

Alice stopped carrying pencils.

Nobody had seen her without one for a very long time.

Now she left them untouched.

Her pencil case remained inside the Treehouse.

---

# **THE FOURTH DAY**

Alice sat at the table during dinner.

Her sketchbook was beside her.

She opened it.

A page contained a ruined drawing of Belle.

The face had almost completely disappeared.

Only the carrot remained recognizable.

Alice stared at it.

Then closed the book.

She didn't draw.

---

# **THE FIFTH DAY**

Star brought her colored pencils.

Alice looked at them.

She picked up a white pencil.

For a moment, everyone thought she might draw.

Instead, she put it back.

---

# **THE SIXTH DAY**

Alice was found sitting beneath the Treehouse window.

Her sketchbook was open.

No pencil.

No drawing.

She was simply looking at the pages.

Corin sat beside her.

Neither spoke.

Eventually, Alice closed the book.

---

# **THE SEVENTH DAY**

Alice stopped drawing entirely.

---

# **PERSONNEL RESPONSE**

Nobody tried to force her.

Nobody told her:

> "You need to draw again."

Nobody told her:

> "It's just a sketchbook."

Nobody told her:

> "We can make another one."

Because it wasn't just a sketchbook.

It contained memories that Alice had been desperately trying to preserve.

And now those memories were physically gone.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin was particularly affected.

She remembered Alice's first journal pages.

The crude drawings.

The little bunny figures.

The descriptions written in imperfect grammar.

Alice had once written:

> **"Alice don't forget."**

Now she had stopped writing.

---

# **STAR**

Star tried something different.

She sat beside Alice.

No lesson.

No grammar correction.

No questions.

Just company.

Alice eventually leaned against her.

Star didn't mention the sketchbook.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle brought Alice a carrot.

Alice accepted it.

Belle sat beside her.

She didn't ask Alice to draw her.

---

# **NEKO**

Neko quietly examined the damaged sketchbook.

He determined that several pages could potentially be preserved.

Alice watched him.

When he asked:

> "Want me to fix it?"

Alice shook her head.

Neko stopped immediately.

> "...Okay."

---

# **THE MOST PAINFUL PART**

Alice isn't angry.

She doesn't seem to think anyone did something wrong.

She doesn't think:

> _"They ruined my drawings."_

She thinks:

> **"Rain ruined them."**

And if asked why she was outside:

> **"Alice forgot."**

Not:

> _"They forgot Alice."_

Alice still places herself at the center of the blame.

---

# **A NEW SKETCHBOOK**

A new sketchbook remains on the table in the Treehouse.

Nobody has touched it.

It is clean.

Empty.

The first page remains completely blank.

A pencil sits beside it.

Alice knows it is there.

She simply doesn't use it.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice used to draw because drawing gave her a way to hold onto things.

People.

Places.

Names.

Memories.

Feelings.

Now she sits with the ruined book and looks at the places where those things used to be.

She isn't angry.

She isn't shouting.

She isn't demanding that anyone fix it.

She simply stopped.

And somehow, that is more painful than if she had screamed.

Because Alice's drawings were never merely drawings.

They were her way of saying:

> **"I remember you."**

And now the pages are blank.

The pencils are untouched.

The new sketchbook remains empty.

Alice still loves everyone.

She still remembers some of them.

She still smiles sometimes.

But she no longer draws.
