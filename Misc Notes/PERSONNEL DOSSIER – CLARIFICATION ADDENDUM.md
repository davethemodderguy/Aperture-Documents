# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **PERSONNEL DOSSIER – CLARIFICATION ADDENDUM**

### **SUBJECT: NEKO / MISS NEKO**

**Classification:** LEVEL 2 – Internal Reference

---

## **FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTION**

One of the most common questions asked by newly assigned personnel is:

> **"Why are Neko and Miss Neko the same person?"**

The answer is considerably simpler than most theories.

---

## **EXPLANATION**

Neko and Miss Neko are two manifestations of the same individual.

Miss Neko represents Neko's feminine side.

Although they possess distinct appearances, personalities, and mannerisms, they are understood within Aperture's records as different expressions of the same person rather than separate individuals.

This distinction is reflected throughout official documentation.

---

## **PERSONALITY DIFFERENCES**

### **Neko**

- Curious.
    
- Inventive.
    
- Constantly designing new technology.
    
- Enjoys experimenting with engineering concepts.
    
- Has a habit of creating things simply because the idea sounded interesting.
    

A common phrase heard around the workshop is:

> "I have an idea."

This sentence has caused Engineering staff varying levels of concern.

---

### **Miss Neko**

- Calm.
    
- Gentle.
    
- Practical.
    
- More conservative in engineering decisions.
    
- Frequently acts as the voice of reason.
    

If Neko proposes something particularly questionable, Miss Neko is often the first to respond.

Usually with:

> "...No."

---

## **WORKING RELATIONSHIP**

Despite their contrasting personalities, both share the same technical expertise and dedication to Aperture Industries.

Many successful projects are the result of Neko's creativity balanced by Miss Neko's practicality.

Engineering personnel often summarize the relationship with a simple equation:

> **Neko creates the impossible.**

> **Miss Neko makes sure it doesn't explode.**

---

## **STAFF REMARK**

When a new researcher once spent nearly an hour trying to understand the situation, a senior engineer finally explained it like this:

> "Don't overthink it."

> "Neko is Neko."

> "Miss Neko is also Neko."

> "You'll get used to it."

The researcher reportedly accepted the explanation immediately.

---

## **STATUS**

Personnel are reminded that both Neko and Miss Neko should be addressed respectfully according to how they are presenting themselves at the time.

Questions asked in good faith are welcome.

Conspiracy theories about cloning, parallel universes, or secret engineering experiments are not supported by any official records.

**End of Addendum.**
