### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — RIGHT OCULAR INJURY**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / PERMANENT OCULAR DAMAGE**

---

# **INCIDENT**

Another injury occurred during one of Alice's repeated attempts to leave SAFEHOUSE.

The resulting trauma severely damaged her **right eye**.

Alice's regenerative response activated.

It repaired surrounding tissue.

It stopped the bleeding.

It prevented further systemic deterioration.

But it could not restore the eye.

The damage was too extensive.

The right eye is now:

> **Permanently blind.**

---

# **REGENERATIVE FAILURE**

This incident has provided further evidence regarding the limitations of Alice's regeneration.

Her body repaired what it could.

However, the regenerative system could not reconstruct the damaged ocular structures to their previous functional state.

The eye remains permanently impaired.

Medical personnel have classified the injury as **irreversible**.

This is now considered one of Alice's permanent physical disabilities.

---

# **THE EYEPATCH**

Alice now wears an eyepatch over her right eye.

It was initially provided as medical protection.

Over time, it became part of her normal appearance.

The patch prevents Alice from repeatedly touching or injuring the damaged eye and provides protection against environmental exposure.

Alice does not complain about it.

When asked if it bothers her:

> **"No."**

When asked whether she knows why she needs it:

> **"Eye hurt."**

A pause.

> **"Eye doesn't work."**

She understands the basic fact.

She does not seem particularly concerned about the loss itself.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

The first time Alice saw herself wearing the eyepatch, she looked into a mirror.

She tilted her head.

> **"Alice look weird."**

Corin immediately answered:

> **"You look like Alice."**

Alice touched the edge of the patch.

> **"One eye."**

> **"Yeah."**

> **"Only one works?"**

Corin nodded.

Alice was quiet for several seconds.

Then:

> **"Okay."**

She did not cry.

She simply accepted it.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin was devastated.

Not because Alice looked different.

Because she knew why.

Another escape attempt.

Another injury.

Another piece of Alice's body permanently lost.

Corin carefully adjusted the eyepatch.

> **"Does it hurt?"**

Alice shook her head.

> **"No."**

Corin knew that the absence of pain did not make the loss less significant.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle immediately made Alice a second eyepatch.

It was slightly crooked.

Alice wore it anyway.

Belle refused to explain why she made it.

She simply said:

> **"So you have a spare."**

Alice smiled.

> **"Thank you, Belle."**

Belle smiled back.

Then looked away.

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi remembers when Alice had both eyes.

She remembers how Alice used to look around the Treehouse.

How she would watch the snow.

How she would stare at drawings she didn't understand.

Now one side of Alice's vision is gone permanently.

Lumi does not mention it unless Alice does.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie added a new restriction to Alice's medical profile.

The right eye is to be considered **non-functional**.

Personnel must account for Alice's reduced field of vision when:

- Guiding her through unfamiliar environments
    
- Assisting her with mobility
    
- Monitoring falls
    
- Moving equipment
    
- Navigating crowded areas
    

Alice is not to be treated as incapable.

She simply requires awareness of her blind side.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice's regeneration has saved her life countless times.

It has closed wounds.

Stopped bleeding.

Repaired broken tissue.

Kept her body functioning through injuries that should have been far more serious.

But it could not give Alice her right eye back.

That loss is permanent.

Now, beneath the familiar head bandages, Alice wears a small eyepatch.

She has learned to navigate with one eye.

She does not complain.

She does not blame anyone.

She simply continues.

And that, perhaps, worries everyone more than anger ever could.

Because Alice has become very good at accepting things that she should never have had to endure.
