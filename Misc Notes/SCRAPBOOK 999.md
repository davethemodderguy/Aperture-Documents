**SUBJECT:** Repeated Accidental Incidents Involving Neri  
**PRIMARY CONCERN:** Resident repeatedly unaccounted for during routine activities

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Neri's near-fatal medical incident, C.O.R.E. identified an unexpected secondary safety problem.

Neri has become substantially more difficult to notice.

She moves slowly.

She cannot speak.

She rarely interrupts people.

She does not call out when something happens.

She is reluctant to inconvenience others.

Consequently, ordinary accidents that would normally be minor have become significantly more dangerous.

Over the following period, multiple incidents were recorded.

None were intentional.

None are currently attributed to negligence by a specific individual.

The common factor is considerably simpler:

**Neri keeps being forgotten.**

---

# INCIDENT 01 — DOOR IMPACT

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Residential Corridor  
**SEVERITY:** Minor

Neri was standing immediately outside a room.

A resident inside opened the door.

Neri was not visible from the resident's initial position.

The door struck Neri directly in the face.

Neri stumbled backward.

The resident immediately apologized.

Neri reportedly waved it off.

She did not verbally respond.

When asked whether she was okay, Neri nodded.

She then continued walking.

**Follow-up:** Medical observation performed.

**Finding:** No major injury identified.

---

# INCIDENT 02 — LOCKED OUT

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Exterior Access  
**SEVERITY:** Moderate

Neri exited the SAFEHOUSE shortly before an automated security door entered its locked state.

She attempted to return.

The system did not recognize her immediate presence outside the door.

Neri could not verbally call for assistance.

She remained outside.

She did not activate the emergency call system.

Weather conditions were poor.

She was eventually discovered standing beside the entrance.

When asked why she had not called anyone, Neri simply shrugged.

---

# INCIDENT 03 — LOCKED INSIDE

**LOCATION:** Storage Room  
**SEVERITY:** Moderate

Neri entered a storage room to retrieve an item.

The door subsequently closed and locked.

Neri attempted to open it.

The mechanism did not respond.

She could not call for assistance.

Rather than trigger a facility-wide alert, Neri sat down.

She waited.

She was eventually discovered during a routine search.

When asked how long she had been inside, Neri indicated that she did not know.

---

# INCIDENT 04 — STAIRWAY FALL

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Main Stairwell  
**SEVERITY:** HIGH

Neri attempted to descend the stairway.

Her current physical condition caused her movement to be significantly slower than normal.

She became unstable.

Neri fell down several steps.

A resident heard the impact.

Neri was conscious when discovered.

She was unable to immediately stand.

When asked what happened, Neri pointed toward the stairs.

Medical personnel were contacted.

No major new injury was identified.

**Assessment:** Her current physical weakness makes ordinary stair navigation a significantly greater risk.

---

# INCIDENT 05 — ELEVATOR ENTrapment

**LOCATION:** Sector 4-C Elevator System  
**SEVERITY:** HIGH

Neri entered an elevator alone.

The elevator subsequently stopped between designated floors.

Neri was unable to establish communication with personnel.

The emergency communication interface required a verbal response that Neri could not provide.

She remained inside.

Facility personnel eventually identified the elevator as stationary and initiated a manual inspection.

Neri was found sitting on the floor.

She appeared tired rather than panicked.

When the doors opened, she slowly stood.

The first thing she did was apologize.

---

# BEHAVIORAL PATTERN

The incidents appear unrelated.

They are not.

Each incident contains the same underlying sequence:

**Neri is somewhere.**

↓

**Nobody notices her.**

↓

**Something happens.**

↓

**Neri cannot effectively call for help.**

↓

**Neri waits.**

↓

**Someone eventually finds her.**

The duration varies.

The outcome varies.

The underlying problem does not.

---

# THE MOST CONCERNING RESPONSE

Personnel repeatedly report that Neri apologizes after these incidents.

She apologizes for:

- being hit by the door;
    
- being locked outside;
    
- being locked inside;
    
- falling;
    
- becoming trapped;
    
- requiring assistance.
    

C.O.R.E. has found no evidence that Neri believes these events are anyone's fault.

Instead, she appears to believe that **requiring other people to deal with the consequences is itself a problem.**

This is unacceptable.

# CORRECTIVE ACTIONS

C.O.R.E. has implemented the following changes.

### 1. Visual Resident Tracking

Neri's presence must be accounted for during:

- severe weather;
    
- emergency evacuations;
    
- facility lockdowns;
    
- scheduled headcounts;
    
- major SAFEHOUSE movements.
    

### 2. Non-Vocal Emergency Access

Emergency systems must provide Neri with methods of requesting assistance that do not require speech.

### 3. Door Safety

High-traffic doors are being reviewed for improved visibility and slower closing behavior where practical.

### 4. Elevator Monitoring

Elevator systems will automatically flag prolonged stops and verify whether a resident is inside.

### 5. Stairway Assistance

Neri is not prohibited from using stairs.

However, personnel are encouraged to accompany her when she is visibly exhausted or medically unstable.

### 6. No "She's Probably Somewhere" Assumption

This phrase has been informally associated with several of the incidents.

It is no longer considered an acceptable assumption regarding Neri.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Neri is not becoming careless.

She is not intentionally placing herself in danger.

She is not seeking attention through accidents.

The problem is almost the opposite.

She has become **too easy to overlook.**

Her inability to speak has removed one of the simplest ways a person can announce:

> _"I'm here."_

Her weakness has reduced her ability to physically seek people out.

Her personality prevents her from demanding attention.

And her old belief that she is a burden makes her reluctant to ask for help even when help is necessary.

As a result, ordinary life keeps producing extraordinary problems for one very quiet resident.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The SAFEHOUSE does not need to turn Neri into the center of everyone's attention.

Nobody needs to follow her around.

Nobody needs to panic every time she walks into another room.

But when a ten-year-old resident can be:

**locked outside,**

**locked inside,**

**hit by a door,**

**fall down a staircase,**

**trapped in an elevator,**

and still quietly decide that she should simply wait until someone notices—

the problem is no longer Neri's quietness.

**The problem is that everyone else has gotten too comfortable with not looking.**
