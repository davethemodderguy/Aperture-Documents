# Neri, As I See Her

Neri doesn't need another document explaining who she is.

She knows who she is.

Or, at least, she knows the parts of herself she has decided are worth keeping.

What she probably doesn't know is what she looks like from the outside.

So that's what this is.

Not _who Neri is._

**Who Neri is to me.**

---

## 1. She's Quiet

Obvious observation.

Almost insultingly obvious.

Neri is quiet.

But people keep interpreting that as something it isn't.

Silence doesn't mean she's angry.

It doesn't mean she's sad.

It doesn't mean she's waiting for somebody to come talk to her.

And it certainly doesn't mean somebody should go looking for her and ask what's wrong.

Sometimes she's just sitting somewhere because she wants to sit somewhere.

I've watched her spend absurd amounts of time doing absolutely nothing.

No emergency.

No crisis.

No hidden message.

Just Neri, sitting somewhere quiet.

That's allowed.

---

## 2. She's Much More Aware Than People Give Her Credit For

Neri notices things.

A lot of things.

She notices footsteps.

She notices when someone's trying to approach her.

She notices changes in people's voices.

She notices when someone is pretending something is fine.

She notices when somebody has moved something in a room.

She notices when people are watching her.

She notices when people are worrying about her.

Especially that last one.

Unfortunately, noticing that someone cares about her doesn't necessarily make her feel better.

Sometimes it makes her leave.

---

## 3. She Doesn't Like Being Taken Care Of

That's not entirely accurate.

She likes being cared about.

She just doesn't like feeling **dependent.**

There's a difference.

Bao gives her blood.

Frost worries about her.

Alice tries to interact with her.

Jane worries about her.

Neri knows all of this.

She also knows that all of these people have lives.

That's where her thinking goes wrong.

She sees every act of care as something that costs the other person something.

Time.

Energy.

Attention.

Resources.

She keeps trying to calculate whether she's worth the expenditure.

She isn't a machine.

There is no acceptable efficiency rating for a child.

---

## 4. She Is Not Difficult

I've heard people describe Neri as difficult.

They're usually talking about the fact that she won't cooperate with something.

That's not the same thing.

Neri isn't difficult.

She's **guarded.**

There is a reason for that.

And honestly, considering where she came from, I'm surprised she lets anyone get as close as she does.

Although lately she doesn't let anyone get close at all.

That's different.

And we're respecting it.

---

## 5. Leave Her Alone

This is important enough that I'm putting it here twice.

**Leave her alone.**

If Neri is sitting somewhere quiet, don't go sit beside her.

If she walks away, don't follow.

If she disappears into another hallway, don't turn it into a search operation.

If she doesn't answer, don't keep asking.

If she wants company, she'll find someone.

Or she won't.

Both are acceptable.

The mistake people keep making is assuming that respecting her solitude means abandoning her.

It doesn't.

We're still here.

We're simply not standing three feet away reminding her that we're here.

---

## 6. She Doesn't Need to Earn Her Place

I've tried explaining this.

It didn't go well.

I don't blame Jane for that. I don't think anyone could have predicted how Neri would interpret the conversation.

But I think I understand the problem now.

Neri spent too much of her childhood being evaluated.

Useful.

Not useful.

Worth keeping.

Not worth keeping.

Good at something.

Bad at something.

She learned the wrong lesson from all of it.

She learned that having value is something other people decide.

So when somebody tells her she doesn't have to be useful, she doesn't necessarily hear:

> _You don't have to prove yourself._

She can hear:

> _You're useless, but we'll keep you anyway._

Those are very different statements.

So I'm done talking about Neri in terms of usefulness.

She's not equipment.

She's not an asset.

She's not a project.

She's Neri.

That should have been enough from the beginning.

---

## 7. Chemistry

Don't.

Seriously.

Don't.

I've seen people notice how good she is with chemistry and immediately think:

_"Maybe we should get her back into it."_

No.

Chemistry is one of the things that reminds her of the people who decided she wasn't worth keeping.

Being talented at something doesn't mean you're obligated to love it.

I can archive the lab equipment.

I can secure the textbooks.

I can make sure nobody assigns her anything.

That's enough.

She doesn't need to prove that her old skills still exist.

---

## 8. Her Room

Her room is probably the strangest thing she's ever built.

And I've worked here long enough that "strange" is an extremely competitive category.

Floating islands.

Artificial stars.

Gravity.

Bridges.

No normal floor.

An entire little piece of reality that exists because Neri apparently looked at an empty room and decided:

_"No. This needs more sky."_

I actually like it.

It feels like her.

Not because it's anomalous.

Because it's quiet.

She made somewhere where nobody can tell her where to stand.

That's probably more important than the floating islands.

---

## 9. The Cake Incident

I don't think Neri realizes how funny this is.

She accidentally incorporated a fragment of an Architect's wing into herself because there was a piece of it in a cupcake.

We now have an entire chain of events that basically amounts to:

**Guiding Light:** "I'll give her a little bit of my power."

**Neri:** _eats cupcake_

**Reality:** "Congratulations."

And somehow we're all just living with this now.

I still haven't decided whether to call that a scientific breakthrough or a workplace safety violation.

Probably both.

---

## 10. She Likes Sweets

Everyone knows this.

But there's a reason.

Her sister liked sweets.

That's why it started.

But I don't think we should reduce it to that anymore.

Neri actually likes cake.

She likes candy.

She likes sweet drinks.

She'll eat something that would make the rest of us question whether sugar should legally be allowed to exist and simply say nothing.

That's hers now.

The memory may have started it.

The preference belongs to Neri.

---

## 11. Her Glasses

She was nearsighted before the Seal transformation.

We got her proper glasses.

She put them on.

Looked around.

Then said:

> "Oh."

Pause.

> "I can see the wall."

One of the funniest things she's ever said.

I don't think she intended it to be funny.

That made it funnier.

---

## 12. Her Coat

The oversized coat is staying.

Yes, the sleeves cover her hands.

Yes, she sometimes gets frosting on them.

Yes, I treated them so frosting doesn't stick.

No, this does not make the coat a towel.

I am recording this because somebody will eventually try to use it as one.

---

## 13. What I Worry About

Not that she's quiet.

Not that she likes being alone.

Not that she doesn't socialize.

Those aren't inherently problems.

I worry about the moments when being alone becomes dangerous.

Neri has a habit of deciding that her problems are not important enough to interrupt somebody else's day.

That's the dangerous part.

She can be severely anemic and think:

_"They're busy."_

She can be in pain and think:

_"I'll wait."_

She can need help and think:

_"I shouldn't bother them."_

That isn't independence.

That's what happens when someone learns that asking for things has consequences.

So if there's a genuine emergency, we intervene.

Not because she owes us cooperation.

Because she's a resident.

Because she's ours to protect.

---

## 14. I Don't Think She Knows How Many People Care

Actually—

That's not quite right.

She knows.

That's the problem.

She knows people care.

She just doesn't understand why.

She keeps looking for the transaction.

What did she give them?

What did she accomplish?

What did she contribute?

Why are they still here?

And there isn't always an answer that fits inside that framework.

Sometimes someone stays because they love you.

That's it.

No exchange.

No debt.

No invoice.

No purpose.

Just staying.

---

# Final Observation

Neri is ten years old.

Sometimes I think everyone forgets that.

Not because she's immature.

She's actually frighteningly perceptive for her age.

But because she's been forced to think about things children shouldn't have had to think about.

Death.

Abandonment.

Worth.

Usefulness.

Survival.

She shouldn't have had to become good at those things.

So I'm not going to ask her to become good at them anymore.

She can read.

She can drink her coffee.

She can sit somewhere quiet.

She can eat cake.

She can build impossible rooms.

She can ignore all of us.

She can come back when she wants.

And if she never does?

We'll still leave the door open.

Not metaphorically.

Actually.

Because she's Neri.

She lives here.

That's enough.
