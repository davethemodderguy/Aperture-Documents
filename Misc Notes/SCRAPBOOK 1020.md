## BEHAVIORAL DETERMINATION

Further observation has resulted in a definitive conclusion:

> **There is currently no effective method of voluntarily getting close to Neri.**

Not through conversation.

Not through invitations.

Not through sitting beside her.

Not through following her.

Not through reassurance.

Not through gifts.

Not through attempts to recreate previous friendships.

Not through emotional appeals.

Not through repeatedly reminding her that she is loved.

Attempts at closeness consistently produce withdrawal.

The more someone attempts to enter Neri's personal space, the further Neri moves away.

At present, this response appears effectively automatic.

---

## THE CORRECT RESPONSE: LEAVE HER BE

Previous recommendations emphasized maintaining availability while allowing Neri to approach voluntarily.

Current observations indicate an even simpler approach is appropriate:

> **Leave Neri alone.**

This is not abandonment.

This is not punishment.

This is not giving up on her.

It is respecting the only boundary Neri is currently communicating consistently.

Neri wants solitude.

She is actively seeking solitude.

She becomes distressed or withdrawn when that solitude is interrupted.

Therefore, her solitude should be respected.

---

## CURRENT SAFEHOUSE PROTOCOL

Residents are instructed:

### If Neri is in her room:

**Leave her alone.**

Do not knock repeatedly.

Do not enter without necessity.

Do not call through the door.

Do not leave repeated messages asking her to come out.

---

### If Neri is in the cafeteria:

**Leave her alone.**

Residents may use the cafeteria normally.

They should not deliberately sit beside her.

They should not move their chair closer.

They should not attempt to start a conversation.

They should not ask why she is sitting alone.

If Neri wants company, she can choose to approach someone herself.

---

### If Neri walks past:

**Let her walk past.**

Do not call her name simply to obtain a response.

Do not follow.

Do not turn around and follow her.

Do not attempt to walk beside her.

---

### If Neri ignores someone:

**Accept it.**

Neri is not required to acknowledge every person she passes.

Her silence is not an invitation to try harder.

---

## EXCEPTION: MEDICAL EMERGENCY

This directive does **not** override emergency intervention.

Neri's severe anemia remains a known medical risk.

If Neri is visibly experiencing a medical emergency, personnel are authorized to obtain medical assistance regardless of her preference for solitude.

However:

> **Medical intervention is not social interaction.**

Personnel should not use medical concerns as an excuse to remain near Neri after the emergency has been addressed.

Once she is medically stable:

**Give her space.**

---

## WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT

The previous approach attempted to communicate:

> **“You can be alone, but we're still here.”**

Neri appears to interpret even this as:

> **“They are still waiting for me to interact with them.”**

Therefore, the message needs to become simpler.

There is no expectation.

There is no requirement.

There is no emotional debt.

There is no obligation to return.

There is no requirement to respond.

There is simply:

> **“Go be alone.”**

And, importantly:

> **Nobody will be angry with you for doing so.**

## C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Neri's current solitude should not be treated as a problem that other residents must solve.

She has made her preference extremely clear.

At this stage, attempting to reconnect with her is counterproductive.

The appropriate response is therefore:

> **Respect the distance.**

Neri can remain alone.

Neri can eat alone.

Neri can read alone.

Neri can drink her coffee alone.

Neri can return to her room alone.

Neri does not need someone beside her simply because someone cares about her.

Her relationships do not disappear because she wants solitude.

They simply become **inactive until she chooses otherwise.**

---

## FINAL DETERMINATION

There is no secret method.

There is no correct phrase.

There is no special approach.

There is no emotional breakthrough capable of bypassing Neri's current boundary.

**Do not try.**

For now, the kindest thing the SAFEHOUSE can do is remarkably simple:

> **Leave Neri be.**

Let her have the silence she wants.

Let her walk away.

Let her sit alone.

Let her exist without being watched for a reaction.

Let her discover that nobody becomes angry when she chooses solitude.

And if, one day, Neri decides she wants somebody beside her again—

**she can be the one to close the distance.**

Until then:

**Leave her alone.**

**She is allowed to be alone.**
