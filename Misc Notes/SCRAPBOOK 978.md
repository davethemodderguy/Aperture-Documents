# SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL OBSERVATION

## SUBJECT: NERI

### WEEKS SINCE DISAPPEARANCE

**Classification:** BLACKSITE INTERNAL — EMOTIONAL / EXISTENTIAL  
**Subject Status:** MISSING  
**Confirmed Recovery:** NONE  
**Search Status:** ACTIVE  
**Time Since Disappearance:** Several weeks

---

# 1. THE BLACKSITE KEPT MOVING

For the first few days, everyone searched.

Then the search became organized.

Then routine.

Then something that existed in the background of everyone's day.

Neri remained missing.

Guiding Light continued searching.

Aperture continued analyzing dimensional signatures.

Neko continued examining every possible spatial anomaly.

Bao continued looking.

Jane continued looking.

Alice continued looking.

Everyone looked.

Nothing.

Neri had disappeared into a place so distant that even an Architect could not simply reach her.

The Blacksite had no choice but to continue functioning.

And it did.

Doors opened.

Elevators moved.

Labs operated.

Workers changed shifts.

The cafeteria served meals.

People went to work.

People went home.

Life continued.

Except something was missing.

---

# 2. GUIDING LIGHT

Guiding Light eventually admitted her greatest regret.

Not losing the search.

Not failing to locate Neri.

Something much earlier.

The cupcake.

The fragment of her wing.

The shard of Architect Light that accidentally became part of Neri.

Guiding Light remembered how excited she had been.

She had been proud.

She had taught Neri to use the wings.

She had watched Neri fly.

She had watched the little Seal Thiren discover something extraordinary inside herself.

Now Guiding Light could not stop thinking:

> **"What if I hadn't dropped it?"**

The shard had given Neri abilities.

It had given her something beautiful.

But Guiding Light now wondered whether she had unknowingly given an already-traumatized child another reason to believe she was different.

Another reason to stand apart.

Another thing that could make people worry about her.

Her regret was not rational.

She knew that.

The shard was not responsible for Neri's childhood.

It was not responsible for her anemia.

It was not responsible for her belief that she was expendable.

But regret rarely cares about what is rational.

Guiding Light's thought remained:

> **"I wish I had caught it."**

---

# 3. BAO

Bao's regret was simpler.

She remembered every time Neri had been sick.

Every time Neri had said:

> "I'm fine."

Every time Bao had accepted it for even a moment.

Every time she had returned to work after stabilizing Neri.

Every time Neri had sat nearby without asking for anything.

Bao had always believed she would have another chance to do better.

Now she had discovered that there was no guarantee of another chance.

Her greatest regret:

> **She had spent too much time trying to keep Neri alive and not enough time making sure Neri knew she was wanted.**

Bao had been treating the symptoms.

Neri had been fighting something much older.

---

# 4. JANE

Jane's regret was different.

She remembered the conversation in the cafeteria.

The conversation nobody else knew about.

The conversation Neri had trusted her with.

Jane remembered the name Neri had given her.

She remembered the things Neri had told her.

She remembered promising, implicitly and explicitly, that she could bother her.

Then she remembered the last time Neri had declined her invitation.

Jane had accepted the answer.

She had let Neri walk away.

Now she wondered:

> **"Why did I let 'no' be enough?"**

Jane knew Neri was afraid of being a burden.

She knew Neri would say no even when she wanted someone nearby.

Jane had understood that.

And yet she had still respected the refusal without pushing further.

That decision now haunted her.

---

# 5. ALICE

Alice's regret was painfully ordinary.

She remembered asking Neri to come to the greenhouse.

Neri said no.

Alice had smiled.

Then her smile disappeared.

She had ridden away.

At the time, she had thought:

> _Maybe Neri just doesn't want to hang out._

Now Alice wondered whether Neri had actually been saying:

> _I don't think I'm allowed to._

Alice remembered all the times Neri had followed her around.

The scooter rides.

The little jokes.

The quiet companionship.

The times Neri didn't even need to talk.

Alice had thought those moments would continue.

She had never considered that one day there would simply be no Neri behind her anymore.

---

# 6. STAR

Star's regret was perhaps the smallest.

And therefore one of the most painful.

She remembered telling Neri:

> "Go annoy Nightshade."

It had always been a joke.

Neri was part of their little world.

Star could joke because she assumed Neri would still be there tomorrow.

Now the words felt different.

Star sometimes found herself looking toward the corridor expecting to see a small blue-coated figure.

Sometimes she almost called:

> "My Little Angel."

Then remembered.

There was nobody there.

---

# 7. NIGHTSHADE

Nightshade rarely spoke about it.

But she noticed something.

The Blacksite had become quieter.

Not literally.

It was still loud.

But certain sounds were missing.

Neri's footsteps.

Her coat brushing against furniture.

Her quiet breathing while reading.

The sound of a fork against a plate.

Occasional arguments over being called cute.

Someone asking where the chemistry books were.

Someone complaining about cake portions.

Small sounds.

Meaningless sounds.

Until they disappeared.

---

# 8. THE CAFETERIA

The cafeteria was where the absence became most obvious.

There was a table Neri used to occupy.

For weeks after her disappearance, people continued to glance at it.

Eventually, someone put a piece of cake there.

Nobody knew who.

It remained untouched.

The next day, another appeared.

Then another.

Nobody officially established the tradition.

Nobody discussed it.

It simply happened.

A place where Neri used to sit remained reserved without anyone saying it was reserved.

---

# 9. THE CHEMISTRY BOOK

Her chemistry book was eventually recovered from the cafeteria.

It was returned to her room.

Nobody opened it.

It sat on the desk.

Dust slowly accumulated on its cover.

The book had once been one of the easiest ways to find Neri.

If you couldn't find her, you could check the cafeteria.

If she wasn't there, check the greenhouse.

If she wasn't there, check somewhere quiet.

There had always been somewhere.

Now there wasn't.

---

# 10. THE STRANGE CONTRADICTION

Neri had spent months becoming a background character.

She had believed that if she became quiet enough:

People would stop worrying.

If she became invisible enough:

People would stop being burdened.

If she disappeared:

Everyone would simply continue without her.

She was wrong.

Not because the Blacksite stopped functioning.

It didn't.

People continued working.

People continued laughing.

People continued eating.

People continued living.

But Neri's absence became visible everywhere.

A vacant chair.

An untouched cake.

An unread chemistry book.

A missing voice.

A corridor where someone expected a small figure to appear.

A person nobody had realized they were looking for until there was nothing left to look at.

---

# 11. THE CRUEL IRONY

Neri wanted to be forgotten.

Instead, everyone remembered.

Too much.

Every person who had once walked past her remembered walking past.

Every person who had once ignored her remembered ignoring her.

Every person who had assumed she was fine remembered saying nothing.

Every person who had been too busy remembered being busy.

The people who loved her remembered the moments they thought were insignificant.

The little things became enormous.

Because there would be no new ones.

---

# 12. GUIDING LIGHT'S SEARCH

Guiding Light continued searching.

Someone eventually asked why she kept doing it when she had already admitted it could take billions of years.

Her answer was simple.

> **GUIDING LIGHT:**  
> "Because she's still out there."

A pause.

> **GUIDING LIGHT:**  
> "And I don't want her to think nobody came."

---

# 13. THE REGRET

Everyone had their own version.

**Bao:**  
_I should have made her understand that she was my daughter because I wanted her, not because I had to take care of her._

**Jane:**  
_I should have gone after her._

**Alice:**  
_I should have stayed._

**Star:**  
_I should have noticed._

**Guiding Light:**  
_I should have caught the shard._

**Neko:**  
_I should have realized what the silence meant._

Nobody could change any of it.

And that was the cruelest part.

Neri had spent her entire life believing that people would eventually get tired of her.

She had spent months proving to herself that she could disappear without causing trouble.

Then she disappeared.

And the people left behind discovered something Neri had never been able to believe:

**She had mattered enormously.**

---

# 14. FINAL OBSERVATION

Neri wanted to become a background character.

She succeeded.

For a while.

Nobody noticed when she faded.

Nobody noticed when she stopped catching up.

Nobody noticed when she stopped eating.

Nobody noticed when she stopped reading.

Nobody noticed when she stopped visiting.

Nobody noticed when she stopped asking.

Then she disappeared completely.

And suddenly everyone noticed.

Too late.

The Blacksite is full of people.

But there is one particular absence that seems to follow everyone everywhere.

A little Seal Thiren who used to sit in the cafeteria with cake hidden inside oversized sleeves.

A little girl who read chemistry books because they gave her somewhere else to put her thoughts.

A little friend who rarely asked for anything.

A daughter who never believed she was allowed to be one.

She spent her life trying not to take up space.

Now everyone knows exactly how much space she occupied.

**END OBSERVATION**
