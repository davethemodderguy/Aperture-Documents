# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Neri has ceased meaningful interaction with nearly every SAFEHOUSE resident.

This behavior is no longer accurately described as simple quietness.

Neri is now **actively avoiding people.**

When approached, she frequently:

- refuses eye contact;
    
- turns away;
    
- moves to another location;
    
- ignores attempts at conversation;
    
- closes or moves her book between herself and the other person;

The only significant exception is **Bao**.

Neri continues interacting with Bao because Bao provides the blood stabilization and medication Neri requires to remain medically functional.

The distinction is clinically significant.

Neri is not completely incapable of social interaction.

She is choosing not to engage.

# 2. BAO — THE EXCEPTION

Neri continues to interact with Bao.

This interaction is substantially different from her interactions with everyone else.

When Bao enters Neri's room, Neri does not leave.

When Bao speaks, Neri listens.

When Bao asks whether she needs medication, Neri responds.

When Neri requires blood stabilization, she allows Bao to assist.

Neri will also occasionally remain beside Bao without communicating.

This is currently the closest thing to normal interaction that remains.

However, C.O.R.E. has identified an uncomfortable aspect of this behavior.

Neri appears to have reduced her relationship with Bao to something she believes is **necessary**.

She requires Bao.

Therefore, Bao is permitted to remain close.

Everyone else is apparently considered optional.

# 7. JANE'S RESPONSE

This is deliberate.

She has been instructed that Neri's withdrawal may be an expression of distress rather than hostility.

Jane nevertheless appears significantly affected by the rejection.

# 8. POSSIBLE INTERNAL LOGIC

C.O.R.E. cannot establish Neri's exact reasoning without her cooperation.

However, behavioral evidence suggests a possible internal conclusion:

> **People get hurt when they care about me.**

Neri nearly died.

Jane witnessed it.

Bao had to bring her back.

Her recovery has been slow.

She has lost her voice.

She has repeatedly been injured by ordinary accidents because people failed to notice her.

And Jane, Alice, Frost, and the others have repeatedly demonstrated fear and distress over her condition.

Neri may therefore be attempting to remove herself from their lives.

Not because she hates them.

Not because she stopped caring.

But because she may believe that **being close to her gives them another person to lose.**

# 11. MEDICAL CONSIDERATION

Neri's current anemia makes this behavior particularly dangerous.

A resident who refuses social interaction can still be monitored medically.

A resident who refuses social interaction **and cannot speak** presents a significantly greater safety problem.

Neri may become ill without notifying anyone.

She may experience weakness without asking for assistance.

She may collapse without being heard.

She may intentionally move away from people who would otherwise notice.

This is not considered an acceptable long-term situation.

# 12. C.O.R.E. RECOMMENDATION

Personnel are explicitly instructed **not to chase Neri.**

Do not corner her.

Do not surround her.

Do not demand explanations.

Do not repeatedly ask:

> "Why are you ignoring us?"

Do not tell her:

> "You're hurting everyone."

Do not guilt her into interacting.

These responses may reinforce precisely the belief that appears to be driving the withdrawal.

Instead:

**Remain available.**

Continue acknowledging her presence.

Continue offering food.

Continue leaving the door open.

Continue sitting nearby when appropriate.

Allow Neri to decide when she is ready to approach.

Most importantly:

**Do not disappear just because Neri is trying to disappear.**

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Neri has stopped interacting with almost everyone.

Not because she has forgotten them.

Not because she no longer loves them.

And not because they have necessarily done something wrong.

She appears to be attempting to make herself socially insignificant again.

The difference now is that this behavior is deliberate.

She has stopped smiling.

She has stopped seeking attention.

She has stopped approaching people.

And now she is actively pushing away the people who refuse to overlook her.

Bao remains the exception because Neri still needs her medical support.

Alice and Frost are being avoided despite their previous closeness.

Jane is being avoided most aggressively.

This is considered the most concerning aspect of the current behavioral change.
