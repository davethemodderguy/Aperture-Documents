# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION REPORT**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – SELF-PORTRAIT ANALYSIS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 7 – Medical & Executive Personnel Only

---

# **OVERVIEW**

During an occupational therapy session, Corin Lockwood was asked to complete a simple exercise.

The instruction was straightforward.

> **"Draw yourself."**

No additional guidance was provided.

Corin remained silent for nearly twenty minutes before beginning.

---

# **THE DRAWING**

The resulting illustration differed significantly from staff expectations.

Corin did not draw herself as the other residents knew her.

She did not draw herself sitting in SAFEHOUSE.

She did not draw herself drinking tea with Belle.

She did not draw herself working in Accounting.

Instead...

She drew the version of herself she believes exists.

A young girl with an exhausted expression.

Her skin was left almost completely colorless.

Heavy surgical markings covered large portions of her body.

Numerous restraints surrounded her, extending beyond the edges of the page as though they continued outside the drawing itself.

Her chest was depicted with cracks filled by glowing crimson Ether rather than warmth.

Where other people might draw a smile...

Corin drew nothing.

Only an empty expression staring directly at the viewer.

Above the figure, written in careful handwriting, were six words.

> **"I don't remember being normal anymore."**

---

# **STAFF RESPONSE**

The room fell silent.

No one criticized the drawing.

No one called it inaccurate.

Medical staff understood that the illustration was not intended to document Corin's appearance.

It documented her self-image.

The drawing was immediately archived as part of Corin's ongoing psychological assessment.

---

# **LUMI**

Later that evening, Lumi found Corin quietly looking at the picture.

She sat beside her.

Neither spoke for several minutes.

Eventually Lumi asked,

> "Is that how you see yourself?"

Corin nodded.

Without looking away from the page.

> "...I know everyone says I'm more than this."

Another pause.

> "...I just can't see it."

Lumi looked at the drawing.

Then at Corin sitting beside her.

Finally she picked up a pencil.

Without asking permission, she drew one small addition.

A second figure.

An older woman kneeling beside the child, gently holding her hand.

Corin watched quietly.

Lumi smiled.

> "You forgot someone."

Corin's eyes began to fill with tears.

---

# **MEDICAL INTERPRETATION**

Psychological Services concluded that Corin's self-portrait demonstrates persistent trauma-related distortions in self-perception.

The illustration overwhelmingly identifies Corin with the period of abuse she endured as a child rather than with her present identity.

Notably absent from the drawing were any representations of:

- Project SAFEHOUSE.
    
- Her siblings.
    
- Belle.
    
- Her friends.
    
- Her work.
    
- Her recovery.
    

Medical staff note that trauma often narrows a person's sense of self until painful experiences overshadow every other part of their identity.

The drawing should therefore be understood as an expression of Corin's emotional state, not an objective depiction of who she is.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin requested that the drawing not be displayed publicly.

It remains sealed within her medical file.

The only alteration ever made was the small figure Lumi quietly sketched beside her daughter.

Corin never erased it.

**End of Psychological Observation Report**
