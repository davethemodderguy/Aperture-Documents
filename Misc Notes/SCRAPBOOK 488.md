## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **RESTRICTED PSYCHOLOGICAL / SAFEHOUSE LOG**

# **THE NOTEBOOK**

**Classification:** LEVEL 7 — Architect-Sealed  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Location:** Remote Blacksite Perimeter Treehouse  
**Status:** **ONGOING PSYCHOLOGICAL DETERIORATION DURING ISOLATION**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Orphie frequently has to leave the treehouse.

The reasons are mundane and unavoidable.

Medical supplies need replacing.

Food runs low.

Stitching materials need restocking.

Corin's clothing and personal necessities require replacement.

The treehouse cannot sustain itself indefinitely.

Orphie always returns.

But every time she leaves, Corin is left alone.

And every time Corin is left alone, she writes.

---

# **THE NOTEBOOK**

The small notebook originally existed to help Corin maintain continuity.

It was supposed to contain reminders.

Names.

Important memories.

Small things she was afraid of forgetting.

Over time, another section began filling with writing.

The entries only appeared when Orphie was away.

The handwriting changes throughout them.

Sometimes neat.

Sometimes rushed.

Sometimes almost unreadable.

Some pages contain only a single sentence.

Others fill entire pages.

---

# **SELECTED ENTRIES**

### **ENTRY — ORPHIE GONE**

> **She's only getting supplies.**

> **She'll be back.**

> **She said she'll be back.**

> **I know she'll be back.**

> **Why am I scared?**

---

### **ENTRY — LATER**

> **It's quiet.**

> **Too quiet.**

> **I don't like this.**

> **I keep listening for her.**

> **There is nothing.**

---

### **ENTRY — UNKNOWN TIME**

> **What if she gets tired of coming back?**

> **What if one day she decides I'm too much work?**

> **She has to stitch me back together every time.**

> **Why wouldn't she get tired?**

---

### **ENTRY — SAME DAY**

> **I hate thinking like this.**

> **I know she's coming back.**

> **I know.**

> **I KNOW.**

> **Then why does it feel like she's already gone forever?**

---

# **THE DARKER ENTRIES**

Some entries are considerably more disturbing.

They do not describe specific plans or actions.

They describe Corin's perception of herself.

> **Maybe the treehouse is where people put things they don't want anymore.**

> **Maybe that's why nobody visits.**

> **Maybe Mom stopped coming because she finally realized I'm not worth the trouble.**

Another:

> **If I disappeared, would anyone notice?**

And another:

> **I don't know how long I can keep being somebody's problem.**

Orphie has stopped allowing Corin to read these entries alone after returning.

Not because Corin is forbidden from expressing herself.

Because Orphie has realized how quickly the thoughts can reinforce themselves.

---

# **ORPHIE'S RETURN**

When Orphie returns, she usually finds Corin sitting somewhere quiet.

Sometimes by the window.

Sometimes on the floor.

Sometimes curled up in the corner.

The notebook is almost always nearby.

Orphie never immediately asks:

> **"What did you write?"**

Instead:

> **"I'm back."**

Corin usually looks up.

Sometimes she smiles.

Sometimes she cries.

Sometimes she simply says:

> **"...Okay."**

---

# **THE SUPPLIES**

Orphie hates leaving.

But she has no choice.

Corin's condition requires supplies.

Her body does not maintain itself reliably.

Food must be brought in.

Medical materials must be replenished.

Even the simplest necessities eventually run out.

Orphie cannot ask someone else to make every trip.

The treehouse is simply too far away.

So she leaves.

And Corin waits.

---

# **ORPHIE'S DISCOVERY**

One evening, Orphie returned earlier than expected.

Corin had not noticed her enter.

She was writing.

Orphie stopped at the doorway.

Corin's voice was barely audible.

> **"If she doesn't come back, maybe that's okay."**

Orphie froze.

Corin continued writing.

> **"She deserves a life where she doesn't have to keep fixing me."**

Orphie immediately crossed the room.

> **"Corin."**

Corin looked up.

Her face went pale.

> **"...You're back."**

> **"Yeah."**

Corin closed the notebook.

> **"Don't read it."**

Orphie sat beside her.

> **"I'm not angry."**

> **"I know."**

> **"You don't have to hide being scared from me."**

Corin looked down.

> **"...I don't like what I become when you're gone."**

---

# **THE NOTEBOOK'S PURPOSE HAS CHANGED**

The notebook was intended to preserve Corin's memories.

Now it preserves something else.

Evidence of what isolation does to her.

The entries demonstrate a repeating pattern:

**Orphie leaves → silence → anxiety → self-blame → abandonment fears → increasingly distorted thoughts → Orphie returns → temporary stabilization.**

The cycle repeats.

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT'S ASSESSMENT**

Guiding Light has read several of the entries.

She does not consider them evidence that Corin is inherently dangerous.

She considers them evidence that Corin is suffering.

> **"The words in this book are not necessarily what Corin believes when she is safe."**

> **"They are what her fear says when nobody is there to contradict it."**

She has therefore prohibited anyone from destroying the notebook.

Even the disturbing entries must remain.

They document Corin's condition.

And, more importantly, they allow Corin herself to look back and recognize:

**The thoughts eventually passed.**

---

# **A NEW ENTRY**

After one particularly difficult absence, Orphie found a new page.

This time, Corin had written:

> **Orphie left.**

> **I got scared.**

> **I thought she wouldn't come back.**

> **I thought I was a burden.**

> **I thought everyone forgot me.**

> **She came back.**

There was a long blank space.

Then:

> **She always comes back.**

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Orphie cannot stop leaving the treehouse.

Corin cannot safely accompany her everywhere.

The supplies still have to be collected.

The medical materials still have to be replaced.

The world outside still exists.

So the cycle continues.

The notebook fills with frightening thoughts.

Then Orphie returns.

And Corin gets another few hours of peace.

The notebook was meant to help Corin remember who she was.

Now it serves another purpose:

**It records who Corin becomes when she believes she has been left alone.**

And perhaps, someday, Corin will read those pages and realize something important.

Every terrible thought in the notebook has one thing in common.

They were written while Orphie was gone.

And every time—

**Orphie came back.**
