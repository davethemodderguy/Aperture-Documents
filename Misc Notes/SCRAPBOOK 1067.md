## Executive Summary

Following the documented loss of Alice's accessible memory regarding Neri, Neri began actively avoiding Alice.

This would ordinarily be unremarkable.

Neri's current condition, however, presents a logistical problem:

> **Neri currently does not voluntarily move herself.**

As a sentient plushie, she retains awareness, reasoning, and the ability to communicate, but has ceased using her own mobility to relocate herself.

Therefore, Neri could not simply leave when Alice approached.

She solved the problem anyway.

Neri manipulated Jane Doe into moving her.

Jane did not initially realize this was what Neri was doing.

---

## Incident Reconstruction

### 1. Initial Position

Neri was resting in the SAFEHOUSE common area in plush form.

Alice entered the room.

Neri immediately recognized her.

No verbal response was given.

No attempt was made to attract attention.

Neri simply stared at Alice for several seconds before turning her head away.

Alice, who currently possesses no accessible memory of Neri, did not understand the significance of the behavior.

She did not approach.

Neri remained where she was.

---

### 2. Jane Doe Arrives

Jane subsequently entered the room.

Neri noticed her.

She then looked toward Jane.

Then toward Alice.

Then back toward Jane.

No movement followed.

Jane initially interpreted this as Neri simply wanting to be picked up.

She was accustomed to Plushie Neri occasionally requesting attention through small gestures.

Jane picked her up.

Neri did not resist.

---

### 3. The Relocation

Jane began walking toward the opposite side of the SAFEHOUSE.

Neri remained completely still in her arms.

After several seconds, Jane noticed that Neri was watching Alice over her shoulder.

Jane stopped.

> **JANE:** "Wait."

Neri looked away.

> **JANE:** "Are you trying to get away from Alice?"

Neri did not answer.

Jane looked back toward Alice.

Then at Neri.

Neri remained motionless.

Jane sighed.

> **JANE:** "You could've just told me."

Neri continued staring at the floor.

Jane resumed walking.

She carried Neri into a quiet hallway considerably farther from the common area.

Only after reaching the destination did Neri relax.

Her body visibly settled against Jane's arm.

---

## Investigation

Jane later reconstructed the sequence.

Neri had not actually _asked_ Jane to move her.

She had instead:

1. Identified Alice's presence.
    
2. Identified Jane as someone capable of moving her.
    
3. Established eye contact with Jane.
    
4. Repeatedly looked between Jane and the exit.
    
5. Allowed Jane to interpret the behavior.
    
6. Accepted relocation without resistance.
    
7. Remained in the new location once sufficiently far from Alice.
    

No words were required.

The behavior is therefore classified as:

> **Passive behavioral manipulation through selective nonverbal communication.**

Jane's assessment was considerably less clinical.

> **JANE:** "She tricked me."

Pause.

> **JANE:** "Very quietly."

---

## Important Distinction

This incident does **not** indicate that Neri has regained normal mobility.

She did not move herself.

She did not crawl away.

She did not roll toward another room.

She did not request transportation verbally.

She simply used the one resource still available to her:

**other people's ability to move her.**

This also does not constitute malicious manipulation.

Neri did not attempt to deceive Jane for personal gain.

She wanted distance from Alice and found a way to obtain it without directly confronting her.

Given Neri's current psychological state, this method was apparently preferable to:

- asking Alice to leave;
    
- asking Jane to intervene;
    
- explaining why Alice's presence was uncomfortable;
    
- expressing that she was hurt;
    
- or otherwise drawing attention to herself.

She asked:

> **ALICE:** "Where's the plushie?"

No immediate answer was provided.

Jane, after a short pause, answered:

> **JANE:** "Somewhere quiet."

Alice accepted the answer.

No confrontation occurred.

Neri remained with Jane for approximately [REDACTED] minutes before Jane placed her somewhere quiet and left.

## Behavioral Assessment

This event is significant because it demonstrates that Neri's passivity should not be mistaken for a complete loss of agency.

She currently has extremely limited physical agency.

Her ability to act socially, however, remains intact.

Neri can still:

- observe;
    
- evaluate;
    
- choose;
    
- communicate nonverbally;
    
- exploit circumstances;
    
- and make decisions about who she wants to be around.
    

She simply does not currently express those decisions in conventional ways.

In this case, the decision was extremely clear:

> **Alice was too close.**

Neri found another person.

That person moved her.

Problem solved.
