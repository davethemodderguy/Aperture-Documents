# Executive Summary

A review of recent SAFEHOUSE incidents has identified a recurring pattern:

**Neri is physically present, but personnel frequently do not know where she is.**

This distinction has produced an increasing number of preventable accidents.

The majority of these incidents do not involve Neri intentionally placing herself in danger.

Instead, they occur because:

- Neri does not announce where she is going.
    
- Neri rarely asks for assistance.
    
- Neri does not consistently write when something happens.
    
- Neri often assumes other residents are busy.
    
- Personnel have become accustomed to her being quiet.
    
- Neri can remain in one location for extended periods without being noticed.
    
- Other residents sometimes assume that another person has already checked on her.
    
- Neri's current inability to speak removes one of the easiest ways to realize that something is wrong.
    

The resulting situation has created what C.O.R.E. is informally referring to as:

> **THE NERI GAP**

A resident can be missing from everyone's awareness without actually being missing from the Blacksite.

# I. INCIDENT — CAFETERIA CHAIR

**Severity:** Low

Neri was sitting at the end of a cafeteria table reading.

A resident approached the table, did not notice her behind the chair, and pulled the chair backward.

Neri was nearly knocked off balance.

The resident immediately apologized.

Neri nodded.

No further communication occurred.

The resident later stated:

> "I didn't even see her."

Neri subsequently returned to reading.

---

# II. INCIDENT — UNATTENDED COFFEE

**Severity:** Low

Neri prepared coffee and left the cafeteria temporarily to retrieve a book.

She did not tell anyone.

The coffee remained unattended for approximately twenty minutes.

A SAFEHOUSE resident eventually found the cup and assumed someone had forgotten it.

The cup was discarded.

Neri returned several minutes later.

She looked at the empty space where the coffee had been.

She did not complain.

She simply made another one.

When asked why she had not told anyone that it was hers, Neri shrugged.

---

# III. INCIDENT — LOCKED HALLWAY

**Severity:** Moderate

Neri exited the cafeteria after hours and entered a secured corridor.

Her keycard had not been properly attached to her coat.

The door closed behind her.

Neri could not immediately return through the door.

Because she could not speak, she could not call through the door.

She remained outside the secured section.

No emergency was declared because nobody initially knew she was there.

Approximately eighteen minutes later, Bao noticed Neri was absent from the expected route and contacted Security.

The Security Center remotely opened the door.

Neri retrieved her keycard from the floor.

No injury occurred.

Following the incident, Bao began keeping Neri's keycard clipped directly to the waist area of her coat.

---

# IV. INCIDENT — STAIRWELL

**Severity:** High

Neri experienced an acute episode of weakness while walking through a stairwell.

She stopped halfway down.

She sat on the stairs.

She did not call anyone.

She did not write a message.

She waited for the symptoms to pass.

Approximately eleven minutes later, another resident discovered her sitting there.

Neri attempted to indicate that she was fine.

She was not.

The resident immediately contacted Bao.

Neri was escorted to medical care.

The incident was determined to be consistent with her ongoing anemia.

The most concerning component was not the medical event itself.

It was the fact that Neri had already decided that **waiting alone was preferable to bothering someone.**

---

# V. INCIDENT — CAFETERIA FLOOR

**Severity:** Critical

Previously documented.

At approximately 02:30, Neri suffered an anemic collapse while alone.

No personnel were aware that she had become critically ill.

She remained undiscovered until approximately 09:00.

By the time Jane found her, Neri was in severe respiratory distress.

Neri attempted to ask for help:

> "Jane, help, I can't breathe, please hel—"

Communication failure prevented immediate assistance from arriving.

Neri subsequently lost consciousness and died temporarily before being restored with Bao's assistance.

This remains the most serious known manifestation of the Neri Gap.

# VII. INCIDENT — UNNOTICED INJURY

Neri sustained a minor injury while handling equipment in her room.

The injury was not reported.

She cleaned the area herself and continued working.

The injury was discovered later when Bao noticed Neri avoiding use of the affected arm.

When asked why she had not said anything, Neri simply looked away.

# VIII. INCIDENT — THE EMPTY SEAT

**Severity:** Low / Psychological

Neri routinely sits at one particular cafeteria seat.

On one occasion, she left without anyone noticing.

Jane later entered the cafeteria and saw the empty seat.

Jane initially assumed Neri was in her room.

Bao assumed Neri was in the cafeteria.

Alice assumed Neri was with Jane.

Jane assumed Neri was with Bao.

None of these assumptions were correct.

Neri was actually in a quiet corridor elsewhere in the Blacksite.

She had been sitting there alone.

No emergency occurred.

The incident nevertheless demonstrated a significant problem:

> **Four people cared about where Neri was. None of them actually knew.**

# XII. INCIDENT — FAILED CHECK-IN

**Severity:** Moderate

A routine welfare check was performed.

Neri did not answer.

Personnel assumed she was asleep.

# XIV. INCIDENT — THE HALLWAY WALK

**Severity:** Low / Significant

A resident encountered Neri walking alone toward the cafeteria.

They asked:

> "Where are you going?"

Neri pointed toward the cafeteria.

The resident offered:

> "Want me to come with you?"

Neri shook her head.

The resident left.

Neri continued alone.

This interaction is not itself considered an accident.

It is included because it demonstrates the behavioral mechanism behind many of the other incidents.

Neri is not necessarily avoiding people.

She simply does not see a reason to make someone accompany her.

She does not appear to recognize companionship as something she is permitted to request without justification.

# XV. General Pattern

The incidents above demonstrate three recurring categories.

### A. Nobody Knows Where Neri Is

Neri moves between:

**ROOM → HALLWAY → CAFETERIA → HALLWAY → ROOM**

without informing anyone.

Because the route is predictable, personnel sometimes stop actively checking.

---

### B. Nobody Knows Something Is Wrong

Neri may:

- Become pale.
    
- Become weak.
    
- Stop moving.
    
- Sit down unexpectedly.
    
- Stop eating.
    
- Stop reading.
    
- Remain unusually still.
    

She may not report any of it.

Her current inability to speak makes this significantly worse.

---

### C. Neri Does Not Correct Incorrect Assumptions

This is arguably the most concerning behavior.

If someone assumes:

> "You're okay."

Neri may nod.

If someone assumes:

> "You're going back to your room?"

Neri may nod.

If someone assumes:

> "Someone else is looking after you?"

Neri may not correct them.

Not because the assumption is true.

Because correcting it requires initiating an interaction.

---

# XVI. C.O.R.E. Assessment

The increasing number of incidents should **not** be interpreted as Neri becoming reckless.

The opposite is closer to the truth.

Neri is attempting to minimize her presence.

Unfortunately, this creates situations where other people cannot account for her.

The resident who is trying hardest not to inconvenience anyone has consequently become one of the residents most difficult to reliably monitor.

This creates a paradox:

> **Neri does not want people worrying about her.**

Therefore she does not tell people when something is wrong.

Because she does not tell people when something is wrong, people cannot intervene early.

Because intervention occurs late, people become more frightened.

Because people become more frightened, Neri becomes more convinced that she causes problems.

The cycle then repeats.

---

# XVII. Revised Welfare Recommendation

C.O.R.E. recommends **against** solving this by demanding that Neri constantly report her location.

That would likely reinforce the exact behavior causing the problem.

Instead:

- Residents should casually acknowledge Neri when they encounter her.
    
- Staff should not assume another person has checked on her.
    
- Lack of response should receive appropriate follow-up.
    
- Medical changes should be acted upon based on observation, not solely self-report.
    
- Neri should be allowed to remain quiet.
    
- Written communication should remain available without becoming mandatory.
    
- Companionship should be offered without making Neri request it.
    
- Personnel should avoid statements implying that Neri is "causing trouble" by needing assistance.
    

Most importantly:

> **Neri should not have to become louder before people become attentive.**

---

# XVIII. Closing Observation

During a later cafeteria observation, Neri was sitting alone with her coffee.

Jane entered.

She saw Neri immediately.

Jane sat beside her.

Neri looked at Jane.

Jane looked at the coffee.

**JANE:**  
"You're becoming a regular."

Neri gave a small nod.

Jane remained seated.

Neri returned to her book.

No conversation followed.

This was recorded as a successful interaction.

Not because Neri talked.

Not because Neri wrote.

Not because anyone extracted information from her.

Simply because:

**Someone noticed she was there.**
