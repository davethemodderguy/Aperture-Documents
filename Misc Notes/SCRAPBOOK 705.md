### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — FIVE MONTHS BEDRIDDEN**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / CHRONIC / NO SIGNIFICANT RECOVERY**

---

# **FIVE MONTHS**

Alice has been bedridden for over five months.

There has been no meaningful physical recovery.

Her condition has not stabilized into a healthy baseline.

Instead, the list of injuries continues to grow.

Every attempted escape adds something new.

A bruise.

A cut.

A reopened wound.

Another head injury.

Another nasal injury.

Another period of exhaustion.

Her regeneration repairs what it can.

Medical personnel treat what it cannot.

Then Alice tries again.

---

# **THE PURPOSE OF THE ATTEMPTS**

Alice does not appear to be trying to escape because she dislikes SAFEHOUSE.

She does not appear to be seeking somewhere better.

She is attempting to leave because she believes her continued presence causes concern.

Her reasoning remains remarkably consistent:

> **"Everyone worries about Alice."**

Therefore:

> **"If Alice goes away, everyone stops worrying."**

This conclusion remains deeply embedded despite repeated reassurance.

---

# **THE CONTRADICTION**

Alice is desperately trying not to make everyone worry.

And that is exactly what makes everyone worry.

She hides pain.

She hides hunger.

She hides exhaustion.

She attempts to stand when she cannot.

She attempts to walk when her body cannot support her.

She tries to leave before anyone notices.

And when she inevitably collapses, the resulting injuries are worse than whatever caused the original concern.

---

# **MEDICAL OBSERVATION**

The medical department has documented a disturbing pattern.

Alice will sometimes appear calm while experiencing obvious physical distress.

If asked:

> **"Are you hurting?"**

She may answer:

> **"No."**

If asked:

> **"Do you need help?"**

She may answer:

> **"Alice okay."**

If someone leaves the room shortly afterward, Alice may attempt to get out of bed.

Not because she has recovered.

Because she does not want to call anyone back.

---

# **THE HEAD BANDAGE**

The head bandage remains almost permanent.

There have been periods where the original injury has healed sufficiently for the bandage to be removed.

Within days, Alice has sustained another injury.

The cycle repeats.

The bandage has become less of a treatment for one specific injury and more of a precaution against the next.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin spends considerable time beside Alice.

She has stopped asking Alice to explain herself.

Instead, she watches.

When Alice moves her legs beneath the blanket:

> **"Are you trying to get up?"**

Alice stops.

> **"...No."**

Corin waits.

> **"Alice."**

A pause.

> **"...Maybe."**

Corin sighs.

She doesn't get angry.

She simply stays.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle is perhaps the most openly distressed.

She remembers Alice as the little rabbit who loved carrots.

The little girl who slept beside Corin.

The child who followed everyone around.

Now Belle sees a child who cannot reliably remember carrots, cannot walk far, cannot maintain adequate nutrition, and repeatedly hurts herself trying to disappear.

Belle sometimes sits beside the bed holding Alice's hand.

She rarely speaks.

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi's observations have become increasingly difficult for her to discuss.

She remembers Alice before all of this.

Weak.

Fragile.

Frequently sick.

But still happy.

Still drawing.

Still trying.

Still wanting to be around everyone.

Lumi summarized Alice's current condition in a single sentence:

> **"She cared too much for everyone around her, so much that she stopped caring for herself."**

The statement has been added to Alice's psychological profile.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has reviewed the case repeatedly.

There is no simple solution.

Alice cannot simply be ordered to stay.

She cannot simply be told that she is loved.

She cannot simply be fed and expected to recover.

Her problem is not only physical.

Alice has developed a deeply ingrained belief that her existence imposes a cost on everyone around her.

Every injury reinforces it.

Every worried expression reinforces it.

Every medical examination reinforces it.

Every attempt to reassure her can accidentally remind her that there is something to be reassured about.

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE**

Nobody has stopped caring.

If anything, everyone has become more attentive.

But they try not to let Alice see the fear.

Meals are prepared quietly.

Medical equipment is kept nearby.

Fresh blankets appear without explanation.

Someone is almost always within hearing distance.

Alice's environment has become carefully controlled without feeling like a hospital.

The objective remains the same:

> **Let Alice feel like a child.**

Not a case.

Not a medical emergency.

Not a burden.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice has spent months trying to disappear.

Not because she stopped loving anyone.

Almost the opposite.

She cares too much.

She sees everyone worrying.

She sees everyone watching her.

She sees the medical staff treating her.

She sees Corin staying awake beside her.

She sees Belle crying.

She sees Lumi becoming quieter.

She sees Orphie constantly checking on her.

And Alice reaches the same conclusion every time:

> **"They'd be happier if Alice wasn't here."**

So she tries to leave.

Her body stops her.

She falls.

Her regeneration repairs what it can.

She becomes weaker.

And then she tries again.

Alice cared so much about everyone around her...

that she stopped caring about herself.

The people around her are now trying to teach her the opposite.

Not that she must become stronger.

Not that she must recover quickly.

Not that she must stop being fragile.

Only this:

> **She is allowed to be cared for.**
