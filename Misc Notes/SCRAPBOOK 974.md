# SAFEHOUSE INCIDENT SUPPLEMENT

## SUBJECT: NERI

### INCIDENT SERIES: UNACCOUNTED PRESENCE

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE Internal / Medical / Safety  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Status:** Permanent Resident  
**Current Concern:** Repeated accidental isolation, unnoticed injuries, and delayed medical discovery

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The number of incidents involving Neri being physically present within Blacksite facilities without being noticed has increased substantially.

The incidents now include:

- accidental confinement inside rooms,
    
- repeated near-lockouts from exterior areas,
    
- falls,
    
- trips,
    
- collisions with doors,
    
- near-entrapment by automated systems,
    
- and multiple anemic episodes.
    

The most concerning commonality is:

> **Neri is almost never noticed immediately.**

Even when an incident occurs in the presence of other personnel, discovery frequently takes **more than one hour**.

In several medical incidents, Neri was already unconscious when personnel first became aware of her condition.

---

# 2. INCIDENT — ARCHIVE STORAGE ROOM

Neri entered a small archive storage room while looking for a reference book.

The automatic door closed behind her.

The room's access system subsequently locked.

Neri attempted to open the door.

It did not respond.

Rather than triggering the emergency intercom, Neri sat down and began reading the book she had found.

Approximately **two hours later**, an archivist opened the room.

The archivist reportedly stared at Neri.

> **Archivist:** “Why are you in here?”

> **Neri:** “I was looking for a book.”

> **Archivist:** “How long have you been here?”

Neri shrugged.

> **Neri:** “I don't know.”

The archivist later discovered the door had been locked the entire time.

Neri had not attempted to attract attention after her initial attempt to leave.

---

# 3. INCIDENT — MAINTENANCE ROOM

Neri entered a maintenance room to retrieve equipment.

The door automatically closed.

The room was subsequently locked during a routine security cycle.

Neri was not included in the personnel count for the area.

She remained inside.

A maintenance worker eventually opened the room more than an hour later.

Neri was sitting on the floor.

She had apparently decided that:

> “Someone will open it eventually.”

No emergency alarm had been activated.

---

# 4. INCIDENT — EXTERIOR ACCESS #1

Neri returned from collecting samples for Bao.

She reached an exterior entrance moments after the door closed.

The lock engaged.

Neri discovered that she had once again left her access card inside.

Instead of contacting someone, she sat beside the entrance.

A worker eventually opened the door approximately **ninety minutes later**.

The worker reportedly froze upon seeing her.

> **Worker:** “Neri?!”

> **Neri:** “Hi.”

> **Worker:** “How long have you been out here?”

> **Neri:** “A while.”

The worker immediately contacted medical personnel due to Neri's exposure to the cold.

---

# 5. INCIDENT — EXTERIOR ACCESS #2

A similar event occurred several days later.

This time Neri was attempting to return from the greenhouse.

The interior door closed before she could enter.

She had no access card.

Instead of returning to another entrance, Neri sat near the wall.

She waited.

A worker eventually opened the door approximately **two hours later**.

Neri had begun shivering.

When asked why she hadn't called anyone:

> **Neri:** “I thought someone would come.”

When asked why she didn't tell anyone she was outside:

> **Neri:** “Everyone was working.”

---

# 6. INCIDENT — STAIRCASE FALL

Neri tripped while descending a staircase.

She fell several steps and struck her knee and elbow.

No one witnessed the fall.

Neri remained seated on the stairs for several minutes before standing.

A worker passed her.

The worker did not notice that she had fallen.

Neri subsequently continued walking.

The injury was discovered only later when medical personnel noticed bruising.

Neri's explanation:

> **“I fell.”**

No further complaint was made.

---

# 7. INCIDENT — HALLWAY TRIP

Neri tripped over a piece of equipment temporarily placed in a hallway.

She fell.

Several workers were present farther down the corridor.

Nobody turned around.

Neri stood back up.

She moved the equipment slightly out of her path.

Then continued walking.

No report was made by Neri.

---

# 8. INCIDENT — CLOSING SECURITY DOOR

Neri was walking through a security corridor when a large automated door began closing.

She was nearly caught within the doorway.

A sensor eventually detected movement and stopped the door.

Neri stepped through.

Nobody nearby reacted.

The system's safety mechanism prevented injury.

The incident was discovered later through automated logs.

The reviewing technician initially assumed the detected movement had been equipment.

The footage showed Neri.

---

# 9. INCIDENT — ANEMIC EPISODE #1

Neri experienced an anemic episode while seated in a quiet corridor.

She became unconscious.

A worker passed within several meters of her.

Neri remained in the same position for approximately **one hour and twenty minutes**.

The worker eventually returned.

Only then did they notice that Neri had not moved.

Medical personnel were called.

Neri was stabilized.

---

# 10. INCIDENT — ANEMIC EPISODE #2

Neri experienced another episode while reading in a common area.

Again, her eyes remained open.

Her book remained in her hands.

She appeared to be reading.

Personnel walked past her repeatedly.

Discovery occurred approximately **one hour and forty minutes** later.

The person who finally noticed her initially assumed she was deeply focused on the book.

They spoke to her.

No response.

They touched her shoulder.

Only then was the emergency recognized.

---

# 11. INCIDENT — ANEMIC EPISODE #3

Neri suffered an episode while sitting near the cafeteria.

She became unconscious.

Nobody noticed for more than an hour.

A resident eventually approached her because they wanted to ask about her book.

Neri did not respond.

Medical personnel were contacted.

Bao was notified.

The emergency blood-response equipment was prepared.

Neri recovered after treatment.

---

# 12. INCIDENT — ANEMIC EPISODE #4

The fourth documented delayed-discovery episode occurred while Neri was sitting alone in a study area.

She was discovered after approximately **two hours**.

Her physical appearance remained sufficiently normal that personnel initially believed she was sleeping.

Medical assessment determined that she had experienced another significant anemic episode.

---

# 13. COMMON FACTOR

The medical episodes share the same pattern:

**Neri does not look like she is having an emergency.**

She does not necessarily fall.

She does not necessarily cry out.

She may remain seated.

Her eyes may remain open.

Her hands may remain positioned around whatever she was doing.

She can therefore remain unnoticed in an occupied facility for extended periods.

---

# 14. NERI'S BEHAVIOR

Perhaps more concerning than the accidents is Neri's reaction afterward.

She rarely asks:

> “Why didn't anyone notice me?”

Instead, she asks:

> “Did I cause trouble?”

When told that personnel were worried, she often responds with an apology.

When told she should have called for help:

> “I didn't want to bother anyone.”

When asked why she waited:

> “They were busy.”

The same answer continues to appear across unrelated incidents.

---

# 15. THE BACKGROUND EFFECT — ESCALATION

The previous report described Neri as becoming socially invisible.

That assessment remains valid.

However, the phenomenon now appears to be affecting physical safety.

Neri's behavioral pattern encourages her to:

- stay quiet,
    
- remain in place,
    
- avoid asking for help,
    
- follow rather than lead,
    
- accept being overlooked,
    
- and assume somebody else will eventually notice.
    

Meanwhile, personnel have become increasingly accustomed to seeing Neri quietly moving around Blacksite.

Consequently, seeing Neri is no longer necessarily memorable.

She has become part of the environment.

---

# 16. THE MOST DANGEROUS SENTENCE

Across interviews and incident reviews, one sentence has appeared repeatedly:

> **“I thought someone would come.”**

Neri says this when trapped.

She says it when locked outside.

She says it when injured.

She says it when she becomes unwell.

She does not necessarily believe nobody cares.

She believes someone will eventually notice.

The problem is that “eventually” has repeatedly meant:

**over an hour.**

Sometimes several hours.

---

# 17. JANE DOE

Jane Doe remains one of Neri's closest friends.

At 28, Jane recognizes the severity of the situation.

Her greatest concern is not simply that Neri is experiencing more anemia.

It is that Neri's instinct when something goes wrong is increasingly:

> **Wait.**

Not:

> **Call Jane.**

Not:

> **Find Bao.**

Not:

> **Get medical help.**

Just:

> **Wait.**

Jane has reportedly become increasingly frustrated with herself because her own workload means she cannot physically remain with Neri at all times.

However, the medical team has emphasized that Jane cannot be expected to become Neri's permanent guard.

The SAFEHOUSE must remain collectively responsible.

---

# 18. ALICE

Alice continues attempting to include Neri in activities.

Neri continues declining with increasing frequency.

Alice has not stopped caring.

She has, however, become visibly less cheerful whenever Neri refuses.

This has created another unintended consequence.

Neri sees Alice becoming sad.

Neri interprets this as:

> **“I made Alice sad.”**

Which reinforces her desire to withdraw.

---

# 19. MEDICAL RECOMMENDATION

Personnel are not being instructed to constantly monitor Neri.

That would be neither practical nor healthy.

Instead, the following behaviors should be treated as warning signs:

- Neri sitting motionless for an unusually long period,
    
- Neri remaining alone in an unusual location,
    
- Neri being absent from normal activities,
    
- Neri failing to respond when spoken to,
    
- Neri being found outside alone,
    
- Neri being found in a locked or restricted room,
    
- unexplained falls or injuries,
    
- or Neri repeatedly saying that she is “fine” without actually engaging with the question.
    

A simple:

> **“Hey, Neri. You okay?”**

may be sufficient to reveal that something is wrong.

---

# 20. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Neri is not disappearing.

She is not intentionally trying to become invisible.

She is not deliberately placing herself in danger.

She is simply becoming increasingly accustomed to existing without acknowledgement.

That has created a dangerous combination:

**A medically fragile child**

**unpredictable anemia**

**extreme reluctance to ask for help**

**a facility full of automated doors, security systems, machinery, stairs, and environmental hazards**

**a growing tendency for personnel to overlook her presence**

=

### AN UNACCEPTABLE RISK.

The most disturbing part is not that Neri has fallen.

It is not that she has been locked outside.

It is not that she has suffered repeated anemic episodes.

It is that, after every incident, she gets up and behaves as though nothing happened.

She doesn't ask:

> **“Why didn't anyone notice?”**

She asks:

> **“Did I bother anyone?”**

That remains the central problem.
