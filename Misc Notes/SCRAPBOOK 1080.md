## Executive Summary

Neri is a person.

Neri is also, by her own voluntary choice, a plushie.

These two facts are not considered contradictory.

While in plush form, Neri permits—and frequently appears to **enjoy**—being treated like a plushie.

Personnel may:

- carry her around;
    
- hold her;
    
- put her on a couch or bed;
    
- let her sit on their shoulders;
    
- carry her like a backpack;
    
- place her beside them while working;
    
- let her sleep somewhere comfortable;
    
- pick her up when she is lying on the floor;
    
- cuddle her when she permits it;
    
- treat her as a small, soft companion.
    

Neri does not appear offended by this treatment.

In many circumstances, she actively participates in it.

---

## Why Neri Does Not Object

Neri's self-perception remains unusually negative.

She has spent much of her life understanding herself through concepts such as:

> **usefulness.**

> **burden.**

> **disposability.**

> **purpose.**

She has repeatedly struggled with the idea that someone might keep her around simply because they want her there.

As a result, being treated as a plushie does not necessarily register to Neri as an insult.

Quite the opposite.

There is no expectation attached to a plushie.

A plushie does not need to:

- accomplish something;
    
- justify its existence;
    
- complete research;
    
- solve a problem;
    
- protect someone;
    
- be productive;
    
- earn its place;
    
- explain why it deserves to stay.
    

It can simply exist.

And Neri seems to find that remarkably comfortable.

---

## Behavioral Observation

When someone picks up Plush Neri, she rarely objects.

She may simply:

- relax;
    
- adjust her position;
    
- close her eyes;
    
- lean against them;
    
- fall asleep;
    
- drink coffee if provided;
    
- remain there until she decides otherwise.
    

If she does not want to be held, she can leave.

She is capable of moving independently and can transform back to normal form at will.

Therefore, her continued participation is considered meaningful.

She is not helplessly accepting treatment she dislikes.

She is **choosing it.**

---

## The Equipment Locker Incident

The recent equipment-locker incident requires contextual revision.

Neri being forgotten inside the locker for more than 25 hours was a safety failure.

However, the fact that she was treated like a misplaced plushie is not, by itself, what caused distress.

Neri did not emerge from the locker expressing:

> _"How could you treat me like an object?"_

She essentially communicated:

> "...Boring."

This is consistent with her current relationship with plushie treatment.

The problem was not:

**"Someone treated me like a plushie."**

The problem was:

**"Someone forgot where I was."**

Even then, Neri did not appear particularly distressed by the emotional implications.

---

## Important Boundary

C.O.R.E. does not interpret Neri's willingness to be treated like a plushie as evidence that she believes she is literally an object.

She remains fully recognized as a person.

Personnel are expected to respect her autonomy.

The distinction is:

> **Neri may be treated like a plushie because she likes being treated that way.**

Not:

> **Neri is a plushie, therefore she has no rights or autonomy.**

If Neri indicates that she wants to be left alone, she is left alone.

If she bites someone's sleeve because she wants to be picked up, she may be picked up if appropriate.

If she climbs onto someone's back, she may ride there.

If she walks away, she is allowed to walk away.

---

## Psychological Consideration

There is a deeper reason personnel have become increasingly careful about how they interpret this behavior.

Neri's internal model of herself is still heavily influenced by the belief that she is disposable.

This means seemingly harmless statements can carry very different meanings to her.

For example:

**"You're useful."**

may be interpreted as:

> _"I only deserve to stay if I am useful."_

While:

**"You're not useful."**

may be interpreted as:

> _"Then I have no reason to exist here."_

The plushie environment accidentally provides an alternative.

Nobody expects a plushie to justify itself.

Nobody asks the plushie what its purpose is.

Nobody needs the plushie to accomplish anything.

It can sit on someone's lap for three hours and contribute absolutely nothing.

And nobody considers that a failure.

---

## C.O.R.E. Assessment

This may explain why Neri is particularly relaxed in plush form.

She does not have to perform.

She does not have to be the exceptional chemist.

She does not have to prove that the 12 TB of research on her laptop was worth storing.

She does not have to demonstrate usefulness.

She can simply be:

**small.**

**soft.**

**quiet.**

**carried around.**

**fed cake.**

**given coffee.**

**occasionally bitten back when she bites someone first.**

And apparently that is enough.

---

## Personnel Guidance

Do not attempt to use plushie treatment as psychological therapy.

Do not tell Neri:

> "See? You're valuable even when you're just a plushie."

That would completely miss the point.

Do not turn her plush form into another performance requirement.

Do not encourage her to remain plush because she is easier to care for.

Do not imply that her normal form is inconvenient.

And, importantly:

**Do not make her prove that she deserves to be treated as a person.**

She already is one.

The plushie is simply the form she happens to enjoy being in.
