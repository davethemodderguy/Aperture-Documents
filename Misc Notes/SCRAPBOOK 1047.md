## Executive Summary

Neri remains in her voluntary plush form.

Contrary to several early assumptions, Neri does **not** appear to regard her current plush state as merely a medical recovery measure.

She enjoys it.

More specifically, Neri appears to particularly enjoy being **hugged** while in plush form.

This is one of the few forms of physical affection Neri consistently accepts without withdrawing.

---

## Preferred Hug Recipients

Observation records indicate that Neri is particularly comfortable being hugged by the following individuals:

|Individual|Neri's Response|
|---|---|
|**Bao**|Strong preference. Neri frequently remains completely still and relaxed.|
|**Alice**|Strong preference. Alice is one of the most frequent carriers of Plushie Neri.|
|**Frost**|Comfortable. Neri generally permits extended hugs without resistance.|
|**Jane Doe**|Comfortable. Neri voluntarily remains close when Jane is present.|
|**Neko**|Comfortable, particularly when Neri initiates contact.|
|**Miss Neko**|Comfortable and generally receptive to prolonged holding.|
|**Star**|Strong preference. Neri readily permits Star to hold or hug her.|

No indication has been observed that Neri considers these interactions burdensome.

This is significant given her usual tendency to withdraw from interpersonal contact.

---

## Behavioral Observation

When hugged in plush form, Neri commonly:

- remains still;
    
- relaxes her limbs;
    
- leans into the person holding her;
    
- allows herself to be carried afterward;
    
- occasionally readjusts herself to become more comfortable;
    
- may quietly fall asleep;
    
- does not appear concerned about being a burden.
    

Importantly, **Neri does not need to be asked repeatedly.**

If she does not want physical contact, she can simply move away.

Personnel are therefore instructed to interpret her continued presence as permission to remain holding her **only while she voluntarily remains there**.

---

## Notable Distinction

Neri's acceptance of hugs should not be interpreted as evidence that she has suddenly become socially outgoing.

She still:

- enjoys being alone;
    
- avoids unnecessary interaction;
    
- withdraws when people deliberately attempt to approach her;
    
- dislikes being treated as a socialization project;
    
- may leave without explanation.
    

The difference is that **plush form appears to make passive affection comfortable for her.**

There is no requirement for conversation.

There is no requirement for eye contact.

There is no requirement for her to respond.

Someone can simply hold her.

For Neri, this appears to be enough.

---

## Incident Note — Bao

During one observation period, Bao picked up Plushie Neri and held her against her chest.

Neri did not move away.

After approximately several minutes, Neri repositioned herself slightly and remained there.

Bao subsequently stopped speaking to her and simply continued holding her.

Neri remained in place.

**Archivist's Note:**

> She doesn't need to say anything.
> 
> She doesn't even need to do anything.
> 
> She's allowed to just be held.

---

## Incident Note — Alice

Alice remains the resident most frequently observed carrying Plushie Neri.

Neri has demonstrated no indication that she finds Alice's frequent carrying behavior bothersome.

Alice has also been instructed that Plushie Neri is still Neri and therefore **not a toy**.

This instruction has been met with the response:

> "I know."

She subsequently hugged Neri again.

---

## Incident Note — Jane Doe

Jane's interactions remain notably different from those of most residents.

Neri voluntarily stays near Jane more often than she does with most other people.

Jane has been instructed not to treat this as a breakthrough, encourage increased contact, or attempt to turn the behavior into a therapeutic exercise.

Jane's response:

> "I'm not going to ruin it."

She then sat down.

Neri climbed into her lap.

Neither said anything.

---

## Final Assessment

Neri remaining in plush form is currently considered **voluntary and stable**.

Her enjoyment of hugs is considered genuine.

Personnel should not attempt to manufacture additional affection, force cuddling, or use physical contact to prevent Neri from leaving.

The correct procedure remains simple:

**If Neri wants a hug, let her have one.**

**If Neri wants to leave, let her leave.**

**If Neri wants to sit quietly in someone's arms without saying anything, that is also acceptable.**

For once, Neri does not appear to believe that accepting something nice automatically creates a debt.

At least while she is a plushie.
