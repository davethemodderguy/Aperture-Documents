# 1. Current Condition

Neri survived her recent cardiopulmonary failure.

She is alive.

She is receiving continued support for her anemia.

She remains unable to speak following the severe injury sustained during the incident.

However, another change has become increasingly apparent.

**Neri is slow.**

Very slow.

Her movements have become noticeably sluggish compared with her pre-incident baseline.

Walking takes longer.

Standing takes longer.

Turning her head takes longer.

Writing takes longer.

Even reaching for an object can require a noticeable amount of effort.

She frequently stops in the middle of an activity and simply remains still.

Medical personnel have attributed this broadly to the aftermath of her near-fatal physiological collapse and her continuing severe anemia. Her body is recovering from an event it was never expected to survive without intervention.

There is currently no reliable estimate for when her normal energy and responsiveness will return.

---

# 2. Return to "Background" Behavior

The most concerning behavioral development is not her physical sluggishness.

It is what has happened socially.

Neri has once again become extremely easy to overlook.

Before her disappearance, Neri had already developed a pattern of quietly existing around the SAFEHOUSE.

She would sit somewhere.

Read.

Work on something.

Eat.

Listen.

Wait.

Unless somebody actively looked for her, it was entirely possible for several hours to pass without anyone realizing she was there.

The recent incident has intensified this.

Her inability to speak means she cannot casually call someone's name.

Her physical weakness means she does not always have the energy to walk over to somebody.

Her sluggishness means she may stand in a doorway or sit at a table for several minutes before deciding whether to approach someone.

And because Neri is already naturally quiet, the result is predictable:

**People miss her.**

---

# 3. Behavioral Observation

**[OBSERVATION — SAFEHOUSE COMMON AREA]**

Neri is sitting at a table.

A group of residents are talking several meters away.

Neri looks toward them.

She appears to consider joining them.

She does not move.

She waits.

One minute.

Two minutes.

Five minutes.

Eventually, the conversation moves elsewhere.

Neri remains at the table.

She lowers her eyes.

She opens her book.

No distress signal is recorded.

No attempt is made to follow.

---

### [OBSERVATION — CAFETERIA]

Neri enters the cafeteria.

She obtains food.

She sits alone.

Several residents pass nearby.

None notice her.

Neri watches them.

She appears to consider waving.

She does not.

Her hand remains on the table.

She begins eating.

---

### [OBSERVATION — HALLWAY]

Neri is standing beside a doorway.

A resident walks past.

Neri turns her head.

She appears to want to say something.

She cannot.

The resident continues walking.

Neri waits.

Then slowly walks in the opposite direction.

---

# 4. Psychological Concern

Medical personnel have identified a concerning pattern.

Neri appears to understand that people are overlooking her.

She does not appear angry.

She does not complain.

She does not attempt to force herself into conversations.

Instead, she increasingly behaves as though being overlooked is something she should simply accept.

This is particularly concerning given her previous psychological history.

Neri has previously internalized the belief that she is expendable and that attracting too much attention makes her a burden.

The current situation risks reinforcing that belief.

The internal conclusion Neri may draw is therefore not:

> _"Nobody noticed me."_

but:

> _"Nobody noticed because I wasn't important enough to notice."_

C.O.R.E. considers the latter interpretation significantly more dangerous.

---

# 5. Jane Doe

Jane has noticed.

She has become increasingly deliberate about checking Neri's location.

Not because Neri is incapable of taking care of herself.

Because Jane has learned that **Neri will not necessarily make herself noticeable when she needs somebody.**

Jane has also noticed the difference in Neri's movement.

During one observation, Jane found Neri sitting alone with a book.

Jane sat beside her.

Neri looked up.

Jane waited.

Neri eventually slid her book slightly toward Jane.

Jane looked at the page.

**JANE:**  
"Good book?"

Neri nodded.

Jane smiled.

**JANE:**  
"Okay."

She remained there.

No interrogation.

No demand that Neri communicate.

No question asking why she was alone.

She simply stayed.

---

# 6. Resident Guidance

SAFEHOUSE residents have been reminded:

### Do not assume silence means absence.

### Do not assume stillness means rest.

### Do not assume Neri will come looking for you.

### Do not assume she will ask for help.

Neri has already demonstrated that she can become critically ill while remaining unnoticed.

The solution is not to constantly monitor her.

The solution is to **notice her.**

Say hello.

Ask if she wants to sit with you.

Invite her to eat.

Sit beside her while she reads.

If she is standing somewhere alone, acknowledge that she is there.

Small interactions are considered beneficial.

---

# 7. Important Distinction

C.O.R.E. does not classify Neri's return to quiet behavior as a failure.

Neri is recovering.

She is tired.

She cannot speak.

Her body is still severely compromised.

She is allowed to be quiet.

She is allowed to be slow.

She is allowed to sit alone.

The problem occurs when **everyone else mistakes those things for a reason to stop noticing her.**

---

# 8. Current Assessment

Neri survived an event that nearly killed her.

She is now physically slower, unable to speak, and substantially less capable of drawing attention to herself.

Ironically, this has returned her to the exact social position she occupied before her disappearance:

**the background.**

Except now she knows what it feels like to be noticed.

She knows people care.

She knows Bao will come.

She knows Jane will look for her.

She knows she has a place in the SAFEHOUSE.

Yet when nobody looks her way, the old feeling still returns.

That she is simply...

**there.**

Quietly.

Waiting.

Hoping somebody notices.

---

## C.O.R.E. Closing Note

Neri does not need everyone to stop what they're doing whenever she enters a room.

She does not need constant supervision.

She does not need to become loud.

She does not need to change who she is.

She simply needs people to remember that **quiet people still occupy space.**

And sometimes the person sitting silently in the corner isn't doing fine.

Sometimes they're just too tired to wave.

Sometimes they cannot speak.

Sometimes they're waiting for someone to notice.

**This time, C.O.R.E. recommends that someone does.**
