# Executive Summary

Plush Neri bit Jane Doe's hand.

No injury occurred.

No pain was reported.

The unusual component of the incident was not the bite itself, but the amount of grip generated by Neri's plush jaw.

Neri was able to clamp onto approximately half of Jane's hand and remain attached.

Despite her plush construction, Neri's bite was sufficiently strong to keep herself physically attached to Jane's hand.

Jane did not appear particularly concerned.

She was, however, mildly surprised.

---

# 1. Incident

Jane Doe was standing in the SAFEHOUSE common area with one hand resting near Neri.

Neri approached.

She looked at Jane's hand.

Jane noticed.

> "What?"

Neri continued looking.

She then opened her mouth.

Jane apparently understood what was about to happen approximately one second too late.

**Chomp.**

Neri clamped onto the lower portion of Jane's hand.

Jane looked down.

Neri remained attached.

---

# 2. Jane's Initial Response

Jane:

> "...Neri."

Neri remained attached.

Jane gently lifted her hand.

Neri came with it.

Her small plush body was now hanging from Jane's hand entirely by her mouth.

Jane stared.

> "You're actually stuck."

Neri did not respond.

She simply remained there.

---

# 3. Physical Characteristics

The bite did not cause Jane pain.

No puncture wound, bruising, tearing, or other injury was observed.

However, the jaw's grip was substantially stronger than would normally be expected from Neri's plush appearance.

The current assessment is that Neri's anomalous plush physiology preserves enough functional jaw strength to maintain a firm bite despite the rest of her body being composed of fabric-like material.

This is consistent with her other retained physical capabilities.

In particular:

- she can maintain a strong grip with her plush hands;
    
- she can securely attach herself to a person's back;
    
- she retains normal voluntary facial movement;
    
- and she retains the ability to bite.
    

The fact that she is soft does not mean every part of her is incapable of exerting force.

---

# 4. Jane Attempts to Determine the Reason

Jane asked:

> "Why are you biting me?"

Neri remained attached.

Jane waited.

Neri blinked.

Jane:

> "...Are you going to answer?"

Neri shook her head.

Jane:

> "Fair enough."

---

# 5. The "Plushie Hand Grip" Problem

Neri's bite demonstrated an unusual combination of properties.

Her body is soft.

Her skin is fabric.

Her muscles have plush-like characteristics.

Yet her jaw can still generate enough force to hold her entire body against another person.

This does not appear to require conscious exertion beyond simply maintaining the bite.

The moment Neri releases her jaw, the attachment ends normally.

C.O.R.E. therefore classifies the behavior as a **retained anomalous physical capability**, rather than an indication of increased aggression.

---

# 6. Neri's Behavior

Neri did not appear distressed.

She did not appear angry.

She did not attempt to injure Jane.

She simply...

**bit her hand.**

When Jane eventually asked:

> "Are you finished?"

Neri released her.

She immediately sat down.

No explanation followed.

---

# 7. Jane's Assessment

Jane examined her hand.

Nothing was wrong.

She flexed her fingers.

Everything worked normally.

She then looked at Neri.

> "You know, most people use their hands to hold onto someone."

Neri stared.

Jane:

> "You used your mouth."

Neri looked away.

Jane laughed.

> "Okay."

No further incident occurred.

---

# 8. Personnel Advisory

The incident establishes that Plush Neri should not be assumed to be physically incapable simply because she is soft.

Personnel should remember:

> **Soft ≠ harmless.**

This does not mean Neri should be treated as dangerous.

It means personnel should respect her physical autonomy.

If she bites someone, the appropriate response is to determine why she did so and give her space if necessary.

Personnel are expressly prohibited from deliberately testing her bite strength.
