# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **LONG-TERM PSYCHOLOGICAL CASE REPORT**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – SELF-PERCEPTION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 8 – Medical & Executive Personnel Only

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Despite continued physical recovery, Corin Lockwood's psychological condition remains the primary concern of Medical Services.

Her body has begun to recover.

Her self-perception has not.

Psychological Services note that Corin's greatest wounds are not physiological.

They are the beliefs about herself that developed after years of abuse, captivity, and dehumanization.

---

# **PERSISTENT TRAUMA**

Corin continues to experience recurring intrusive memories of the experimentation performed on her as a child.

The memories are vivid.

She recalls operating lights.

Cold instruments.

Restraints.

Voices discussing her condition as though she were equipment rather than a person.

To Corin, these are not distant recollections.

They remain emotionally immediate.

She has repeatedly stated that what affects her most is not the pain itself.

It is the memory of being treated as something expendable.

Not a daughter.

Not a sister.

Not a child.

Only a subject.

---

# **SELF-PERCEPTION**

During multiple therapy sessions, Corin has expressed deeply negative beliefs about herself.

Among the most common are:

> "...I'm worthless."

> "...I'm useless."

> "...They already decided I wasn't a person."

Psychological Services note that these statements reflect Corin's own self-image and are **not** consistent with how those around her view her.

She continues to judge herself through the perspective of the people who abused her, even though everyone currently in her life rejects that view entirely.

---

# **PHYSICAL APPEARANCE**

Although Corin's organs have resumed normal biological function, several changes caused by the experimentation remain permanent.

Medical examinations confirm:

- Persistently chalk-pale skin.
    
- Hair permanently altered to a pale, chalky lime coloration.
    
- Extensive surgical scarring across her body.
    
- Residual Ether signatures integrated within healed tissue.
    

Researchers have found no evidence that these characteristics will naturally return to their pre-experiment appearance.

---

# **THE MIRROR**

Corin continues to avoid mirrors whenever possible.

When she does look at her reflection, she often focuses on the changes rather than recognizing herself.

One evening, Belle quietly found Corin standing in front of a bathroom mirror.

Corin touched a lock of her pale green hair.

> "...I used to have a different color."

She looked at her own skin.

> "...I don't recognize this person."

Belle stood beside her.

She looked into the same mirror.

Then answered softly.

> "I do."

Corin remained silent.

Belle continued.

> "I see Corin."

> "Not what happened to Corin."

Corin lowered her eyes.

She couldn't bring herself to believe it.

But she remembered the words.

---

# **LUMI**

Later that night, Corin quietly asked her mother,

> "...Do I still look like your daughter?"

Lumi's expression immediately softened.

She cupped Corin's face with both hands.

> "Every single day."

Corin blinked.

> "...Even now?"

Lumi smiled.

> "Your hair changed."

> "Your skin changed."

> "The scars weren't your choice."

She gently brushed a strand of Corin's pale hair behind her ear.

> "But none of those things changed who you are."

Corin closed her eyes.

A tear slipped down her cheek.

She still struggled to believe those words.

But hearing them from her mother made it just a little harder to dismiss them.

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Psychological Services conclude that Corin's difficulty accepting herself stems not from her appearance alone, but from the meaning she associates with it.

To Corin, every scar and permanent physical change serves as a reminder of the abuse she survived.

Treatment therefore focuses not on changing her appearance, but on helping her separate her identity from the crimes committed against her.

This remains a long-term therapeutic objective.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin's physical appearance has changed permanently.

Her pale skin and chalk-lime hair are expected to remain for the foreseeable future.

She continues to struggle with seeing herself as worthy of the life she now has.

The people around her cannot erase the years that taught her otherwise.

They can only continue reminding her—day after day—that the people who called her a test subject were wrong.

Her scars tell the story of what was done to her.

They do not define the value of the person who survived it.

**End of Long-Term Psychological Case Report**
