# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / MEMORIAL ARCHIVE**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — SECONDARY ERASURE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **EXISTENTIAL VOIDING — RECORD DEGRADATION**

---

# **INITIAL DISCOVERY**

The first missing letter was found in Alice's journal.

Nobody noticed it immediately.

The page had originally read:

> **"Big sis is nice."**

Later, it read:

> **"Big sis is ni e."**

The missing character was not torn out.

There was no ink residue.

No damage to the paper.

The letter had simply ceased to exist.

---

# **THE PROCESS**

The phenomenon is spreading.

Anything created by Alice is being affected.

Her handwriting.

Her drawings.

Her journals.

Her notes.

Even small markings she made on objects.

The erasure does not occur all at once.

It happens **letter by letter.**

Sometimes a single character disappears.

Sometimes an entire word loses several letters.

Eventually, the remaining text becomes incomprehensible.

The paper itself remains.

The ink remains.

Only the parts connected to Alice disappear.

---

# **JOURNAL PAGE — CORIN**

The original:

> **"Big sis, I don't remember her name. Big sis found me in snow, big sis takes care of me. Big sis have wings and cat ears and 2 tail. Big sis is cute. Big sis tired because of me, Alice don't want to see big sis tired. Alice love big sis. Alice don't want to forget big sis."**

Current state:

> **"Big sis, I don't remem er her name. Big sis found me in snow, big sis takes care of me. Big sis have wings and cat ears and 2 tail. Big sis is cute. Big sis tired because of me..."**

The word **"Alice"** has begun disappearing from multiple pages.

---

# **PAGE — BELLE**

Originally:

> **"Belle is pink bunny. Alice is white bunny. Alice and Belle friends because we rabbits."**

Now:

> **"Belle is pink bunny. Ali e is white bunny. Alice and Belle friends because we rabbits."**

Several letters disappeared while the page was being observed.

Belle watched it happen.

She reached for the notebook.

> "Stop."

It didn't.

---

# **PAGE — STAR**

The red warning:

> **DO NOT FORGET**

has lost several characters.

It now reads:

> **DO NOT FORG T**

Star immediately covered the page.

She refused to let anyone touch it.

---

# **DRAWINGS**

The drawings are disappearing too.

Alice's drawings do not simply become faded.

Individual details vanish.

A face loses an eye.

A hand loses fingers.

A flower loses petals.

A rabbit loses an ear.

A drawing of Corin once contained two tails.

One disappeared.

Then the wings.

Then the face.

Eventually, only a few pencil marks remained.

Corin couldn't watch anymore.

---

# **THE WORST DRAWING**

One drawing depicts Alice sitting between her friends.

Everyone is holding hands.

Alice is in the center.

The drawing was one of her most detailed.

Now the figures around Alice remain.

Alice herself is disappearing from the picture.

First her ears vanished.

Then her hair.

Then her clothes.

Then her face.

Eventually, there was only an empty space between everyone else.

The others remained.

Alice did not.

---

# **THE STAFF RESPONSE**

The Archives attempted to preserve the records.

Copies were made.

Scans were made.

Photographs were taken.

Digital backups were created.

None worked.

The copies degrade as well.

A scan taken at 14:02 contained 100% of the original text.

At 14:11, several letters were missing.

At 14:27, entire words were gone.

At 14:41, the file contained blank sections where Alice's handwriting had been.

The phenomenon does not appear to care whether the record is physical or digital.

---

# **NEKO**

Neko attempted to isolate the data.

He created a completely separate archival system.

Offline.

Air-gapped.

No network connection.

No external synchronization.

The text still disappeared.

Neko stared at the screen.

> "...You're kidding."

Another character vanished.

He shut the system down.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has stopped allowing anyone to take Alice's journal away.

She sits with it.

Reading the remaining words.

Trying to memorize them before they disappear.

Sometimes she reads a sentence aloud.

Then reads it again.

And again.

Because she is afraid that one day the page will be blank.

---

# **THE MOST CRUEL PART**

Alice created the journal because she was afraid of forgetting everyone.

Now the journal itself is forgetting **her.**

The names she carefully wrote down are disappearing.

The descriptions are disappearing.

The drawings are disappearing.

Her reminders are disappearing.

Even her own handwriting is being erased.

The very thing she created to prevent herself from being forgotten is becoming incapable of remembering her.

---

# **FINAL PAGE**

One page remains almost completely intact.

It contains only:

> **"Alice"**

Below it:

> **"Alice is..."**

Everything after that has disappeared.

The final line is incomplete.

Nobody knows what Alice originally intended to write.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

The phenomenon is ongoing.

No reversal has been discovered.

No preservation method has succeeded.

Personnel have been instructed not to panic when individual letters disappear.

This instruction has been largely ignored.

Every missing character feels like losing Alice again.

---

# **ARCHIVE NOTE**

There is now a growing fear among the staff.

Not that Alice will be forgotten.

But that eventually—

there will be nothing left to remember her **from.**

No photograph.

No intact journal.

No complete drawing.

No written record.

Nothing she personally left behind.

Only the memories of those who knew her.

For now, those memories remain unaffected.
