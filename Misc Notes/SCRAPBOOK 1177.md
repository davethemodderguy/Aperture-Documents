# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### MEDICAL & RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-MED-4C-NERI-305  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — MEDICAL / RESIDENT WELFARE  
**STATUS:** CRITICAL INCIDENT — RESOLVED  
**SUBJECT:** Neri Tachyon  
**INCIDENT:** Undisclosed Cardiac Failure Following Post-Impact Complications

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At approximately 08:17, Neri was observed returning to her room following breakfast.

She was in her normal humanoid form.

She was walking independently.

She appeared tired.

She was also experiencing significant pain in the chest and cardiac region.

Neri recognized the pain.

She did not report it immediately.

This was not unusual.

Neri has an established tendency to suppress symptoms until they become impossible to ignore.

What happened next, however, was entirely consistent with a much older problem.

Neri attempted to call Bao.

Then she remembered something Bao had told her that morning.

> **“I'll be very busy today, okay? Try not to call me unless it's an emergency.”**

Neri stopped.

She looked at her phone.

She did not make the call.

Instead, she entered a nearby storage area.

She transformed into Plush Form.

She closed the storage locker.

And she remained there.

For more than five hours.

---

# 2. THE MORNING CONVERSATION

Bao had warned Neri earlier that morning that her schedule would be unusually demanding.

The statement was not intended to discourage Neri from seeking medical assistance.

It was simply a request to avoid unnecessary interruptions.

Bao had no reason to believe Neri would interpret it differently.

Unfortunately, Neri's definition of:

> **“Emergency”**

has historically excluded:

> **herself.**

Neri has previously demonstrated that she may consider the following insufficient reason to seek assistance:

- significant pain;
    
- weakness;
    
- dizziness;
    
- exhaustion;
    
- difficulty breathing;
    
- prolonged illness;
    
- and symptoms she believes somebody else could handle better.
    

Her internal threshold remains dangerously distorted.

If someone else is in danger:

> **Emergency.**

If Neri herself is in danger:

> **“I'll be okay.”**

---

# 3. RETURN TO ROOM

At approximately 08:17, corridor monitoring recorded Neri walking toward her room.

Her pace was slow.

She occasionally stopped.

One hand remained near her chest.

No other resident was immediately nearby.

Neri eventually stopped beside a wall.

She closed her eyes.

After several seconds, she continued.

Approximately thirty meters later, she reached the storage corridor.

She removed her phone from her coat.

The screen was already open.

**BAO — CONTACT**

Neri's thumb hovered over the call button.

She did not press it.

---

# 4. HESITATION

Neri stood completely still.

She looked at the phone.

Then at the corridor.

Then back at the phone.

The following sequence was reconstructed from later device records and corridor footage.

Neri appeared to consider calling Bao.

She did not.

Instead, she quietly said:

> **Neri:**  
> “She's busy.”

Pause.

> **Neri:**  
> “It's okay.”

Another pause.

> **Neri:**  
> “Not an emergency.”

She put the phone away.

---

# 5. STORAGE LOCKER

At 08:21, Neri entered an unused storage locker.

She closed the door.

She transformed into Plush Form.

The transformation itself was uneventful.

Neri then curled up inside the confined space.

The locker remained closed.

No distress signal was activated.

No message was sent.

No resident was notified.

No medical alert was triggered.

Neri had deliberately removed herself from everyone's immediate awareness.

This was not an attempt to disappear permanently.

It was, in her mind, an attempt to:

> **“Wait until it passes.”**

---

# 6. WHY SHE DID NOT CALL

The distinction is important.

Neri did not believe she was forbidden from contacting Bao.

She believed she was supposed to determine whether her problem was important enough to justify interrupting her.

She decided it wasn't.

This is the same reasoning pattern documented repeatedly throughout Neri's medical history.

> **Other people's work is important.**

> **My problems can wait.**

> **If I can still move, it isn't an emergency.**

> **If I'm conscious, it isn't an emergency.**

> **If I can handle it myself, I should handle it myself.**

The final assumption is particularly dangerous.

Because Neri often remains capable of functioning until she suddenly isn't.

---

# 7. FIVE HOURS

For more than five hours, the locker remained undisturbed.

Neri did not emerge.

No one noticed her absence immediately.

Her room was empty.

Her laptop remained untouched.

Her coffee remained unfinished.

Her phone was inside the locker with her.

Her normal routine provided no obvious indication that anything had gone wrong.

She had simply:

> **Gone somewhere quiet.**

Which, under ordinary circumstances, would not have been unusual.

---

# 8. BAO REALIZES

At approximately 13:34, Bao finally noticed something was wrong.

Not because an alarm had sounded.

Not because a medical system had detected Neri.

Not because someone had found her.

Because Bao realized:

> **Neri had not been seen for several hours.**

Bao checked Neri's room.

Empty.

Cafeteria.

Empty.

Library.

Empty.

Quiet hallways.

Empty.

Bao's concern increased rapidly.

She checked Neri's usual locations.

Nothing.

Then she stopped.

> **BAO:**  
> “Her phone.”

Neri almost always carried it.

The device could not be reached.

Bao immediately began searching the Blacksite.

---

# 9. DISCOVERY

At approximately 13:47, a storage worker reported that one locker appeared to be occupied.

The locker was opened.

Inside was Neri.

Still in Plush Form.

Completely motionless.

Bao arrived seconds later.

She immediately recognized the problem.

Neri was unresponsive.

There was no meaningful response to voice or touch.

The usual softness of Plush Form was still present.

But Neri was not.

Bao picked her up.

> **BAO:**  
> “Neri?”

Nothing.

> **BAO:**  
> “Neri, wake up.”

Nothing.

Bao's voice changed.

> **BAO:**  
> “Neri.”

Still nothing.

---

# 10. CARDIAC FAILURE

Medical personnel confirmed the worst possibility.

Neri had suffered a complete cardiac arrest.

She had been dead for an unknown portion of the preceding interval.

The precise time of death could not be established.

The locker had provided no useful physiological monitoring.

Neri had not been discovered until well after the event.

Under ordinary circumstances, the situation would have been considered extraordinarily grave.

Neri, however, is not physiologically ordinary.

Her medical team immediately initiated the established fictional emergency revival protocol used specifically for her altered physiology.

---

# 11. EMERGENCY REVIVAL

Neri's established emergency treatment requires three components to be coordinated:

> **Bao's blood.**

> **Pure medical adrenaline.**

> **A controlled 4-joule defibrillation shock delivered through the designated medical defibrillator system.**

The procedure is performed exclusively by trained medical personnel using Blacksite medical equipment.

No improvisation was permitted.

No experimental substitutions were attempted.

Bao remained present throughout.

---

# 12. THE REVIVAL

The medical team administered the established treatment.

For several seconds:

Nothing happened.

Bao stared at Neri.

The monitor remained unchanged.

Then—

A response.

A weak cardiac signal appeared.

The medical team immediately continued stabilization.

Another moment passed.

Neri's heart rhythm began to establish itself.

Bao exhaled sharply.

> **BAO:**  
> “Come on.”

The monitor continued.

One beat.

Then another.

Then another.

Neri was alive.

---

# 13. RETURN TO CONSCIOUSNESS

Neri did not wake immediately.

She remained unconscious during the initial stabilization period.

When consciousness eventually returned, her eyes opened only slightly.

Bao was beside her.

Neri looked at her.

She appeared confused.

Bao leaned closer.

> **BAO:**  
> “Neri?”

Neri blinked.

Her voice was barely audible.

> **Neri:**  
> “...Sorry.”

Bao's expression tightened.

> **BAO:**  
> “Don't.”

Neri looked away.

> **BAO:**  
> “Don't apologize.”

Neri was silent.

After several seconds:

> **Neri:**  
> “You were busy.”

Bao closed her eyes briefly.

---

# 14. BAO'S RESPONSE

Bao understood immediately.

She remembered what she had said that morning.

> **“Try not to call me unless it's an emergency.”**

And she understood what Neri had heard.

Not:

> _“Don't interrupt me unless you genuinely need help.”_

But:

> _“Don't bother me unless you're dying.”_

And, unfortunately—

Neri had apparently decided that even dying did not count.

Bao took Neri's hand.

> **BAO:**  
> “Neri.”

Neri looked toward her.

> **BAO:**  
> “You were an emergency.”

Silence.

> **BAO:**  
> “You were always an emergency.”

Neri's eyes began watering.

> **BAO:**  
> “I don't care how busy I am.”

Pause.

> **BAO:**  
> “If you're hurting, you call me.”

Neri said nothing.

Bao continued:

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't decide whether you're worth interrupting me for.”

Neri's fingers tightened slightly around Bao's hand.

---

# 15. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri remained silent for a long time.

Eventually:

> **Neri:**  
> “...I thought you were busy.”

Bao nodded.

> **BAO:**  
> “I was.”

Neri looked down.

> **BAO:**  
> “And I still wanted you to call.”

Neri's eyes watered again.

She did not speak.

She simply held Bao's hand.

---

# 16. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

Neri remains under observation following the event.

Her cardiovascular system has stabilized.

The post-impact pain remains under active management.

Her physical recovery has been temporarily set back by the cardiac event.

Medical staff have therefore suspended:

- Hell Fire trials;
    
- Limited Merchandise production;
    
- accessory orders;
    
- intensive chemistry work;
    
- strenuous movement;
    
- and recreational activities requiring significant stamina.
    

Neri is not being asked to “catch up.”

Her only current responsibility is recovery.

---

# 17. POLICY CHANGE

The incident has resulted in a modification to Neri's emergency communication protocol.

Personnel are no longer permitted to give Neri vague instructions such as:

> **“Don't call unless it's an emergency.”**

When speaking to Neri, personnel must instead use explicit language.

Recommended:

> **“Call me if you're in pain.”**

> **“Call me if you're worried.”**

> **“Call me even if you're not sure.”**

> **“You won't be bothering me.”**

Most importantly:

> **“You don't have to decide whether it's an emergency. Just call.”**

This distinction is now part of Neri's medical communication profile.

---

# 18. THE STORAGE LOCKER

The locker has been removed from general storage use.

Not because Neri intentionally harmed herself.

She did not.

She entered it because she wanted somewhere quiet.

She transformed because Plush Form was comfortable.

She closed the door because she expected to recover without bothering anyone.

The problem was not the locker.

The problem was that nobody knew she was inside it.

---

# 19. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

This incident is classified as a **major preventable welfare failure**.

Neri did not conceal her condition because she wanted to die.

She concealed it because she believed her condition did not qualify for assistance.

That distinction matters.

She had:

- chest pain;
    
- worsening physical illness;
    
- access to her phone;
    
- access to Bao;
    
- and a medical support system specifically designed around her unusual physiology.
    

She still chose silence.

Because she believed:

> **Bao was busy.**

And because Neri's internal definition of an emergency continues to exclude herself.

---

# ARCHIVIST NOTE

Neri has spent years learning one very unfortunate lesson:

> **“Don't bother people.”**

Apparently, she has learned it extremely well.

Too well.

She was hurting.

She had a phone.

She could have called Bao.

Instead, she found a storage locker, turned herself into a plushie, curled up somewhere nobody would notice, and waited for the pain to stop.

It didn't.

She died there.

For more than five hours, nobody knew where she was.

And then Bao found her.

Bao brought her back.

Not because Neri suddenly decided she wanted to live.

Not because she finally decided the situation was serious enough.

But because someone else finally made the decision Neri refuses to make for herself:

> **Neri is an emergency too.**

Afterward, Neri apologized.

Of course she did.

Her first thought after coming back was still:

> **“You were busy.”**

Bao's answer was simple.

> **“I was.”**

> **“And I still wanted you to call.”**

Neri didn't answer.

She just held her mother's hand.

For once, she didn't try to let go.
