## Executive Summary

A significant correction has been made to the previous assessment of Neri's relationship with Alice.

Neri does **not** actually want to hate Alice.

She is, however, experiencing an apparent involuntary psychological response that is forcing her toward hostility whenever Alice is involved.

The distinction is critical.

Neri's hatred is real.

Her desire to hate Alice is not.

She appears to be fighting the response internally while simultaneously being unable to prevent it.

---

# 1. The Contradiction

Neri's behavior toward Alice has become increasingly hostile.

She avoids her.

She refuses contact.

She writes that she hates her.

She becomes distressed when Alice approaches.

However, recent observations reveal a contradiction that cannot be explained by ordinary resentment.

When Alice is absent, Neri has occasionally been observed looking at photographs of their previous interactions.

She does not destroy them.

She does not discard them.

She keeps looking.

She remembers.

The emotional response is inconsistent with someone who genuinely wishes to sever every attachment.

---

# 2. Observed Episode

Alice approached Neri in a quiet room.

Neri immediately stiffened.

Her expression changed.

She reached for her notebook.

She wrote:

> **"Go away."**

Alice stopped.

Neri stared at the words.

After several seconds, she crossed them out.

She wrote:

> **"Please."**

Then crossed that out as well.

She wrote:

> **"I don't want to hate you."**

The sentence remained on the page.

Alice began crying.

Neri looked at her.

Her hand began shaking.

She wrote again:

> **"I don't want this."**

Then:

> **"Make it stop."**

No mechanism capable of producing the response was immediately identified.

---

# 3. Internal Conflict

Neri appears to possess two conflicting responses to Alice.

### Conscious attachment

Neri remembers:

- Alice carrying her;
    
- Alice hugging her;
    
- sleeping beside Plushie Neri;
    
- sharing quiet moments;
    
- and the affection that existed before the memory anomaly.
    

These memories have not disappeared.

Neri still values them.

### Involuntary defensive response

When Alice is physically present, another response activates.

Neri experiences:

- anger;
    
- distrust;
    
- rejection;
    
- fear of being hurt again;
    
- and an overwhelming urge to create distance.
    

The response is sufficiently strong that Neri describes herself as **being forced to hate Alice.**

---

# 4. The Important Difference

Neri is not pretending to hate Alice.

She is not manipulating Alice.

She is not secretly enjoying the conflict.

She genuinely experiences hatred when the response activates.

But internally, another part of her is resisting it.

This produces an unusual emotional state:

> **"I hate you."**

followed by:

> **"I don't want to."**

Both statements can be true simultaneously.

Neri does not have complete control over which one reaches the surface.

---

# 5. Possible Mechanism

C.O.R.E. currently considers several hypotheses.

The leading possibility is that Neri's prolonged exposure to forgetting, accidental injury, abandonment, and emotional distress has produced an anomalous defensive response.

The response may have effectively learned:

> **Alice = danger.**

It does not distinguish between:

> _Alice intentionally hurt me._

and:

> _Alice accidentally hurt me while suffering from an anomalous memory condition._

It simply recognizes Alice as the person associated with repeated emotional and physical harm.

Once activated, the defensive response produces hostility automatically.

This remains a hypothesis.

No definitive mechanism has been identified.

---

# 6. Why Neri Cannot Simply Forgive Her

Personnel are specifically instructed not to tell Neri:

> "You know Alice didn't mean it."

She knows.

That information does not deactivate the response.

Likewise, she cannot simply decide:

> _I forgive her._

and expect the hostility to disappear.

Her conscious feelings and involuntary response are currently operating independently.

This is why Neri appears confused by her own behavior.

She can remember loving Alice.

She can understand that Alice's memory loss was involuntary.

She can genuinely want their relationship back.

And she can still feel overwhelming hatred when Alice approaches.

But it is not entirely hers.

That is what makes the situation so painful.

Some part of Neri still remembers the girl who carried her.

The girl who hugged her.
