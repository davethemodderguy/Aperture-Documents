# **PERSONAL STATEMENT**

I have spent every day asking myself the same question.

**How did I fail to understand my own daughter?**

I thought I understood her pain.

I knew about the experiments.

I knew about the years she lost.

I knew she hated looking in mirrors.

I knew she believed she was broken.

I thought that was enough.

It wasn't.

I understood **what** happened to Corin.

I did not understand what those years had taught her to believe about herself.

When she asked me,

> **"Mom, am I a burden to you?"**

I answered,

> **"No."**

That part was easy.

Then I tried to tell her what was in my heart.

I told her I wanted my little girl back.

What I meant was that I missed the laughter that had been stolen from her.

I missed the carefree child who should have grown up surrounded by her family.

I never meant that I wished for a different daughter.

But that wasn't what Corin heard.

She heard that the daughter standing in front of me wasn't enough.

I saw it in her eyes the moment those words reached her.

By the time I understood...

It was already too late to take them back.

---

Later, when I finally found the treehouse...

I wanted to climb the ladder.

I wanted to hug her.

I wanted to apologize.

Instead...

She stood at the entrance.

She looked at me with exhausted eyes.

Then she quietly said,

> **"Please leave."**

I took one step anyway.

She raised her hand.

Not to strike me.

Only to stop me.

> **"Don't come any closer."**

There was no anger in her voice.

Only hurt.

The kind of hurt that makes someone build walls instead of asking for help.

I stopped.

She lowered the rope ladder herself.

Not so I could climb.

So she could pull it out of reach.

Then she disappeared back inside.

She had, with her own hands, closed the only door to the place where she still felt safe.

I stood beneath that tree for a long time.

Eventually...

I turned around and walked away.

Not because I wanted to.

Because she had asked me to.

---

People have told me not to blame myself.

They're right.

One conversation did not create fifteen years of trauma.

One sentence did not put those scars on my daughter.

But I still regret that day.

Because when Corin reached out with a question...

She wasn't asking for the perfect answer.

She was asking whether she was still my daughter.

If I could live that moment again...

I wouldn't talk about the past.

I wouldn't talk about the little girl I missed.

I'd look at the young woman standing in front of me.

I'd hold her hands.

And I'd say only this:

> **"Corin."**

> **"I don't want anyone else."**

> **"I want you."**

> **"Exactly as you are."**

Until the day she believes those words...

I'll keep waiting beneath that tree.

Because mothers don't stop being mothers when their children tell them to leave.

Sometimes...

They just wait a little farther away.

**— Lumia Lockwood**
