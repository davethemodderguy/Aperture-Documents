### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — REGENERATIVE PRIORITY FAILURE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / MULTIPLE CHRONIC INJURIES**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice has accumulated a significant number of injuries since her recovery.

Most are directly associated with her repeated attempts to escape SAFEHOUSE.

Her body repeatedly fails during these attempts.

She falls.

She strikes the ground.

She continues trying to move despite injury.

Her natural regeneration activates.

But it does not repair everything equally.

---

# **DOCUMENTED INJURIES**

Alice currently exhibits or has recently exhibited:

- Numerous cuts and lacerations
    
- Repeated head bruising
    
- Facial bruising
    
- Recurrent nasal injuries
    
- Previous nasal fractures
    
- Minor soft-tissue trauma
    
- Persistent head injuries
    
- Repeated impact injuries from falls
    

Many of these injuries have occurred multiple times.

The repeated head impacts are of particular concern.

---

# **THE HEAD BANDAGE**

Alice is almost always wearing a medical bandage around her head.

It has become an unfortunately familiar sight around SAFEHOUSE.

The reason is not that her regeneration cannot repair injuries.

It can.

The problem is **priority.**

---

# **REGENERATIVE PRIORITY**

Alice's regenerative system does not appear to operate according to conscious choice.

It follows an internal biological priority system.

Critical functions appear to receive the greatest allocation of regenerative resources.

In simplified terms:

> **"Keep the organism alive first."**

Everything else comes afterward.

This means that injuries that do not immediately threaten vital function may receive dramatically reduced regenerative priority.

A wound can therefore remain open.

A bruise can remain.

A fracture can heal incompletely or slowly.

A head injury can persist.

Meanwhile, Alice's body continues allocating resources toward maintaining vital organs and basic physiological function.

---

# **VITAL-ORGAN PROXIMITY**

An especially concerning observation is that injuries near vital organs do not always receive the expected regenerative priority.

The system appears to distinguish between:

**Immediate life-threatening damage**

and

**Serious but temporarily survivable damage.**

If Alice's body determines that something can remain damaged without immediately causing death, it may postpone repair.

This creates a dangerous situation.

Alice can appear physically functional while carrying injuries that still require conventional medical treatment.

---

# **WHY MEDICAL TREATMENT REMAINS NECESSARY**

Regeneration cannot be treated as a replacement for medicine.

Medical staff continue to:

- Clean wounds
    
- Dress lacerations
    
- Stabilize fractures
    
- Treat infections
    
- Monitor head injuries
    
- Control inflammation
    
- Provide nutritional support
    
- Monitor vital signs
    
- Protect damaged tissue
    

The regeneration is treated as an additional biological process.

Not as the primary treatment.

---

# **RECENT INCIDENT**

Alice attempted to leave SAFEHOUSE again.

She reached the exterior corridor.

Her legs failed.

She fell forward.

Her head struck the floor.

A wound opened near her scalp.

Alice attempted to stand.

She couldn't.

By the time personnel reached her, the bleeding had already slowed.

The regenerative process had begun.

But the wound did not completely close.

Alice was conscious.

She whispered:

> **"Alice can go."**

The medic answered:

> **"No. You're hurt."**

Alice looked confused.

> **"It fix."**

The medic looked at the wound.

> **"Not enough."**

# **CORIN**

Corin has become accustomed to seeing the bandage.

She changes it carefully.

Sometimes Alice asks:

> **"Why Alice always wear this?"**

Corin does not say:

> "Because you keep hurting yourself."

Instead:

> **"Because your head needs help healing."**

Alice accepts the explanation.

Sometimes she touches the bandage.

> **"It hurts."**

Corin answers:

> **"I know."**

---

# **BELLE**

Belle hates seeing the bandages.

She knows Alice's regeneration should make injuries temporary.

Yet Alice keeps accumulating them.

Belle once asked the medical department:

> **"Why doesn't she just heal?"**

The doctor answered:

> **"Because healing costs something."**

Belle already knew.

She simply didn't want to hear it again.

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi has become particularly concerned about the repeated head trauma.

Alice does not always remember previous injuries.

Sometimes she asks why something hurts.

Lumi remembers.

She remembers every time.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has classified repeated escape-related injuries as a **persistent medical emergency**.

The objective is not merely preventing Alice from leaving.

It is preventing her from repeatedly injuring herself in the process.

However, physical restraint is considered a last resort.

Alice must not learn:

> **"SAFEHOUSE is a place that traps me."**

That would reinforce exactly the belief everyone is trying to undo.

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice's regeneration does not mean she is invulnerable.

It means her body has learned how to survive damage.

Unfortunately, survival is not the same as recovery.

A broken nose can heal.

A cut can close.

A bruise can fade.

But her body must decide what deserves its limited resources first.

And when the choice is between repairing a wound on Alice's head...

or keeping her heart, lungs, and other vital systems functioning...

her regeneration chooses survival.

So Alice remains alive.

She remains injured.

She remains weak.

And she remains almost permanently wrapped in a bandage around her head.

Not because her body cannot heal her.

Because right now, it has more important things to spend its strength on.
