# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – FIREARMS ENGINEERING DIVISION**

### **TECHNICAL EVALUATION REPORT**

## **SUBJECT: MX-R SERVICE LIFE ANALYSIS (PROPERTY OF DIALYN)**

**Classification:** LEVEL 8 – Engineering & Security Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Following the recovery of Dialyn's personal **Aperture Industries MX-R**, Engineering Division performed a complete mechanical analysis to estimate the weapon's historical usage.

Initial expectations suggested a moderate service history consistent with a former mercenary.

The findings significantly exceeded those expectations.

---

# **SERVICE LIFE ESTIMATION**

Using:

- Barrel erosion measurements.
    
- Bolt carrier wear.
    
- Locking lug deformation.
    
- Gas system fatigue.
    
- Receiver stress analysis.
    
- Trigger group wear patterns.
    
- Internal microscopic tooling marks.
    

Engineering estimated the rifle has fired:

> **Approximately 1,500,000 rounds.**

Estimated uncertainty:

**± 70,000 rounds**

---

# **ENGINEERING RESPONSE**

The estimate was initially rejected as impossible.

The rifle was disassembled and examined by three independent engineering teams.

Each reached nearly identical conclusions.

Further testing confirmed that nearly every original component had been replaced during the rifle's service life.

The serialized upper and lower receivers remain original.

Almost everything else has been rebuilt, refurbished, or replaced over time.

As one engineer remarked:

> "This isn't the same rifle."

> "It's the same _receiver_."

---

# **MAINTENANCE HISTORY**

The weapon's exceptional condition is attributed to meticulous preventative maintenance.

Recovered maintenance logs indicate Dialyn routinely:

- Cleaned the rifle after every mission.
    
- Replaced worn springs before failure.
    
- Gauged barrel wear regularly.
    
- Re-zeroed optics after major servicing.
    
- Logged replacement dates for critical components.
    

Engineering concluded that neglect was virtually nonexistent.

# **RANGE TEST**

Following inspection, Security Division requested a controlled function test.

The MX-R completed the evaluation without malfunction.

Accuracy remained within Aperture Industries specifications.

Engineers noted that the trigger exhibited an unusually smooth break, consistent with years of careful maintenance rather than excessive modification.

One technician summarized the firing session.

> "...It feels broken in."

Another corrected him.

> "...No."

> "...It feels experienced."

---

# **DIALYN'S COMMENT**

When informed that her rifle had likely exceeded one and a half million rounds over its lifetime, Dialyn appeared genuinely surprised.

She quietly counted on her fingers for several moments before laughing.

> "...I guess that sounds about right."

She rested a hand on the receiver.

> "...Every time something wore out..."

> "...I just fixed it."

Another pause.

> "...Throwing it away never really crossed my mind."

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Engineering Division has concluded that Dialyn's MX-R represents one of the highest documented round-count service rifles still capable of meeting factory performance standards within Aperture Industries.

The report is now used internally as an example of the impact proper maintenance can have on firearm longevity.

A small plaque has since been placed in the Engineering workshop.

It reads:

> **"Maintenance is cheaper than replacement."**

Below it, in much smaller lettering:

> **"Ask Dialyn."**
