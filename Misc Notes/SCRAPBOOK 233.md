# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **MEDICAL SUPPORT RECORD**

## **SUBJECT: SUPPORTIVE ACCOMMODATIONS – COREY LOCKWOOD**

**Classification:** LEVEL 8 – Medical & Executive Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Following a series of severe mental health crises experienced by Corey Lockwood, Medical Services implemented additional long-term supportive measures in consultation with Corey, Corin Lockwood, Bao, and Psychological Services.

The objective of these accommodations is to support Corey's recovery, respect her privacy, and encourage continued engagement with treatment.

---

# **KNITTED ARMBANDS**

Among the accommodations are a pair of hand-knitted wristbands created jointly by Bao and Corin.

The wristbands were made only after Corey agreed to wear them.

They are soft, insulated, and comfortable enough for daily use.

Their purpose is not to conceal Corey from others.

Their purpose is to allow Corey to feel more comfortable without drawing unwanted attention to old injuries while they heal.

Medical personnel are reminded that the wristbands are a personal item.

They are not to be removed without Corey's permission except during necessary medical examinations.

---

# **DESIGN**

Bao selected the yarn.

Corin insisted on reinforcing the stitching so they would withstand everyday use.

The finished pair features:

- Thick winter wool.
    
- Soft inner lining.
    
- Elastic cuffs to prevent slipping.
    
- Small embroidered snowflakes along the edge.
    

On the inside of the left wristband, stitched where only Corey can see it, are two simple words.

> **Keep Going.**

The right wristband bears matching embroidery.

> **We're Here.**

Neither inscription is visible while worn.

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL ROLE**

Psychological Services describe the wristbands as a grounding object rather than a medical device.

They serve as a quiet reminder that Corey is supported by people who care about her.

They are not considered a substitute for ongoing mental health care, regular follow-up, or the support of those around her.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corey continues to participate in ongoing medical and psychological care.

The knitted wristbands have become part of her everyday clothing and are now rarely seen without them.

Within Project SAFEHOUSE, no one comments on them anymore.

To the residents, they are simply another part of Corey's winter attire.

Those who know their story understand they represent something much quieter than clothing.

They are a reminder that healing is rarely immediate, often uneven, and that sometimes the smallest handmade gifts can become symbols of hope carried every day.
