# **HEAVEN'S BLACKSITE**

## **CONFIDENTIAL PSYCHOLOGICAL EVALUATION**

### **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – IDENTITY DISTURBANCE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 – Medical Director & Executive Personnel Only

---

## **CLINICAL SUMMARY**

Corin Lockwood continues to experience persistent disturbances in her sense of identity following prolonged childhood trauma and forced experimentation.

The defining feature of these episodes is not memory loss.

Corin remembers much of her childhood.

Instead, she struggles to believe that the child she remembers and the person she is today are the same individual.

This belief becomes more pronounced during periods of emotional exhaustion or when confronted with reminders of her life before the experiments.

---

## **SELF-PERCEPTION**

During multiple therapy sessions, Corin has consistently referred to her childhood self in the third person.

Examples include:

> "...She was happy."

> "...She laughed a lot."

> "...She trusted people."

When asked if she was describing herself, Corin remained silent before answering.

> "...No."

Another pause.

> "...I don't think that girl exists anymore."

Counselors noted that Corin rarely uses "I" when discussing memories from before her abduction.

Instead, she speaks as though describing someone she once knew.

---

## **IDENTITY CRISES**

These episodes are infrequent but severe.

During them, Corin may question fundamental aspects of who she is.

Common statements include:

> "...Which one is the real Corin?"

> "...Did she die... or did I?"

> "...If that little girl still existed, would she even recognize me?"

Medical staff emphasize that these episodes are trauma-related disruptions in self-perception rather than confusion about factual identity.

Corin remains fully oriented to time, place, and person throughout these events.

---

## **THE PHOTOGRAPHS**

A review of Corin's personal belongings revealed that the overwhelming majority of photographs depicting her childhood have been intentionally damaged over the years.

Many have been:

- Torn into small fragments.
    
- Burned.
    
- Water-damaged.
    
- Covered in ink.
    

Several were recovered only after being discarded.

Conservation specialists concluded that most are **beyond meaningful restoration**.

Only a small number of images remain intact.

When informed that many photographs could not be repaired, Corin's response surprised the attending counselor.

She did not cry.

She simply nodded.

> "...I know."

Then, after a long silence:

> "...I was the one who destroyed them."

She closed her eyes.

> "...I wish I hadn't."

---

## **ARCTURUS**

When Arcturus learned that many of the photographs were permanently lost, he did not express anger.

Instead, he quietly began writing down every childhood memory he could still remember.

He filled notebooks with ordinary moments.

Fishing trips.

Snow days.

Birthday cakes.

Arguments over toys.

Bedtime stories.

When Corin asked why he was doing it, he smiled.

> "If the pictures are gone..."

> "...Then we'll keep the memories another way."

---

## **NORMA**

Norma approached the loss differently.

Being an engineer, her instinct was to fix the problem.

When told the photographs could not be recovered, she spent several evenings trying anyway.

Eventually she accepted the restorers' conclusion.

She quietly closed the last damaged album.

> "...Some things can't be rebuilt."

She looked toward Corin.

> "...That doesn't mean they're forgotten."

---

## **CLINICAL ASSESSMENT**

The attending psychologist summarized Corin's condition as follows:

> "Corin is not mourning the loss of photographs."

> "She is mourning the loss of certainty that the child in those photographs was truly her."

Treatment therefore focuses not on recovering damaged objects, but on helping Corin gradually reconnect with her own life story through trusted relationships, shared memories, and present-day experiences.

---

## **DIRECTOR LUCIOUS' ANNOTATION**

Director Lucious reviewed the report before writing a final observation.

> "Photographs preserve appearances."

> "They do not preserve identity."

He closed the file.

> "The child who loved her family."

> "The teenager who protects the KCA."

> "The young woman who still sits with her siblings over tea."

> "These are not three different people."

> "They are one life that was interrupted—but not erased."

---

## **STATUS**

Corin continues to receive specialized trauma-focused psychological care.

While she still experiences periods of identity disturbance, she has also shown moments—often while spending time with Arcturus, Norma, Belle, and the SAFEHOUSE residents—where she speaks about both her past and present self without separating them.

The treatment team considers these moments small but meaningful signs of progress.

They are careful not to rush that process.

Healing, they note, is not about convincing Corin to forget what happened.

It is about helping her believe that the child she mourns never truly disappeared—she survived, grew, and is still worthy of the same compassion today.

**End of Confidential Psychological Evaluation**
