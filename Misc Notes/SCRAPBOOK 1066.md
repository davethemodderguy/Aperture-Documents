## Executive Summary

Alice forgot Neri again.

This time, she forgot her on the SAFEHOUSE balcony.

The balcony was outdoors.

A prolonged rainstorm subsequently passed through the area.

Neri remained there for approximately **three days**.

She was eventually discovered completely soaked from head to toe.

The incident is not considered intentional neglect.

The primary contributing factor was Alice's increasingly apparent **subsequent memory loss regarding Neri's whereabouts**.

Alice does not merely become distracted for several hours.

She can genuinely lose the memory that she was handling Neri in the first place.

This has now become a recurring and increasingly dangerous pattern.

---

## Incident Timeline

### DAY 1 — 16:11

Alice was observed carrying Plushie Neri onto the SAFEHOUSE balcony.

Neri was passive and allowed herself to be carried.

Alice reportedly intended to remain outside briefly.

She placed Neri down near the balcony seating area.

Alice then left.

At this point, Neri remained where she had been placed.

---

### DAY 1 — 16:38

Rain began.

Neri remained outside.

No movement was recorded.

---

### DAY 1 — 19:00

Alice was observed elsewhere in the SAFEHOUSE.

When asked where Neri was, Alice reportedly answered:

> "She's with me."

This statement appears to have been genuine.

Alice believed Neri was still with her.

She did not remember leaving her on the balcony.

---

### DAY 2

Heavy rain continued intermittently.

Neri remained outside.

She did not attempt to return indoors.

She did not seek shelter.

She did not call for anyone.

She did not voluntarily move.

This behavior is consistent with the severe loss of self-directed behavior documented following the previous outdoor incident.

---

### DAY 2 — 22:14

Alice was asked about Neri again.

Alice looked confused.

> **Alice:** "Neri?"

She then searched the SAFEHOUSE.

She did not check the balcony.

This is significant.

Alice had apparently forgotten not only where Neri was, but that she had brought Neri onto the balcony at all.

---

### DAY 3 — 11:52

A resident opened the balcony door.

They immediately noticed a small soaked seal plush sitting motionless near the railing.

They initially assumed it was an ordinary plush toy.

Then they noticed the glasses.

> **Resident:** "...Oh no."

Neri was retrieved immediately.

---

# Physical Condition

Neri was soaked **head to toe**.

Her fabric exterior was saturated.

Water dripped from her ears, sleeves, coat, and tail.

Her glasses were covered in droplets.

Her hair was completely flattened.

She was cold and exhausted.

Despite the prolonged exposure, no permanent physical damage was identified.

Her plush physiology again prevented the situation from becoming immediately catastrophic.

Medical personnel nevertheless classified the exposure as unsafe and unnecessary.

---

## Neri's Response

When brought indoors, Neri did not complain.

She simply sat in the arms of the resident who had found her.

She was shivering.

After several minutes, someone asked:

> "Neri, are you okay?"

Neri shook her head.

> **Neri:** "Cold."

That was the entirety of her initial complaint.

---

# Alice's Memory Loss

Alice was eventually informed that Neri had been found outside.

Her first reaction was confusion.

> **Alice:** "Outside?"

She was told that she had placed Neri on the balcony.

Alice stared blankly.

> **Alice:** "I did?"

She genuinely did not remember.

When shown where she had left Neri, Alice became increasingly distressed.

> **Alice:** "I don't remember putting her there."

This was not an excuse.

There was no indication that Alice was attempting to avoid responsibility.

She genuinely could not recall the event.

This distinction has become increasingly important.

---

## Recurrent Pattern

Alice's behavior is now documented as follows:

1. Alice obtains Plushie Neri.
    
2. Alice interacts with Neri affectionately.
    
3. Alice places Neri somewhere.
    
4. Alice becomes distracted or transitions to another activity.
    
5. Alice's memory of having Neri begins to disappear.
    
6. Alice later genuinely believes Neri is elsewhere, or has no immediate recollection of handling her.
    
7. Neri remains wherever she was left.
    

This is no longer adequately described as simple forgetfulness.

The frequency and duration have reached the point where C.O.R.E. Welfare is documenting it as **subsequent memory loss involving Neri**.

---

# Alice's Reaction

When Alice finally saw Neri after the three-day exposure, she immediately ran toward her.

> **Alice:** "Neri!"

She stopped herself before picking her up.

Neri was still dripping water onto the floor.

Alice looked at her.

> **Alice:** "I forgot you again."

Neri said nothing.

Alice's eyes filled with tears.

> **Alice:** "I forgot you for three days."

Neri remained still.

Alice whispered:

> **"I'm sorry."**

Neri looked toward her.

She did not move closer.

She did not move away.

---

# Decontamination / Cleaning

Neri was subsequently taken to clean herself.

She was capable of doing so independently.

No washing machine was used.

**This point has been explicitly emphasized following the previous laundry-related near incident.**

Neri later showered and cleaned the accumulated rainwater, dirt, and other outdoor residue from her plush exterior.

Afterward, she was wrapped in dry towels and allowed to rest.

She remained quiet.

---

# Behavioral Significance

The most concerning aspect is no longer simply that Alice forgets Neri.

It is that **Neri has stopped compensating for being forgotten.**

Previously, if left somewhere, Neri could move herself.

She could crawl away.

She could find somewhere comfortable.

She could seek food.

She could return indoors.

She could find another resident.

She no longer does these things.

Therefore:

> **Alice's memory loss + Neri's loss of voluntary movement = prolonged unattended exposure.**

The combination is now considered a significant welfare hazard.

---

## Important Distinction

C.O.R.E. does **not** consider Alice uncaring.

Quite the opposite.

Alice's affection for Neri remains extremely strong.

The problem is that affection is followed by memory loss.

Alice can genuinely love Neri intensely at 16:00 and genuinely have no recollection that Neri exists in her immediate care at 18:00.

The two states can coexist.

This is precisely what makes the situation difficult.

---

# Current Directive

Alice is temporarily prohibited from being the **sole person responsible for Plushie Neri outdoors**.

This is not a punishment.

It is a safety measure.

If Alice wants to carry Neri, she may.

If Neri wants to be held by Alice, she may.

If Alice wants to sit with Neri, she may.

However, another resident must remain aware of Neri's location when Alice is responsible for her.

The objective is not to separate Alice and Neri.

It is to compensate for Alice's unreliable memory.

---

## Final Assessment

Three days of rain did not permanently harm Neri.

But the incident demonstrated how dangerous the current situation has become.

Alice's memory of Neri is becoming unreliable.

Neri's ability—or willingness—to move herself has almost completely disappeared.

Neither condition alone necessarily produces a catastrophe.

Together, they can leave Neri sitting motionless outdoors for days.
