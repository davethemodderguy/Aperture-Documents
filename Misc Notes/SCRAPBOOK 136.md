### **PSYCHOLOGICAL CASE NOTE**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – ENDURING EFFECTS OF CHILDHOOD TRAUMA**

**Classification:** LEVEL 8 – Medical & Executive Personnel Only

---

# **CLINICAL OBSERVATION**

Personnel occasionally ask why Corin does not simply "move on."

Psychological Services consider that question to misunderstand the nature of her trauma.

Corin did not lose a single happy memory.

She lost years of her childhood.

Years that should have been spent learning, growing, making friends, and returning home to her family were instead replaced by captivity, experimentation, fear, and dehumanization.

Those experiences became part of the foundation upon which her identity was built.

For Corin, the past is not something distant.

It continues to shape how she sees herself every day.

---

# **COMPARISON**

Belle Merryweather and Corin Lockwood have both survived painful childhoods.

Their experiences are different.

Belle grew up feeling unwanted and worthless.

Corin grew up being treated as though she were no longer a person at all.

Neither experience diminishes the other.

Trauma cannot be measured like numbers on a page.

Each left wounds that required different kinds of healing.

---

# **RECORDED CONVERSATION**

One evening, Corin quietly asked Lumi:

> "...Will this ever stop hurting?"

Lumi did not answer immediately.

Instead, she sat beside her daughter.

After a long silence, she spoke.

> "Some wounds leave scars."

Corin looked down.

> "...Then they'll never go away."

Lumi gently nodded.

> "No."

> "Some things cannot be erased."

Corin closed her eyes.

> "...Then what's the point?"

Lumi took her daughter's hand.

> "The point isn't pretending it never happened."

> "The point is making sure it isn't the only thing your life will ever be."

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Psychological Services do not expect Corin to simply "get over" what happened.

That is not a realistic therapeutic goal.

The objective is not to erase her past.

It is to help her live a meaningful life without the past defining every moment of her present.

Her trauma may always remain part of her story.

It does not have to be the entirety of it.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin continues to carry profound emotional wounds from her childhood.

The affection and support of her family and the residents of Project SAFEHOUSE cannot undo the years that were stolen from her.

No one—not even Lumi—believes love can erase those memories.

What it can do is ensure that, when those memories return, Corin does not have to face them by herself.

Recovery, for Corin, is not the absence of pain.

It is slowly building a life in which pain is no longer the only thing she has.

**End of Psychological Case Note**
