### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — ISOLATION INCIDENTS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — SAFEHOUSE PERSONNEL  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 5  
**Condition:** Fragile / Nonverbal / Extremely Quiet

---

# **OVERVIEW**

The SAFEHOUSE has begun to notice something unsettling.

Alice is quiet.

That was never new.

But lately, people have started realizing just **how** quiet she is.

A normal child makes noise.

They ask questions.

They complain.

They call for someone.

They cry when frightened.

They get angry when left behind.

Alice does none of these things.

She simply waits.

And when personnel eventually discover her sitting somewhere alone, the first reaction is often the same:

> "...How long have you been there?"

Alice usually doesn't know.

Or doesn't think it matters.

---

# **INCIDENT 18 — THE STAIRWELL**

Alice followed a group toward a lower-level facility.

The group entered an access-controlled area.

Alice stopped at the stairwell.

The others continued.

The door closed.

Alice sat on the stairs.

She waited.

Thirty minutes later, an employee opened the door.

Alice was still sitting there.

Her sketchbook was open.

She had drawn the stairwell.

The employee asked:

> "Why didn't you come find someone?"

Alice pointed toward her mouth.

Then shook her head.

The employee understood.

She couldn't call.

But the whistle around Alice's neck remained unused.

---

# **INCIDENT 19 — THE EMPTY BREAK ROOM**

Alice entered a break room while following several employees.

They eventually left.

Alice stayed.

The lights automatically turned off.

She didn't leave.

She sat beneath the table.

A maintenance worker eventually entered and found two small bunny ears underneath it.

He nearly screamed.

Alice looked up.

The worker stared.

> "...Alice?"

She nodded.

> "How long have you been here?"

Alice shrugged.

The worker checked the room's logs.

**47 minutes.**

---

# **INCIDENT 20 — THE LOCKER ROOM**

Alice was waiting outside while someone retrieved equipment.

The employee entered the locker room.

Alice followed.

The employee became distracted by another conversation.

They left through another exit.

Alice remained.

The locker room eventually emptied.

Alice sat on a bench.

She wasn't crying.

She wasn't frightened.

She simply waited.

A security officer found her during a routine patrol.

Alice was holding her hat in her lap.

---

# **INCIDENT 21 — THE GARDEN**

Alice went to the garden.

She liked the flowers.

She sat beside a flowerbed and began drawing.

A gardening team arrived.

They worked around her.

They left.

Another team arrived.

They left too.

Alice remained in exactly the same place.

Three hours passed.

Nobody realized she had been there the entire time.

When Corin finally found her, Alice looked up.

> "Alice."

Alice nodded.

Corin crouched beside her.

> "Were you waiting for someone?"

Alice shook her head.

> "Were you lost?"

Alice shook her head again.

> "Then why were you sitting here?"

Alice pointed toward the flowers.

She wanted to draw them.

That was all.

---

# **INCIDENT 22 — THE STORAGE ELEVATOR**

Alice entered a freight elevator.

Nobody noticed.

The elevator descended.

The doors opened.

Alice remained inside.

The doors closed again.

This repeated several times.

Eventually, a technician noticed the small figure sitting on the floor.

Alice was asleep.

Her scooter remained upstairs.

---

# **INCIDENT 23 — THE LIBRARY**

Alice sat in the corner of the Blacksite library.

She was reading through her journal.

Personnel passed her repeatedly.

Nobody spoke to her.

Alice didn't speak to them.

Eventually, the library closed.

The lights went out.

The automated doors locked.

Alice remained.

Security discovered her after detecting movement inside the closed facility.

Alice was sitting beneath a reading lamp.

Still drawing.

---

# **INCIDENT 24 — THE SNOW PATH**

Alice went outside after snowfall.

She was supposed to return with another employee.

The employee returned.

Alice didn't.

The employee assumed she had already gone inside.

Alice had stopped to watch snow falling from a tree.

She sat beneath it.

The snow accumulated on her hat.

She didn't move.

She didn't call.

She didn't use the whistle.

When she was eventually found, Frost asked:

> "Alice, why didn't you come inside?"

Alice wrote:

> **"Snow pretty."**

Frost stared at her.

Then at the snow covering Alice.

> "...You could have told me."

Alice pointed toward her mouth.

Frost's expression softened.

> "Right."

---

# **INCIDENT 25 — THE CONFERENCE ROOM**

Alice entered a conference room during a meeting.

She sat quietly against the wall.

The meeting ended.

Everyone left.

Nobody noticed her.

The lights switched off.

Alice remained.

Several hours later, Orphie entered to retrieve documents.

She found Alice sitting exactly where she had been.

Orphie stopped.

> "...Alice."

Alice looked up.

Orphie checked the time.

Her expression changed.

> "You've been here since the meeting?"

Alice nodded.

---

# **THE UNSETTLING PART**

None of these incidents involved Alice panicking.

That is what troubles personnel most.

She doesn't bang on doors.

She doesn't cry.

She doesn't scream.

She doesn't try to force her way out.

She doesn't even appear particularly angry when someone finally finds her.

She simply looks up.

Sometimes she smiles.

Sometimes she returns to whatever she was doing.

Sometimes she writes:

> **"Alice okay."**

---

# **PERSONNEL INTERVIEWS**

Several employees independently described the same feeling.

Finding Alice after an extended period of isolation is unsettling.

Not because Alice herself is frightening.

Because of how **quietly she endured it.**

One employee described finding Alice alone in a dark room:

> "She wasn't crying."

> "She wasn't scared."

> "She was just sitting there."

Another said:

> "I felt horrible."

> "She didn't even seem to understand why I was apologizing."

Another:

> "She's five."

> "She shouldn't be this good at being alone."

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has become increasingly disturbed by these reports.

She knows Alice doesn't complain because she doesn't want to bother people.

But sometimes Corin wonders whether Alice has gone beyond simply being considerate.

Perhaps Alice has learned that being forgotten is something she should simply accept.

That possibility frightens her.

---

# **ALICE'S JOURNAL**

Alice wrote about being left behind.

The page contained only a few lines:

> **"Sometimes Alice alone."**
> 
> **"People don't mean it."**
> 
> **"Alice wait."**
> 
> **"People come back."**

Below the writing was a drawing.

Alice sitting beneath a tree.

Nobody else around her.

She had drawn herself smiling.

---

# **NEW CONCERN**

The SAFEHOUSE's greatest concern is no longer simply:

> **"Will someone accidentally leave Alice behind?"**

It is now:

> **"Will Alice eventually stop expecting anyone to come back?"**

Because Alice does not demand company.

She doesn't ask anyone to stay.

She doesn't complain when someone leaves.

She simply waits.

And the longer everyone takes to notice—

the more normal waiting alone becomes for her.

---

# **CURRENT DIRECTIVE**

Personnel are reminded:

Alice's silence must **never** be interpreted as evidence that she is comfortable.

Her lack of complaint must **never** be interpreted as consent to remain alone.

Her ability to wait must **never** be interpreted as proof that she does not need someone.

If Alice is missing from a group:

**STOP.**

**CHECK.**

**FIND HER.**

Do not assume she wandered back to the SAFEHOUSE.

Do not assume someone else has her.

Do not assume she will call.

She won't.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice is not becoming louder.

She is not becoming more demanding.

She is not becoming more difficult.

If anything—

she is becoming quieter.

And that is beginning to frighten everyone.

Because a five-year-old should not be capable of disappearing into an empty room and waiting there for hours without making a sound.

Alice doesn't want to bother anyone.

So she waits.

Quietly.

Patiently.

Until someone remembers.
