# Executive Summary

Neri experienced another severe anemic episode.

This episode lasted approximately **five hours before discovery**.

During that period, Neri deliberately remained in the background of the SAFEHOUSE and concealed the severity of her condition.

She did not request assistance.

She did not write for help.

She did not approach Bao.

She did not alert nearby residents.

She instead attempted to remain unnoticed while experiencing significant physical pain.

The incident represents a substantial escalation of the behavioral pattern previously identified as the **Neri Gap**.

Neri was not merely overlooked.

**She actively made herself difficult to notice.**

---

# I. Initial Onset

The episode began during ordinary SAFEHOUSE activity.

Neri developed the familiar symptoms associated with her anemia.

The onset was accompanied by significant pain.

Rather than seeking medical assistance, Neri moved away from the main areas of activity.

She selected a location where residents regularly passed through but where she could remain relatively inconspicuous.

She stayed there.

---

# II. Five Hours

For approximately five hours, Neri remained in the background.

Residents passed nearby.

Conversations occurred.

Normal SAFEHOUSE activity continued.

Neri remained quiet.

She did not attempt to interrupt anyone.

She did not visibly call attention to herself.

When movement became difficult, she remained seated or leaned against nearby surfaces.

When someone looked in her direction, she attempted to appear normal.

She was not normal.

She was in pain.

---

# III. Why Nobody Initially Recognized the Problem

The circumstances were unusually difficult to identify because Neri's current baseline already includes:

- Spending considerable time alone.
    
- Rarely initiating conversation.
    
- Rarely writing.
    
- Remaining quiet around other residents.
    
- Enjoying solitary activities.
    
- Returning frequently to her room or the cafeteria.
    
- Avoiding unnecessary interaction.
    
- Being unable to speak.
    

Consequently, her silence did not immediately register as abnormal.

She had effectively become part of the background.

This is precisely what Neri wanted.

It is also precisely what made the episode dangerous.

---

# IV. Neri's Deliberate Concealment

Evidence indicates that Neri understood she was experiencing an episode.

She knew she was hurting.

She nevertheless chose not to communicate it.

When later questioned, Neri's explanation was extremely brief.

She wrote:

> **"I didn't want to bother anyone."**

No additional explanation was provided.

The statement is consistent with the established Non-Interference Rule.

---

# V. Medical Severity

The episode produced significant pain and physical deterioration.

The duration of approximately five hours substantially increased concern.

Neri's blood instability can progress independently of activity or food intake.

She can therefore become severely anemic while doing nothing more strenuous than sitting quietly.

Remaining still does not guarantee safety.

In this case, remaining still merely allowed the episode to continue without detection.

---

# VI. Discovery

Neri was eventually noticed because her behavior had become sufficiently abnormal to attract attention.

She was unusually pale.

Her posture was guarded.

She was barely moving.

She did not respond normally to an initial attempt at interaction.

Personnel immediately recognized that this was not ordinary quietness.

Bao was contacted.

Medical assessment began.

---

# VII. Bao's Reaction

Bao was visibly distressed by the discovery.

The primary concern was not only the severity of the anemia.

It was the realization that Neri had been suffering for hours without approaching her.

Bao asked:

> **"Why didn't you come find me?"**

Neri did not answer.

She looked away.

Bao asked again, more quietly:

> **"Why didn't you tell me?"**

Neri eventually wrote:

> **"You were busy."**

Bao reportedly remained silent for several seconds.

She then told Neri:

> **"I would rather be interrupted."**

Neri did not respond.

---

# VIII. Jane Doe's Reaction

Jane's reaction was similarly severe.

Jane had previously been attempting to respect Neri's preference for solitude.

This incident forced her to confront the limits of that approach.

Neri genuinely likes being alone.

Jane understands that.

But Jane also now understands that Neri can hide a life-threatening medical event inside that solitude.

Jane's assessment:

> **"Being alone is fine."**

Followed by:

> **"Suffering alone for five hours isn't."**

---

# IX. Communication Failure

This episode demonstrates why Neri's limited communication cannot be addressed simply by providing her with a notebook or writing tablet.

She already has access to writing.

She simply does not use it when she believes communicating would inconvenience someone.

Alice's previous aphonia was substantially different.

Alice compensated for losing her voice by writing frequently.

Neri does not.

The communication system therefore exists.

**Neri is choosing not to activate it.**

This is a behavioral welfare problem rather than a technological one.

---

# X. Current Risk Assessment

### Medical Risk

**CRITICAL**

Neri's anemia can progress while she remains stationary.

### Communication Risk

**CRITICAL**

Neri may experience severe symptoms without communicating them.

### Isolation Risk

**HIGH**

Neri's genuine comfort with solitude makes prolonged unnoticed deterioration easier.

### Intentional Self-Harm Assessment

**No evidence established by this incident.**

Neri's behavior is currently better explained by avoidance of perceived burden and her established expendability beliefs.

The danger is nevertheless real.

---

# XI. Required Behavioral Understanding

Personnel must understand that Neri's statement:

> **"I didn't want to bother anyone."**

is not reassurance.

It is a warning sign.

The phrase should be treated as evidence that Neri may have overridden her own medical needs in order to avoid interrupting someone else.

Personnel should not respond with anger.

Statements such as:

> "Why didn't you tell us?!"

may reinforce the exact behavior responsible for the incident.

Instead:

> "I'm glad you're here."

> "You can interrupt me."

> "You are not bothering me."

> "You don't have to wait."

> "If you're hurting, I want to know."

---

# XII. Solitude Remains Permitted

C.O.R.E. explicitly does **not** recommend removing Neri's ability to be alone.

Neri genuinely enjoys solitude.

She should not be punished for preferring it.

The objective is not to surround her constantly.

The objective is to ensure that **being alone does not become equivalent to being medically unobserved.**

Quiet check-ins, passive observation, and accessible assistance are preferred over forced socialization.

---

# XIII. Post-Episode Observation

After stabilization, Neri returned to a quiet resting area.

She did not speak.

She did not write further.

Bao remained nearby.

Neri eventually closed her eyes.

After several minutes, Bao asked:

> **"Do you want me to stay?"**

Neri looked at her.

She nodded.

Bao stayed.

No further conversation occurred.

---

# XIV. Final Assessment

This incident is considered more serious than the previous anemic episodes not solely because of the physical pain involved, but because of its duration.

Neri suffered for approximately five hours.

She had opportunities to seek assistance.

She chose not to.

Her reasoning was simple:

> **She did not want to bother anyone.**

That belief is now presenting a direct medical hazard.

Neri's preference for solitude must be respected.

Her preference for suffering unnoticed must not be.
