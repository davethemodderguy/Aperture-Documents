# SAFEHOUSE PSYCHOLOGICAL RECORD

## SUBJECT 002 — THE NAME, THE CAKE, AND THE MEMORY

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL — EXTREMELY SENSITIVE  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Registered Name:** **002**  
**Common Name:** **Neri**  
**Origin of Name:** Given by younger sister  
**Status:** Permanent Resident  
**Primary Psychological Concern:** Chronic bereavement / childhood trauma

---

## 1. Correction to Previous Understanding

Neri does **not** have an inherent preference for sweets.

She never did.

The cake, candy, pastries, and other sweets associated with her are not originally _hers_.

They belonged to someone else.

Her younger sister.

Her sister was the one who loved sweet things.

Cake was something her sister liked.

Candy was something her sister liked.

When Neri eats cake now, she is not necessarily satisfying a craving.

She is remembering.

---

# 2. "Neri" Was Never Her Given Name

The name **Neri** was given to her by her younger sister.

It was a nickname.

It was something her sister called her.

Her parents did not give her a conventional name.

They designated her:

> **002**

That was what she was called.

Not because she was the second-born.

Not because it was a family tradition.

Because, to her parents, their children were effectively **assets with assigned numbers**.

Their value determined whether they were worth keeping.

---

# 3. The Family's Rule

Neri's childhood environment operated under a particularly brutal principle:

> **A child who provides no value is disposable.**

Children who were useful were kept.

Children who were considered useful could receive attention.

Children who were considered worthless could be abandoned, discarded, or otherwise disposed of.

There was no meaningful concept of unconditional parental love.

There was only usefulness.

Neri learned this very young.

Her sister learned it too.

---

# 4. 002

Neri was not expected to be loved.

She was expected to be useful.

Her intelligence, particularly her aptitude for chemistry, should have provided value.

Instead, her family dismissed it.

They saw little reason to invest in her.

The number remained:

> **002**

Her sister was different.

She was loving.

She was affectionate.

She liked sweets.

She loved Neri.

But she could not provide the kind of value their parents demanded.

And that made her disposable.

---

# 5. The Last Memory

Neri remembers her sister being killed.

She remembers seeing the weapon.

She remembers the blood.

She remembers being there.

She remembers being unable to stop it.

Her sister was disposed of because, in the eyes of the people responsible, she was not worth keeping.

Neri witnessed it.

There was no rescue.

No intervention.

No adult arriving in time.

No one telling them that their lives mattered.

There was only Neri and her sister.

And then there was only Neri.

---

# 6. The Name That Remained

Afterward, the name **Neri** remained.

Not because her parents called her that.

They did not.

It remained because her sister had called her that.

Eventually, "002" became something Neri associated with the person her parents believed she was.

"Neri" became the name associated with the person her sister loved.

That distinction is important.

When SAFEHOUSE personnel call her Neri, they are using the name given to her by the person who loved her without requiring her to prove her worth.

---

# 7. The Cafeteria

This is why Neri's cafeteria routine should not be interpreted as a simple preference for sweets.

She sits alone.

She gets cake.

She eats slowly.

Sometimes she stares at the cake for a long time before taking a bite.

She is remembering her sister.

The fact that Neri genuinely enjoys some sweets now does not change their origin.

The ritual came first.

The taste came afterward.

---

# 8. The Unspoken Conversation

Neri sometimes looks at the cake and quietly says:

> **“You used to like these.”**

Not:

> “I like these.”

Not:

> “This makes me feel better.”

Just:

> **“You used to like these.”**

There is no expectation that anyone answer.

She knows nobody can.

She is speaking to someone who has been gone for years.

---

# 9. Why Nobody Interrupts

The cafeteria tradition therefore remains.

Neri sits alone.

She eats cake.

Nobody bothers her.

The staff understand now.

This is not necessarily a crisis.

It is not necessarily a symptom that needs to be interrupted.

Sometimes Neri is simply remembering her sister.

She is allowed to remember.

She is allowed to miss her.

She is allowed to sit in silence.

And she is allowed to eat something that her sister loved.

---

# 10. The Tragedy of the Name

There is an uncomfortable irony in the fact that the person who gave Neri her name was also the person Neri could not save.

Her parents called her **002**.

Her sister called her **Neri**.

Her parents saw a number.

Her sister saw her.

That distinction may be one of the most important pieces of Neri's surviving identity.

---

# 11. Psychological Note

Neri's grief should not be framed as something that she needs to "get over."

Her sister is dead.

That fact cannot be repaired.

What SAFEHOUSE can do is give Neri something her childhood never gave her:

A life in which her value does not have to be demonstrated.

She does not have to be useful to stay.

She does not have to earn food.

She does not have to justify having friends.

She does not have to prove that she deserves to exist.

She is not **002** here.

She is Neri.

And someone chose that name because they loved her.

---

## 12. Closing Observation

At 22:17, Neri was observed sitting alone in the cafeteria.

A slice of cake sat in front of her.

She took a small bite.

She looked at the empty seat across the table.

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then:

> **“You would've liked this one.”**

She smiled faintly.

Not because the cake was particularly good.

Because she could remember her sister liking it.

Neri continued eating.

The cafeteria remained quiet.

No one interrupted.

No one needed to.

She wasn't asking to be saved.

She was remembering someone.

And for tonight, that was enough.
