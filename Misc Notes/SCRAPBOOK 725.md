# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL CARE DIRECTIVE**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — NEVER ALONE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **PROTECTED / CONTINUOUS COMPANIONSHIP**

---

# **DIRECTIVE**

Effective immediately, Alice Lockwood is to have continuous companionship.

Under **no circumstances** is Alice to be intentionally left alone for extended periods.

This is not a punishment.

It is not containment.

It is not surveillance.

It is a care requirement.

Alice has experienced far more than a child her age should ever have had to endure.

The SAFEHOUSE's response is simple:

> **She should not have to endure anything else alone.**

---

# **CONTINUOUS COMPANY**

At least one trusted SAFEHOUSE resident should remain with Alice whenever reasonably possible.

Preferred companions include:

- Corin Lockwood
    
- Orphie
    
- Lumi Lockwood
    
- Belle Merryweather
    
- Star Dawnbringer
    
- Neko
    
- Other residents approved by SAFEHOUSE personnel
    

Companionship does not require constant conversation.

Alice may:

- Sleep
    
- Read
    
- Draw
    
- Ride her scooter
    
- Sit outside
    
- Visit the Treehouse
    
- Eat Corin's soup
    
- Play with Bunny
    
- Simply sit quietly
    

Someone should simply be there.

---

# **IMPORTANT DISTINCTION**

Alice is **not** to be treated as incapable of being independent.

She is allowed personal space.

She is allowed privacy.

She is allowed to say:

> **"Alice wants alone."**

However, "alone" in this context means quiet personal time—not abandonment.

A trusted person should remain nearby and available.

Alice must always know:

> **Someone is there.**

---

# **WHY**

Alice has already experienced:

Loss.

Separation.

Hunger.

Cold.

Injury.

Fear.

Confusion.

Memory loss.

Isolation.

She has spent months believing that disappearing would make things easier for everyone around her.

She has repeatedly tried to run despite her body being incapable of safely carrying her away.

She is five years old.

That is enough.

The SAFEHOUSE will not allow loneliness to become another wound.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin is particularly attentive to the directive.

She understands Alice's silence better than most.

Sometimes Alice doesn't want to talk.

Corin doesn't force her.

She simply sits nearby.

Alice might lean against her.

Sometimes Alice falls asleep.

Corin stays.

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi has returned to an old habit.

Drawing with Alice.

Alice is still terrible at it.

Lumi doesn't care.

> **"She doesn't have a natural for arts."**

She laughs whenever she says it.

Sometimes she gets teary afterward.

But she keeps drawing with her.

Because Alice doesn't need to be good at drawing.

She just needs someone willing to sit beside her and draw badly together.

---

# **STAR**

Star frequently keeps Alice company during the quieter hours.

She talks to her.

Teaches her words.

Answers her strange questions.

Sometimes Alice asks the same question several times.

Star answers every time.

No irritation.

No impatience.

Just:

> **"Yeah, Alice. I'm here."**

---

# **BELLE**

Belle has become one of Alice's favorite companions.

She carries Alice when the scooter isn't practical.

She fixes her hat.

She makes sure Alice is comfortable.

And she has absolutely no problem letting Alice sleep against her.

Belle's explanation:

> **"She's little."**

Then, after a pause:

> **"Little sisters need people."**

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie treats the directive differently.

She doesn't constantly hover.

She simply makes sure Alice can always find her.

If Alice looks around the room and asks:

> **"Where Orphie?"**

The answer is always nearby.

> **"Here."**

Alice relaxes.

That's enough.

---

# **THE TREEHOUSE**

The Treehouse remains one of Alice's safest places.

She may visit it.

She may sleep there.

She may sit outside beneath the snow.

But she is no longer expected to remain there completely alone.

If Alice goes to the Treehouse, someone accompanies her.

If she wants to sit outside, someone sits nearby.

If she falls asleep, someone watches over her.

The treehouse is no longer a place where Alice disappears.

It is a place where Alice can rest.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

When the directive was explained to Alice, she didn't fully understand it.

She asked:

> **"Why people stay?"**

Corin answered:

> **"Because we love you."**

Alice became quiet.

> **"Alice trouble?"**

Corin immediately shook her head.

> **"No."**

> **"Then why?"**

Corin hugged her.

> **"Because you're Alice."**

Alice thought about that.

Then quietly hugged back.

> **"...Okay."**

---

# **FINAL DIRECTIVE**

Alice is not a burden.

Alice is not a problem to be solved.

Alice is not required to become stronger before she deserves companionship.

She does not have to earn affection by recovering.

She does not have to smile.

She does not have to speak correctly.

She does not have to eat perfectly.

She does not have to walk.

She does not have to ride her scooter.

She does not have to pretend she's okay.

She is five years old.

And she has already seen enough.

Therefore:

> **Keep Alice company.**

> **Give her warmth.**

> **Give her patience.**

> **Give her love.**

And most importantly—

> **Don't leave her alone with everything she's carrying.**

**End of CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL CARE DIRECTIVE**
