# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Long-term observation of Neri has identified five recurring subjects around which a substantial portion of her emotional life appears to revolve.

These are:

> **I. Cake**  
> **II. Her Friends**  
> **III. Her Mom and Big Sister — Bao and Frost**  
> **IV. Her Little Sister — Deceased**  
> **V. Hopelessness and Helplessness**

At first glance, the five subjects appear unrelated.

They are not.

They form a fairly consistent representation of Neri's internal world.

Four represent things she desperately wants to **have or preserve**.

The fifth represents the state she is desperately trying to **never experience again.**

---

# 2. FIXATION I — CAKE

Cake is one of Neri's strongest and most consistent positive interests.

She enjoys cake regardless of emotional state.

When happy:

> She eats cake because she likes cake.

When sad:

> Cake can provide comfort.

When celebrating:

> Cake is immediately requested.

When someone asks what dessert she wants:

> Cake is frequently the first answer.

It should be emphasized that cake does **not** automatically cause Neri to discuss her past.

Most of the time, she simply eats it.

---

## 2.1 THE EMOTIONAL ASSOCIATION

There is a suspected association between sweets and memories of Neri's deceased younger sister.

Her sister reportedly loved sweets.

Neri has repeatedly mentioned her sister while eating desserts during periods of significant sadness.

However:

> **THE ASSOCIATION HAS NOT BEEN CONFIRMED BY NERI.**

Personnel must not assume that every piece of cake is secretly connected to her trauma.

Sometimes:

> **Cake is just cake.**

---

## 2.2 MEDICAL OBSERVATION

Neri's dental health remains excellent despite her unusually high sweet consumption.

She brushes regularly, maintains proper oral hygiene, and balances her diet with vegetables, meat, fish, and other normal foods.

The Dentistry Division has therefore officially classified:

> **"Neri eats an unreasonable amount of cake"**

as a behavioral observation rather than a medical emergency.

---

# 3. FIXATION II — HER FRIENDS

Neri places extraordinary emotional importance on her friends.

Once someone becomes part of her trusted circle, she becomes strongly protective of them.

Her behavioral pattern can be summarized as:

> **Friend is safe → Neri is calm.**

> **Friend is threatened → Neri intervenes.**

> **Friend is injured → Neri becomes extremely aggressive.**

> **Friend is killed → severe psychological and behavioral destabilization risk.**

This behavior is strongly associated with her previous experiences of loss.

Neri does not merely enjoy having friends.

She appears to view their continued existence as something she must actively protect.

---

# 4. THE PROTECTIVE RESPONSE

Neri has demonstrated a willingness to place herself in danger when protecting people she cares about.

This creates a significant medical concern.

Her body is already physiologically unstable.

Her cardiovascular system can fail when her blood degrades.

Her psychological response to a threatened friend may nevertheless cause her to push herself beyond safe physical limits.

This creates the following potential sequence:

> **Friend threatened**  
> ↓  
> **Neri experiences fear**  
> ↓  
> **Neri attempts immediate protection**  
> ↓  
> **Physical overexertion**  
> ↓  
> **Cardiovascular strain**  
> ↓  
> **Potential anemia/collapse**

Neri may therefore endanger herself precisely because she is afraid of losing someone else.

---

# 5. FIXATION III — BAO AND FROST

Neri's relationship with **Bao** and **Frost** is particularly significant.

Bao is her mother.

Frost is her big sister.

These relationships provide Neri with something she previously lacked:

> **A family that stays.**

Neri's attachment to Bao is especially strong.

She frequently refers to her as:

> **"Mom."**

There is little hesitation in the terminology.

Bao is not merely a trusted adult to Neri.

She is family.

---

## 5.1 FROST

Neri refers to Frost as:

> **"Big sis."**

The relationship provides Neri with a sibling connection that exists in the present rather than exclusively in memory.

This distinction appears important.

Neri lost her original younger sister.

She now has people in her life whom she is afraid to lose.

Frost therefore occupies an especially meaningful position within Neri's current family structure.

---

# 6. FIXATION IV — HER LITTLE SISTER

This is the most painful fixation.

Neri's younger sister is deceased.

She remembers her vividly.

She remembers things she liked.

She remembers her personality.

She remembers sweets.

Most importantly, she remembers being unable to save her.

The memory has never fully left Neri.

It is not something she brings up constantly.

She does not spend every moment talking about her.

But the memory remains readily accessible beneath the surface.

A seemingly ordinary moment can bring it back.

---

# 7. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN REMEMBERING AND BEING STUCK

Neri is capable of enjoying the present.

This is important.

She can genuinely be happy.

She can laugh.

She can play.

She can enjoy food.

She can make friends.

She can spend time with Bao and Frost.

She can be annoyed when somebody calls her cute.

She can live like a child.

None of this means she has forgotten.

Her past exists alongside her present.

The objective of care is therefore **not to remove her memories.**

It is to ensure those memories do not control her entire life.

---

# 8. FIXATION V — HOPELESSNESS & HELPLESSNESS

This is the most concerning of the five.

Neri appears unusually sensitive to situations where she believes there is:

> **No way out.**

> **No one coming.**

> **Nothing she can do.**

> **Someone she loves is going to disappear.**

These experiences resemble the emotional conditions surrounding much of her childhood.

She has developed a strong aversion to feeling powerless.

This is likely one reason she reacts so strongly when someone she cares about is threatened.

---

# 9. THE CENTRAL FEAR

Neri's deepest fear may not simply be death.

It may be:

> **Being unable to stop it.**

Her previous experience taught her that terrible things can happen while she is standing there unable to intervene.

She could not save her sister.

She could not prevent what happened to herself.

She could not protect the friends she later lost.

This created an internal rule:

> **"If I can do something, I have to do something."**

Unfortunately, Neri's body does not always allow her to do so safely.

---

# 10. THE FIVE FIXATIONS CONNECT

The five subjects can be represented as follows:

|Fixation|What it represents|
|---|---|
|**Cake**|Comfort, pleasure, normal childhood experiences|
|**Friends**|Connection and people she can protect|
|**Bao & Frost**|Present-day family and stability|
|**Little sister**|Love, grief, memory, unresolved loss|
|**Hopelessness / helplessness**|The state she fears returning to|

The resulting psychological cycle is approximately:

> **She loves people.**

> ↓

> **She becomes afraid of losing them.**

> ↓

> **She remembers losing someone before.**

> ↓

> **She remembers being unable to stop it.**

> ↓

> **She becomes determined to never be helpless again.**

> ↓

> **She protects her friends aggressively.**

> ↓

> **She risks herself doing so.**

And then:

> **Someone gives her cake.**

Sometimes, that's enough to bring her back to the present.

---

# 11. CARE GUIDANCE

Personnel should not attempt to remove any of these five elements from Neri's life.

Instead, staff should help her develop healthier relationships with them.

### CAKE

Let her enjoy it.

Maintain dietary balance.

Do not use it as leverage.

### FRIENDS

Encourage healthy attachment.

Remind her that protecting someone does not require sacrificing herself.

### BAO & FROST

Allow the family relationship to develop naturally.

Do not interfere unnecessarily.

### HER LITTLE SISTER

Allow Neri to remember her.

Do not force her to discuss her.

Do not tell her to "move on."

### HOPELESSNESS / HELPLESSNESS

This is the primary area requiring support.

When Neri feels powerless, remind her:

> **She does not have to solve everything alone.**

There are people who will help her now.

---

# 12. THE MOST IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE

Neri's childhood taught her:

> **"Nobody is coming."**

SAFEHOUSE life needs to teach her:

> **"Someone will come."**

Not once.

Not occasionally.

Repeatedly.

Until eventually she believes it.

---

# 13. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Neri is not defined by her trauma.

She is not defined by her fixation on sweets.

She is not defined by her violent protective behavior.

She is not defined by her unusual biology.

She is a child who has experienced an extraordinary amount of loss and is attempting to build a life around the people and things she still has.

Her five fixations reflect that struggle.

**Cake gives her comfort.**

**Friends give her connection.**

**Bao and Frost give her family.**

**Her little sister gives her a memory of someone she loved.**

**Hopelessness reminds her of what she never wants to experience again.**

The medical team's recommendation is therefore simple:

> **Give her reasons to hope.**

> **Give her people who stay.**

> **Give her the freedom to remember.**

> **And, when appropriate, give her cake.**
