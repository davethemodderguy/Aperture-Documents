## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **PSYCHOLOGICAL / MISSING PERSON ANALYSIS**

# **"SOMETHING LIKE ME"**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Status:** **MISSING — NO TRACE**  
**Source:** Recovered Personal Writings  
**Assessment:** **SEVERE SELF-DEVALUATION**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Among the writings recovered from Corin's notebook is a statement that has received particular attention from the Architects.

The entry reads:

> **"All of you don't deserve something like me."**

> **"Guiding Light, I'm sorry, I wasted the chance you gave me."**

At first glance, the statement appears consistent with Corin's established feelings of guilt, worthlessness, and perceived burden.

However, the wording contains a more concerning detail.

Corin did not refer to herself as **someone**.

She referred to herself as **something**.

---

# **LINGUISTIC OBSERVATION**

The distinction is significant.

Corin has previously written about herself as:

- A burden.
    
- Something broken.
    
- Something that requires maintenance.
    
- Something that should not have survived.
    
- Something that causes problems.
    

The latest entry continues this pattern.

> **"Something like me."**

The phrasing does not merely express self-hatred.

It suggests a gradual separation between Corin's understanding of herself and the concept of being a person.

She is not simply saying:

> _"You don't deserve me."_

She is saying:

> _"You don't deserve something like me."_

The implication is that she increasingly views herself as an **object, condition, or problem** rather than an individual worthy of care.

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT**

The second sentence is equally significant.

> **"Guiding Light, I'm sorry, I wasted the chance you gave me."**

Guiding Light immediately recognized the reference.

She had previously given Corin another chance at life.

Not by restoring her body.

Not by undoing her death.

But by helping her continue living as the person she had become.

Corin apparently interprets that opportunity as something she has now failed.

Guiding Light strongly disagrees.

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT'S RESPONSE**

Upon reading the entry, Guiding Light remained silent for several minutes.

Curious Light eventually asked:

> **"What are you thinking?"**

Guiding Light answered:

> **"She thinks I gave her a chance to become someone else."**

> **"But I didn't."**

She looked at the notebook.

> **"I gave her a chance to be herself."**

---

# **THE PROBLEM**

Corin's writing suggests that she has begun evaluating her existence according to what she provides to others.

She sees herself as requiring:

- Medical attention.
    
- Stitching.
    
- Food.
    
- Supplies.
    
- Emotional support.
    
- Constant reassurance.
    
- Orphie's presence.
    
- The Architects' intervention.
    

She appears to interpret these needs as evidence that she is inherently defective.

The resulting reasoning becomes circular:

> **I need help.**

Therefore:

> **I am a burden.**

Therefore:

> **I don't deserve help.**

Therefore:

> **Anyone helping me is wasting their time.**

Therefore:

> **I have wasted the chance they gave me.**

This is not an objective assessment.

It is the internal logic of someone experiencing severe self-worthlessness.

---

# **ORPHIE'S RESPONSE**

Orphie was particularly affected by the wording.

She pointed to the sentence.

> **"Something."**

She then shook her head.

She wrote beneath it:

> **"You're not something."**

Then:

> **"You're Corin."**

She stopped.

After a moment, she added:

> **"My sister."**

---

# **CURIOUS LIGHT**

Curious Light initially approached the statement analytically.

Then he crossed out his own notes.

He eventually wrote:

> **"The most concerning part isn't that she hates herself."**

> **"It's that she appears to have stopped categorizing herself as a person."**

He underlined the final sentence.

---

# **MISCHIEVIOUS LIGHT**

Mischevious Light read the entry once.

Then again.

He said nothing for a long time.

Eventually:

> **"That's not an apology."**

Guiding Light looked at him.

> **"It's a goodbye."**

Neither of them wanted to consider the possibility.

---

# **THE ARCHITECTS' CONCERN**

The three Architects have encountered self-destructive behavior before.

This is different.

Corin's writing is not merely expressing:

> _"I hate myself."_

It is approaching:

> _"I am not worth treating as a person."_

That distinction is considered significantly more concerning.

The disappearance occurring shortly after these writings makes the wording particularly difficult to dismiss.

---

# **CORIN'S PREVIOUS WRITING**

Several earlier statements show a gradual progression.

> **"I'm already broken."**

> **"I can't even talk anymore."**

> **"Everyone has to take care of me."**

> **"What am I giving back?"**

> **"Orphie deserves someone better."**

And finally:

> **"All of you don't deserve something like me."**

The progression suggests that Corin's self-image had deteriorated over time.

---

# **WHAT GUIDING LIGHT WANTS HER TO KNOW**

If Corin's message was intended for Guiding Light, there is one part Guiding Light wants to correct.

Corin did not waste her chance.

She did not fail the opportunity she was given.

She survived.

She learned to live with her body.

She learned to see color again.

She cared for Jay.

She cared for Orphie.

She continued writing.

She continued trying.

None of those things were wasted.

Guiding Light did not give Corin a chance to become perfect.

She gave her a chance to continue existing.

---

# **FINAL ASSESSMENT**

The wording **"something like me"** is considered a significant psychological marker.

Corin is increasingly distancing herself from her own personhood.

This does not establish her intentions at the time of disappearance.

It does, however, demonstrate the severity of the self-worthlessness present immediately beforehand.

The investigation remains active.

The Architects continue searching.

And Guiding Light refuses to accept Corin's own description of herself as an objective truth.

Because whatever Corin currently believes she is—

**She is not a thing.**

**She is not a burden.**

**She is not a failed second chance.**

She is Corin.

And somewhere, hopefully, she is still listening.

**End of Psychological / Missing Person Analysis**
