### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / MEMORIAL ARCHIVE**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — THE PHOTOGRAPH THAT NEVER EXISTED**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — MEMORIAL RECORD  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **DECEASED / EXISTENTIAL VOIDING**

---

# **MEMORIAL NOTE**

There are photographs of almost everyone in the Blacksite.

Personnel photographs.

Security photographs.

Research photographs.

Group photographs.

Birthday photographs.

Photographs taken for documentation.

Photographs taken because someone happened to have a camera nearby.

There are thousands.

Perhaps millions.

There is not one photograph of Alice.

Not a single one.

---

# **THE REALIZATION**

Nobody noticed while she was here.

There was always tomorrow.

Tomorrow, someone would take one.

Tomorrow, Alice would be healthier.

Tomorrow, she would be outside again.

Tomorrow, she'd ride her scooter.

Tomorrow, she'd sit with Belle.

Tomorrow, she'd smile for a picture.

There was always another day.

Until there wasn't.

---

# **ALICE NEVER HAD A BIRTHDAY**

Alice didn't reliably remember her birthday.

So Corin chose one.

**November 1st.**

The day she had originally been found.

It wasn't her biological birthday.

Nobody knew what that was.

But it became Alice's birthday.

Her first real birthday was supposed to be celebrated.

There would be food.

There would be decorations.

There would be everyone she loved.

There would be a cake.

There would be a little birthday hat.

There would be photographs.

At least—

everyone assumed there would be.

Alice never experienced it.

She disappeared before she ever got the chance.

---

# **THE EMPTY PHOTOGRAPH**

After Alice's disappearance, someone asked a simple question:

> "Does anyone have a picture of her?"

Silence.

Someone checked the SAFEHOUSE records.

Nothing.

The security department checked archived footage.

There was surveillance footage of her.

There were blurry camera captures of her scooter.

There were distant recordings of her passing through corridors.

But no photograph.

Not one where someone had deliberately stopped and said:

> **"Alice, smile."**

---

# **CORIN**

Corin took the longest to accept it.

She searched everything.

Old personal devices.

SAFEHOUSE archives.

Security records.

Medical records.

Anything that might contain even a single photograph.

Nothing.

Eventually, she found Alice's hat.

She held it.

And cried.

> "I should've taken one."

Nobody told her it wasn't her fault.

Because Corin already knew.

It wasn't anyone's fault.

That's what made it hurt.

There had been no reason to think time would run out.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle looked through her own belongings.

She found hundreds of photographs.

Pictures with Endmin.

Pictures with friends.

Pictures from ordinary days.

Pictures where nothing important was happening.

She found none of Alice.

Belle began crying when she realized there were photographs of days she barely remembered—

but none of the little bunny she would never forget.

She whispered:

> "I thought we'd have more time."

---

# **NEKO**

Neko checked the scooter.

The tracker had records.

Movement data.

Battery statistics.

Maintenance history.

Everything except what he actually wanted.

A picture of Alice riding it.

He had built the scooter specifically for her.

He had optimized it.

Secured it.

Tracked it.

Maintained it.

But he never thought to take a photograph of the person it was built for.

---

# **STAR**

Star eventually found one of her old stickers.

The same kind she used to give Alice.

She held it beside an empty space on a sheet of paper.

Then she drew Alice herself.

White hair.

Bunny ears.

Little smile.

She stared at the drawing for a long time.

Then wrote beneath it:

> **"This isn't a photo."**

She cried.

> "I wish it was."

---

# **THE STAFF**

The regret spread throughout the Blacksite.

Not because anyone had neglected Alice.

Quite the opposite.

People had cared about her.

They had talked to her.

Fed her.

Carried her.

Built things for her.

Played with her.

Watched her ride around the Blacksite.

They had loved having her around.

But nobody had thought:

> **"We should take a picture."**

Because Alice was supposed to still be here.

---

# **THE BIRTHDAY THAT NEVER HAPPENED**

The most painful part was November 1st.

The date remained on the calendar.

Someone had already written:

> **ALICE'S BIRTHDAY**

Nobody erased it.

The decorations were never put up.

The cake was never ordered.

The candles were never lit.

Alice never sat at a table surrounded by everyone who loved her.

She never opened a present.

She never heard everyone sing for her.

She never had someone take a terrible, slightly blurry photograph of her blowing out a candle.

She never got to complain about being embarrassed.

She never got to forget how old she was.

She never got to turn six.

---

# **THE MEMORIAL**

Eventually, someone printed a photograph of the Treehouse.

It was empty.

Alice's scooter sat outside.

Her hat was inside.

Her notebook rested on the desk.

The photograph was placed on the memorial wall.

It was the closest thing the Blacksite had to a photograph of Alice.

Underneath it, someone wrote:

> **ALICE LOCKWOOD**  
> **November 1 — [YEAR UNKNOWN]**  
> **Remembered by everyone who knew her.**

---

# **A FINAL REGRET**

The Blacksite has countless photographs of ordinary moments.

People eating.

People laughing.

People working.

People sleeping.

People doing absolutely nothing.

Nobody thought those moments would become important.

Until Alice disappeared.

Now every ordinary photograph feels like something precious.

Because Alice should have been in one.

Sitting on someone's lap.

Holding a carrot.

Riding her scooter.

Drawing in her notebook.

Sleeping beside Corin.

Standing beside Belle.

Looking at Star's wings.

Doing absolutely nothing.

Just being Alice.

---

# **CORIN'S NOTE**

A small handwritten note was found beneath the empty photograph.

It reads:

> **"I wish I had taken one."**

Below it, after several lines of empty space:

> **"Not because I need proof that she existed."**
> 
> **"I know she did."**
> 
> **"I just wanted one picture of my little sister."**

---

# **FINAL ARCHIVE STATEMENT**

Alice never got her birthday.

She never got her photograph.

She never got to grow older.

She never got to fill another journal.

She never got to ride her scooter until she was tired and ask someone for a piggyback ride home.

She never got to see how many people would miss her.

But there is one thing Alice was wrong about.

She wrote:

> **"No one loves me."**

The Blacksite has spent every day since proving that sentence wrong.

There is no photograph of Alice.

But there are memories.

There are drawings.

There are journals.

There is a scooter.

There is a hat.

There are hundreds of people who still remember a little white bunny riding through the corridors.

And there are people who cry because they wish they had taken one picture.

Not because Alice needed a photograph to prove she mattered.

Because **she mattered so much that everyone wishes they had one more piece of her.**

**Alice was here.**

**Alice was loved.**

**Alice will be remembered.**

**End of Memorial Archive.**
