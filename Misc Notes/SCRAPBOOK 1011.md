**SUBJECT:** Neri — Minor Positive Affect Following Shared Meal  
**RELATED RECORDS:** C.O.R.E.-WEL-4C-NERI-106

---

# Executive Summary

Neri smiled.

The expression was brief.

It was small.

It was not a full smile.

It was not accompanied by laughter, excitement, or a significant change in demeanor.

It was, however, unmistakably a smile.

The event occurred when Jane Doe shared part of her cheeseburger with Neri.

No attempt was made to make Neri smile.

No joke was told.

No encouragement was given.

Jane simply offered her food.

Neri accepted.

For a brief moment, the corners of Neri's mouth lifted slightly.

Jane noticed.

She did not comment.

This response is considered important.

---

# I. Incident Details

Jane Doe was eating a cheeseburger in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria.

Neri was nearby, quietly drinking coffee.

Jane looked at her meal.

Then at Neri.

Jane separated part of the cheeseburger and held it toward her.

Neri looked at the food.

She looked at Jane.

Jane did not say anything.

Neri accepted it.

She took a bite.

There was a short pause.

Neri's expression changed.

Only slightly.

The corners of her mouth lifted.

The expression lasted approximately several seconds before returning to neutral.

Jane did not point it out.

She simply continued eating.

Neri continued eating as well.

---

# II. Expression Assessment

C.O.R.E. emphasizes the following:

**This was a slight smile.**

It was not:

- A broad grin.
    
- Laughter.
    
- A sudden return to previous behavior.
    
- A dramatic emotional breakthrough.
    
- Evidence that Neri has "recovered."
    
- Evidence that her previous emotional state has disappeared.
    

It was simply a small, spontaneous positive expression.

That distinction is important.

The expression should not be exaggerated in subsequent reports.

---

# III. Jane Doe's Response

Jane noticed the smile.

She deliberately did not react.

This was significant.

Given Jane's established tendency to tease SAFEHOUSE residents, she could easily have said:

> "There it is!"

She did not.

She did not point at Neri.

She did not say:

> "I made you smile."

She did not attempt to recreate the reaction.

She simply continued eating.

This prevented the moment from becoming a performance.

---

# IV. Neri's Response

Neri remained silent.

She did not write anything.

She did not gesture.

She did not attempt to explain the expression.

She simply ate the food Jane had given her.

After finishing the piece, Neri gave Jane a small nod.

Jane returned the nod.

No further communication occurred.

---

# V. Behavioral Significance

The significance of this event is not that Neri smiled.

The significance is **why** she smiled.

Nobody asked her to.

Nobody attempted to cheer her up.

Nobody told her that she looked sad.

Nobody attempted to force a reaction.

A person she trusted shared something with her.

Neri enjoyed the gesture.

Her expression reflected that enjoyment naturally.

This is consistent with C.O.R.E.'s previous recommendation:

> **Allow positive affect to occur rather than attempting to manufacture it.**

---

# VI. Interaction Context

The event also demonstrates that Neri's lack of smiling does not indicate an inability to experience positive emotion.

Neri can still enjoy things.

She simply does not consistently express that enjoyment outwardly.

In this case, the cheeseburger was apparently enough to produce a very small exception.

Jane's decision not to comment may have been equally important.

Neri was allowed to smile without being observed as though she had accomplished something.

---

# VII. Follow-Up Observation

Approximately ten minutes later, Jane offered Neri another piece.

Neri looked at it.

Then at Jane.

She accepted it.

No additional smile occurred.

Jane did not attempt to produce one.

This is considered the correct outcome.

The first smile was not a goal to reproduce.

It was simply something that happened.

---

# VIII. C.O.R.E. Assessment

The event should not result in a new expectation that Neri will become visibly happier.

Personnel are specifically instructed **not** to say:

> "See? You can smile!"

or:

> "You should smile more often."

or:

> "Jane made you smile!"

Such comments would risk turning a spontaneous moment into another expectation placed upon Neri.

Instead, the event should be allowed to remain what it was:

A small moment.

A cheeseburger.

A quiet gesture from Jane.

And a very slight smile.

---

# IX. Final Observation

Jane later finished the remainder of her cheeseburger.

Neri remained beside her.

Neri drank her coffee.

She still did not speak.

She still did not write.

Her expression returned to its usual neutral state.

But for a few seconds earlier that evening—

She smiled.

Just slightly.

And nobody asked her to do it again.

---

**RECOMMENDATION:** Do not attempt to reproduce or force the response.
