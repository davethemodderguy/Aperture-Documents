### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — EIGHT MONTHS OF WINTER**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 — Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Former designation:** Bunny  
**Age:** **Approximately 5 years old**  
**Adoptive Guardian / Big Sister:** Corin Lockwood  
**Status:** **SAFEHOUSE RESIDENT / MEDICALLY FRAGILE**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin and Alice spent part of the afternoon sitting outside the Treehouse.

The weather was cold.

Snow covered the surrounding area.

Alice sat quietly beside Corin.

Then she reached down.

Picked up some snow.

And ate it.

Corin immediately noticed.

> **"Alice..."**

Alice looked at her.

> **"Snow."**

> **"I know."**

Alice took another handful.

Corin sighed.

She understood why.

---

# **THE EIGHT-MONTH PERIOD**

Alice spent more than **eight months** surviving almost entirely on whatever she could find outdoors.

Her documented diet during that period consisted primarily of:

- Snow
    
- Grass
    
- Wild flowers
    

Occasionally, she managed to find other edible vegetation.

There was no reliable supply of proper food.

No stable shelter.

No adult caretaker.

No one consistently monitoring her health.

And Alice was barely five years old.

---

# **AGE**

C.O.R.E. has revised Alice's age estimate.

Alice is approximately:

# **5 YEARS OLD**

This makes her the **youngest known permanent resident in Blacksite records**, excluding **Star's unique chronological circumstances**.

The discovery has caused considerable concern among medical personnel.

A child of this age should not have been capable of surviving eight months under those conditions.

Alice did.

That does not mean she was unharmed.

---

# **MEDICAL CONSEQUENCES**

Alice's naturally fragile physiology made the situation significantly worse.

Her:

- Muscle development
    
- Bone strength
    
- Immune system
    
- Physical endurance
    

were already below normal expectations.

Eight months of inadequate nutrition and extreme environmental exposure placed additional stress on her developing body.

Medical personnel have identified multiple long-term concerns.

Alice's body survived.

But it did not thrive.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin sat beside Alice in the snow.

Alice continued eating small pieces of snow.

Corin eventually held out a hand.

> **"Come inside."**

Alice shook her head.

> **"Cold okay."**

Corin looked at her.

> **"You don't have to stay outside anymore."**

Alice paused.

She looked toward the Treehouse.

Then toward Corin.

> **"...Inside warm."**

> **"Very warm."**

Alice thought about it.

Then stood.

She immediately slipped slightly in the snow.

Corin caught her.

Alice giggled.

> **"Alice okay."**

Corin smiled.

> **"I know."**

---

# **A CHILD WHO LEARNED TO SURVIVE**

Alice's behavior has been shaped by her early experiences.

She does not naturally understand that food will continue to be available.

She does not naturally assume that warmth will remain.

She does not naturally expect someone to come looking for her.

When she sees snow, she knows it can be eaten.

When she sees grass, she knows it can be eaten.

When she sees flowers, she knows they can be eaten.

She learned survival before she learned childhood.

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE RESPONSE**

Alice now has unrestricted access to:

- Proper meals.
    
- Clean water.
    
- Medical care.
    
- Warm clothing.
    
- Heating.
    
- A permanent bed.
    
- Safe recreational spaces.
    
- The Treehouse.
    
- SAFEHOUSE common areas.
    

Personnel have also been instructed **not to shame Alice for eating snow or wild plants.**

The behavior is survival conditioning.

It will take time to replace.

---

# **CORIN'S APPROACH**

Corin does not simply tell Alice:

> **"Don't eat snow."**

Instead, she offers alternatives.

> **"Want some soup?"**

Alice looks at the bowl.

> **"...Warm."**

> **"Yep."**

Alice takes it.

After a few bites, she looks toward the snow outside.

> **"Snow later?"**

Corin smiles.

> **"Maybe."**

Alice nods.

Small steps.

---

# **THE YOUNGEST RESIDENT**

Alice's age has also changed how SAFEHOUSE residents interact with her.

She is no longer regarded simply as:

> **"The little white rabbit."**

She is a five-year-old child.

She needs supervision.

She needs education.

She needs play.

She needs someone to teach her things that other children normally learn naturally.

Belle has enthusiastically volunteered.

Her first lesson was apparently:

> **"Carrots are better than snow."**

Alice disagreed.

---

# **BELLE'S EXPERIMENT**

Belle placed a carrot beside a pile of fresh snow.

Alice stared at both.

She picked up the carrot.

Ate it.

Then ate some snow.

Belle looked offended.

> **"Alice!"**

Alice smiled.

> **"Both good."**

Belle sighed.

> **"...Fine."**

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin and Alice sat beneath the Treehouse.

Snow fell around them.

Alice picked up another handful.

> **"Alice..."**

> **"Snow."**

Corin sighed.

For more than eight months, this had been normal for Alice.

Snow was water.

Grass was food.

Flowers were food.

Cold was simply the environment.

Nobody had taught her that she deserved anything better.

Now she has a warm room.

A bed.

Food.

Water.

A family.

And Corin sitting beside her.

Alice is barely five years old.

She should never have needed to learn how to survive alone.

But she did.

Now SAFEHOUSE has a different responsibility.

Not merely keeping Alice alive.

**Teaching her that she is finally allowed to live like a child.**
