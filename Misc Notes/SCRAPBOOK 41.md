**Subject:** Director Belle Merryweather

# **ABILITY DESIGNATION**

**Designation:** **Oneiric Infiltration**

**Type:** Dream-Based Anomalous Cognition

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Director Belle Merryweather possesses a rare anomalous ability allowing her to enter the dreamscape of a sleeping individual.

Within the dream, Belle is able to observe, interact with, and question the dreamer in an environment shaped by the target's subconscious mind.

The ability has proven valuable during intelligence gathering because sleeping individuals often think and respond with fewer deliberate defenses than they would while awake.

Dream content remains symbolic and emotionally driven, requiring careful interpretation and corroboration through conventional investigative methods.

---

# **LIMITATIONS**

Oneiric Infiltration is **not** infallible.

The ability has one significant weakness.

If the dreamer personally knows Belle and immediately recognizes her as an intruder inside the dream, the subconscious may instinctively reject the anomaly.

When this occurs:

- The dream destabilizes.
    
- Belle's connection is interrupted.
    
- She is forcibly expelled from the target's dream.
    

For this reason, the technique is generally reserved for individuals with **no prior familiarity** with Director Belle.

---

# **OPERATIONAL PROCEDURE**

When intelligence must be gathered from a subject familiar with Belle, KCA personnel employ a modified protocol.

Senior Manager **Norma Lockwood** enters the operation first as the initial point of contact within the dream.

Because most targets have never encountered Norma in person, they rarely perceive her as an anomaly.

Norma's role is to:

- Assess the dream's stability.
    
- Establish dialogue.
    
- Determine whether the subject recognizes Belle.
    
- Signal whether Belle can safely proceed or whether the operation should be aborted.
    

This greatly reduces the likelihood of Belle being identified immediately upon entry.

---

# **CCD OBSERVATION**

Dream environments differ dramatically between individuals.

Researchers have documented recurring locations including:

- Childhood homes.
    
- Endless corridors.
    
- Empty cities.
    
- Forests.
    
- Oceans.
    
- Impossible landscapes inconsistent with physical reality.
    

Belle reports that no two dreams are ever exactly alike.
