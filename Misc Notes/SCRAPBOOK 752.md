## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — UNINTENTIONAL OVERSIGHT COLLECTION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — SAFEHOUSE PERSONNEL  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 5  
**Condition:** Fragile / Nonverbal / Easily Overlooked  
**Primary Concern:** **SUBJECT DOES NOT COMPLAIN**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice is not difficult to find because she hides.

She is difficult to find because she does not make herself noticeable.

She is five years old.

She is small.

She is quiet.

She cannot speak.

Her ears do not remain raised constantly, meaning she cannot reliably be identified by her silhouette in a crowd.

She does not shout.

She does not cry for help.

She does not bang on doors.

She does not complain when left behind.

Most of the time, she simply wanders.

Sometimes she follows people.

Sometimes she rides her scooter.

Sometimes she sits somewhere and draws.

Sometimes she simply watches everyone else work.

If Alice doesn't want to be noticed—

she is remarkably difficult to notice.

The following incidents demonstrate the problem.

---

# **INCIDENT 06 — THE SNOW**

Alice went outside after snowfall.

Several personnel were moving equipment through the exterior access area.

Alice was nearby.

Nobody noticed when the group returned inside.

Alice remained outside.

She sat in the snow.

She didn't knock.

She didn't wave.

She didn't attempt to force the door open.

She simply waited.

Approximately forty minutes later, Frost noticed a small shape through a window.

Alice was sitting underneath the edge of an awning.

Her hat was covered in snow.

Frost immediately went outside.

Alice looked up.

Frost asked:

> "How long have you been here?"

Alice shrugged.

---

# **INCIDENT 07 — THE RAIN**

This incident was considerably worse.

Alice had been walking outside during a particularly heavy rain.

Several people had seen her earlier.

Everyone assumed she was with someone.

She wasn't.

She eventually stopped beneath a tree.

The rain became heavier.

Alice remained there.

Her clothes became soaked.

Her white hair stuck to her face.

Her hat became completely saturated.

She couldn't call anyone.

She didn't try.

She simply sat.

A staff member eventually found her while returning from another assignment.

Alice looked up.

The staff member froze.

> "...Alice?"

Alice nodded.

She was shivering.

The staff member immediately carried her inside.

---

# **INCIDENT 08 — THE ELEVATOR**

Alice entered an elevator with several employees.

The group talked among themselves.

The elevator arrived.

Everyone left.

The doors closed.

Alice remained inside.

Nobody noticed.

She sat down in the corner.

The elevator continued its route.

Several minutes later, the doors opened again.

An employee entering the elevator found Alice sitting there.

Alice looked up.

No distress.

No anger.

Just:

> **"...?"**

The employee immediately contacted SAFEHOUSE personnel.

---

# **INCIDENT 09 — THE LOCKED ROOM**

Alice entered a storage room while following someone.

The person left.

The door automatically locked.

Alice remained inside.

She could not reach the emergency controls.

She couldn't call for help.

She couldn't shout.

So she sat down.

She eventually drew something in her notebook.

A picture of a door.

And a very small bunny sitting behind it.

She was discovered during the next scheduled inventory check.

---

# **INCIDENT 10 — THE CROWD**

Alice wandered through a crowded corridor.

She was wearing her usual hat.

Her ears were lowered.

Her scooter was not with her.

She walked between dozens of employees.

Nobody noticed her.

She stopped.

She looked around.

She didn't recognize where she was.

She simply continued walking.

It took nearly twenty minutes before anyone realized she had left the SAFEHOUSE.

The security cameras eventually located her.

She was sitting beside a vending machine.

Drawing.

---

# **INCIDENT 11 — THE CAFETERIA**

The cafeteria was crowded.

Alice sat at a table.

People came and went around her.

Someone sat beside her.

Someone else placed equipment on the table.

Someone took the chair she had been sitting in earlier.

Alice simply moved.

Eventually, everyone she had been near left.

Alice remained.

She didn't seem bothered.

She was drawing a carrot.

A staff member finally noticed her.

> "Alice?"

Alice looked up.

She pointed toward her drawing.

It was finished.

---

# **INCIDENT 12 — THE TREEHOUSE**

Alice returned to the Treehouse after riding her scooter.

She parked it.

Then wandered inside.

Everyone assumed she had gone to someone else's room.

Nobody checked.

Three hours later, Corin entered the Treehouse.

Alice was sitting on the floor.

She had fallen asleep.

Her journal was open beside her.

Corin stared at her.

> "...You've been here this whole time?"

Alice woke up.

She nodded sleepily.

---

# **INCIDENT 13 — THE DOOR CLOSING**

Alice followed Belle through a security door.

Belle entered.

Alice entered.

Belle continued walking.

The door closed behind them.

Alice stopped.

Belle continued for several seconds before realizing she couldn't hear Alice's footsteps.

She turned around.

Alice was standing on the other side of the door.

Belle immediately opened it.

> "Alice!"

Alice walked through.

Belle hugged her.

Alice's ears flattened.

She didn't complain.

---

# **INCIDENT 14 — THE SNOW AGAIN**

Alice went outside with Frost.

Frost became distracted helping with an environmental-control issue.

When she returned to the path, Alice wasn't there.

Frost panicked.

A search began.

Alice was eventually found behind a snow-covered structure.

She had stopped to watch falling snow.

She was sitting quietly.

Frost asked:

> "Why didn't you come when I called?"

Alice looked confused.

She hadn't heard.

Or perhaps she had.

She simply hadn't known she was supposed to answer.

---

# **INCIDENT 15 — THE WORKSHOP**

Neko was working.

Alice wandered into the workshop.

She sat on a nearby stool.

Neko became absorbed in his work.

Several hours passed.

He eventually turned around.

Alice was still there.

Neko stared.

> "...How long have you been sitting there?"

Alice held up five fingers.

Neko's expression changed immediately.

> "You should've told me."

Alice pointed toward her mouth.

Then shook her head.

Neko went quiet.

He understood.

---

# **INCIDENT 16 — THE EMPTY ROOM**

Alice was accidentally left in a conference room after a meeting.

The lights turned off automatically.

The door locked.

Alice sat on the floor.

She wasn't frightened enough to cry.

She simply waited.

When the room was reopened, she was still there.

Her hat had been removed.

She was using it as a pillow.

---

# **WHY THIS KEEPS HAPPENING**

The common factor is always the same.

Alice does not draw attention to herself.

She doesn't have a loud voice.

She doesn't make demands.

She doesn't interrupt conversations.

She doesn't complain when uncomfortable.

She doesn't ask where everyone went.

She doesn't ask someone to stay.

She doesn't even always appear distressed when something goes wrong.

A busy employee can walk past her without realizing she is there.

A group can leave a room without noticing she didn't follow.

A door can close behind her.

An elevator can depart with her inside.

Rain can begin.

Snow can fall.

And Alice may simply sit there and wait.

---

# **CROWD VISIBILITY**

Security personnel performed a controlled observation.

Alice was placed in a crowded SAFEHOUSE corridor.

She was instructed to walk normally.

Within seconds, she disappeared into the crowd.

Not literally.

Visually.

People simply stopped noticing her.

Her ears were lowered.

Her hat was not particularly distinctive.

Her height placed her below the natural sightline of most adults.

She did not push through people.

She did not make noise.

She simply walked.

The observer lost track of her in **eleven seconds.**

Alice was eventually found sitting against a wall.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

When asked whether she knows people sometimes forget her, Alice wrote:

> **"Alice know."**

Then:

> **"Everyone busy."**

Then:

> **"Alice can wait."**

The third sentence resulted in an immediate SAFEHOUSE policy review.

Because Alice shouldn't have to wait alone.

Especially not outside.

Especially not in snow.

Especially not in heavy rain.

Especially not inside a locked room.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has become particularly strict about this.

Before anyone leaves with Alice, Corin asks:

> "Where is she?"

Before anyone leaves a room:

> "Alice?"

Before anyone closes a door:

> "Check Alice."

Sometimes Alice is standing directly beside her.

Sometimes she isn't.

Corin checks anyway.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie summarized the problem more bluntly:

> "Alice won't tell us when something is wrong."

She paused.

> "So we have to notice."

---

# **BELLE**

Belle has started holding Alice's hand in crowded areas.

Not constantly.

Only when there are too many people.

Alice doesn't resist.

Sometimes she looks down at their hands.

Then smiles.

---

# **NEW SAFEHOUSE RULE**

A new reminder has been placed throughout frequently used areas:

> **IF YOU ARE LEAVING:**
> 
> **LOOK DOWN.**
> 
> **LOOK BEHIND YOU.**
> 
> **CHECK THE ROOM.**
> 
> **CHECK THE DOOR.**
> 
> **CHECK THE ELEVATOR.**
> 
> **CHECK OUTSIDE.**
> 
> **WHERE IS ALICE?**

Below it, someone added:

> **"If you don't know, find out."**

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Nobody forgets Alice because they don't love her.

They forget her because Alice has become exceptionally good at being invisible without actually being invisible.

She is a tiny, quiet five-year-old bunny.

She doesn't speak.

She doesn't complain.

She doesn't demand attention.

She can stand in the middle of a crowd and become part of the background.

She can sit outside in the rain and simply wait.

She can be trapped behind a door and make no sound.

She can ride her scooter away and nobody may notice immediately.

And if someone asks why she didn't tell them—

Alice can't.

So she waits.

Quietly.

Patiently.

Because somewhere in her mind, she still believes everyone is simply too busy.

The SAFEHOUSE has learned that this cannot be allowed to become normal.

Alice may be quiet.

She may be difficult to notice.

She may wander.

But she is still five years old.

And someone needs to notice her.

**Every time.**
