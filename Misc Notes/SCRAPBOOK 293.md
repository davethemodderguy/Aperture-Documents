---

# **CORRECTION NOTICE**

Following extended observation, previous anatomical sketches have been revised.

Earlier reports incorrectly depicted Marts with small limbs.

This is inaccurate.

Marts possess **no arms, legs, hands, feet, or similar appendages.**

They are simply...

Blue blobs of water.

With deer antlers.

And a tiny black top hat.

---

# **LOCOMOTION**

Despite lacking limbs, Marts move surprisingly efficiently.

Documented methods include:

- Bouncing.
    
- Rolling.
    
- Stretching and recoiling.
    
- Sliding across smooth surfaces.
    
- Floating while swimming.
    

They are capable of navigating stairs through repeated hopping, although elevators remain their preferred method.

---

# **OBJECT MANIPULATION**

Without limbs, Marts interact with the environment by reshaping portions of their gelatinous body.

Examples include:

- Gently enveloping small objects.
    
- Pushing lightweight items.
    
- Nudging doors open.
    
- Carrying tools inside their body.
    
- Pressing buttons by leaning against them.
    

The magical containment field surrounding each Mart prevents the enclosed objects from becoming wet.

---

# **MERGING PHENOMENON**

During routine observation, Research Division made an unexpected discovery.

The Ether signatures of separate Marts indicate they are **fully compatible with one another.**

In theory...

Multiple Marts should be capable of combining into a single larger organism.

Fortunately...

The Marts appear completely unaware that this is possible.

To date, no Mart has intentionally attempted to merge with another.

As such, no merged individual has ever been documented.

Research has unanimously agreed on one course of action.

> **Do not tell them.**

---

# **RISK ASSESSMENT**

Current Risk Level:

**Negligible.**

The Marts show no curiosity regarding their own physiology.

Their daily priorities continue to consist primarily of:

- Helping people.
    
- Swimming.
    
- Wearing tiny hats.
    
- Following Mart around.
    
- Making sad people smile.
    

Research believes they are far too easily distracted to accidentally discover the merging phenomenon.
