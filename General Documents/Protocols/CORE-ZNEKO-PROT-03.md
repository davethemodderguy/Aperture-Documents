# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **CORE-ZNEKO-PROT-03**

### **Neko / Miss Neko Behavioral Adaptation Protocol**

### **Classification:** INTERNAL

### **Status:** ACTIVE

### **Applies To:** All Aperture Industries Personnel

---

# **PURPOSE**

Recent observations have confirmed that Z-NEKO's voluntary gender transformation is not random.

---

It is not a cosmetic preference.

It is not an anomaly event.

It is not a separate identity.

---

Rather, it functions as a psychological and behavioral adaptation mechanism.

---

Neko subconsciously assumes the form best suited for his current emotional state, environment, workload, and social circumstances.

---

Personnel are reminded that:

> **Neko and Miss Neko are the same individual.**

Both forms possess:

- Identical memories
    
- Identical intelligence
    
- Identical scientific capabilities
    
- Identical authority
    
- Identical classification
    

---

Differences exist primarily in behavior, social interaction, and problem-solving approach.

---

# **PROTOCOL 01**

## Do Not Treat Either Form As More "Real"

Statements including:

- "Which one is the real Neko?"
    
- "When are you changing back?"
    
- "I liked the other version better."
    

Are strongly discouraged.

---

The correct answer is:

> Both are Neko.

---

Personnel who continue such questioning will likely receive a lecture.

---

If Miss Neko is present, the lecture will be polite.

---

If Neko is present, the lecture will not be polite.

---

# **PROTOCOL 02**

## Do Not Request A Specific Form

Personnel may not request:

- Neko to become Miss Neko
    
- Miss Neko to become Neko
    

For convenience.

Entertainment.

Personal preference.

Or scientific curiosity.

---

Transformation decisions belong exclusively to Z-NEKO.

---

### Example

### Incorrect

> "Can you become Miss Neko? You're easier to ask questions."

---

### Correct

> "Hello Neko."

---

# **PROTOCOL 03**

## Recognize Situational Indicators

While exact transformation triggers remain partially understood.

Several patterns have emerged.

---

### Neko More Commonly Appears During:

- Engineering emergencies
    
- Combat situations
    
- Security incidents
    
- Competitive events
    
- Large-scale repairs
    
- Situations requiring rapid decision-making
    

---

### Miss Neko More Commonly Appears During:

- Research projects
    
- Educational activities
    
- Long-term planning
    
- Medical assistance
    
- Administrative discussions
    
- Extended collaborative work
    

---

This pattern is not absolute.

---

But has proven remarkably consistent.

---
