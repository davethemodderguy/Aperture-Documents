### I. SESSION RECORD

---

**███████:** So Charlie, what does it feel like to take care of Star and Stitch?

**Charlie:** Quite the angels, in a literal meaning. They almost never fight, or be _that_ mischievous

**███████:** This means we’ll expect more of your siblings?

**Charlie:** I’ve only have two. Ask the others… if only Curious is here

---

**█████ Manager:** How did you get the name Charlie?

**Charlie:** Well, people just call me the “Guardian Angel of The Starlands” when I first got here. I have a real name, which I have to redact… but reading some Earth novels gave me that name

**█████ Manager:** What do you think of the Blacksite?

**Charlie:** Comfy and safe

---

**████████:** What is happening at the Starlands?

**Charlie:** An internal conflict turned into a huge war across all regions. Starladians or wandering souls just seek shelter at the Abyssal Express or, well, if I know them, I’ll invite them here—because this place won’t hold if too many people come

**████████:** So this means more people will come here?

**Charlie:** Not something I can confirm, but yes…

---

**██████:** What do you think about Director Lucious?

**Charlie:** Grounded, controlled, nice, friendly, surprisingly cold and responsible. Someone who’s hard on the outside but soft and fragile on the inside

**██████:** Are you forced to say this?

**Charlie:** What do you think? I have this weird feeling of familiarity when I first met him, like someone in my family—but I can’t remember

### III. EXTENDED ENTRIES

---

**Senior Researcher:** You described the Starlands as being in a state of war. Can you explain what kind of war this is, and why it escalated to such a large scale?

**Charlie:** It started small—conflicts between groups that didn’t agree on how things should be run. But the Starlands isn’t like Earth. Power there isn’t equal, and disagreements don’t stay small for long. One side pushes too far, another responds harder, and suddenly entire regions get pulled into it. It’s not just territory—it’s beliefs, authority, and control over things that shouldn’t even be controlled. Once it spread, it stopped being a conflict and became something much worse. Now it’s not about winning anymore… it’s about surviving it

---

**Containment Specialist:** Given your role as the “Guardian Angel,” what limits do you have? Are there things you _cannot_ do, even if you want to?

**Charlie:** Of course there are limits. I can protect, guide, and intervene—but I can’t control everything. I can’t force people to make the right choices. I can’t stop every conflict before it starts. And there are things… higher than me. Rules I don’t fully understand, but I know they exist. If I cross them, the consequences won’t just affect me

---

**Psychologist:** You show strong emotional attachment to Star and Stitch. Does that ever interfere with your judgment?

**Charlie:** It does. I won’t pretend it doesn’t. When it’s about them, I don’t think like a guardian—I think like family. That means I hesitate less, act faster, and sometimes ignore what I _should_ be doing. But if protecting them is a mistake… then I’ll keep making it

---

**Diplomatic Officer:** If more Starladians arrive at the Blacksite, how do you plan to manage potential cultural or behavioral conflicts with human personnel?

**Charlie:** Carefully. Not everyone from the Starlands understands how things work here—and not everyone here will understand them either. If too many come, tension is inevitable. I can mediate, but I can’t guarantee peace. This place feels safe now because it’s controlled. Add too many unknowns… and that changes

---

**Archivist:** You mentioned a sense of familiarity with Director Lucious that you cannot explain. If you had to describe that feeling in detail, what would it be?

**Charlie:** It’s not just familiarity—it’s… closeness. Like I’ve known him for a long time, even before we met. Not as a stranger, not as an ally—something deeper. It’s comforting, but also frustrating, because I can’t trace it back to anything. It feels like remembering something that isn’t there anymore
