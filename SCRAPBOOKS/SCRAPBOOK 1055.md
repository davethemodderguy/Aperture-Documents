## Executive Summary

Neri is fully capable of swimming.

This is unsurprising given her Seal Thiren physiology.

What is considerably less conventional is that Neri can apparently decide that swimming is unnecessary.

When presented with a sufficiently calm water surface, Neri may simply **walk across it**.

No bridge.

No platform.

No visible support.

Just Neri walking on top of the water as though the water were a normal floor.

This ability is available in both her normal and plush forms.

---

## NORMAL FORM

When Neri enters water normally, she can swim without difficulty.

She demonstrates competent aquatic movement and does not require flotation equipment under ordinary circumstances.

However, if she decides she would rather remain above the water, she can simply step onto the surface.

Once standing on the water, Neri appears to treat the surface as solid ground.

She can:

- walk;
    
- stop;
    
- turn around;
    
- sit down;
    
- stand still;
    
- carry objects;
    
- drink coffee;
    
- apparently reconsider where she is going.
    

The water does not visibly deform beneath her feet beyond ordinary surface movement.

---

## PLUSH FORM

The same phenomenon occurs while Neri is a plushie.

This has produced considerably more confusion among SAFEHOUSE personnel.

A small seal/teddy-bear hybrid can be observed walking across a pool.

No floating.

No paddling.

No sinking.

Just:

**step**

**step**

**step**

across the water.

## Observation Log — 1

**LOCATION:** Sector 4-C Aquatic Testing Pool

Neri entered the pool area in normal form.

She approached the water.

A researcher asked:

> **RESEARCHER:** "Are you going to swim?"

Neri looked at the pool.

She then stepped onto the water.

She walked across it.

The researcher remained silent.

Neri reached the opposite side.

She turned around.

She walked back.

> **RESEARCHER:** "...You can swim."

Neri nodded.

> **RESEARCHER:** "Then why did you walk?"

Neri shrugged.

No further explanation was provided.

---

## Observation Log — 2

**FORM:** Plush

Neri was observed approaching a shallow pool.

Alice was nearby.

> **ALICE:** "Are you going to swim?"

Neri looked at the water.

Then at Alice.

She stepped onto the surface.

Alice watched her walk across.

> **ALICE:** "...You're walking on water."

Neri continued walking.

> **ALICE:** "Neri."

Neri stopped.

> **ALICE:** "That's so cool."

Neri resumed walking.

## Observation Log — 3

Jane Doe witnessed the phenomenon for the first time.

She stared at Plushie Neri walking across the surface of a pool.

> **JANE:** "You're a seal."

Neri nodded.

> **JANE:** "You're supposed to swim."

Neri continued walking.

> **JANE:** "You know what? Fair enough."

## Swimming vs. Walking

C.O.R.E. personnel are reminded that Neri's ability to walk on water does **not** replace her ability to swim.

Her apparent preference is situational.

### If Neri wants to swim:

She swims.

### If Neri wants to walk:

She walks.

### If Neri wants to sit on the water:

She apparently can.

### If Neri wants to turn into a plushie and walk across the pool:

She can do that too.

No additional explanation has been obtained.

---

## Medical Note

Neri's aquatic locomotion should not be interpreted as evidence that her cardiovascular condition has been cured.

Her normal medical limitations remain applicable.

In particular:

**Walking on water does not mean she has infinite stamina.**

Personnel should not challenge Neri to cross large bodies of water simply because she can walk across the surface.

If she stops and sits down halfway across, **do not assume she is performing a demonstration.**

She may simply be tired.

---

## Final Assessment

Neri possesses two perfectly valid methods of crossing water:

**Method A:** Swim.

**Method B:** Decide that water is now a floor.

C.O.R.E. has no current explanation for why Method B works.

Neri does not appear particularly interested in providing one.

### Neri's Written Response When Asked Why She Can Walk on Water

> **"Because I can."**

No further comment.

**C.O.R.E. Assessment:**

Fair enough.
