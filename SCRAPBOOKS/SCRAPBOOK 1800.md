# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana has undergone a disturbing behavioral change.

He no longer behaves like himself.

Following the recent five-hour exposure to subzero conditions, Jake has become almost completely passive. He sits wherever he is placed and remains there until someone moves him.

He does not initiate conversation.

He does not respond reliably when addressed.

He does not reach for familiar people.

He does not ask to be carried.

He does not follow Aries.

He does not protest when someone takes his belongings out of reach, moves him to another seat, or physically relocates him.

Most concerningly, Jake has stopped resisting unwanted physical interaction.

Personnel have observed him being pushed, poked, lifted, and moved without any visible objection.

His eyes remain open during many of these interactions.

His expression remains blank.

He does not appear to be choosing where he wants to be. Instead, he allows other people to make that decision for him.

One staff member described the behavior as:

> "Like someone had turned a little boy into a doll, except the doll was still breathing and looking at us."

The comparison is unofficial.

The concern behind it is not.

Jake's behavior represents a significant deterioration from his previous baseline and requires urgent medical and psychological assessment.

C.O.R.E. cannot determine from observation alone whether this presentation reflects a severe stress response, a dissociative state, neurological impairment, physical exhaustion, or another medical or psychological condition.

**Jake's lack of resistance must not be interpreted as consent, comfort, recovery, or evidence that he no longer cares about the people around him.**

# 1. OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

The first reports described Jake as unusually quiet.

That description was subsequently withdrawn.

Jake has always been quiet.

He has never needed to fill a room with conversation. He frequently communicates through gestures, facial expressions, or a single word. He can spend hours reading without speaking to anyone.

His current behavior is different.

Previously, Jake's silence was accompanied by signs of preference.

He chose where to sit.

He decided whom to approach.

He asked for uppies when he wanted to be carried.

He held onto Aries's sleeve.

He leaned against Bao.

He followed people he trusted.

He turned away when he did not want to participate.

Even when he said nothing, he continued to make decisions about his surroundings.

Those decisions are becoming increasingly difficult to observe.

Jake now appears to accept almost any arrangement imposed upon him.

He sits in a chair.

Someone moves him to a couch.

He remains there.

Someone picks him up.

He allows it.

Someone sets him down in a different room.

He stays where he is placed.

Someone asks him a question.

He does not answer.

Someone calls his name.

He may look toward the sound, but no further response follows.

His eyes remain open.

His breathing continues.

His body remains physically present.

Yet the ordinary signs of engagement that once characterized his interactions have become markedly less apparent.

# 3. THE MOVEMENT TEST

**PURPOSE:** To determine whether Jake continues to initiate movement in response to ordinary environmental changes.

**NOTE:** This was an informal observation, not an approved diagnostic test. Personnel are instructed not to reproduce it.

During a later incident, a resident approached Jake while he was sitting on a couch.

Jake was occupying the center cushion.

The resident wanted the seat.

Rather than asking Jake to move, the resident pushed him sideways.

Jake's body shifted under the applied force.

He did not brace himself.

He did not object.

He did not look toward the resident.

The resident stopped.

**RESIDENT:**  
"Jake?"

No response.

The resident then took Jake by the arms and moved him to the adjacent cushion.

Jake's legs moved with the relocation.

Once placed down, he remained in the new position.

The resident stared at him.

**RESIDENT:**  
"You know you can tell me to stop, right?"

Jake did not answer.

The resident stepped away.

A nearby staff member witnessed the interaction and intervened.

The resident was not accused of deliberately causing harm. However, the interaction was documented because Jake's complete lack of resistance appeared to encourage the assumption that moving him without asking was acceptable.

It was not.

The resident later reported feeling as though they had moved an object rather than a person.

C.O.R.E. reiterates that Jake is a resident with full bodily autonomy.

His current inability or unwillingness to express a preference does not transfer that authority to everyone around him.

**Personnel must ask before touching or moving him, use the least intrusive assistance necessary, and stop any nonessential interaction if he shows discomfort.**

If Jake requires urgent medical movement, trained personnel may intervene as medically necessary.

Otherwise, he is not to be treated as a convenient object that can be placed wherever someone wants him.

# 5. MEDICAL DIFFERENTIAL — DO NOT ASSUME PSYCHOLOGICAL CAUSE

The timing of Jake's behavioral deterioration makes a psychological response to the recent incident a significant concern.

However, his physical collapse following prolonged cold exposure makes it unsafe to attribute the change to emotional distress alone.

Jake requires prompt reassessment for medical causes of altered responsiveness, including possible complications of cold exposure, metabolic abnormalities, neurological impairment, and other physiological disturbances.

His anomalous physiology may complicate interpretation of conventional findings.

Medical personnel are instructed to compare his current condition with his established baseline rather than assuming that ordinary measurements will fully explain his presentation.

The assessment must include consideration of:

- level of alertness and responsiveness;
    
- orientation and recognition of familiar people;
    
- ability to communicate through speech or gesture;
    
- spontaneous movement;
    
- food and fluid intake;
    
- sleep and activity changes;
    
- signs of pain or physical discomfort;
    
- and any new or worsening neurological symptoms.
    

Jake's teddy bear must remain with him as medically appropriate because it is his cardiac vessel.

If he is difficult to wake, cannot be roused normally, becomes unresponsive, or exhibits further deterioration, emergency medical intervention takes priority over psychological interpretation.

**A child who appears emotionally absent may also be medically unwell. The two possibilities must not be treated as mutually exclusive.**

---

# 6. PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

If acute medical causes do not fully explain the behavior, the psychological team will assess whether Jake is experiencing a severe stress response, dissociative symptoms, depressive withdrawal, or another condition.

No diagnosis is established by this report.

Nevertheless, the change is sufficiently pronounced that it cannot be dismissed as ordinary quietness.

Jake's previous behavior showed a clear capacity to express preferences even when he did not use words.

He sought particular people.

He avoided others when he wished to be alone.

He requested physical affection.

He sometimes refused food, conversation, or assistance.

He could be stubborn.

He could be irritable.

He could make his displeasure unmistakable with a single look.

Those responses were evidence of engagement with the world around him.

Their sudden reduction is concerning.

One possibility is that Jake has become overwhelmed by the accumulation of distressing experiences and is no longer responding to his environment in his usual way.

Another is that he has begun to withdraw from decisions because choosing, asking, resisting, and trusting have become emotionally exhausting.

It is also possible that he has learned to minimize his reactions because he does not want to inconvenience or distress the people around him.

These possibilities are not equivalent, and C.O.R.E. will not choose one simply because it fits the incident narrative.

The team must observe Jake carefully, assess him directly when he is able to participate, and avoid imposing an explanation upon him.

---

# 7. THE MOST CONCERNING CHANGE: THE LOSS OF RESISTANCE

Before the incident, Jake's quietness did not mean that he would allow everything.

He had preferences.

He expressed them.

Sometimes he used words.

Sometimes he used a gesture.

Sometimes he simply moved away.

Now, personnel report that he can be relocated without any visible objection.

That does not necessarily mean Jake has consciously decided that his preferences no longer matter.

It may mean that he cannot currently express them.

It may mean that he is too exhausted to resist.

It may mean that he is disengaging from his surroundings.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet determine which explanation is correct.

What matters operationally is that **the absence of resistance must not be mistaken for permission.**

If Jake is sitting in the wrong chair, he may remain there.

If someone wants him to move, they must ask.

If he needs assistance, they must explain what they are doing.

If he does not respond, they must consider whether he understands, whether he is able to answer, and whether medical assessment is necessary.

His silence does not make the decision for them.

# 8. ARIES'S FIRST OBSERVATION

Aries found Jake sitting in the SAFEHOUSE lounge.

He had been looking for his younger brother for several minutes.

Normally, he would have found Jake near a familiar person, in the library, or sitting somewhere quiet with his teddy bear.

This time, Jake was sitting alone on the couch.

His hands rested in his lap.

His eyes were open.

He was not reading.

Aries stopped at the entrance.

**ARIES:**  
"Jake?"

No response.

Aries approached.

**ARIES:**  
"Little star?"

Jake did not turn.

Aries crouched in front of him.

**ARIES:**  
"Hey. It's me."

Jake's eyes moved toward him.

Aries smiled faintly, trying to offer a familiar expression rather than demand a response.

**ARIES:**  
"Can I sit with you?"

Nothing.

Aries sat beside him, leaving a small space between them.

He waited.

Jake did not move closer.

He did not move away.

Aries looked at the space between them.

It was barely wide enough for another person.

It felt enormous.

**ARIES:**  
"I can stay for a while."

Jake continued staring ahead.

Aries glanced toward Jake's hands.

There was no familiar grip on his sleeve.

No small hand reaching for his fingers.

No request to be carried.

Aries held out his own hand, palm upward, without touching Jake.

**ARIES:**  
"If you want my hand, it's here."

Jake did not take it.

Aries left his hand where it was for a few seconds before lowering it.

He did not ask why.

He did not tell Jake that he missed being hugged.

He did not remind Jake that they were brothers.

He did not say that he was sorry again.

He had already apologized.

Repeating the apology would not make Jake capable of answering.

Aries sat beside him.

After several minutes, Jake's head tilted slightly to one side.

Aries immediately checked whether he was physically comfortable and alerted the welfare team.

He did not assume Jake had fallen asleep.

He did not pick him up simply because he normally would have.

He waited for guidance.

For the first time since their reunion, Aries was confronted with a situation in which Jake's silence offered him no indication of what his younger brother wanted.

He could not rely on familiarity.

He could not rely on habit.

He could not rely on the fact that Jake usually liked being carried.

He had to begin again with the simplest question.

What does Jake need right now?

And, for the moment, neither brother could answer it.

# 10. RESPONSE PROTOCOL — ACTIVE

Effective immediately:

1. Jake is to receive prompt medical reassessment for his altered responsiveness and behavioral change.
    
2. Personnel must not deliberately provoke, startle, poke, or physically manipulate Jake in an attempt to elicit a reaction.
    
3. No resident may treat Jake as a doll, prop, or convenient object for relocation.
    
4. Staff must continue to address Jake directly, using calm, simple language and allowing adequate time for responses.
    
5. When physical assistance is necessary, personnel should explain the intended action before proceeding whenever circumstances permit.
    
6. Jake's established preferences, medical needs, and bodily autonomy remain in force even when he does not actively express them.
    
7. Trusted residents may remain nearby if doing so is safe and does not overwhelm him.
    
8. Aries may visit Jake when medically and psychologically appropriate, but he must not pressure Jake to speak, accept affection, or reassure him.
    
9. Bao and the welfare team will coordinate observation of Jake's food intake, hydration, sleep, mobility, responsiveness, and other relevant changes.
    
10. Any sudden worsening of alertness or responsiveness must be treated as a potential medical emergency.
    

The objective is not to force Jake to become animated.

It is to establish whether he is medically safe, reduce unnecessary stress, and give him the support needed to recover his ability to participate in his own life.

---

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake is currently demonstrating a profound departure from his previous behavior.

His blank expression, reduced communication, lack of spontaneous movement, and apparent indifference to being physically relocated are not consistent with his established baseline.

The recent cold-exposure incident may be a significant contributing factor, but the full cause remains undetermined.

His medical condition must be reassessed before personnel attribute the change exclusively to psychological distress.

If the behavior is primarily stress-related, it may reflect an overwhelming accumulation of experiences rather than a response to the recent incident alone.

Jake has repeatedly demonstrated an ability to understand the intentions of people who hurt him.

That ability must not be mistaken for an absence of suffering.

He knows Aries did not intend to leave him outside.

He knows Aries loves him.

He may even understand that Aries is suffering because of what happened.

None of those facts explains why Jake has stopped expressing his own preferences.

None of them makes the current condition acceptable.

And none of them gives the people around him permission to decide that his silence means he is fine.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake used to be quiet.

He could sit in a corner for hours with a book.

He could drink his coffee without saying a word.

He could follow Aries down a corridor without speaking, then climb onto his back when he became tired.

He could refuse a conversation with one small shake of his head.

He could look at Alice until she understood that she was being ridiculous.

He could stare at Bao until she gave him the answer he wanted.

He could sit beside someone without talking and still make it clear that he had chosen to be there.

His silence had personality.

His stillness had preference.

His refusal was still a refusal.

Now, personnel walk into a room and find him sitting somewhere.

They call his name.

Nothing.

They ask whether he wants to move.

Nothing.

They offer food.

Nothing.

They pick him up, and he lets them.

They put him down, and he stays.

They leave, and he does not follow.

He looks like a little boy who has decided that the world may do whatever it wants with him.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet say that this is what Jake believes.

We must not pretend to know his mind merely because his expression no longer tells us much.

But we can recognize the danger of what we are seeing.

A child who has stopped objecting is not necessarily a child who has stopped hurting.

A child who no longer asks for comfort has not necessarily stopped needing it.

And a child who allows everyone else to decide where he belongs may not have found peace.

He may simply have run out of ways to ask for something different.

Aries wanted his little brother back.

He wanted the boy who climbed onto his back, stole his attention, and fell asleep against him.

He wanted the little star who would look at him and ask for uppies as though the answer had never been in doubt.

But Jake cannot be ordered back into that version of himself.

He cannot be coaxed into performing happiness.

He cannot be carried around until he looks normal again.

And he cannot be made responsible for reassuring Aries that he is still loved.

Right now, Jake is sitting somewhere.

His eyes are open.

His hands are still.

His teddy bear is beside him.

And the people who care about him are finally beginning to understand that they cannot measure his well-being by how little trouble he causes.

They must learn to notice him even when he does not ask to be noticed.

They must protect his right to refuse, even when he no longer visibly refuses.

They must keep offering him choices without forcing him to make them immediately.

And they must remember that the purpose of caring for Jake is not to make him easier to handle.

It is to help him become a person who can once again decide, for himself, where he wants to sit, whose hand he wants to hold, when he wants to leave, and whether he wants to be carried home.

**Jake is still here.**

But simply being present is not the same as being okay.
