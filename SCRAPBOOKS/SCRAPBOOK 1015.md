# Executive Summary

Neri experienced another acute anemic episode.

Unlike a mild episode characterized primarily by fatigue or pallor, this event produced **significant physical pain**.

Neri's existing blood instability remains unchanged: her altered physiology causes her red blood cells to become progressively depleted, while her white blood cells mistakenly target functioning red blood cells.

Bao's blood continues to provide temporary stabilization by suppressing this abnormal white-cell activity and replenishing Neri's usable blood supply.

The episode reinforces an existing concern:

> **Neri can be in considerable pain without asking anyone for help.**

This is particularly concerning because she currently cannot speak and rarely compensates through writing.

---

# I. Incident

Neri was discovered during an acute episode of anemia.

She was visibly pale and experiencing significant discomfort.

Her posture had changed noticeably.

She remained unusually still, with one arm held close to her body.

When approached, Neri initially attempted to indicate that she was alright.

She was not.

The pain was sufficiently severe that normal activity had become difficult.

Neri did not call for assistance.

She did not write a request for medical attention.

She attempted to wait for the episode to pass.

---

# II. Pain Response

The episode was notably different from Neri's previous presentations.

Neri's anemia does not merely make her tired.

When sufficiently severe, it can produce substantial physiological distress and pain.

Observed signs included:

- Sudden cessation of normal activity.
    
- Significant pallor.
    
- Guarded posture.
    
- Difficulty maintaining normal movement.
    
- Visible physical discomfort.
    
- Reduced responsiveness.
    
- Attempts to remain still.
    
- Refusal to voluntarily draw attention to herself.
    

Neri's inability to speak made verbal assessment impossible.

Her body language therefore became the primary immediate indicator that something was wrong.

---

# III. The Communication Problem

Neri still does not communicate medical distress reliably.

This remains one of the most serious aspects of her condition.

A speaking resident experiencing severe pain might say:

> "Something hurts."

Neri cannot currently do that.

A resident accustomed to writing might produce a note.

Neri generally does not.

Instead, she may simply sit quietly and wait.

This behavior is directly consistent with her established **Non-Interference Rule**:

> **Do not bother anyone.**

Unfortunately, severe pain does not become less serious because Neri decides not to mention it.

---

# IV. Discovery

Personnel eventually recognized that Neri's behavior was abnormal.

She was not simply sitting quietly.

She was attempting to remain motionless because movement appeared uncomfortable.

When Bao arrived, Neri looked toward her.

No words were exchanged.

Neri did not need to explain what was happening.

Bao recognized the presentation.

Medical intervention began immediately.

---

# V. Bao's Response

Bao remained with Neri throughout the episode.

The temporary blood-support treatment was administered according to Neri's established medical protocol.

As previously documented, Bao's blood temporarily suppresses the abnormal white-cell targeting and provides Neri with sufficient functioning red blood cells to stabilize her.

The treatment addresses the immediate crisis.

It does not permanently correct Neri's underlying physiology.

---

# VI. Neri's Behavior During Recovery

Neri remained quiet.

She did not complain.

She did not attempt to describe the pain in writing.

She primarily followed Bao's presence and medical direction through gestures and eye contact.

At one point, Bao asked:

> "Does it hurt?"

Neri nodded.

Bao asked:

> "A lot?"

Neri hesitated.

Then nodded again.

No additional communication occurred.

The exchange was brief.

The severity of the response was nevertheless clear.

---

# VII. Welfare Significance

This incident further demonstrates why Neri's quietness must not be interpreted as comfort.

Neri can experience substantial pain while producing almost no outward communication.

Her current behavioral presentation may therefore look deceptively calm.

This is especially dangerous because Neri genuinely enjoys being alone.

She is comfortable spending long periods by herself.

Consequently, simply finding her alone is not abnormal.

Finding her alone **while unusually still, pale, withdrawn, or physically guarded** is different.

---

# VIII. Comparison With Previous Episodes

Neri's anemic episodes have presented differently over time.

Some have involved:

- Weakness.
    
- Pallor.
    
- Fatigue.
    
- Fainting.
    
- Cardiovascular distress.
    
- Respiratory distress.
    

This episode was particularly notable for the degree of pain.

C.O.R.E. therefore recommends that personnel avoid relying on any single symptom as the definition of an anemic event.

Neri does not need to faint before an episode is considered serious.

She does not need to lose consciousness.

She may simply be sitting there, hurting.

---

# IX. Important Reminder to SAFEHOUSE Residents

If Neri appears unusually still:

**Check on her.**

If she appears pale:

**Check on her.**

If she stops her normal activity suddenly:

**Check on her.**

If she appears to be protecting a part of her body:

**Check on her.**

If she gives a minimal nod indicating that she's "fine":

**Do not automatically accept that as a medical assessment.**

Neri has repeatedly demonstrated that she will minimize her own condition rather than inconvenience someone.

---

# X. Final Assessment

The current episode has resolved sufficiently for Neri to return to observation and recovery.

No evidence suggests that Neri deliberately caused the episode.

No evidence suggests that she sought attention through it.

If anything, the opposite occurred.

Neri attempted to endure significant pain without bothering anyone.

That remains the central welfare concern.

The appropriate response is not to demand that Neri become more communicative.

It is to ensure that the people around her become better at recognizing when her silence is unusual.

---

# XI. Closing Observation

After the episode had stabilized, Neri was resting quietly.

Bao remained beside her.

Neri eventually reached for her coffee.

Bao looked at her.

> **BAO:**  
> "Coffee?"

Neri nodded.

Bao handed it to her.

Neri took a small sip.

Her expression remained neutral.

No smile.

No complaint.

Just quiet recovery.

For once, however, she was not alone.
