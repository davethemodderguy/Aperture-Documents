### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

After several weeks of written correspondence, Sora has officially relocated from his maintenance-locker den at the Blacksite to Floor 7 of SAFEHOUSE.

The relocation followed an extended period of communication between Sora and Jake Akana.

Sora initially remained reluctant to interact directly. However, the two gradually established communication through letters. Jake continued writing to him, and Sora eventually began responding.

The arrangement developed into something neither child had previously been able to establish through ordinary conversation: a friendship that did not require Sora to speak.

Jake subsequently provided Sora with a small whiteboard and marker.

Sora now uses these to communicate.

He has also been given a dedicated space on Floor 7: a small room built into the wall, resembling a doghouse. This space is officially designated **Sora's Den**.

Since moving in, Sora has become a constant presence beside Jake.

He is not entirely confined to his den anymore. He occasionally ventures outside, often carried by Jake.

For the first time since his discovery, Sora appears to have found somewhere he can stay without having to disappear.

### 2. THE LETTERS

The transition did not happen overnight.

For several weeks, Jake and Sora communicated through letters.

Jake continued reaching out, but the correspondence allowed Sora to decide when to respond. There was no requirement for immediate answers, eye contact, or spoken conversation.

This arrangement proved successful.

Sora gradually became more willing to communicate with Jake. The letters eventually established enough trust for him to accept a more permanent arrangement.

C.O.R.E. considers this an important development.

Sora had previously chased Jake away whenever he attempted to visit. Now, after weeks of correspondence, Sora has voluntarily chosen to live on the same floor as him.

The change should not be interpreted as an instant recovery from his previous experiences.

Rather, it demonstrates that Sora can form attachments when his boundaries are respected and communication is allowed to happen at his pace.

### 3. COMMUNICATION — THE WHITEBOARD

<badge color="success">ESTABLISHED COMMUNICATION METHOD</badge>

Jake gave Sora a small whiteboard and a marker.

Sora uses them to express himself.

This is particularly significant because Sora reportedly misses being able to talk.

His vocal cords were severed, leaving him unable to speak. His silence is not a preference, nor is it an indication that he has nothing to say.

He has thoughts, feelings, questions, and things he wants to tell people.

He simply cannot express them through his voice.

The whiteboard gives him another way to do so.

Personnel interacting with Sora are instructed to:

- Give him time to write.
    
- Never take the whiteboard away as punishment.
    
- Never demand that he write faster.
    
- Read his messages carefully rather than assuming their meaning.
    
- Allow him to communicate privately with Jake when appropriate.
    
- Never treat his inability to speak as an intellectual or emotional limitation.
    

**Sora has not lost his ability to communicate. He has lost his voice. Those are not the same thing.**

### 4. RESIDENTIAL ARRANGEMENT — FLOOR 7

Sora's relocation has been completed.

A small room has been constructed inside the wall of Floor 7. Its entrance and proportions resemble a little doghouse, providing Sora with a compact, enclosed space of his own.

The room is officially designated:

**SORA'S DEN**

The design intentionally preserves the concept of his original den.

Sora does not have to abandon the comfort of having a small, private hiding place simply because he has moved somewhere safer.

The den is his personal space. It is not a containment cell, storage compartment, or punishment area.

Its purpose is to provide:

- A private place to rest.
    
- A familiar enclosed environment.
    
- Space for his personal belongings.
    
- A place to retreat when the common area becomes overwhelming.
    
- A permanent home within Floor 7.
    

Unlike his previous maintenance locker, the new den is integrated into a residential environment and is not exposed to the same broken-window conditions.

**The den is part of the home. Sora is not being stored inside a wall.**

### 5. OBSERVATIONS — JAKE AND SORA

Sora is almost always beside Jake.

The two have developed a remarkably close routine.

Sora occasionally leaves his den and explores Floor 7. During these outings, Jake often carries him.

This appears to be an accepted and comfortable arrangement between them.

Jake has become Sora's primary companion, although personnel must remember that neither child should be made responsible for the other's entire well-being.

There is an important contrast between the earlier cafeteria incidents and the present situation.

At the Blacksite, Sora ran away from people.

Now, he voluntarily stays close to someone.

At the Blacksite, Jake repeatedly visited a closed door and was chased away.

Now, Sora lives on Jake's floor and is rarely far from him.

The change is substantial.

C.O.R.E. has no intention of turning this relationship into a mandatory therapeutic arrangement. Their friendship should be allowed to develop naturally.

### 6. RESIDENT WELFARE DIRECTIVES

**For Sora:**

1. His den is to remain private and accessible at all times.
    
2. His whiteboard and marker must remain available.
    
3. He is not required to participate in group activities.
    
4. He may leave his den whenever he feels comfortable.
    
5. He must have reliable access to proper food and drinking water.
    
6. Medical support must remain available without requiring him to speak.
    

**For Jake:**

1. Jake may accompany or carry Sora when both are comfortable with the arrangement.
    
2. Jake is not expected to supervise Sora continuously.
    
3. Jake must retain time for his own activities and rest.
    
4. Personnel must not interpret Sora's attachment as permission to burden Jake with additional responsibilities.
    

**For SAFEHOUSE residents:**

Sora is a resident, not a visitor.

His silence does not make him invisible, and his den is not an invitation for people to intrude.

All residents are encouraged to respect his communication method and personal space.

### 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Sora missed talking.

That detail is easy to overlook when looking at everything else that happened to him.

We found a child who could not speak, hiding in a cold maintenance locker. He ate discarded food because approaching people was frightening. He chased away the one person who kept trying to visit him.

And Jake kept writing.

For weeks.

Eventually, Sora wrote back.

Now there is a little doghouse-shaped room built into the wall of Floor 7. A whiteboard sits within reach. A marker is always nearby. And there is usually a small puppy boy somewhere beside Jake.

Sometimes Jake carries him around.

Sometimes Sora stays in his den.

Sometimes they simply sit together.

We do not know whether Sora will ever regain his voice. We will not promise him something we cannot guarantee.

But he has a way to speak now.

He has a place to sleep.

He has food that does not come from a waste bucket.

And, most importantly, he has someone who keeps making room for him.

Once, Sora did everything he could to avoid inconveniencing anyone.

Now, he has a home where he is allowed to take up space.
