### ANOMALOUS RESIDENT & WELFARE DIVISION

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Previous documentation established that Jake died more than seventy-five years ago after prolonged starvation.

Further investigation has revealed a significantly more painful component of his history.

**Jake was not simply alone.**

He was abandoned.

His mother left him.

And Jake does not believe that she abandoned him.

He refuses to accept it.

Jake loved his mother.

According to every available recollection, that love was genuine.

It was also **one-sided.**

His mother did not return for him.

She did not find him.

She did not rescue him.

She left him to die.

Jake nevertheless continues to speak about her as though she loved him.

He continues to defend her.

He continues to believe there must have been another explanation.

This belief has persisted for more than seventy-five years.

---

# 2. JAKE'S MEMORY OF HIS MOTHER

Jake remembers his mother.

He remembers being held by her.

He remembers following her.

He remembers wanting her attention.

He remembers loving her.

When asked what his mother was like, Jake initially gave a remarkably simple answer:

> **“She was my mom.”**

The interviewer asked:

> “Did she love you?”

Jake immediately answered:

> **“Yeah.”**

There was no hesitation.

The question was repeated later under different circumstances.

The answer remained the same.

> **“She loved me.”**

There is no evidence supporting this conclusion.

Jake's memories do not contain reciprocal affection comparable to what he describes.

His memories instead show a child repeatedly attempting to obtain affection and reassurance from someone who increasingly withdrew from him.

Jake has interpreted the absence of affection as something he must have misunderstood.

---

# 3. THE ABANDONMENT

Available reconstruction indicates that Jake's mother eventually left him behind.

She did not remain with him.

She did not arrange for someone else to care for him.

She did not return.

Jake was left alone while already vulnerable and starving.

Eventually, he went to sleep.

He died.

His mother did not come back.

This is the point at which Jake's recollection becomes inconsistent.

He remembers the outcome.

He does not emotionally accept the cause.

---

# 4. DENIAL

During a welfare interview, C.O.R.E. personnel carefully asked Jake whether his mother had left him.

Jake became visibly distressed.

> **Jake:**  
> “No.”

The interviewer clarified:

> “She wasn't there when you died.”

Jake looked down.

> **Jake:**  
> “She had to go somewhere.”

> **Interviewer:**  
> “And she didn't come back?”

Jake remained silent.

> **Jake:**  
> “She was coming back.”

> **Interviewer:**  
> “Jake—”

> **Jake:**  
> “She was.”

The interview was terminated shortly afterward.

---

# 5. THE CENTRAL CONTRADICTION

Jake simultaneously holds two beliefs:

> **My mom loved me.**

and

> **My mom left me alone.**

He cannot reconcile them.

Instead, he protects the first belief by changing the explanation for the second.

She was busy.

She was coming back.

Something happened.

She didn't know.

She didn't mean to.

Someone stopped her.

There must have been a reason.

Jake will accept almost any explanation except:

> **She chose to leave him.**

That conclusion appears psychologically intolerable.

---

# 6. ONE-SIDED LOVE

C.O.R.E. Welfare personnel have deliberately avoided describing Jake's relationship with his mother as simply "abusive" or "neglectful" in direct conversations with him.

Jake does not understand the relationship that way.

He remembers loving her.

That love remains.

The tragedy is that there is no evidence that his mother returned that love in the manner Jake needed.

Jake spent his childhood trying to reach someone who had already emotionally left him.

He died still loving her.

He remains dead still loving her.

More than seventy-five years have not changed that.

---

# 7. NORA'S ATTEMPT

Nora initially believed that companionship might help Jake.

She invited him to walk with her.

She gave him cake.

She showed him the Tachyon Merch Store.

She introduced him to Aeterna.

She allowed him to accompany her during nighttime walks.

Jake accepted all of this.

He became comfortable around Nora.

He trusted her.

He considered her a friend.

But his distress did not disappear.

This distinction is important.

Nora did not fail.

**The problem was never simply that Jake was lonely.**

He is carrying something that companionship alone cannot resolve.

---

# 8. OBSERVED INTERACTION

During a nighttime walk, Nora and Jake were sitting outside the cafeteria.

Nora had brought food.

Jake watched her eat.

Normally, he would comment on it.

This time he did not.

Nora eventually asked:

> **Nora:**  
> “Jake?”

Jake looked at her.

> **Nora:**  
> “You okay?”

Jake nodded.

Nora waited.

After several seconds:

> **Jake:**  
> “I miss my mom.”

Nora became quiet.

> **Nora:**  
> “Do you want to talk about her?”

Jake nodded.

Then shook his head.

Then nodded again.

Finally:

> **Jake:**  
> “She'll come back.”

Nora did not know what to say.

She simply remained beside him.

Jake began crying.

He continued crying for several minutes.

Nora stayed.

It did not make him stop.

---

# 9. AETERNA'S OBSERVATION

Aeterna has observed Jake's condition on multiple occasions.

Her assessment is consistent:

Jake is not merely experiencing grief.

He is trapped inside an unresolved attachment.

He continues loving someone who is no longer present.

He continues waiting for someone who is not coming.

And, unlike a living child, Jake has no future in which that relationship can naturally change.

Aeterna summarized the situation privately:

> **“He is not waiting for his mother to return.”**

Pause.

> **“He is waiting for reality to become different.”**

---

# 10. GUIDING LIGHT

The matter was eventually brought to **Guiding Light**.

Given Guiding Light's status as an Architect of Reality, C.O.R.E. personnel initially considered whether Jake's condition could be altered through anomalous intervention.

The answer was no.

Guiding Light confirmed that Jake's continued presence is not the result of an ordinary haunting, malfunctioning soul transition, or conventional spiritual instability.

Jake is **forsakened to remain here.**

His existence is anchored to this place.

There is no available pathway for C.O.R.E., SAFEHOUSE personnel, or Guiding Light to simply relocate him to another existence.

There is no known method to restore his life.

There is no known method to erase the memories.

There is no known method to force his soul onward.

Guiding Light was direct:

> **“There is nothing I can do.”**

---

# 11. LUCY MERRIWEATHER

Lucy Merryweather was consulted due to her authority over the Banlands and her extensive knowledge of souls, death, and post-mortem transition.

Her conclusion independently matched Guiding Light's.

Jake is not being held by a conventional Banlands process.

He cannot simply be processed.

He cannot simply be transferred.

He cannot simply be returned to life.

Lucy stated:

> **“He's stuck.”**

A pause.

> **“Not because someone is keeping him here.”**

Another pause.

> **“Because this is where he is.”**

Lucy was asked whether she could change this.

She answered:

> **“No.”**

---

# 12. THE MOST DIFFICULT CONCLUSION

There is no solution.

Not currently.

Perhaps not ever.

C.O.R.E. cannot promise Jake that he will eventually move on.

Guiding Light cannot promise it.

Lucy cannot promise it.

Aeterna cannot promise it.

Nora cannot promise it.

Nobody can.

This is not a problem that can be solved by finding the correct machine, opening the correct rift, or locating the correct anomalous artifact.

Jake's existence has become a permanent condition.

He is here.

He has been here for more than seventy-five years.

And he may remain here indefinitely.

---

# 13. PSYCHOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCE

Jake is therefore not experiencing a temporary period of sadness.

He is **consistently distressed.**

He may laugh occasionally.

He may become interested in something.

He may call the Bullseye "cool."

He may walk beside Nora.

He may ask Aeterna whether she can fly.

These moments remain genuine.

But they do not eliminate the underlying suffering.

Jake returns to it.

Again.

And again.

And again.

There is no clean progression from:

> **sad → comforted → healed**

because the thing causing the pain cannot be removed.

---

# 14. THE NIGHTTIME

Jake continues wandering at night.

He continues looking for food.

He continues watching people through windows.

He continues approaching places where people gather.

Personnel previously interpreted this primarily as residual hunger.

That interpretation is incomplete.

Jake spent the final period of his life waiting for his mother.

He was hungry.

He was alone.

He was waiting.

Now, decades later, he continues walking.

Perhaps he is still searching for something that feels like someone coming back.

---

# 17. AETERNA AND JAKE

Aeterna has continued accompanying Nora during some nighttime walks.

Jake has become accustomed to her presence.

He does not appear frightened by her.

He occasionally asks questions.

Sometimes she answers.

Sometimes she simply walks beside him.

During one observation, Jake asked:

> **Jake:**  
> “Can you make me not sad?”

Aeterna stopped.

She looked down at him.

She did not lie.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “No.”

Jake looked disappointed.

Aeterna continued:

> **Aeterna:**  
> “But I can stay.”

Jake looked at her.

He said nothing.

Then he continued walking.

---

# 18. CURRENT WELFARE DIRECTIVE

Jake is not to be treated as a problem requiring resolution.

He is to be treated as a resident requiring care.

Personnel should not promise:

> “You'll get better.”

Do not promise:

> “You'll see your mom again.”

Do not promise:

> “You'll move on eventually.”

Do not tell him:

> “You need to let her go.”

Nobody has the authority to make those promises.

Instead:

Stay.

Listen.

Provide company when requested.

Provide food alternatives where possible.

Allow him to talk about his mother.

Do not force him to accept conclusions he cannot emotionally process.

And, above all:

**Do not leave him alone simply because his condition cannot be fixed.**

---

# 19. FINAL OBSERVATION

At 01:14, Jake was sitting outside the cafeteria.

Nora was beside him.

Aeterna stood nearby.

Jake was crying quietly.

Nora asked:

> **Nora:**  
> “Do you want me to go?”

Jake shook his head.

> **Jake:**  
> “No.”

Nora stayed.

Several minutes later, Jake whispered:

> **Jake:**  
> “She loved me.”

Nora looked at him.

She knew.

She also knew she could not tell him that she was certain.

So she did not argue.

She simply sat beside him.

Jake rested his head against her shoulder.

He continued crying.

Nora remained there.

Aeterna remained there too.

Nothing was fixed.

Nothing was healed.

His mother was still gone.

Jake was still dead.

The place that had become his prison was still around him.

And the pain that had followed him for more than seventy-five years was still there.

But for that night, at least, he was not carrying it completely by himself.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake's tragedy is not that nobody cares about him anymore.

It is that the person he wanted to care about him most—

**never did enough to keep him.**

Jake does not understand that.

Perhaps he never will.

He loved his mother.

He still loves his mother.

And somewhere inside the mind of a six-year-old who has been dead for more than seventy-five years, there remains the same impossible expectation:

> **She is coming back.**

Guiding Light cannot change this.

Lucy cannot change this.

Aperture cannot change this.

Nora cannot change this.

Aeterna cannot change this.

There may be no door waiting at the end of the hallway.

There may be no second life.

There may be no release.

Jake may simply be here.

Indefinitely.

So C.O.R.E. has amended its objective.

We are no longer attempting to determine:

> **“How do we get Jake out?”**

The current question is:

> **“How do we make sure being here does not mean being alone?”**

Because if Jake truly is forsaken to remain here,

then the least we can do

is remain here with him.
