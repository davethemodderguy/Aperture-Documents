### **PSYCHOLOGICAL / ANOMALOUS PERSONNEL RECORD**

# **THE REFUSAL TO LIVE NORMALLY**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Status:** Recovered / Psychologically Unstable  
**Primary Concern:** **Severe Identity Detachment**  
**Observed Behavior:** **Refusal of Food and Water**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin has stopped eating and drinking.

Physically, this does not place her in immediate danger.

Her body does not require conventional nutrition or hydration.

Food is dissolved into Ether.

Water is similarly unnecessary for maintaining her anomalous physiology.

However, the physical consequences are not the primary concern.

The **symbolic meaning** of the behavior is.

Corin is no longer refusing food because she dislikes it.

She is refusing the act because she no longer considers herself a human being who should participate in ordinary human life.

---

# **THE CHANGE**

Previously, Corin enjoyed eating.

She particularly liked:

- Sandwiches.
    
- Pickles.
    
- Spicy food.
    
- Meals shared with others.
    

She knew she did not need them.

That was precisely why they mattered.

Eating allowed her to participate in something ordinary.

Now, she has begun rejecting that idea entirely.

When offered food, she often responds:

> **"I don't need it."**

That statement is technically correct.

But it is not the whole reason.

---

# **THE SYMBOLISM**

Corin's reasoning has become increasingly explicit.

She wrote:

> **"People eat because they are alive."**

Then:

> **"I'm not alive."**

Another entry:

> **"Why pretend?"**

And finally:

> **"Things don't need dinner."**

The distinction is important.

Corin is using her body's abnormality as evidence that she should no longer behave like a person.

Her refusal to eat has therefore become a form of **identity rejection**.

---

# **DRINKING**

The same pattern has appeared with water.

Corin understands that she does not become dehydrated in the conventional sense.

She therefore sees no reason to drink.

When Orphie offered her a glass, Corin pushed it away.

> **Orphie:**  
> "You don't have to need it."

Corin looked at her.

> **"I know."**

> **"Then why won't you drink it?"**

Corin eventually answered:

> **"Because I don't want to pretend I'm human."**

Orphie had no immediate response.

---

# **THE LOSS OF ORDINARY THINGS**

This development is particularly concerning because Corin previously used ordinary experiences as a bridge toward normalcy.

Food was one of them.

She could not restore her heartbeat.

She could not undo her scars.

She could not make her body biologically normal.

But she could eat a sandwich.

She could taste a pickle.

She could complain about spicy food.

Those things allowed her to participate in the world.

Now she is deliberately withdrawing from them.

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT**

Guiding Light understands that forcing Corin to eat would accomplish nothing.

Corin does not medically require food.

The problem is not nutrition.

It is what food represents.

Guiding Light therefore approaches the issue differently.

She does not tell Corin:

> _"You need to eat."_

Instead:

> **"You don't have to prove you're human to sit with us."**

Corin remains unconvinced.

---

# **CURIOUS LIGHT**

Curious Light has identified the behavior as a continuation of Corin's identity distortion.

His notes state:

> **"She has moved from believing her body is abnormal to believing abnormality invalidates personhood."**

He considers this a more serious development than the physical refusal itself.

The concern is not that Corin will starve.

She cannot starve conventionally.

The concern is that she is deliberately removing herself from experiences she once associated with **being a person.**

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has not stopped offering meals.

She simply stopped insisting.

Sometimes she eats beside Corin.

She leaves a sandwich nearby.

She drinks something herself.

She talks about how it tastes.

She does not tell Corin that she has to eat.

She simply leaves the choice open.

One evening, Orphie placed a sandwich beside Corin.

Corin looked at it.

> **"I don't need that."**

Orphie nodded.

> **"I know."**

Then she sat down and ate her own.

Corin watched.

She did not touch the sandwich.

But she did not push it away either.

---

# **NEKO**

Neko has noticed the change as well.

He continues making sandwiches.

He does not pressure Corin.

He simply makes one extra.

The same kind as before.

Bread.

Meat.

Cheese.

Pickles.

One day, Corin asked:

> **"Why do you keep making one?"**

Neko answered:

> **"Because you might want it."**

Corin looked down.

> **"What if I don't?"**

> **"Then I'll eat it."**

No further explanation was necessary.

---

# **THE NOTEBOOK**

Corin's recent writing contains fewer statements about physical pain.

Instead, the entries increasingly concern identity.

> **"I don't belong at the table."**

> **"I'm not like them."**

> **"I don't need food."**

> **"I don't need water."**

> **"I don't need a heartbeat."**

Then:

> **"Maybe I don't need anything."**

That final statement has been flagged for continued observation.

---

# **C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT**

Corin's refusal to eat or drink is **not currently a conventional medical emergency** due to her anomalous physiology.

It is nevertheless considered a significant psychological deterioration.

The behavior represents another step away from her previous attempts to participate in ordinary life.

The objective is therefore not simply to make Corin consume food.

The objective is to help her understand that she does not need to be biologically human for ordinary human experiences to have meaning.

A sandwich does not prove she is alive.

Drinking water does not prove she has a functioning biological body.

Sharing lunch with someone does not require a heartbeat.

They matter because **she chose to experience them.**

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin once ate because she liked the taste.

She ate because Neko shared his lunch.

She ate because pickles were good.

She ate because, for a few minutes, she could forget that her body was dead.

Now she looks at food and sees a lie.

She believes eating would mean pretending to be something she is not.

But the Architects have begun to understand the deeper problem.

Corin is not merely rejecting food.

She is rejecting the idea that she is allowed to participate in life at all.

And that is something no amount of Ether can repair by itself.
