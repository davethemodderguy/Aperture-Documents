## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTIAL WELFARE DIVISION

**Document ID:** CORE-SH-OBS-147

**Status:** ACTIVE OBSERVATION

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A significant positive development has been observed in Sora's post-recovery behavior.

For the first time since his arrival at the Blacksite, Sora smiled.

The expression was brief but unmistakable.

Jake Akana witnessed the event and reacted with considerable excitement, reportedly becoming so startled by the unfamiliar sight that he briefly lost his composure.

Bao, widely regarded as the maternal figure of the Blacksite and SAFEHOUSE community, was equally delighted.

The incident became even more significant when Sora addressed Bao as:

**"Mom."**

This is the first recorded instance of Sora assigning a maternal designation to a SAFEHOUSE resident.

C.O.R.E. considers both developments important indicators of growing comfort and familial attachment.

---

## 2. INCIDENT OBSERVATION – THE FIRST SMILE

Sora has historically displayed a very limited range of visible positive expressions.

His shyness, communication difficulties, and extensive history of trauma have made it difficult for personnel to determine when he is comfortable.

During the observed interaction, Sora smiled.

There was no indication that the expression was forced or prompted as part of an exercise.

It appeared naturally.

Jake noticed almost immediately.

His reaction was disproportionate to the physical size of the expression.

He reportedly became extremely excited and startled, struggling to contain his reaction.

For someone who had spent months worrying about Sora, seeing him smile was evidently an extraordinary event.

Personnel should avoid repeatedly asking Sora to smile again.

The expression must not become a performance expected of him.

---

## 3. INCIDENT OBSERVATION – "MOM"

The second development occurred when Sora addressed Bao.

He called her:

**"Mom."**

The significance of this designation cannot be understated.

Bao has consistently provided maternal care and emotional support to SAFEHOUSE residents.

However, Sora's willingness to use the word suggests that he may now regard her as someone who occupies a similar place of safety and affection in his life.

For a child who once had a grandmother and subsequently lost his family, this is an important development.

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Bao is not replacing Sora's grandmother.

Nor is she replacing Mia.

She is becoming another person Sora can love.

Bao's reaction was reportedly very positive.

She was happy to hear it.

No further details are required to establish the significance of the moment.

---

## 4. BEHAVIORAL SIGNIFICANCE

These two developments are being evaluated together.

Sora's first smile and his use of a maternal designation may indicate increasing emotional security within SAFEHOUSE.

His current social environment includes:

- Jake, whom he treats like an older brother.
    
- Bao, whom he has now called Mom.
    
- Other SAFEHOUSE residents who have gradually become familiar faces.
    

This is a meaningful change from his previous behavior.

Previously, Sora avoided people, hid from personnel, and refused assistance even when his physical condition deteriorated.

Now, he has begun displaying spontaneous positive emotion and voluntarily expressing familial attachment.

This does not mean his fear has disappeared.

He may still become frightened, withdraw, or refuse interaction.

Such setbacks must not be interpreted as the loss of his progress.

---

## 5. PERSONNEL GUIDANCE

The following recommendations are issued:

1. Do not pressure Sora to smile again.
    
2. Bao may continue interacting with him naturally and affectionately, respecting his boundaries.
    
3. Jake should be allowed to celebrate the moment without overwhelming Sora.
    
4. Do not insist that Sora call Bao "Mom" every time they interact.
    
5. Continue maintaining Sora's personal den and communication equipment.
    
6. Preserve his relationship with Jake without making either child responsible for the other's wellbeing.
    
7. Treat spontaneous expressions of affection as voluntary and meaningful.
    

A final note for personnel:

If Sora calls Bao "Mom" again, there is no need to correct him, question him, or turn it into an official ceremony.

Just answer him.

---

## 6. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The first smile.

It took us a long time to see one.

For a while, Sora was a little boy who hid in a maintenance locker, ate discarded food, and ran whenever someone approached.

Then he disappeared.

We found him in the snow, holding Mia's collar and crying.

He ran again.

Jake searched for him, collapsed, recovered, and searched again.

Eventually, they came home.

And now, Sora has smiled.

Jake apparently reacted as though he had just witnessed the greatest anomalous event in the history of Aperture.

We cannot entirely blame him.

He has spent so long worrying about his friend that seeing him genuinely happy must have felt almost unbelievable.

And Bao?

Bao was happy too.

Then Sora called her Mom.

There are many things Sora has lost that nobody can give back.

His grandmother cannot return. Mia cannot return. The farm cannot become the home it once was.

But perhaps he can have a home again.

Perhaps he can have someone who brings him food without making him feel guilty.

Someone who lets him stay close.

Someone who answers when he calls.

And perhaps he can have a mother again, even if the word takes a little while to become familiar.

**Today, Sora smiled. Today, he called someone Mom.**

For a child who had spent so long believing that everyone he loved would eventually disappear, these are not small things.

Bao has another child to worry about now.

Judging by her reaction, she doesn't seem to mind.
