## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — FIFTEEN HOURS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD SAFETY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 5  
**Condition:** Fragile / Nonverbal / Extremely Quiet  
**Incident Duration:** **15+ HOURS**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

This incident is considered one of the most serious examples of Alice's tendency to disappear into the background.

Alice was accidentally left inside a Blacksite locker room.

She remained there for more than fifteen hours.

The most concerning detail was not simply that nobody realized she was missing.

It was that—

**Alice deliberately hid herself.**

Not to escape.

Not because she was playing.

Because she believed staying out of the way was the correct thing to do.

---

# **INITIAL ENTRY**

Alice entered the locker room while following an employee.

She remained nearby while they collected equipment.

The employee left.

The door closed.

Alice was still inside.

Nobody noticed.

---

# **THE FIRST HOURS**

Alice initially waited near the door.

She expected someone to return.

When nobody did, she did not attempt to leave.

She sat down.

Eventually, she moved farther into the room.

She found a space underneath a bench.

It was dark.

Quiet.

Out of the way.

Alice crawled underneath it.

And stayed there.

---

# **WHY SHE HID**

When asked later why she had gone underneath the bench, Alice wrote:

> **"People look."**

The interviewer asked:

> "You wanted people to find you?"

Alice shook her head.

She wrote:

> **"No."**

Then:

> **"People busy."**

The interviewer asked:

> "Then why hide?"

Alice wrote:

> **"So don't make trouble."**

---

# **THE SEARCH**

Eventually, personnel realized Alice was missing.

A search began.

The SAFEHOUSE was checked.

The Treehouse was checked.

Her usual routes were checked.

The scooter tracker was checked.

The garden was checked.

The cafeteria was checked.

Several exterior areas were checked.

The locker room was **not initially identified as a likely location.**

Alice's scooter was not there.

No one had seen her enter.

No one had heard her.

And Alice wasn't answering calls.

Because she couldn't.

And she wasn't using her whistle.

---

# **THE LOCKER ROOM**

The locker room had already been checked once.

Nothing was found.

This was because the searcher looked at the obvious locations.

Benches.

Lockers.

Showers.

Storage cabinets.

The center of the room.

They did not look underneath every bench.

Alice was there.

Curled up in the darkness.

Completely silent.

---

# **FIFTEEN HOURS**

The next morning, a maintenance employee entered the locker room.

While cleaning beneath a bench, they noticed something unusual.

A small white ear.

They stopped.

> "...Alice?"

The ear moved.

Alice slowly looked out.

She was alive.

Conscious.

Exhausted.

Still holding her journal.

---

# **DISCOVERY**

The employee immediately contacted SAFEHOUSE personnel.

Corin arrived.

She found Alice still beneath the bench.

Corin crouched down.

> "Alice."

Alice looked at her.

No crying.

No anger.

No complaint.

She simply crawled out.

Corin picked her up.

Alice rested against her.

---

# **THE JOURNAL**

Alice's journal contained several entries from the previous evening.

They were short.

> **"Big room empty."**

> **"Alice wait."**

Later:

> **"Maybe everyone sleep."**

Later:

> **"Alice sleep too."**

And finally:

> **"Don't make people look."**

The last sentence had been written very lightly.

---

# **THE MOST DISTURBING DETAIL**

Alice had apparently heard personnel searching elsewhere.

She could hear people walking through nearby corridors.

She could hear doors opening.

She could hear voices.

But she did not reveal herself.

She did not use the whistle.

She did not turn on the flashlight.

She did not crawl out.

She remained hidden.

Because she believed that if people were searching, they were already busy.

She did not want to make their job harder.

---

# **WHEN ASKED WHY SHE DIDN'T COME OUT**

Corin asked:

> "Alice, if you heard people, why didn't you come out?"

Alice looked at her.

She pointed toward her mouth.

Then toward the hallway.

Then shook her head.

She wrote:

> **"They looking for Alice."**

Corin nodded.

> "Yes."

Alice wrote:

> **"Alice make more work."**

Corin's expression fell.

---

# **ALICE'S UNDERSTANDING**

Alice had misunderstood the search entirely.

She didn't understand:

> **"We are looking for you because we are worried."**

She understood:

> **"Everyone is busy looking for something."**

And therefore, in her mind, the kindest thing she could do was remain quiet.

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Alice was examined immediately.

She was dehydrated and extremely tired.

She had not eaten normally during the period of isolation.

Fortunately, no permanent injury resulted.

Her stronger immune system prevented illness from developing.

She was returned to the Treehouse and placed under observation.

Corin stayed with her.

---

# **THE AFTERNOON**

Alice slept for several hours.

When she woke, she was still wearing her hat.

Her journal was beside her.

Corin was sitting nearby.

Alice looked at her.

Then wrote:

> **"Sorry."**

Corin immediately shook her head.

> "No."

Alice looked confused.

> "You don't apologize for being forgotten."

Alice stared at her.

She didn't seem to understand.

Corin hugged her.

> "You never have to."

Alice remained quiet.

---

# **PROTOCOL REVISION**

The incident resulted in an immediate revision of SAFEHOUSE missing-person procedures.

Alice is no longer treated as a conventional missing child during searches.

Personnel must assume:

**Alice may be nearby.**

**Alice may be silent.**

**Alice may be hiding.**

**Alice may be able to hear the search.**

**Alice may deliberately avoid being found.**

Every enclosed area must therefore be physically inspected.

Not visually glanced at.

**Inspected.**

Under furniture.

Behind equipment.

Inside safe spaces.

Behind doors.

Everywhere a small child could reasonably fit.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

The fifteen hours themselves were horrifying.

But the part that stayed with everyone was what happened when Alice was found.

She didn't cry.

She didn't say she was scared.

She didn't ask why nobody came.

She didn't blame anyone.

She simply crawled out from underneath the bench and allowed herself to be picked up.

As though fifteen hours alone was merely something that had happened.

As though it wasn't worth mentioning.

As though the only thing she had done wrong was potentially make someone search longer than necessary.

Alice's silence is no longer considered merely a communication problem.

It is a **survival risk.**

Because if Alice is frightened, she may hide.

If she is lost, she may wait.

If she is trapped, she may stay quiet.

And if people come looking—

she may hide even deeper.

Not because she doesn't want to be found.

But because she doesn't want to be a burden.
