### MEDICAL RESEARCH & TREATMENT EXECUTIVE

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-MED-4C-JAKE-341  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — MEDICAL / RESIDENT WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE RECOVERY  
**SUBJECT:** Jake — First Vocalization Following Nullwalker Incident  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — JAKE'S ROOM

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake spoke.

For the first time in approximately **five weeks**, Jake produced a recognizable vocalization.

The sound was extremely quiet.

Barely above a whisper.

His vocal cords remain significantly damaged following prolonged exposure to the blizzard and subsequent physiological deterioration during his Nullwalker state.

However:

> **Jake's voice has returned.**

The first person he spoke to was **Anby.**

No medical staff were present.

No speech assessment was being conducted.

No one had asked him to try.

He simply decided to speak.

---

# 1. PRECEDING CONDITIONS

Jake had been resting in his room for approximately forty minutes.

Anby was sitting beside his bed.

She had been reading aloud.

The material was unimportant.

Anby had selected a completely ordinary document from the SAFEHOUSE archive.

Jake had been listening.

His teddy bear was resting against his chest.

His eyes were partially closed.

His breathing was steady.

Anby continued reading.

Jake remained quiet.

---

# 2. THE VOCALIZATION

Anby reached the end of a page.

She paused.

Jake opened his eyes.

He looked at her.

Anby noticed.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Hmm?"

Jake hesitated.

His mouth opened slightly.

Nothing came out.

Anby did not react.

She simply waited.

Jake tried again.

The sound was extremely faint.

Almost impossible to hear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Anby."

Anby stopped breathing for approximately one second.

She stared at him.

Jake looked slightly confused.

He had not expected such a reaction.

Anby leaned closer.

> **ANBY:**  
> "...Did you just say my name?"

Jake nodded.

Anby's expression changed immediately.

She smiled.

Not the usual small smile.

A much larger one.

Her eyes became visibly wet.

She looked away for a moment.

Then back at him.

> **ANBY:**  
> "You talked."

Jake nodded again.

His voice was barely present.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...yeah."

---

# 3. ANBY'S RESPONSE

Anby immediately lowered her voice.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Don't force it."

Jake nodded.

She carefully placed the book aside.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Your throat still hurts?"

Jake shook his head.

Then hesitated.

He touched his throat.

He nodded.

Anby understood.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Okay."

She did not ask him to say anything else.

She did not ask him to repeat himself.

She did not ask:

> "Can you say my name again?"

She simply remained beside him.

Jake looked at her.

After several seconds, he whispered:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...missed you."

Anby froze.

---

# 4. SECOND VOCALIZATION

Anby looked at him.

Jake's voice had been so quiet that she initially wondered whether she had imagined it.

She leaned slightly closer.

> **ANBY:**  
> "What?"

Jake swallowed.

His throat visibly hurt.

He still tried again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...missed you."

Anby's eyes filled with tears.

She covered her mouth with one hand.

Jake watched her.

His expression became concerned.

He reached toward her.

Anby immediately took his hand.

> **ANBY:**  
> "I'm here."

Jake nodded.

Anby squeezed his hand gently.

> **ANBY:**  
> "I'm still here."

Jake closed his eyes.

His grip remained around her fingers.

---

# 5. MEDICAL OBSERVATION

Approximately thirty seconds after the second vocalization, Jake became visibly tired.

His breathing became slightly heavier.

His voice did not return to conversational volume.

Medical personnel were notified.

Twiggy arrived shortly afterward.

She did not ask Jake to speak.

She assessed him through nonverbal communication and basic observation.

Jake indicated:

- mild throat discomfort;
    
- fatigue;
    
- no respiratory distress;
    
- no dizziness;
    
- no acute pain.
    

Twiggy documented the event.

She specifically noted that **vocal recovery should not be accelerated through forced speech exercises without medical clearance.**

Jake's ability to speak does not mean his vocal system has fully recovered.

---

# 6. ANBY'S ACCOUNT

Anby was later asked what Jake had said.

She remained quiet for several seconds.

Then:

> **ANBY:**  
> "He said my name."

Pause.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Then he said he missed me."

The interviewer asked whether Jake had said anything else.

Anby shook her head.

> **ANBY:**  
> "That's enough."

---

# 7. SIGNIFICANCE

The first words Jake chose to speak after five weeks were not related to:

- food;
    
- pain;
    
- his condition;
    
- the Nullwalker event;
    
- the blizzard;
    
- his teddy bear;
    
- his room;
    
- or his medical treatment.
    

They were directed toward another person.

Specifically, Anby.

This should not be interpreted as evidence that Jake has fully recovered emotionally or physically.

It does, however, demonstrate that his voice is beginning to return.

More importantly, the event occurred spontaneously.

Jake was not pressured.

He was not tested.

He was not instructed to speak.

He simply wanted to.

---

# 8. C.O.R.E. RECOMMENDATION

Personnel are instructed:

**Do not celebrate loudly around Jake.**

**Do not repeatedly ask him to speak.**

**Do not request demonstrations of his voice.**

**Do not make him repeat words for confirmation.**

**Do not treat every sound as a medical milestone requiring observation.**

If Jake chooses to speak:

Listen.

Respond normally.

Allow him to stop whenever he wants.

His voice returning should remain **his decision**, not a performance expected by everyone around him.

---

# 9. ANBY — LATER OBSERVATION

Several hours later, Anby returned to Jake's room.

Jake was awake.

Anby sat beside him.

Neither spoke.

She opened the same book.

She began reading again.

After several pages, Jake looked toward her.

Anby glanced over.

Jake smiled faintly.

Then, barely audible:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Again."

Anby blinked.

> **ANBY:**  
> "You want me to keep reading?"

Jake nodded.

Anby smiled.

She turned the page.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Okay."

She continued reading.

Jake closed his eyes.

His teddy bear's heartbeat remained steady beneath his hand.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Five weeks of silence.

Five weeks without a single word.

Then, finally—

One name.

One sentence.

Barely louder than breathing.

> **"...Anby."**

> **"...missed you."**

It was not a speech.

It was not a declaration.

It was not proof that everything was fixed.

It was simply Jake's voice.

Still fragile.

Still quiet.

But his.

And Anby heard it.

**— C.O.R.E. Medical Research & Treatment Executive**
