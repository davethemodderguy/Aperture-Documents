### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

New information regarding Sora's physiology requires a fundamental revision of his medical classification.

Sora cannot die.

Sora cannot fully live, either.

He exists in a suspended state between the two conditions. His body remains susceptible to physical injury, but does not possess the regenerative mechanisms ordinarily associated with recovery.

This distinction is critical.

Sora is not invulnerable. He does not automatically recover from injuries. His inability to reach death does not mean that damage to his body is inconsequential.

An injury can remain an injury.

A wound can continue bleeding.

Pain can persist.

### 2. CONFIRMED PHYSIOLOGICAL CHARACTERISTICS

**2.1 — Non-Terminal State**

Sora exists in an anomalous condition that prevents his death.

The exact mechanism responsible for this state remains undetermined.

C.O.R.E. will not classify him as conventionally immortal without further investigation. His condition is better described as a state of biological suspension between life and death.

**2.2 — Absence of Regeneration**

Sora has no known regenerative factor.

Damaged tissue does not automatically restore itself. Injuries must be treated through appropriate medical intervention, where treatment is possible.

**2.3 — Persistent Hemorrhage**

A severe injury, including an injury to the neck, can cause Sora to bleed continuously.

The bleeding does not necessarily stop because of his anomalous condition. It must be stopped through appropriate intervention.

This establishes that his inability to die does not prevent potentially catastrophic physical damage.

**2.4 — Pain and Physical Suffering**

There is no basis for assuming that Sora is insensible to pain.

Personnel must treat him as a physically vulnerable child, not as an indestructible anomalous object.

### 3. REASSESSMENT OF THE DEN INCIDENT

This information substantially changes the urgency of Sora's prolonged isolation.

His failure to obtain food and water cannot be dismissed on the assumption that he will eventually die and thereby be rescued from his suffering.

Nor can personnel assume that he will recover naturally from the consequences of prolonged deprivation.

His body may remain in a state of suffering without reaching a terminal endpoint.

This creates a particularly serious welfare concern:

**Sora may be capable of enduring harm indefinitely without being capable of recovering from it independently.**

The current welfare intervention must therefore proceed regardless of his anomalous inability to die.

Medical personnel are instructed to assess hydration, nutrition, existing injuries, and general physical condition. His anomalous physiology must be considered when determining treatment, rather than used as a reason to delay it.

### 4. MEDICAL AND PERSONNEL DIRECTIVES

- Never describe Sora as invulnerable.
    
- Never assume an injury will heal without treatment.
    
- Treat bleeding as an active medical emergency.
    
- Do not use his inability to die to justify dangerous experiments, punishment, or deliberate injury.
    
- Do not interpret prolonged survival as evidence of acceptable physical condition.
    
- Maintain ordinary standards of pain management, dignity, and child welfare.
    
- Record the duration and progression of injuries, especially those that fail to resolve.
    

Any employee claiming that Sora cannot be harmed because he cannot die is to be corrected immediately.

### 5. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There is a terrible misunderstanding hidden inside the phrase _cannot die_.

People hear it and imagine safety.

They imagine someone who can endure anything, someone who never needs protection, someone for whom pain has no lasting consequence.

Sora is none of those things.

He can be hurt. He can bleed. He can be frightened. He can remain wounded.

He simply cannot reach the ending that would ordinarily follow.

We must not allow that condition to turn him into someone the Blacksite believes it can neglect.

A child who cannot die still deserves to live comfortably.

And a child who cannot recover on his own deserves even more care, not less.
