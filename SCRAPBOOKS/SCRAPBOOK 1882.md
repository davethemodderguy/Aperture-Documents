# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### PERSONNEL CONDUCT & SAFEHOUSE OVERSIGHT DIVISION

**Document ID:** CORE-SH-INC-127  
**Classification:** INTERNAL — PERSONNEL CONDUCT / CHILD WELFARE  
**Status:** ACTIVE INVESTIGATION  
**Subject:** Deliberate Harassment of Resident Sora During Nighttime Cafeteria Visits  
**Location:** Blacksite Cafeteria  
**Involved Personnel:** Multiple employees, identities pending confirmation  
**Affected Resident:** Sora

---

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

C.O.R.E. has received reports that several employees have been deliberately waiting in the cafeteria during nighttime hours to frighten Sora.

Their apparent objective is entertainment.

Sora, who has been avoiding human contact since his arrival at the Blacksite, occasionally leaves his den at night to search the cafeteria's discarded-food bucket. He does not approach employees, request assistance, or attempt to interact with anyone.

Certain personnel have apparently recognized this routine and begun treating it as an opportunity for amusement.

When Sora appears, they deliberately startle him.

Sora's response is immediate flight.

He runs on all fours.

This is not a game. It is not an invitation to chase him. It is a frightened child attempting to escape people who have deliberately made his already limited food-seeking routine unsafe.

C.O.R.E. considers this conduct unacceptable.

### 2. INCIDENT OBSERVATIONS

The reported pattern is consistent:

1. Employees remain in or around the cafeteria during nighttime hours.
    
2. They wait for Sora to appear.
    
3. They intentionally frighten him through sudden movements, noises, or other deliberate interruptions.
    
4. Sora immediately flees, frequently dropping whatever food he has managed to collect.
    
5. The employees reportedly find his reaction amusing.
    

The most concerning detail is Sora's method of escape.

When sufficiently frightened, he drops to all fours and runs.

Personnel are reminded that Sora is a young Dog Thiren. His movement on all fours is not a performance for their entertainment. It is an instinctive response to perceived danger.

There is no justification for deliberately provoking it.

### 3. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Sora already demonstrates considerable difficulty trusting people.

He has established a private den, avoids daytime activity, refuses ordinary meals, and relies almost exclusively on discarded cafeteria leftovers. Even when physically unwell, he has shown a tendency to manage the consequences alone rather than seek assistance.

The cafeteria is therefore one of the few places he currently feels able to visit.

By turning it into a place where employees lie in wait to frighten him, personnel are actively undermining what little sense of security he has managed to establish.

C.O.R.E. is particularly concerned that Sora may respond by abandoning nighttime food collection altogether.

Given his existing eating habits, this would constitute a significant welfare risk.

**The fact that Sora runs away does not mean the incident ends without consequence.**

He may return to his den hungry, frightened, and increasingly convinced that being seen by another person is dangerous.

### 4. PERSONNEL DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

- Deliberately waiting for Sora to appear in order to frighten him is prohibited.
    
- Chasing Sora, blocking his escape, cornering him, or attempting to provoke further reactions is prohibited.
    
- Recording, photographing, or circulating footage of his frightened behavior for entertainment is prohibited.
    
- Employees who encounter Sora must give him a clear route away and avoid sudden movements.
    
- Cafeteria personnel are instructed not to interfere with his access to discarded food while a safer, non-threatening feeding arrangement is being developed.
    
- Witnesses must report further incidents to C.O.R.E. Personnel Conduct.
    

Identified participants will be subject to formal investigation and disciplinary action.

**This is not a prank. It is targeted harassment of a vulnerable resident.**

### 5. ADDENDUM — THE PURPOSE OF THE CAFETERIA

C.O.R.E. wishes to emphasize a distinction.

Sora does not visit the cafeteria because he enjoys being there. He visits because he is hungry.

He does not run on all fours because he wants an audience. He does so because he is frightened.

And he does not return to his den because he has finished playing.

He returns because he believes the people in the cafeteria are dangerous.

Employees who have participated in this behavior are instructed to consider what they have actually accomplished: they have made a six-year-old child afraid to collect food.

That is not a joke worth defending.

---

### 6. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

We have already found Sora shivering in a broken locker, eating food that even our cafeteria staff considered unfit for consumption, and cleaning up after himself when he became sick.

He asks for nothing.

He avoids everyone.

And when someone frightens him, he runs.

Some employees apparently found that last part funny.

Let this document make one thing clear: **Sora is not cafeteria entertainment.**

He is a child who has not yet learned that the people around him can be trusted.

We are supposed to be giving him reasons to learn otherwise.
