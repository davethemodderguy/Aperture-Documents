# Executive Summary

Phoenix Raffedella is an exceptionally capable cook.

This was initially considered an incidental personal skill.

It is no longer considered incidental.

Phoenix demonstrates a consistently high level of culinary competence, particularly when preparing meals for other people.

Her cooking has become sufficiently well regarded among SAFEHOUSE residents that several residents have independently begun requesting meals from her.

Phoenix insists this is:

> “Just cooking.”

C.O.R.E. disagrees.

---

# 1. COOKING ABILITY

Phoenix demonstrates strong competency across:

- meal preparation;
    
- seasoning;
    
- baking;
    
- soups and stews;
    
- meat preparation;
    
- vegetables;
    
- sauces;
    
- desserts;
    
- food presentation;
    
- portion control;
    
- adapting meals to available ingredients.
    

She also demonstrates an unusual ability to prepare food while simultaneously handling other tasks.

This is particularly impressive given her tendency to become tired without warning.

---

# 2. HER COOKING STYLE

Phoenix does not cook extravagantly.

She cooks **comfortably.**

Her meals tend to be warm, filling, practical, and carefully prepared.

She appears particularly interested in making food that people will actually want to eat.

When asked why she puts so much effort into cooking:

> **Phoenix:** “Because they're hungry.”

That was the entire explanation.

---

# 3. SAFEHOUSE RESPONSE

Residents have developed several informal opinions regarding Phoenix's cooking.

### Bao

Bao has described Phoenix's food as:

> “Very good.”

Given Bao's own cooking ability, this was considered a significant compliment.

Phoenix became visibly embarrassed.

---

### Neri

Neri rarely comments on food.

However, when Phoenix placed a slice of cake in front of her, Neri ate the entire piece.

She then quietly pushed the plate back toward Phoenix.

Phoenix interpreted this correctly.

She provided another slice.

No further communication was required.

---

### Nora

Nora is considerably less subtle.

After tasting Phoenix's cooking:

> **Nora:** “PHOENIX.”

Phoenix looked up.

> **Phoenix:** “Yes?”

> **Nora:** “YOU CAN COOK.”

Pause.

> **Phoenix:** “...Thank you?”

Nora immediately asked whether there was more.

There was.

Nora returned approximately four minutes later.

There was still more.

---

### Jake

Jake initially watched Phoenix cook from a safe distance.

He eventually approached.

> **Jake:** “What is that?”

> **Phoenix:** “Dinner.”

Jake looked at the food.

Then at Phoenix.

> **Jake:** “Can I have some?”

Phoenix smiled.

> **Phoenix:** “Of course.”

Jake ate.

Afterward:

> **Jake:** “I like it.”

Phoenix stopped moving for approximately three seconds.

She appeared unusually pleased.

---

# 4. PHOENIX AS A COOK

C.O.R.E. has identified a notable difference between Phoenix's medical work and her cooking.

When treating someone:

Phoenix is clinical.

Focused.

Efficient.

When cooking:

Phoenix becomes quieter.

More relaxed.

She pays attention to small details.

She tastes sauces.

Adjusts seasoning.

Checks temperatures.

Rearranges plates.

And occasionally hums.

This is one of the few activities in which Phoenix appears to work without treating the activity as an obligation.

---

# 5. THE IRONY

Phoenix spends much of her professional life dealing with:

**injury.**

**pain.**

**blood.**

**Ether instability.**

**death.**

**resuscitation.**

And yet one of her favorite ways to care for people involves something considerably simpler.

Food.

A warm meal.

A cup of something hot.

A plate placed in front of someone without asking for anything in return.

It is perhaps unsurprising that she is good at it.

Phoenix has spent most of her life learning how to keep people alive.

Cooking is simply a much nicer way of doing that.

---

# 6. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

There is one recurring behavior worth noting.

Phoenix will cook for everyone else.

Then forget to make herself a plate.

This has happened repeatedly.

When confronted:

> **Phoenix:** “I wasn't hungry.”

This statement is frequently followed by someone placing a plate in front of her.

Phoenix usually stares at it.

Then eats.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Phoenix is not merely competent in the kitchen.

She is **very good.**

Her cooking has become another form of care.

Unlike her medical work, it does not involve emergency procedures.

Unlike Transcendense, nobody has to die first.

Unlike her sleep schedule, it generally works exactly as intended.

And unlike most of Phoenix's medical records:

**the kitchen has a remarkably low mortality rate.**

Phoenix can save your life.

Phoenix can treat your injuries.

Phoenix can survive being crushed by a security door.

And apparently—

Phoenix can also make an excellent dinner.
