# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The most effective immediate method identified for calming Jake following prolonged, involuntary isolation is the provision of a warm beverage.

More specifically:

**Matcha tea.**

C.O.R.E. acknowledges that this is not the intervention most personnel would have predicted.

Following approximately five hours of being locked alone in his room by an unidentified individual, Jake was found severely distressed.

He was frightened, physically withdrawn, and reluctant to separate himself from the immediate vicinity of his teddy bear.

Attempts to reassure him verbally produced limited results.

Repeated explanations that the door was now unlocked did not immediately restore his sense of safety.

Questions concerning the incident were similarly ineffective.

However, when a trusted caregiver offered him a warm cup of matcha, Jake accepted it.

He held the cup with both hands.

He drank slowly.

And, over the following minutes, his distress began to decrease.

He did not immediately become cheerful.

He did not suddenly stop fearing that he might be left alone again.

He simply began to settle.

His breathing became more regular.

His shoulders relaxed.

He stopped repeatedly looking toward the door.

Eventually, he leaned against the person sitting beside him.

No further verbal reassurance was necessary at that moment.

The intervention was therefore recorded as a successful component of his immediate recovery.

**Important qualification:** Matcha is not a treatment for trauma, nor is its calming effect guaranteed. The apparent benefit may be associated with warmth, familiarity, predictable routine, taste, and the presence of a trusted caregiver. C.O.R.E. has not established which factor is most significant.

Nevertheless, Jake's preference is sufficiently consistent to warrant inclusion in his individualized care plan.

# 1. INCIDENT RECONSTRUCTION

At approximately 08:10, Jake entered his room.

At approximately 13:12, personnel discovered that he had been unable to leave.

The door had been locked from the outside.

Jake had no means of opening it.

The identity and intent of the individual responsible remain under investigation.

No assumption is to be made that the confinement was accidental.

No assumption is to be made that the responsible party understood the psychological consequences.

The immediate outcome is clear regardless of motive.

Jake had been confined alone for approximately five hours.

For a resident who has recently experienced a violent assault and repeated separations from trusted caregivers, this duration represents a significant welfare incident.

During the initial recovery period, Jake was reluctant to move away from his teddy bear.

He repeatedly looked toward the door.

When personnel approached, he watched them carefully before allowing them closer.

He did not initially provide a detailed account of what had happened.

This was not interpreted as refusal to cooperate.

Personnel were instructed not to demand an explanation while he remained visibly distressed.

The immediate priority was to restore a sense of safety.

Investigation could follow.

---

# 2. INITIAL RESPONSE — INEFFECTIVE REASSURANCE

The first responding caregiver attempted to reassure Jake that the incident was over.

**CAREGIVER:**  
"Jake, it's okay. The door is open now."

Jake looked at the doorway.

He did not move.

**CAREGIVER:**  
"You can come out."

No response.

**CAREGIVER:**  
"Nobody is keeping you in here anymore."

Jake tightened his grip on the teddy bear.

**CAREGIVER:**  
"You're safe now."

Jake looked toward the corridor again.

The caregiver stopped speaking.

The statements were not necessarily incorrect.

The door was open.

Jake was no longer physically confined.

But the statements did not address the problem Jake was experiencing.

The fact that a door had opened did not establish that it would remain open.

The fact that someone had arrived did not establish that they would stay.

And being told that he was safe did not immediately make him feel safe.

Personnel were reminded that reassurance cannot substitute for observable changes in the environment.

Jake needed the door to remain accessible.

He needed someone trustworthy to remain nearby.

And he needed to regain a sense of control over what happened next.

---

# 3. THE MATCHA INCIDENT

**LOCATION:** JAKE'S ROOM  
**TIME:** 13:24 HOURS

Bao was contacted to assist.

She entered only after Jake had acknowledged her presence.

She did not immediately approach him.

Instead, she sat nearby and waited.

For several minutes, neither spoke.

Eventually, Bao noticed a cup of tea on a nearby service trolley.

She asked whether Jake wanted some.

**BAO:**  
"Would you like some tea, sweetheart?"

Jake looked at her.

Then at the cup.

He gave a small nod.

Bao checked the beverage before offering it to him, ensuring that it was a safe temperature.

It was matcha.

She placed the cup within his reach rather than insisting that he take it from her hands.

Jake hesitated.

Then he reached for it.

He held the cup with both hands.

For a while, he did nothing else.

Bao did not ask him to drink.

She did not ask him to explain what had happened.

She simply remained beside him.

Eventually, Jake took a sip.

Then another.

His attention shifted from the doorway to the cup.

He looked at the surface of the tea.

He rotated the cup slightly between his hands.

He drank again.

Several minutes passed.

Bao remained where she was.

Jake eventually moved closer and rested against her side, keeping the cup securely supported.

**BAO:**  
"Is it good?"

Jake nodded.

**BAO:**  
"Do you want me to stay?"

A pause.

Jake nodded again.

Bao settled more comfortably beside him.

**BAO:**  
"All right. I'll stay."

Jake returned to his tea.

He did not ask how long she would remain.

He did not ask when she would leave.

For the first time since the door had been opened, his attention remained on something other than the possibility of being confined again.

### OBSERVATION

Jake's distress decreased gradually during the interaction.

The most significant change was not his acceptance of the tea itself.

It was his willingness to redirect his attention toward an ordinary activity while a trusted person remained beside him.

He was no longer required to monitor the doorway continuously.

He could hold something warm.

He could taste something familiar.

He could sit without being questioned.

He could allow Bao to remain near him without needing to explain why her presence mattered.

The matcha appeared to provide a useful point of stability during the initial recovery period.

---

# 4. WHY MATCHA?

C.O.R.E. has not identified a definitive explanation for Jake's preference.

Several possibilities are under consideration.

### A. Familiarity

Jake may associate matcha with a familiar routine.

A recognizable drink can provide continuity when the surrounding circumstances feel unpredictable.

### B. Warmth

Holding a warm cup may provide a simple sensory anchor.

The physical sensation gives Jake something immediate and predictable to focus on.

### C. Taste and preparation

Jake may simply prefer matcha.

Not every behavioral preference requires a complicated anomalous explanation.

### D. Caregiver association

If the beverage is offered by someone he trusts, the experience may become associated with companionship rather than isolation.

The tea is not merely something he drinks.

It becomes part of a familiar interaction: someone prepares it, brings it to him, and remains while he drinks.

### E. Predictability

Tea has a beginning, a middle, and an end.

The cup is prepared.

The beverage is offered.

Jake decides whether to drink.

He can take small sips at his own pace.

Nobody needs to rush him.

In contrast, the events leading to his confinement were beyond his control.

The difference may be significant.

### F. Unknown individual preference

It is also possible that Jake simply finds matcha particularly comforting for reasons he cannot or does not wish to articulate.

C.O.R.E. does not intend to interrogate him until a definitive explanation is obtained.

**A coping preference does not need to be fully explained before personnel are permitted to respect it.**

---

# 5. MATCHA RESPONSE PROTOCOL

Matcha may be offered as an optional comfort measure when Jake is frightened, provided he is alert enough to drink safely and has no medical restriction against it.

The recommended procedure is as follows.

**Step 1 — Restore immediate safety.**

Ensure the door is accessible and that Jake is no longer confined.

Do not attempt to calm him while leaving the source of the distress unresolved.

**Step 2 — Establish a trusted presence.**

A familiar caregiver should remain nearby.

Do not place the responsibility for calming Jake on the beverage alone.

**Step 3 — Offer, do not impose.**

Ask whether he would like matcha.

If he refuses, do not insist.

**Step 4 — Check the beverage.**

Ensure it is an appropriate temperature and prepared in a manner suitable for Jake.

Because matcha contains caffeine, offer a small, age-appropriate portion and avoid repeated servings, particularly near bedtime. Decaffeinated alternatives may be preferable when available.

**Step 5 — Allow time.**

Do not demand that Jake drink quickly.

Do not ask repeated questions while he is trying to settle.

**Step 6 — Remain present.**

The caregiver should continue providing predictable companionship.

If the caregiver must leave, an explicit handover must occur before departure.

**Step 7 — Monitor his condition.**

If Jake remains severely distressed, becomes unusually unresponsive, has difficulty breathing, or shows other concerning symptoms, personnel must not assume that tea is sufficient. Appropriate medical assessment remains necessary.

**Step 8 — Do not make the beverage conditional.**

Jake must never be told that he can have tea only if he stops crying, explains what happened, or behaves calmly.

The tea is offered because he may find it comforting.

It is not a reward for being an easy patient.

---

# 6. WHAT PERSONNEL MUST NOT DO

Following an incident of prolonged confinement, personnel are prohibited from:

- telling Jake that five hours was not particularly long;
    
- insisting that the incident is over simply because the door has been unlocked;
    
- leaving him alone to see whether he can manage;
    
- using matcha as a reason to withdraw supervision;
    
- repeatedly questioning him about the incident while he is distressed;
    
- demanding that he explain why he is frightened;
    
- making jokes about locking doors in front of him;
    
- deliberately closing the door to demonstrate that it is safe;
    
- or treating his eventual calmness as proof that the incident had no lasting effect.
    

Personnel must also avoid assuming that every future episode of distress can be resolved with tea.

The matcha response is an observed coping preference, not a universal treatment.

If Jake accepts the tea and remains frightened, the correct response is to continue supporting him.

If he refuses it, the correct response is to find another appropriate way to help.

If he drinks it and falls asleep, the caregiver must still ensure that his environment remains safe and that his teddy bear stays within reach.

**The objective is Jake's welfare, not successful completion of a tea-based calming procedure.**

---

# 7. FOLLOW-UP OBSERVATION

Approximately twenty minutes after receiving the matcha, Jake was sitting beside Bao.

The cup was nearly empty.

His teddy bear remained pressed against his side.

He was no longer staring continuously at the doorway.

Bao asked whether he wanted another cup.

Jake shook his head.

She accepted the answer.

After a short silence, Jake spoke.

**JAKE:**  
"Are you staying?"

**BAO:**  
"Yes."

Jake looked at her.

**JAKE:**  
"Even if I sleep?"

**BAO:**  
"Yes. And if I need to leave later, I'll arrange for someone else to stay with you before I go."

Jake considered this.

**JAKE:**  
"Promise?"

Bao did not offer an unconditional promise that she could never be interrupted.

Instead, she answered the question she could reasonably guarantee.

**BAO:**  
"I promise I won't knowingly leave you without telling you who's staying with you."

Jake lowered his eyes to the cup.

He appeared to consider the answer for several seconds.

Then he leaned against her.

Bao put an arm around him.

Jake closed his eyes.

He did not fall asleep immediately.

But he stopped watching the door.

For that afternoon, it was enough.

# 8. SECURITY INVESTIGATION

The welfare response does not replace the investigation into the confinement.

The following questions remain unresolved:

- Who locked Jake's room from the outside?
    
- How was the door secured?
    
- Was the individual authorized to access the residential sector?
    
- Did the individual understand that Jake was inside?
    
- Was the confinement deliberate?
    
- Were access records altered or bypassed?
    
- Were there witnesses or relevant surveillance recordings?
    
- Has any other resident been subjected to similar treatment?
    

C.O.R.E. Security is instructed to preserve relevant access records and surveillance evidence.

The incident must not be dismissed as a harmless prank.

Jake's age, recent assault, and established fear of isolation make prolonged confinement a serious welfare concern regardless of the perpetrator's claimed intentions.

No conclusion regarding motive should be made until sufficient evidence is available.

At the same time, Jake must not be repeatedly interviewed merely to accelerate the investigation.

Any additional account should be obtained at an appropriate time, with a familiar support person present and without leading questions.

The investigation is the responsibility of C.O.R.E.

Jake is not responsible for solving the incident that frightened him.

---

# 9. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's response to matcha is unusual only in its specificity.

The broader principle is familiar.

When distressed, people may benefit from predictable sensory experiences, familiar routines, and the presence of someone they trust.

For Jake, matcha appears to combine several of these elements.

It gives him something to hold.

Something familiar to taste.

Something he can do at his own pace.

And, most importantly, a reason to remain in a quiet interaction with a trusted caregiver without being expected to explain himself.

The tea does not undo the five hours he spent locked in his room.

It does not establish that he feels safe behind closed doors.

It does not guarantee that he will remain calm the next time someone leaves.

What it provides is a manageable starting point.

A warm cup.

A quiet room.

A familiar person.

An unlocked door.

And the experience of being allowed to settle without being asked to prove that he has recovered.

C.O.R.E. therefore approves matcha as an optional component of Jake's individualized comfort plan, subject to appropriate preparation and medical considerations.

The primary intervention remains reliable supervision, restoration of his control over his environment, and consistent reassurance through observable actions.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There is something quietly heartbreaking about discovering that the best way to calm a frightened six-year-old after five hours of involuntary confinement is to make him a cup of tea.

Not because tea is inadequate.

But because the tea is not really the point.

Jake does not need someone to explain that the door is open.

He can see that.

He does not need someone to explain that he is allowed to leave.

He knows where the doorway is.

What he needs is time to believe that the world beyond it is not about to take control away from him again.

So Bao makes matcha.

Jake holds the cup.

He drinks slowly.

She stays beside him.

Nobody asks him to be brave.

Nobody tells him to stop being frightened.

Nobody demands an explanation before offering comfort.

For a little while, the most important things in Jake's world become very small.

A warm cup between his hands.

His teddy bear beside him.

Bao sitting close enough that he can lean against her.

And a door that remains open because he is allowed to decide whether he wants to look at it.

The tea cannot promise that nobody will ever frighten him again.

But the person sitting beside him can demonstrate something more immediate.

He does not have to recover alone.

He does not have to explain his fear to deserve help.

And he does not have to finish his matcha before someone decides he is worth staying for.
