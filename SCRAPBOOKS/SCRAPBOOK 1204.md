# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Nora Tachyon possesses an anomalous visual capability that allows her to perceive the trajectory of moving objects when sufficiently focused.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- bullets;
    
- thrown objects;
    
- projectiles;
    
- rapidly moving debris;
    
- and other objects traveling through her field of perception.
    

Nora does not appear to perceive the projectile itself as moving slowly.

Instead, when she deliberately focuses, she can perceive a **visual trail associated with the object's movement.**

The trail appears to provide sufficient information for her to determine where the object is going.

She can then position another object into its path.

C.O.R.E. has provisionally classified this as:

> **Predictive Trajectory Interception.**

Nora calls it:

> **“Seeing where things go.”**

---

# 2. VISUAL EFFECT

Under ordinary conditions, Nora's vision appears normal.

When focused on a moving object, however, observers have reported a faint trail extending along its recent and projected movement.

The exact appearance varies according to lighting and object velocity.

Nora describes it simply as:

> **“A line.”**

When asked whether she sees the entire future trajectory, she answered:

> **“No.”**

> **“Just enough.”**

This distinction is important.

Nora does not appear to possess unlimited predictive perception.

Her ability provides a usable indication of movement rather than an absolute knowledge of future events.

---

# 3. INTERCEPTION

Nora can use this information to intercept moving objects.

The interception does not necessarily require a matching object.

She can use another object to disrupt the incoming trajectory.

In controlled demonstrations, Nora has successfully intercepted objects using:

- handheld objects;
    
- thrown objects;
    
- other projectiles;
    
- and firearms.
    

Her accuracy is considerably beyond ordinary human capability.

The phenomenon appears to combine:

**visual prediction + reaction speed + exceptional spatial awareness.**

The exact contribution of each component remains undetermined.

---

# 4. THE 5.56 INCIDENT

The most significant demonstration occurred during a controlled MSAC observation.

Nora was provided with a **.45 ACP pistol**.

A separate controlled system produced a sustained sequence of incoming **5.56 mm projectiles**.

Nora was instructed to attempt interception.

She agreed.

The resulting observation lasted considerably longer than expected.

Nora successfully intercepted the incoming projectiles repeatedly.

The final confirmed count exceeded:

> **150 intercepted projectiles.**

The .45 ACP pistol was not being used because the two ammunition types were equivalent.

They are not.

The significance of the demonstration was Nora's ability to use the smaller number of available movements of the pistol to place the projectile path of the incoming objects into conflict.

MSAC personnel initially assumed she would fail after the first few attempts.

She did not.

---

# 5. OBSERVER REACTION

The first ten interceptions produced surprise.

The first fifty produced confusion.

The first hundred produced silence.

After the count passed 150, the observation team reportedly stopped attempting to verbally describe what they were witnessing.

One MSAC observer eventually stated:

> **“She's parrying bullets.”**

Another corrected him:

> **“She's predicting them.”**

A third responded:

> **“That doesn't make it less ridiculous.”**

C.O.R.E. agrees with the third assessment.

---

# 6. NORA'S PERSPECTIVE

Following the demonstration, Nora was asked whether she understood why the observers were surprised.

She looked confused.

> **NORA:**  
> “They were right there.”

The examiner clarified that the objects were moving too quickly for ordinary perception.

Nora shrugged.

> **NORA:**  
> “I can see the lines.”

She was then asked whether she found the task difficult.

Nora thought for several seconds.

> **NORA:**  
> “At first.”

> **EXAMINER:**  
> “And after that?”

> **NORA:**  
> “It got easier.”

---

# 7. LIMITATIONS

The phenomenon should **not** be interpreted as omniscience.

Nora cannot automatically intercept everything.

Observed limitations include:

- loss of focus;
    
- visual obstruction;
    
- insufficient reaction time;
    
- multiple conflicting trajectories;
    
- unexpected movement;
    
- fatigue;
    
- unfamiliar environments;
    
- objects outside her effective visual field;
    
- and situations where the incoming object cannot be visually tracked.
    

Her ability also appears substantially stronger when she deliberately concentrates.

When distracted, Nora behaves like an ordinary six-year-old.

This distinction is significant.

---

# 8. THE RANGE PERSONA

The phenomenon also provides additional context for Nora's previously documented behavior at the range.

When Nora begins focusing on moving targets, her personality changes dramatically.

The energetic child disappears.

She becomes quiet.

Still.

Focused.

Her eyes track the movement.

Her body makes only the movements necessary to respond.

Jane Doe, after observing the phenomenon, summarized it:

> **“That's the same kid.”**

Pause.

> **“But she's looking at the world differently.”**

---

# 9. RELATIONSHIP TO DEADEYE

The existence of this capability may explain why Nora has shown such immediate enthusiasm for the Deadeye.

However, C.O.R.E. emphasizes that the Deadeye did **not** create this capability.

Nora possessed the visual phenomenon independently.

The weapon merely provides another medium through which she can interact with moving trajectories.

Her ability should therefore be considered:

> **Nora's capability.**

Not:

> **A property of the Deadeye.**

---

# 10. NON-FIREARM APPLICATIONS

The phenomenon is not restricted to firearms.

Nora has demonstrated similar predictive behavior with ordinary objects.

During informal observation, a researcher tossed a marker toward her.

Nora caught it without looking directly at it.

When asked how she knew where it would go:

> **NORA:**  
> “The line.”

She subsequently demonstrated the same behavior with:

- balls;
    
- thrown paper;
    
- falling objects;
    
- small toys;
    
- and objects tossed across a room.
    

At least one researcher has requested that personnel stop throwing things at Nora for scientific curiosity.

This request has been approved.

---

# 11. IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTE

Nora's ability should **not** be treated as proof that she is invulnerable.

She can still be surprised.

She can still be distracted.

She can still become tired.

She can still be physically injured.

She can still make mistakes.

Most importantly:

> **The existence of predictive perception does not remove the need for supervision.**

Nora is six years old.

Her extraordinary ability does not change that fact.

# 13. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Nora does not appear to consciously calculate projectile trajectories.

She appears to **see them.**

The distinction is substantial.

To Nora, an incoming object is not simply:

> **“Something moving very fast.”**

It is:

> **“Something moving along this line.”**

Once that line becomes visible, interception becomes possible.

The resulting performance is extraordinary.

More than 150 incoming 5.56 mm projectiles were successfully intercepted during a single controlled observation using a .45 ACP platform.

The event has been internally nicknamed:

> **THE 150+ PARRY INCIDENT**

Nora has requested that the name be changed.

Her proposed replacement:

> **“The Bullet Thing.”**

It has not been adopted.
