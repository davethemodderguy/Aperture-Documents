# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has acquired a top hat.

It is black, properly fitted to his small head, and decorated with a **glowing azure ribbon** that produces a soft celestial shimmer under low-light conditions.

He has also begun wearing a monocle.

Both accessories appear to have been selected deliberately.

The top hat is, according to Jake, “professional.”

The monocle is medically necessary.

Jake has one eye that is nearsighted. The monocle provides the optical correction required to balance his vision between both eyes, reducing the disparity that otherwise makes focusing uncomfortable.

C.O.R.E. Medical has confirmed that the monocle is a legitimate visual aid and not merely an ornamental accessory.

The fact that Jake has chosen to combine it with a top hat, a formal waistcoat, and the general demeanor of a six-year-old financial executive is considered unrelated to the medical issue.

Probably.

The more concerning discovery occurred when Neko attempted to explain a mathematical problem to him.

Jake corrected her answer.

Then he explained why it was wrong.

Then he opened a ledger.

C.O.R.E. would like to emphasize that the last part was not a joke.

**Jake is exceptionally good at mathematics.**

He is also learning accounting from Clover.

This has produced an entirely new category of concern.

# 1. PHYSICAL PRESENTATION

## 1.1 — The Top Hat

Jake's top hat is a miniature formal hat, proportioned to fit his child-sized human manifestation.

Its exterior is matte black.

A luminous azure ribbon encircles the base of the crown.

The ribbon's glow is consistent with the celestial luminescence already observed in Jake's eyes. No additional power has been confirmed, and the hat itself is not currently classified as anomalous.

Jake wears it at an angle that he considers appropriate for business.

No personnel have successfully persuaded him that the angle is unnecessary.

### Observed exchange

**C.O.R.E. WELFARE OFFICER:**  
“Jake, why the hat?”

**JAKE:**  
“Professional.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Professional for what?”

Jake looked at the officer.

He adjusted the brim.

**JAKE:**  
“Business.”

**OFFICER:**  
“What business?”

**JAKE:**  
“Mine.”

The officer requested clarification.

Jake declined to provide it.

---

## 1.2 — The Monocle

The monocle is fitted to the eye requiring optical correction.

Jake's nearsighted eye does not focus distant objects as clearly as his other eye. Without correction, the difference can produce uneven visual clarity and unnecessary strain.

The monocle compensates for that difference.

It is therefore a functional medical device.

It is not evidence of an anomalous ocular transformation.

It is not a weapon.

It is not a targeting instrument.

And it is absolutely not permission for personnel to remove it to see what happens.

Jake has already expressed considerable displeasure toward the suggestion that he might look more “normal” without it.

His response was brief.

**JAKE:**  
“I can see better with it.”

The matter was closed.

### Medical clarification

Jake's unusual celestial eyes do not automatically eliminate ordinary visual impairments.

His status as a literal star does not exempt his physical manifestation from the need for corrective lenses.

C.O.R.E. recommends that personnel remember this before attempting to explain his own eyes to him.

# 4. JAKE VS. NEKO — MATHEMATICAL COMPARISON

A formal assessment was conducted after Neko requested confirmation that the initial result had not been a coincidence.

The assessment included arithmetic, algebra, geometry, symbolic manipulation, numerical reasoning, and selected engineering calculations.

Jake was not given access to Neko's answer sheet.

Neko was not given access to Jake's working.

Both were provided with identical problems.

The results were reviewed by an independent evaluator.

## Findings

Jake demonstrated superior performance in the assessed mathematical tasks.

His advantages were most noticeable in:

- rapid numerical calculation;
    
- recognizing mathematical patterns;
    
- reducing unnecessarily complicated expressions;
    
- detecting inconsistent assumptions;
    
- checking results using alternative methods;
    
- and identifying errors in otherwise plausible calculations.
    

Neko remained highly competent, particularly when mathematical work was integrated into practical engineering problems.

However, Jake was faster and more accurate across the specific test set.

The result was independently verified.

**Jake beat Neko in mathematics.**

Neko has requested a second assessment.

Jake has agreed.

He asked whether the second assessment would be harder.

Neko answered that it would.

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:**  
“Okay. Bring snacks.”

Neko has not yet decided whether this was an insult.

# 5. THE CLOVER PROBLEM

Mathematics alone would have been manageable.

Unfortunately, Jake has also been learning accounting from Clover.

Clover's instruction began innocently enough.

Jake asked what she was writing in a notebook.

Clover explained that she was tracking expenses.

Jake asked why.

Clover explained income, expenditure, balances, and the importance of knowing where one's money went.

Jake listened.

He asked several questions.

Clover answered them.

Jake asked for an example.

Clover provided one.

Jake requested another.

By the end of the conversation, he had developed an interest in bookkeeping.

By the end of the week, he understood the basic structure of a ledger.

By the end of the following week, he was checking totals.

Clover initially found this adorable.

Then Jake found an error in her arithmetic.

Then another.

Then a discrepancy between two entries.

Clover stopped finding it adorable quite so quickly.

**CLOVER:**  
“I was teaching him accounting.”

**C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**

“He learned accounting.”

**OFFICER:**  
“That sounds successful.”

“He started checking my books.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Also successful.”

**CLOVER:**  
“He found an error from last month.”

**OFFICER:**  
“That's good, isn't it?”

“He asked whether I wanted him to audit the rest.”

A pause.

**CLOVER:**  
“He was wearing the hat.”

# 6. ACCOUNTING PROFICIENCY

Jake's accounting knowledge remains introductory relative to a professional accountant's full responsibilities.

Nevertheless, his progress is unusually rapid.

He can now perform basic bookkeeping tasks, including:

- organizing transactions;
    
- distinguishing income from expenses;
    
- calculating balances;
    
- reconciling simple records;
    
- identifying arithmetic discrepancies;
    
- tracking outstanding amounts;
    
- and preparing clear summaries of financial activity.
    

He also understands a principle that several residents have historically neglected:

> **Money does not stop being spent simply because nobody is keeping track of it.**

Jake has begun applying this principle to small personal projects.

He records what he receives.

He records what he spends.

He checks the totals.

He asks questions when something does not reconcile.

He has not been authorized to access Aperture's corporate accounts.

This restriction is unrelated to his competence.

It is related to the fact that he is six years old.

C.O.R.E. considers this an important distinction.

# 7. THE LEDGER INCIDENT

The incident began when Neko asked Jake to help verify a small departmental expense sheet.

The task was intended to be simple.

Jake reviewed the entries.

He checked the arithmetic.

He compared the listed total against the recorded transactions.

Then he raised his hand.

**JAKE:**  
“This doesn't match.”

**NEKO:**  
“What doesn't?”

Jake turned the page around.

**JAKE:**  
“The total.”

Neko checked it.

The total was wrong.

She recalculated it.

Jake waited.

**NEKO:**  
“Where did you find the discrepancy?”

Jake pointed to two entries.

One had been added incorrectly.

The other had been carried forward using the wrong subtotal.

Both errors were corrected.

Neko examined the ledger again.

**NEKO:**  
“You caught both of these?”

**JAKE:**  
“Yes.”

**NEKO:**  
“Who taught you to reconcile accounts?”

**JAKE:**  
“Clover.”

Neko closed the ledger.

Slowly.

**NEKO:**  
“Of course she did.”

Jake tilted his head.

**JAKE:**  
“Is that bad?”

**NEKO:**  
“No.”

A pause.

**NEKO:**  
“I need to speak with Clover.”

Jake returned to his seat.

He wrote something in his notebook.

Neko glanced at it.

The page contained a list of expenses.

At the top, in neat handwriting, was the heading:

**BUSINESS.**

# 9. WELFARE AND EDUCATIONAL GUIDANCE

C.O.R.E. supports Jake's intellectual development.

His abilities should be encouraged without turning them into an obligation.

Personnel are reminded that Jake is a child and a resident, not a departmental resource.

Accordingly:

- Do not assign him corporate work simply because he is capable of completing it.
    
- Do not use him to settle mathematical disputes between adults.
    
- Do not ask him to audit financial records without appropriate authorization.
    
- Do not interrupt his meals or rest for additional assessments.
    
- Do not assume that his aptitude makes him emotionally older than he is.
    
- Do not remove his teddy bear, hat, or monocle as part of an experiment.
    
- Do not treat his monocle as a decorative accessory or disregard his visual needs.
    

Jake may choose to study mathematics and accounting.

He may also choose to abandon both subjects and spend the afternoon reading, playing, or sitting with Clover.

Both choices are acceptable.

His competence does not create a debt.

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

A six-year-old boy lives in SAFEHOUSE.

He wears a black top hat with a glowing blue ribbon.

He wears a monocle because one of his eyes is nearsighted and needs optical correction.

He carries a teddy bear whose heartbeat is his own.

He has wings.

He has feline ears, a tail, and retractable claws.

He is a literal star.

He is also, apparently, better at mathematics than Neko.

And now he is learning accounting from Clover.

Aperture Industries has spent years developing advanced engineering systems, financial controls, anomalous containment procedures, and increasingly complicated administrative structures.

Somewhere in the middle of all that, a six-year-old put on a hat and began reconciling ledgers.

He did not ask for a position.

He did not ask for a salary.

He did not ask for access to corporate accounts.

He simply noticed that the numbers were wrong.

And corrected them.
