### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — ONE WEEK WITHOUT A VOICE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Duration:** **7 DAYS**  
**Status:** **NONVERBAL / PSYCHOLOGICAL CONCERN**

---

# **ONE WEEK**

It has been one week since Alice lost her ability to speak.

Her voice has not returned.

Medical examination continues to show no definitive explanation.

Physically, Alice has adapted remarkably quickly.

She writes.

She draws.

She gestures.

She communicates.

But her private writing has become increasingly concerning.

---

# **SELF-REFLECTION**

Alice has started filling pages in her notebooks.

Much of the writing is ordinary.

Small observations.

Questions.

Drawings of Corin.

Drawings of Orphie.

Drawings of the Treehouse.

Drawings of her scooter.

But between them are pages that are noticeably different.

Alice has begun writing about **herself.**

Not what she likes.

Not what she wants.

What she thinks she **is.**

---

# **EXCERPTS**

> **"Alice is weak."**

---

> **"Alice gets sick."**

---

> **"Alice makes everyone worry."**

---

> **"Alice cannot walk good."**

---

> **"Alice cannot see right eye."**

---

> **"Alice cannot talk."**

---

> **"Alice needs soup."**

---

One sentence appears repeatedly.

> **"Alice is broken."**

---

# **THE MOST CONCERNING PAGE**

One page contains a drawing of Alice sitting alone.

The drawing is unusually detailed.

Alice is wearing her soft hat.

Her eyepatch is visible.

Her ears are lowered.

There are no other figures.

Underneath it, Alice wrote:

> **"Everyone take care Alice."**

A second line:

> **"Alice take care nobody."**

Then:

> **"Alice only make work."**

At the bottom of the page:

> **"Maybe Alice should be less."**

---

# **CORIN'S REACTION**

Corin found the page.

She didn't read further.

She closed the notebook.

Then sat beside Alice.

Alice looked at her.

Corin pointed gently toward the notebook.

> **"You don't have to hide your thoughts from me."**

Alice lowered her head.

Corin took a piece of paper.

She wrote:

> **"You aren't broken."**

Alice stared at it.

She wrote underneath:

> **"Body is broken."**

Corin wrote:

> **"Then we'll take care of your body."**

Alice:

> **"Forever?"**

Corin:

> **"If that's what it takes."**

Alice didn't answer.

She simply leaned against Corin.

---

# **LUMI'S OBSERVATION**

Lumi noticed something else.

Alice's drawings of other people remain colorful and expressive.

Her drawings of herself have become increasingly empty.

When Alice draws Corin:

Corin smiles.

When she draws Belle:

Belle is laughing.

When she draws Star:

Star has glowing wings.

When she draws Orphie:

Orphie is holding her.

When she draws herself:

Alice is usually alone.

Lumi wrote in her observation:

> _She's not drawing how she looks._

> _She's drawing how she thinks she belongs._

---

# **STAR**

Star tried something different.

She gave Alice a piece of paper.

On it, Star wrote:

> **"Write three things you like about yourself."**

Alice stared at the paper for a long time.

She wrote:

> **"Alice likes..."**

Nothing followed.

She erased it.

Tried again.

> **"Alice has..."**

Nothing.

She erased that too.

Eventually, she drew a small carrot.

Star smiled.

> **"You like carrots?"**

Alice shook her head.

She crossed it out.

Then wrote:

> **"Forgot."**

Star's smile disappeared.

---

# **THE POSITIVE**

There is one important distinction.

Alice is still capable of recognizing affection.

When Corin hugs her, she hugs back.

When Belle carries her, she relaxes.

When Orphie sits beside her, she moves closer.

When Star compliments her drawings, she smiles.

Alice has not stopped loving the people around her.

She has simply become increasingly convinced that she is **only receiving** and never **giving.**

That belief is incorrect.

But Alice does not currently understand that.

---

# **CARE RESPONSE**

The SAFEHOUSE has agreed on several principles.

Alice will not be told to "toughen up."

She will not be told that others have it worse.

She will not be pressured into speaking.

She will not be made to feel guilty for needing care.

And nobody will tell her that she is a burden.

Instead, the people around her will continue showing her something words have repeatedly failed to communicate:

**Her presence itself matters.**

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice cannot speak.

So she writes.

And because she writes, people can finally see some of the thoughts she previously kept hidden.

That has made one thing painfully clear.

Alice's body is fragile.

Her voice is gone.

Her memories are fragmented.

But the most dangerous thing currently happening to Alice may not be physical.

It is the story she has begun telling herself.

> **"Alice is broken."**

> **"Alice makes everyone worry."**

> **"Alice only makes work."**

The SAFEHOUSE intends to make sure that story does not become the one thing Alice believes completely.

**End of CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION LOG**
