### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / EDUCATION LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — LANGUAGE DEVELOPMENT**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Program:** Basic Language & Grammar Rehabilitation  
**Instructors:** Corin Lockwood, Lumia "Lumi" Lockwood, Star Dawnbringer  
**Status:** **ONGOING / MINIMAL PROGRESS**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice's vocabulary has steadily expanded since her recovery.

Her grammar has not.

Alice primarily communicates by assembling individual words into short statements.

Her speech is understandable most of the time, but sentence structure remains extremely inconsistent.

Examples include:

> **"Bunny hungry."**

> **"Alice go Treehouse."**

> **"Corin make soup?"**

> **"No want sleep."**

> **"Big sis stay?"**

She understands significantly more language than she can properly produce.

---

# **THE LESSONS**

Corin began teaching Alice first.

She keeps lessons simple.

> **"Alice is hungry."**

Alice repeats:

> **"Alice hungry."**

Corin:

> **"Alice _is_ hungry."**

Alice:

> **"Alice... is hungry."**

Corin smiles.

> **"Good."**

The next day, Alice says:

> **"Alice hungry."**

Corin:

> **"...We'll try again."**

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi focuses heavily on sentence construction.

She writes simple examples on a small board.

**I am hungry.**

**I am tired.**

**I want soup.**

**I like flowers.**

Alice studies them.

Then points to the first sentence.

> **"Alice am hungry."**

Lumi pauses.

> **"Almost."**

Alice looks proud anyway.

> **"Almost."**

---

# **STAR**

Star takes a different approach.

She turns grammar into games.

She asks Alice questions.

> **"Who made the soup?"**

Alice immediately answers:

> **"Corin."**

Star:

> **"Good! Now make it a sentence."**

Alice thinks.

> **"Corin... make soup."**

Star:

> **"Corin _made_ the soup."**

Alice stares at her.

> **"...Why made?"**

Star sighs.

> **"That's a very good question."**

---

# **PROGRESS**

Progress remains minimal.

Alice can occasionally produce grammatically correct sentences.

Usually only when:

- The sentence is extremely familiar
    
- Someone has recently taught it to her
    
- She repeats something another person said
    
- She has practiced it many times
    

The moment the conversation changes, her grammar frequently collapses again.

# **A SMALL SUCCESS**

One evening, Corin returned with Alice's soup.

Alice looked up.

Normally she would say:

> **"Corin soup!"**

Instead, she paused.

Then carefully said:

> **"Corin made soup for Alice."**

Corin froze.

Alice looked nervous.

> **"...Wrong?"**

Corin immediately shook her head.

> **"No."**

She smiled.

> **"Perfect."**

Alice smiled back.

> **"Perfect."**

It was one of the longest grammatically correct sentences Alice had ever produced spontaneously.

---

# **THE NEXT MORNING**

Alice woke up.

She looked at Corin.

> **"Corin made soup for Alice."**

Corin stared.

> **"...You remembered."**

Alice nodded proudly.

Then added:

> **"Alice hungry."**

Corin laughed.

> **"We'll work on that one."**

---

# **STAR'S ASSESSMENT**

Star's official assessment was simple:

> **"She's learning."**

When asked how long it would take Alice to speak normally:

> **"...A while."**

When asked whether Alice was making good progress:

Star smiled.

> **"A tiny bit."**

---

# **LUMI'S NOTE**

Lumi wrote:

> _Don't rush her._

> _Alice understands us._

> _She just doesn't know how to put everything into words yet._

> _Every correct sentence is progress._

---

# **CORIN'S NOTE**

Corin added one sentence underneath.

> _Even if she says "Alice hungry" forever, I'll still understand her._

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice still speaks strangely.

Her grammar remains fragmented.

She leaves words out.

She mixes tenses.

She sometimes refers to herself by name.

She occasionally assembles entire sentences in an order that makes absolutely no grammatical sense.

But she is learning.

Slowly.

Painfully slowly.

One word at a time.

And whenever Alice manages to construct a proper sentence without assistance, three people are always there to notice.

Corin.

Lumi.

And Star.

They don't care how long it takes.
