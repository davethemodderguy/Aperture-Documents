## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tom Akana is gone.

Not absent from SAFEHOUSE.

Not asleep.

Not waiting somewhere outside the observable world.

Gone.

During an incident involving Jake Akana's Genesis Construction ability, Jake accidentally demanifested Tom. The ability is known to allow Jake to demanifest his Genesis Construction creations either temporarily or permanently. In this instance, the effect appears to have been permanent.

Tom has not returned.

No subsequent manifestation has been observed. No reliable means of reversing the demanifestation has been identified. C.O.R.E. cannot presently determine whether Tom remains recoverable through an unknown application of Genesis Construction or whether the event has irreversibly ended his existence.

The distinction matters.

**C.O.R.E. will not declare Tom recoverable without evidence. It will not declare him irretrievable without evidence, either.**

Jake has been informed that Tom is not coming back through the usual means. His response has been severe.

This is not merely the loss of a companion.

Tom was the person who remained beside Jake during his four-day death. He was the person who panicked when Jake did not return. He was the person who accidentally kicked Jake in the leg immediately before the white fire activated, and who spent the following moments crying, shouting, and holding Jake's hand because he could not bear the thought of losing him.

Tom was also the person who understood Jake's fear of being alone—not because he could always prevent it, but because he had witnessed what that fear could become.

Now Jake has accidentally caused Tom's disappearance.

The child who feared that everyone would eventually become too busy to stay has lost the person who repeatedly stayed.

And the ability that Jake used to create companionship has become the means by which he may have destroyed his closest relationship.

This report is not a determination of guilt.

It is an emergency record of a loss that C.O.R.E. does not yet know how to reverse.

## II. INCIDENT RECONSTRUCTION

**Exact time:** Under investigation  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, Floor 7  
**Witness:** Jake Akana  
**Additional witnesses:** None confirmed at the time of initial report

The incident occurred during an ordinary use of Genesis Construction.

Jake has the ability to manifest imagined creations into reality. He can also demanifest those creations, either temporarily or permanently. The full operational boundaries of this ability remain unknown.

At some point during the incident, Jake's ability affected Tom.

The available evidence indicates that Jake did not intend to permanently demanifest him.

The effect occurred accidentally.

Tom disappeared.

Jake attempted to reverse the effect.

Tom did not return.

Jake attempted again.

Nothing happened.

He tried to understand what he had done, initially treating the event as though it might be a temporary manifestation error. The expectation was understandable: Genesis Construction creations had been demanifested before, and temporary demanifestation was a known possibility.

But time passed.

Tom did not reappear.

There was no familiar voice from behind Jake.

No annoyed correction.

No quiet reminder that Jake had done something reckless.

No Tom appearing at his shoulder to ask what he had broken this time.

The absence continued.

The incident was escalated to C.O.R.E. after Jake's attempts to restore Tom failed.

### Reconstructed dialogue

The following is a non-verbatim reconstruction of Jake's reported behavior and statements. Exact wording has not been established.

**JAKE:** "Tom?"

No response.

**JAKE:** "...Tom, come back."

Nothing happened.

**JAKE:** "Okay. Stop hiding."

He waited.

**JAKE:** "It's not funny."

Jake attempted to reverse the demanifestation.

There was no observable change.

**JAKE:** "Tom?"

He tried again.

**JAKE:** "I didn't mean to."

Silence.

**JAKE:** "Tom, I didn't mean to!"

The next attempt produced the same result.

**JAKE:** "Come on! You're supposed to come back!"

No response.

**JAKE:** "You always come back!"

That statement is consistent with Jake's experience of Tom as a persistent companion. It is not evidence that Genesis Construction can reverse a permanent demanifestation.

Jake continued trying.

When the attempts failed, he reportedly began asking whether Tom was angry with him.

This question is of particular concern.

Jake was not merely trying to restore an anomalous manifestation. He was attempting to understand whether the person he loved had left him—and whether he had caused that departure.

## III. THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN DISAPPEARANCE AND DEATH

C.O.R.E. must exercise caution when describing Tom's current status.

The term *demanifestation* describes the observed anomalous event. It does not, by itself, establish what happened to Tom's underlying existence.

The following possibilities remain unresolved:

- Tom may have been permanently demanifested in a manner that cannot currently be reversed.
- The manifestation may be recoverable through a mechanism that Jake has not yet discovered or cannot presently access.
- Genesis Construction may have altered Tom's state in a way that prevents ordinary re-manifestation.
- The event may have affected the relationship between Jake's imagination and Tom's independent existence in a manner not yet understood.

No hypothesis is to be presented to Jake as established fact.

C.O.R.E. will not instruct him to keep trying indefinitely, nor will it tell him that he has no chance of ever seeing Tom again.

Repeated attempts could intensify his distress, and uncontrolled use of Genesis Construction may create additional risks. Conversely, prematurely declaring Tom permanently lost could compound Jake's grief without adequate evidence.

A controlled investigation is authorized, subject to strict limits and psychological oversight.

No procedure may involve deliberately harming Jake, inducing death, or forcing an uncontrolled anomalous response in an attempt to recover Tom.

**The absence of a known recovery method is not proof that recovery is impossible. It is also not permission to experiment without restraint.**

## IV. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT — JAKE AKANA

Jake's circumstances make this incident especially dangerous.

This is not his first experience of profound loss, isolation, or death. He previously endured starvation and isolation for seventy-five years. More recently, he experienced a seven-day period of escalating suicidal crises, during which his resurrection mechanism eventually remained inactive for more than four days.

The loss of Tom follows immediately after that period of instability.

Tom was not simply another person in Jake's social circle.

He was the person who could remain present when Jake was frightened, argue with him when he was being reckless, and reassure him through ordinary acts of companionship.

Tom had his own fears and limitations, but he repeatedly returned.

Jake may now interpret the incident through several overlapping beliefs:

- He destroys the people he loves.
- He cannot be trusted with his own abilities.
- His attempts to create or preserve companionship inevitably end in loss.
- He has lost the one person who understood him best.
- If Tom does not return, he will be alone again.
- If he caused Tom's disappearance, he does not deserve to be comforted.

These are risks to assess, not conclusions to impose upon him.

C.O.R.E. personnel must not tell Jake that his fears are irrational. Nor should they reassure him with promises that Tom will definitely return.

The immediate objective is to keep Jake safe, establish what he understands about the event, and provide a stable presence without making him feel responsible for managing everyone else's emotions.

### 4.1. Suicide Risk

Given Jake's recent history, the incident requires an immediate and sustained suicide-risk assessment.

His previous ability to return from death must not be used to minimize this risk.

The loss of Tom may produce grief, panic, self-blame, or an urge to reverse the event at any cost. Personnel must not assume that Jake's resurrection will protect him from further harm.

If Jake expresses an intention to die, staff must respond as they would to any acute suicidal crisis: immediate intervention, appropriate medical care, close support, and restriction of access to means of self-harm where indicated.

No one is to tell him that he can simply bring Tom back if he tries hard enough.

That would turn an unresolved anomalous question into a burden Jake might attempt to solve through increasingly dangerous actions.

### 4.2. Guilt Is Not the Same as Intent

The available account indicates that Tom's demanifestation was accidental.

That distinction must be preserved.

Jake may feel responsible because his ability caused the event. He may believe that responsibility makes him unforgivable. Neither belief establishes that he intended Tom's disappearance or that he deserves to suffer.

C.O.R.E. must not excuse the consequences of the incident, but it must also not turn an accident into a moral condemnation of a child.

The immediate priority is not to decide whether Jake should forgive himself.

It is to help him survive the period in which he may be unable to imagine a future without Tom.

## V. RESIDENTIAL RESPONSE — THE EMPTY PLACE

The loss of Tom has affected the entire seventh floor, but Jake's response requires particular attention.

A support network cannot replace Tom.

It can provide care while Jake grieves. It can ensure that he is not left alone with escalating distress. It can help him investigate the event without making him solely responsible for finding a solution.

It cannot honestly promise that another person will occupy Tom's place.

C.O.R.E. expressly prohibits residents from telling Jake that he can simply make another Tom.

Genesis Construction may reproduce familiar appearances, voices, or remembered behaviors. The ability's full limits are unknown, and no evidence establishes that a recreated manifestation would possess Tom's independent identity, history, or continuity of experience.

A copy that looks like Tom would not establish that Tom had returned.

Attempting to substitute a new manifestation for the missing individual could deepen Jake's distress, particularly if the result behaved differently or failed to remember their shared experiences.

Any proposal to use Genesis Construction in an attempt to restore Tom must therefore be treated as a recovery investigation—not as permission to replace him.

Jake must be allowed to miss Tom without being presented with a substitute.

He must be allowed to speak about Tom without being asked to move on.

He must be allowed to express anger at himself, at his ability, or at the situation without being punished for the emotion.

And he must not be expected to comfort the residents who are frightened by what happened.

## VI. C.O.R.E. DIRECTIVES

### DIRECTIVE 01 — IMMEDIATE SAFETY

Jake is to receive an immediate welfare assessment and a structured support plan.

Given the combination of recent suicidal behavior, acute grief, and self-blame risk, he must not be left unsupported during the initial crisis period.

Support must be provided by designated personnel and trusted residents, with responsibilities shared across the residential team.

No single child is to be assigned sole responsibility for Jake's safety.

### DIRECTIVE 02 — GENESIS CONSTRUCTION RESTRICTIONS

Unsupervised experiments involving permanent demanifestation are suspended pending investigation.

Jake is not to be pressured into repeated attempts to restore Tom. All testing must be voluntary where applicable, carefully scoped, and conducted under appropriate anomalous and psychological oversight.

No attempt may intentionally place Jake in danger.

No one may demand that he recreate Tom from memory and treat the result as proof of recovery.

### DIRECTIVE 03 — RECOVERY INVESTIGATION

C.O.R.E. will investigate the mechanism by which Tom was demanifested and determine whether a reversible state, residual signature, or other evidence of continuity remains.

The investigation must distinguish between:

1. Evidence that Tom's manifestation ended.
2. Evidence concerning whether Tom's underlying existence persists.
3. Evidence that could support a safe and reliable recovery method.

These are separate questions.

A successful reconstruction of Tom's appearance or voice will not, by itself, satisfy the third criterion.

### DIRECTIVE 04 — NO FALSE PROMISES

Personnel must not tell Jake that Tom will definitely return.

Personnel must also not tell him that Tom is certainly gone forever.

Both claims exceed the available evidence.

The approved response is:

*"We know Tom disappeared after your ability affected him. We know he hasn't returned, and we know that nothing we've tried has brought him back yet. We're investigating what happened. We won't promise you something we can't prove, and we won't stop looking for answers just because we don't have them today."*

### DIRECTIVE 05 — TOM'S IDENTITY MUST BE PRESERVED

All records, communications, and investigation materials relating to Tom must be preserved.

His name is to remain in the SAFEHOUSE resident records with the status **MISSING — PERMANENT DEMANIFESTATION SUSPECTED; RECOVERY UNKNOWN**.

He is not to be recorded as a confirmed death unless further evidence supports that classification.

No synthetic replacement is to be registered as Tom Akana.

### DIRECTIVE 06 — SUPPORT FOR THE RESIDENTS

The SAFEHOUSE residents who witnessed Jake's previous crises must be informed of the incident through appropriate channels.

They are to be reminded that Jake's grief must not become a reason to ostracize him or treat his presence as dangerous by default.

C.O.R.E. will also monitor for the opposite risk: residents promising impossible levels of availability because they fear what will happen if they say no.

Jake needs dependable relationships, not promises that will inevitably collapse under the pressure of being perfect.

## VII. PERSONAL STATEMENT — JAKE AKANA

**Status:** Reconstructed from initial observations; not a formal interview.

After repeated unsuccessful attempts to reverse Tom's demanifestation, Jake reportedly sat in the room where Tom had last been present.

He stopped attempting to use Genesis Construction.

For a time, he did not speak.

When approached by a member of the residential team, he asked one question.

**JAKE:** "If I made him, can I unmake what I did?"

The staff member did not answer immediately.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "We don't know yet."

**JAKE:** "But I can demanifest things."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "Yes."

**JAKE:** "And I can bring things back."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "You've manifested things again before. We don't know whether that will work here."

Jake looked down at his hands.

**JAKE:** "I didn't mean to."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "I believe you."

**JAKE:** "I didn't want him to go."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "I believe that, too."

Jake began crying.

**JAKE:** "He was supposed to come back."

The staff member sat beside him.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "I know."

**JAKE:** "He always comes back."

No one corrected him.

No one told him that this time would be different.

No one told him that it would be the same.

After a long silence, Jake spoke again.

**JAKE:** "I don't want another Tom."

The staff member looked at him.

**JAKE:** "I want *my* Tom."

The interview was terminated at that point to allow Jake time to recover.

## VIII. CLOSING NOTE — C.O.R.E.

We have spent considerable time documenting Jake's fear of being left alone.

We have studied the seventy-five years he endured in isolation. We have documented his repeated attempts to find companionship. We have recorded the days when everyone was busy, the nights when Tom returned to find him dead, and the four days when Tom waited for a resurrection that did not come until an accident preceded the white fire.

We have repeatedly said that Genesis Construction cannot guarantee that someone will always stay.

Now we face something worse.

Tom did stay.

He stayed when Jake died.

He stayed when the fire did not come.

He stayed when he was frightened, exhausted, and unable to understand why the person he loved would not wake up.

And now Tom is gone.

Jake did not ask for this outcome.

He did not deliberately choose to lose his friend.

But the distinction between intention and consequence may offer little comfort to a child staring at an empty space where someone used to be.

He may replay the moment again and again, searching for the instant at which he could have done something differently.

He may wonder whether his ability is safe to use.

He may wonder whether every person he loves is only one mistake away from disappearing.

He may decide that the only way to prevent another loss is never to create anything, never to reach for anyone, or never to allow himself to be happy enough to fear losing it.

We must not let that conclusion become his future.

We will investigate Tom's disappearance.

We will preserve every available record and pursue every safe avenue of recovery.

We will not fabricate certainty.

And we will not allow Jake to believe that the only person who can care for him has vanished because he made one terrible mistake.

Tom cannot presently be reached.

That is the truth.

Whether Tom can ever return remains unknown.

That is the truth, too.

But Jake is still here, and his life matters regardless of what we discover.

**He must not become the next person we lose while searching for the one who disappeared.**

*— C.O.R.E. Anomalous Incident Response & Residential Welfare Division*

**FINAL ENTRY:**

In the event that Tom Akana is recovered, no personnel are to treat the reunion as a simple restoration of the previous status quo.

Jake will need time to understand what happened. Tom may need time to understand what he experienced, if he experienced anything during the demanifestation. Neither should be expected to resume their relationship as though the intervening absence never occurred.

If Tom cannot be recovered, Jake will need help grieving a loss that has no confirmed explanation and no established finality.

We will not tell him to replace Tom.

We will not tell him to forget Tom.

And we will not tell him that the fact he loved someone—and accidentally lost him—means he should never love anyone again.

For now, Tom's place remains empty.

C.O.R.E. will continue looking for him.

And until we know more, we will take care of the child who keeps asking us to bring his friend home.
