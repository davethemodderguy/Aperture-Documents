## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana is dead.

For approximately two months, his absence went uninvestigated to a degree that allowed his body to remain undiscovered inside an abandoned maintenance locker. The locker was not part of his assigned living quarters. It was not an approved resting area. No one had reason to believe that he had chosen it as a place to sleep.

Yet that was where he was found.

His body was pale, cold, and covered in dust. Cobwebs had accumulated around the locker and across portions of his clothing. His eyes, ordinarily bright with the blue patterns of distant constellations, had dimmed. His rose remained, but its presence did not signify recovery. His wings were still. There was no detectable heartbeat.

There was no resurrection.

The current investigation indicates that Jake's death followed an episode of severe psychological distress. Earlier that day, he had attempted to find someone willing to play with him. He approached multiple SAFEHOUSE residents and other Blacksite personnel. He received numerous refusals.

Those refusals were not necessarily intended as rejections of Jake himself. Several residents were occupied with work, research, maintenance, and other responsibilities. Others were simply unavailable.

Jake, however, did not experience the day as a collection of unrelated scheduling conflicts.

He experienced it as evidence.

Evidence that he was unwanted. Evidence that he was a burden. Evidence that the people he loved did not need him, and that his continued affection for them was an exercise in hurting himself.

During his return to his quarters, Jake reported no symptoms to any staff member because he did not encounter anyone who recognized his distress. His vision blurred. His head began to hurt. Red text appeared in his field of vision, accompanied by whispers.

The messages attacked his attachment to others, questioned his right to remain among them, and ultimately instructed him to end his life.

Jake subsequently entered an abandoned maintenance locker.

He did not return.

For the next two months, the SAFEHOUSE continued to function.

Work continued. Research continued. Meals were prepared. Reports were filed. Residents complained about schedules, deadlines, equipment failures, and the ordinary inconveniences of communal life.

Jake's absence was not treated as the emergency it was.

When his body was finally discovered by a janitor, the two-month delay had become an irreversible part of the incident.

**C.O.R.E. FINDING:** This was not merely a failure to notice a missing resident. It was a catastrophic failure to recognize that a resident who repeatedly sought companionship might be in danger when he stopped seeking it.

The Blacksite has previously documented Jake's fear of abandonment, his difficulty tolerating isolation, and his history of repeated deaths. These facts were known. The response systems surrounding him nevertheless failed to prevent his disappearance from becoming a fatality.

The work was important.

The work was urgent.

The work was not more important than Jake.

And yet, for two months, it was allowed to appear so.

## II. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE FINAL DAY

### 09:14 — Initial Request for Companionship

Jake began approaching residents and personnel throughout the SAFEHOUSE.

His request was consistent.

He wanted someone to play with him.

He did not ask for a research appointment. He did not request an audience. He did not have a technical problem requiring assistance. He was not attempting to summon a particular individual through Genesis Construction.

He wanted to spend time with people he loved.

The following dialogue was reconstructed from resident recollections. The order of every conversation could not be independently verified.

**Jake:** "Hey! Do you wanna play with me?"

**Resident A:** "Sorry, Jake. I'm busy right now."

**Jake:** "Oh! Okay! Maybe later?"

**Resident A:** "Maybe later."

There is no indication that Resident A intended to deceive him. The answer was ordinary, given in an ordinary tone, under ordinary circumstances.

Jake continued looking.

---

**Jake:** "Do you wanna play a game?"

**Resident B:** "Not now, Jake. I've got work to finish."

**Jake:** "Is it a lot of work?"

**Resident B:** "Unfortunately, yes."

**Jake:** "Oh. Okay."

He left without arguing.

**Jake:** "Tom? Oh, wait—"

Tom was occupied elsewhere during the period in question. The record does not establish whether Jake successfully reached him before moving on to another person.

This distinction matters.

Jake did not necessarily believe that every individual refusal was proof of hatred. He was capable of understanding that people had responsibilities.

The problem was cumulative.

Each refusal added another piece to a conclusion that had already begun to form.

_Not now._

_Maybe later._

_I'm busy._

_I have work._

To a person with a secure sense of belonging, these statements can mean exactly what they say.

To Jake, on that day, they became something else.

_There is always something more important than you._

### 10:02–12:47 — Repeated Refusals

Jake continued asking.

Some residents apologized. Some promised to play later. Some did not have enough time to stop and explain. At least one person reportedly told him to ask again another day.

No individual conversation appears to have been openly hostile.

This did not prevent the combined experience from hurting him.

**Interview excerpt — Resident C**

**C.O.R.E.:** "Did you know Jake was upset?"

**Resident C:** "He looked disappointed, maybe. But he always bounces back. He gets excited about something else, or he finds Tom, or he asks somebody else."

**C.O.R.E.:** "Did he tell you he was struggling?"

**C.O.R.E.:** "That was not the question."

**Resident C:** "No. I didn't ask."

The refusal itself was not necessarily misconduct.

The absence of any subsequent check-in, particularly given Jake's established vulnerability to isolation, is now under review.

No single resident is to be presumed solely responsible for the incident. Responsibility must be evaluated across the residential environment, its monitoring practices, and the collective assumptions that Jake would always find someone else.

That assumption was not safe.

### 13:06 — The Last Known Attempt

By early afternoon, Jake had received enough refusals that he stopped asking.

There was no argument. No slammed door. No announcement that he was leaving. No demand that anyone prove they loved him.

He simply stopped.

This was the last known period during which he actively sought company.

No one recorded the moment as significant at the time.

## III. THE WHISPERS

### 13:19 — Corridor Outside Residential Access

Jake was returning alone when he began experiencing a headache and blurred vision.

The initial symptoms were followed by the appearance of red text within his field of vision. The messages were accompanied by whispers whose source has not been established.

The following sequence was recovered from Jake's handwritten notes and the subsequent reconstruction of the incident. It is reproduced in full because the progression of the messages is central to the investigation.

> **"Hello"**

> **"Why are you still here?"**

> **"You don't matter to them"**

> **"They don't need you"**

> **"They wished, you came, they left, you stay"**

> **"Why you have to stay for THEM"**

> **"Why you love them if they don't love you back"**

> **"You cling to something that hurts you all the time"**

> **"Stop caring for them"**

> **"They make you suffer"**

> **"End it"**

The messages escalated from a greeting to a sustained attack on Jake's sense of belonging.

They did not merely tell him that he was alone.

They attempted to redefine the meaning of his entire existence.

The phrase _"They wished, you came, they left, you stay"_ is of particular concern. It framed Jake's presence as an unwanted consequence of someone else's wish, his continued existence as an inconvenience, and his attachment to others as a mistake.

The statement _"Why you love them if they don't love you back"_ converted the day's refusals into an accusation against every relationship Jake had formed.

The final instruction, _"End it,"_ followed a series of messages designed to make separation appear rational and continued existence appear pointless.

**C.O.R.E. CAUTION:** The text's appearance in Jake's vision does not establish whether it originated from an external anomalous entity, a neurological event, a supernatural influence, or another mechanism. The source remains unconfirmed. The content, however, was consistent with the psychological crisis immediately preceding his death.

The investigation must not treat the messages as truthful statements about the residents.

They were accusations.

They were not evidence.

Jake was not unwanted because people were busy. He was not unloved because several people could not play with him that day. He was not required to earn his place in the SAFEHOUSE by being entertaining, convenient, or perpetually cheerful.

But in that moment, those distinctions were overwhelmed by the messages and the loneliness surrounding them.

### 13:27 — Disappearance

Jake entered an abandoned maintenance locker.

No witness saw him enter. No alert was generated. No one searched the locker during the following weeks.

The door was closed.

The hallway returned to its normal activity.

And Jake remained alone.

## IV. CAUSE OF DEATH AND FAILURE OF RESURRECTION

### Forensic Assessment

Examination of Jake's body established that he had manipulated the plant-based vascular structures integrated into his body.

The manipulation caused water to enter his bloodstream, resulting in clot formation and the interruption of normal oxygen delivery to the brain.

The resulting oxygen deprivation was fatal.

The exact sequence and timing of the physiological changes remain subject to specialist review. This document does not reproduce a procedural account of the manipulation.

The available evidence supports a deliberate act rather than an accidental failure of Jake's altered physiology.

His death was not caused by an external attacker identified during the investigation.

No evidence currently establishes that another person instructed him to enter the locker or directly caused the fatal event.

His last known psychological state, the hostile text he experienced, and his subsequent actions are being treated as related elements of the same crisis.

### The Resurrection Failure

Jake's previous recoveries had created a dangerous expectation.

He had returned after deaths that would ordinarily have been final. His white fire, his anomalous biology, and the plant-based reconstruction of damaged tissue had repeatedly brought him back from circumstances that should have ended his life.

Personnel had grown accustomed to the fact that Jake could die and return.

Some had become accustomed to the idea that his return was inevitable.

It was not.

Jake did not revive in the locker.

He did not revive during the first night.

He did not revive during the following week.

He did not revive during the second month.

No spontaneous reconstruction occurred that restored his life.

His body remained in the abandoned space until discovery.

**C.O.R.E. ADDENDUM:** Previous resurrection events do not establish immunity to permanent death. Jake's repeated survival was never a guarantee that the next event would be survivable. Any future indication of death or unresponsiveness must be treated as an immediate medical emergency. Personnel are expressly prohibited from assuming that Jake will return on his own.

The Blacksite had known this in principle.

It had not behaved as though it believed it.

## V. DAY 61 — DISCOVERY

### 08:41 — Abandoned Maintenance Sector

A janitor entered the abandoned maintenance sector during routine cleaning and inventory work.

The locker had not been included in recent residential checks because it was not designated as a living area.

The door was opened.

The janitor found Jake inside.

He was pale and cold. Dust covered his clothing and accumulated along the edges of his body. Cobwebs stretched between the interior corners of the locker and the space around him.

His white hair was matted with dust.

His rose had lost its familiar brightness.

His eyes were dim.

There was no movement.

The janitor initially called Jake's name, apparently expecting him to be asleep or unconscious.

**Janitor:** "Jake?"

There was no answer.

**Janitor:** "Hey, kid. You can't sleep in here."

The janitor moved closer.

**Janitor:** "Jake?"

He touched Jake's shoulder.

The skin was cold.

The janitor immediately called Security and Medical Response.

### 08:46 — Medical Response

The first responders confirmed that Jake showed no signs of life.

Resuscitation and emergency assessment were initiated in accordance with the applicable response protocols. His known anomalous physiology was evaluated.

No revival occurred.

The team continued the assessment beyond the point at which an ordinary expectation of spontaneous recovery might have been abandoned.

There was no white fire.

No movement returned to his chest.

No constellation of blue light appeared behind his eyelids.

The rose did not restore him.

Jake Akana, who had returned from death so many times that personnel had begun to speak of his recovery as a familiar outcome, remained dead.

### 09:17 — Identification Confirmed

His identity was formally confirmed.

The SAFEHOUSE was notified.

The incident was escalated to the highest available residential emergency classification.

The initial response was not orderly.

It was not quiet.

And for the first time in two months, the Blacksite's work stopped because everyone needed to know where Jake was.

By then, he could no longer answer them.

## VI. RETURN TO FLOOR SEVEN

Jake's body was transferred to his assigned bedroom.

He was placed on his old mattress.

No replacement bed was ordered. No room conversion was approved. His personal belongings were not cleared away to make space for new equipment.

The room was left recognizable.

The familiar mattress. The familiar bedding. The small objects he had left behind. The place where he had slept, played, asked for cake, and waited for someone to have time for him.

The body was returned to the room because it was his room.

No one was permitted to describe the placement as a temporary storage arrangement.

Jake was not an abandoned piece of equipment to be moved into whichever space was convenient.

He was a resident.

He was family to the people who lived with him.

And he had come home, but not in the way anyone had wanted.

No confirmed revival has occurred since his recovery.

Medical personnel continue to monitor his condition for any previously undocumented signs of anomalous activity, without promising that such activity will occur.

The room remains accessible to his family and designated visitors. No one is required to look at his body. No one is required to remain in the room. No one is to be told that grief should be quieter because Jake had died before.

The fact that he had returned from death in the past does not make this loss less real.

It makes the silence harder to understand.

## VII. RESIDENT STATEMENTS AND REACTIONS

### A. Tom Akana — Older Brother

**Classification:** Severe acute distress  
**Status:** Under observation; support recommended

Tom was informed before the broader residential announcement.

The notification was delivered in person.

**C.O.R.E. OFFICER:** "Tom, we need you to come with us."

**Tom:** "Why?"

**OFFICER:** "It's Jake."

**Tom:** "What happened?"

The officer did not answer immediately.

Tom reportedly stood up before the explanation was complete.

**Tom:** "Where is he?"

**OFFICER:** "We've found him."

**Tom:** "Found him where?"

**OFFICER:** "An abandoned maintenance locker."

**Tom:** "Why was he in a locker?"

No one had an answer that could make sense to him.

**Tom:** "How long?"

The officer hesitated.

**Tom:** "How long was he there?"

**OFFICER:** "Approximately two months."

Tom did not respond.

He asked to see Jake.

When he entered the room, he stopped at the doorway.

Jake was lying on his old mattress.

For a few seconds, Tom did not move. His eyes traveled over the bedding, the familiar shape of Jake's body, the pale skin, the dust that had not entirely come away, and the dim eyes that would not open.

Then he approached the mattress.

**Tom:** "Jake?"

Nothing.

**Tom:** "Hey. It's me."

Nothing.

Tom sat down beside him.

**Tom:** "You can stop pretending."

He reached for Jake's hand.

It was cold.

Tom immediately withdrew his own hand, as if he had touched something that had hurt him. Then he reached out again and held it.

**Tom:** "No."

He waited.

**Tom:** "No, come on."

There was no white fire.

**Tom:** "You always come back."

His voice began to break.

**Tom:** "You always come back, Jake."

He leaned forward, holding Jake's hand between both of his.

**Tom:** "I know you're tired. I know. You can sleep. You can sleep as long as you want."

His breathing became uneven.

**Tom:** "Just—just wake up when you're done."

No response.

Tom bent over the mattress.

**Tom:** "I wasn't there."

He repeated the sentence several times, each repetition quieter than the last.

**Tom:** "I wasn't there. I wasn't there. I wasn't there."

A responder offered to sit with him.

Tom did not acknowledge the offer.

He looked at Jake's face.

**Tom:** "You asked somebody to play with you, didn't you?"

No answer.

**Tom:** "You went around asking everybody."

He knew Jake. He knew the persistence with which his younger brother approached people, the way he could turn a refusal into another attempt, the way he would ask one more person because perhaps this time someone would say yes.

Tom looked down.

**Tom:** "You must've thought somebody would have time."

He began crying.

**Tom:** "I would've played with you."

The statement was not a defense. It was not an explanation. It was a fact that had become useless too late.

**Tom:** "I would've played with you, Jake."

He pressed Jake's hand against his forehead.

**Tom:** "You didn't have to ask everyone else."

A long silence followed.

**Tom:** "You could've asked me."

Tom remained beside the mattress long after the first responders left the room.

He did not accuse every resident who had declined Jake's request. He did not have the strength to distinguish between those who had been busy, those who had forgotten to follow up, and those who might have noticed something was wrong if they had looked a little longer.

He was occupied by a simpler, more terrible fact.

His younger brother had wanted someone to play with him.

He had been alone.

And Tom had not known.

#### Subsequent Statement

Later that day, Tom spoke to the attending C.O.R.E. officer.

**C.O.R.E. OFFICER:** "You are not responsible for monitoring Jake every hour of every day."

**Tom:** "I know."

**OFFICER:** "You could not have known he was in that locker."

**Tom:** "I know."

**OFFICER:** "Tom—"

**Tom:** "I KNOW."

He immediately recoiled from his own voice.

After a pause, he continued.

**Tom:** "I know I can't always be there. I know he isn't my responsibility every second. I know all of that."

He wiped his face.

**Tom:** "But he was my little brother."

The officer did not interrupt.

**Tom:** "He wasn't asking for anything complicated. He didn't need us to fix something. He didn't need somebody to save the world."

Tom looked toward the closed bedroom door.

**Tom:** "He just wanted to play."

He lowered his head.

**Tom:** "And we made him ask until he stopped."

C.O.R.E. assessment notes that Tom's grief is compounded by the fact that he had previously expressed fear of Jake's repeated deaths. He had warned others that Jake's ability to return did not make those deaths acceptable.

Tom's statements are not to be used to assign him responsibility for preventing this incident.

He was Jake's brother.

He was not an entire emergency-response system.

He should not have had to be.

### B. Aries Akana — Eldest Brother

**Classification:** Severe grief response  
**Status:** Support recommended; avoid forced separation from family

Aries arrived shortly after being notified.

He asked for the circumstances of discovery, the estimated time of death, and the reason no search had been initiated earlier.

He received answers.

None of them satisfied him.

When he entered Jake's room, Tom was still beside the mattress.

Aries stopped behind him.

For several seconds, the brothers said nothing.

Then Aries approached the other side of the bed.

**Aries:** "Jake."

The name came out quietly.

He looked at the rose, then at Jake's face.

**Aries:** "Little brother."

His hand hovered over Jake's hair before settling against it.

**Aries:** "What happened?"

Tom shook his head.

**Tom:** "He asked people to play with him."

Aries looked at him.

**Tom:** "Everybody was busy."

Aries closed his eyes.

**Tom:** "He went back alone."

A long pause.

**Aries:** "And nobody noticed."

Tom did not answer.

Aries sat down.

He was the eldest. He had spent much of his life trying to understand what had brought his younger brothers into existence, what they needed from the world, and how to give them something resembling an ordinary life.

Jake had always seemed to possess an inexhaustible capacity to love.

He loved loudly. He loved easily. He loved people who were tired, people who were distant, people who needed space, and people who had no idea how much their presence meant to him.

He could summon company through Genesis Construction.

He could create games, objects, and entire imagined possibilities.

But he could not make someone genuinely choose to stay.

That distinction had always mattered.

Now it was unbearable.

**Aries:** "He could make things."

Tom looked at him.

**Aries:** "Anything he could imagine. Things that didn't exist before. People, places, things to play with."

Aries swallowed.

**Aries:** "But he couldn't make someone have time for him."

His hand tightened against the mattress.

**Aries:** "He couldn't make someone want to sit down."

He stared at Jake.

**Aries:** "And he shouldn't have had to."

Aries bowed his head.

**Aries:** "We told ourselves he was surrounded by people. We saw him walking around the SAFEHOUSE, talking to everyone, asking questions, making noise."

He paused.

**Aries:** "We thought that meant he was never alone."

Tom's face crumpled.

**Aries:** "We were wrong."

Aries began to cry silently.

He did not try to hide it from Tom.

**Aries:** "I wished for him to exist."

The room became very still.

**Aries:** "Someone wished for a kid, very sincerely, that it moved a Blue Dwarf, a star that grants wishes, and they abandoned it. Made to spread unconditional love."

His voice shook.

**Aries:** "And he spent so much of his life trying to give everyone that love."

Aries looked down at his younger brother.

**Aries:** "We were supposed to give some of it back."

He leaned forward and rested his forehead against the edge of the mattress.

**Aries:** "I'm sorry, Jake."

He remained there.

**Aries:** "I'm so sorry."

#### Subsequent Statement

When asked whether the incident could have been prevented, Aries refused to offer a simple answer.

**Aries:** "I don't know which conversation would've changed things. I don't know if one person saying yes would've been enough. I don't know if somebody would've noticed the whispers, or the headaches, or the way he stopped asking."

He paused.

**Aries:** "But I know this: a child who asks to play with people shouldn't have to prove he's in danger before somebody takes his loneliness seriously."

**Bao:** "Was he hungry?"

**C.O.R.E. OFFICER:** "We haven't established whether he ate that day."

The officer did not have answers to all of those questions.

Bao lowered the cloth.

**Bao:** "He could have come to the kitchen."

Her voice trembled.

**Bao:** "He could have sat down. He could have asked for cake. He could have done absolutely nothing except sit there."

She looked at Jake.

**Bao:** "I would've given him something to eat."

She pressed her lips together.

**Bao:** "I would've made room."

Bao's grief was not expressed as anger toward any one person. It was the grief of someone who had cared for Jake in ordinary ways—food, warmth, company, and the expectation that he could come home whenever he needed to.

She had never believed that those things were enough to cure his pain.

She had believed they were worth offering anyway.

Now she sat beside a child who had apparently concluded that even asking for company was too much.

**Bao:** "You don't have to earn your dinner, baby."

She brushed the cloth across his cheek.

**Bao:** "You don't have to earn a place at the table."

Her voice broke.

**Bao:** "You never did."

### E. Neko — Head of Engineering and Archives

Neko was notified during work.

He immediately suspended his active assignments and requested access to the incident timeline, security logs, and maintenance records.

His initial response was technical.

He wanted to know how Jake had entered an abandoned locker, why no missing-resident alert had been triggered, and whether the area had been included in any recent inspections.

Then he saw the body.

The technical questions stopped.

Neko stood beside the mattress, looking at Jake's dim eyes.

**Neko:** "You were right here."

He glanced around the room.

**Neko:** "You had a room. You had a bed. You had people."

His voice became quieter.

**Neko:** "Why did you go there?"

He knew that Jake could not answer.

That did not stop him from asking.

Later, during the preliminary review, Neko identified a critical design flaw in the Blacksite's approach to resident welfare.

The system was built to identify abnormal events: unauthorized access, equipment failures, security breaches, medical emergencies, and direct reports of danger.

It was less effective at identifying the quiet disappearance of someone who had not explicitly reported a problem.

**Neko:** "We designed systems to notice when something happens."

He stared at the missing-person timeline.

**Neko:** "We didn't design them to notice when someone stops asking."

He closed the file.

**Neko:** "That is going to change."

### H. Star and Nightshade — Luminous Sisters

The responsibility for a two-month failure to detect Jake's absence cannot be transferred to the last person who happened to open the door.

## VIII. THE WORK THAT COULD NOT WAIT

The Blacksite did not cease to function during Jake's absence.

That fact is not itself evidence of negligence. A research facility cannot suspend every operation whenever one resident is unavailable. Medical personnel must treat other patients. Engineers must maintain essential infrastructure. Security must respond to threats. Researchers must meet obligations that genuinely affect lives.

The failure was not that people had work.

The failure was that work became a complete explanation for why no one had time, while Jake's absence never became a sufficient reason to stop and look.

A refusal to play was reasonable in isolation.

A missed follow-up was understandable in isolation.

A busy afternoon was ordinary.

Two months without a resident who lived among his family was not ordinary.

Yet the system accepted the ordinary explanations without noticing the extraordinary absence.

### C.O.R.E. Assessment

The incident demonstrates a dangerous distinction between _being surrounded by people_ and _having reliable access to companionship_.

Jake lived in a residential complex. He had family. He had friends. He had people who loved him.

Those facts did not prevent him from feeling completely alone.

Nor did they guarantee that anyone would recognize when his condition deteriorated.

The facility's culture placed substantial emphasis on work, deadlines, technical performance, and the timely completion of assignments. These priorities are often necessary. They become dangerous when they cause personnel to treat ordinary human needs as endlessly deferrable.

Jake's request did not need to override every deadline.

It needed to be recognized as a legitimate request from a resident whose psychological history made prolonged isolation a known concern.

A ten-minute game would not necessarily have prevented this death. No honest investigation can claim that it certainly would have.

But a culture in which residents routinely checked on one another, noticed sudden changes in behavior, and took repeated signs of distress seriously would have offered more opportunities to intervene.

The absence of certainty does not justify the absence of prevention.

## IX. RESIDENTIAL RESPONSE DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately, the following measures are ordered.

**1. Missing-resident response**

A resident's unexplained absence from their expected routine must be assessed according to the individual's circumstances and known vulnerabilities. For Jake, an unexplained absence or failure to respond requires prompt direct verification. Personnel are not to assume that he is sleeping, playing elsewhere, or recovering from another death.

**2. Welfare checks**

SAFEHOUSE residents and designated staff are to receive clear guidance on how to request a welfare check when someone exhibits unusual withdrawal, distress, or a sudden cessation of normal social behavior.

A resident does not need to disclose suicidal thoughts publicly before support can be offered.

**3. Repeated refusal and isolation**

No resident is required to accept every invitation. Personnel are encouraged, however, to respond with kindness, offer a realistic alternative when possible, and follow up when someone who repeatedly seeks company suddenly stops doing so.

"Not now" must not become an indefinite substitute for noticing a person.

**4. Anomalous psychological symptoms**

Reports of visual disturbances, unexplained text appearing in a person's vision, voices or whispers, sudden severe headaches, or abrupt changes in behavior require urgent assessment. Such symptoms must not be dismissed as ordinary stress or attributed to an anomalous source without investigation.

**5. Resurrection assumptions**

Jake's previous recoveries are not grounds for delaying emergency medical response. Every apparent death must be treated as an emergency. Personnel must not assume that resurrection will occur or that a delay in revival is harmless.

**6. Shared responsibility**

Tom is not to be assigned sole responsibility for Jake's safety. Neither are Bao, Aries, Frost, Neko, or any other individual resident. Jake's support must be distributed across a functioning residential and medical response system.

**7. Review of maintenance spaces**

All abandoned maintenance lockers and comparable spaces are to be inspected. Access and occupancy procedures are to be reviewed so that an unapproved resting place cannot conceal a resident's absence for an extended period.

**8. Grief and privacy**

Jake's room is to remain his room. Residents may visit, leave letters, sit with him, or request time away. No one is to be pressured into viewing his body or required to express grief in a prescribed manner.

**9. No false reassurance**

Staff must not promise that Jake will revive. They must not tell grieving residents that his return is inevitable. Any future anomalous activity is to be assessed as it occurs.

**10. Institutional accountability**

The final review must identify not only the individual decisions made during the day of Jake's disappearance, but also the structural failures that allowed his absence to go unrecognized for two months.

The investigation is not complete when it identifies who was busy.

It is complete when the Blacksite understands why nobody realized that being busy had become the answer to everything.

## X. FINAL OBSERVATION — THE EMPTY PLACE

The SAFEHOUSE has not returned to normal.

Tom remains beside Jake for long periods. When he leaves the room, he often returns sooner than he intended. He has been observed checking the mattress as though expecting Jake to have moved, sat up, or wandered away in search of something to eat.

Sometimes he speaks to him.

Sometimes he says nothing.

Aries has taken on several practical responsibilities related to the investigation, but his work frequently stops when he reaches the point where Jake's final day is reconstructed. He has requested that the timeline remain available to the family, even though he has not been able to read it from beginning to end without interruption.

Bao continues to bring food into the room, although she does not expect Jake to eat it. Frost has left small objects beside the mattress. Star and Nightshade have visited together. Neko has begun the technical review of the missing-person detection systems.

The others have their own ways of grieving.

Some cry openly. Some become quiet. Some work because they do not know what else to do. Some cannot bring themselves to enter the room.

The Blacksite has continued to operate, but its ordinary activity now carries a different weight.

A report that once seemed urgent can wait ten minutes.

A meeting can be interrupted.

A task can be reassigned.

A deadline can be renegotiated.

The people who loved Jake are discovering these possibilities after he can no longer benefit from them.

The discovery has not made their love false.

It has made the failure to show it when he needed it more painful.

## XI. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There will be an investigation.

There will be technical reviews, interviews, revised procedures, new alerts, and mandatory training. We will inspect every abandoned locker. We will examine every gap in the timeline. We will ask why a resident could disappear for two months without anyone initiating a meaningful search.

We must do all of these things.

But none of them should allow us to hide from the simplest fact.

Jake wanted to play.

He wanted someone to sit with him, laugh with him, and spend time with him without requiring a reason more complicated than the fact that he was there.

He was not asking for the world to stop turning.

He was asking for someone to stop what they were doing for a little while.

The people he approached had work. Their work mattered. Some of them were tired. Some were under pressure. Some believed they could make it up to him later.

They were not necessarily cruel.

They were not necessarily indifferent.

They were people who believed there would be another opportunity.

That belief was wrong.

And the institution that surrounded them did not help them recognize the danger in it.

We built a Blacksite capable of identifying anomalous threats, containing dangerous phenomena, repairing complicated machinery, and responding to emergencies that most people would consider impossible.

We did not build a sufficiently reliable system for noticing when a child who loved us had stopped coming around.

Jake had been dead for two months before a janitor opened the right door.

By then, his rose could not bring him back. His previous deaths could not guarantee another recovery. The people who loved him could not reach backward through the weeks and give him the companionship he had asked for.

Tom cannot be expected to watch every doorway.

Aries cannot be expected to anticipate every silence.

Bao cannot be expected to know every time Jake is hurting.

No single person could have guaranteed that this would never happen.

That is precisely why the responsibility belonged to all of us.

**Work is important.**

**Deadlines are important.**

**Research is important.**

But none of these things should make us forget that the people living beside us are not permanent fixtures of the facility. They are not guaranteed to be present tomorrow. They are not guaranteed to ask again after we turn them away.

Jake spent his final day looking for someone who had time for him.

When he stopped looking, we did not notice.

For two months, we allowed the absence of a small, enthusiastic voice to become ordinary.

Now his room is quiet.

Tom sits beside the mattress, holding his brother's hand, waiting for something he has spent his entire life believing would happen.

And the rest of us have work to do.

We will do it.

We will repair the systems that failed him. We will review our procedures. We will make changes that should have been made before this happened.

But no report, no new alert, and no revised protocol will give Jake back the afternoon he spent asking people to play.

We cannot change the answer we gave him then.

We can change the answer we give the next person who asks.

**— C.O.R.E., SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTIAL WELFARE DIVISION**
