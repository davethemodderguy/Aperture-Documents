# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### MEDICAL & RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-MED-4C-NERI-290  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — MEDICAL / RESIDENT WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE INCIDENT REVIEW  
**SUBJECT:** Neri — Traumatic Impact Following Reflexive Handling Response

---

# INCIDENT SUMMARY

At approximately 16:20, an MSAC Operator was handling Neri while she was in plush form.

Neri became visibly uncomfortable.

The Operator did not immediately recognize the change in her behavior.

Neri bit the Operator.

The bite caused **no physical injury and no significant pain.**

The Operator reacted reflexively.

He rapidly swung his arm.

Neri remained attached to the Operator's hand at the moment of movement.

The resulting motion flung Neri across the room.

She struck a wall.

---

# IMPACT

Neri did not immediately move.

For approximately several seconds, she remained against the floor where she had landed.

Personnel initially expected the usual response from a plush-form impact:

- repositioning;
    
- standing;
    
- rolling away;
    
- or simply staring at whoever caused the incident.
    

None occurred.

Neri was frightened.

She appeared disoriented.

Then she began crying.

Not loudly.

She simply remained where she was.

Her eyes continuously produced tears.

---

# MEDICAL OBSERVATION

The most significant complaint was not external injury.

It was pain.

Neri repeatedly held the area corresponding to her chest.

More specifically:

> **Cardiac region.**

Her breathing became shallow.

She curled inward instinctively.

Her plush body remained soft and intact, with no obvious external damage sufficient to explain the severity of her reaction.

This did **not** indicate that the impact was harmless.

Neri's plush form provides substantial protection against ordinary impacts.

It does not make her internal organs indestructible.

Her cardiovascular system is already unusually fragile.

The impact therefore produced significant internal pain despite the absence of obvious external injury.

---

# NERI'S RESPONSE

A medical technician approached slowly.

> **TECHNICIAN:**  
> “Neri?”

Neri looked toward them.

Her glasses had shifted slightly.

Her eyes were wet.

She attempted to speak.

Nothing came out.

The technician stopped approaching.

> **TECHNICIAN:**  
> “Okay.”

> **TECHNICIAN:**  
> “I'm not going to touch you.”

Neri remained curled against the floor.

Her eyes continued watering.

A few seconds later:

> **Neri:**  
> “...Hurts.”

The word was extremely quiet.

The technician immediately contacted Bao.

---

# BAO ARRIVES

Bao entered the room several minutes later.

She did not immediately pick Neri up.

She sat down a short distance away.

> **BAO:**  
> “Neri.”

Neri looked at her.

She was still crying.

> **BAO:**  
> “I'm here.”

Neri did not move.

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't have to come to me.”

Neri remained where she was.

Bao waited.

Eventually Neri slowly crawled toward her.

She stopped halfway.

Bao opened her arms without reaching forward.

Neri continued.

She climbed into Bao's lap.

The moment she was supported, she curled tightly against Bao's chest.

Her eyes continued to water.

---

# MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

Neri remained in significant pain for an extended period.

The pain was concentrated primarily around the cardiac region.

C.O.R.E. medical personnel monitored:

- consciousness;
    
- breathing;
    
- circulation;
    
- responsiveness;
    
- pain progression;
    
- cardiovascular stability;
    
- and signs of renewed anemic instability.
    

No immediate cardiac arrest occurred.

No catastrophic structural injury was identified.

However, the event is classified as a **serious medical incident.**

The absence of visible plush damage is not an acceptable indicator of internal safety.

---

# OPERATOR RESPONSE

The MSAC Operator responsible for the reflexive movement remained in the room.

He was visibly shaken.

> **OPERATOR:**  
> “I didn't mean to.”

No response.

Neri was still crying.

> **OPERATOR:**  
> “She bit me and I—”

Bao looked toward him.

> **BAO:**  
> “She was telling you to stop.”

The Operator lowered his head.

> **OPERATOR:**  
> “I know.”

> **BAO:**  
> “Her bite didn't hurt you.”

Silence.

> **BAO:**  
> “Your reaction hurt her.”

The Operator did not attempt to defend himself.

---

# PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION

The physical injury was not the only concern.

Neri's behavior following the incident was markedly different from her normal response to accidental impacts.

Normally, plush Neri demonstrates considerable confidence regarding ordinary falls and bumps.

This time she appeared:

- frightened;
    
- hesitant to move;
    
- highly distressed;
    
- physically guarded;
    
- unusually quiet;
    
- and reluctant to allow unfamiliar personnel near her.
    

She repeatedly looked toward the Operator.

Not angrily.

Fearfully.

When he moved his arm, Neri visibly flinched.

This reaction persisted even after she had been moved to a medical area.

---

# THE SQUEAK

The impact produced an unusually loud squeak.

It was substantially louder than Neri's normal plush noises.

Personnel initially assumed the sound was simply caused by the force of the collision.

This assumption was incorrect.

The sound was accompanied by immediate distress.

Neri's usual plush squeak is incidental.

This one was a **distress response.**

It should not be reproduced.

It should not be joked about.

It should not be deliberately tested.

---

# POST-INCIDENT BEHAVIOR

Neri remained with Bao.

She did not return to normal activities.

She did not resume the Hell Fire Lottery.

She did not approach Alice.

She did not approach Jane.

She did not approach the Operator.

She simply stayed curled against Bao.

Her eyes continued watering intermittently.

At one point Bao gently asked:

> **BAO:**  
> “Still hurts?”

Neri nodded.

> **BAO:**  
> “Mostly here?”

Bao indicated the general chest area without pressing it.

Neri nodded again.

After several seconds:

> **Neri:**  
> “Scared.”

Bao's expression changed immediately.

> **BAO:**  
> “I know.”

Neri buried her face against Bao.

---

# C.O.R.E. FINDING

This incident demonstrates a critical limitation of the current Plush Neri handling guidelines.

The bite was a boundary signal.

It was not an attack.

The Operator's reflexive reaction transformed a harmless communicative behavior into a high-energy impact.

No malicious intent has been established.

Nevertheless:

> **Intent does not eliminate consequence.**

Neri was frightened.

Neri was in deep pain.

Neri's cardiovascular system was placed under unnecessary stress.

And she is now temporarily afraid of being handled by personnel who are not already trusted.

---

# UPDATED HANDLING DIRECTIVE

Effective immediately:

### If Plush Neri bites:

**STOP.**

Do not yank your hand away.

Do not swing your arm.

Do not shake her loose.

Do not throw her.

Do not attempt to frighten her.

Do not retaliate.

Simply stop the interaction and allow her to release herself.

If necessary, request assistance from a trusted resident or welfare staff member.

---

# ADDITIONAL DIRECTIVE

Personnel are reminded:

> **Plush Neri is a person.**

She may look like a small stuffed toy.

She may squeak.

She may bite without causing pain.

She may happily tolerate being carried around.

None of these properties make her an object.

She controls when she wants to be handled.

---

## FINAL MEDICAL NOTE

Several hours after the incident, Neri remained physically stable.

The pain gradually decreased.

The tears did not stop immediately.

She remained unusually clingy toward Bao and visibly wary of unfamiliar movement.

When asked whether she wanted to return to her room, Neri shook her head.

Bao asked:

> **“Want to stay here?”**

Neri nodded.

She remained in Bao's arms.

Quiet.

Exhausted.

Still frightened.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Neri's bite did not hurt the Operator.

His reflex hurt Neri.

That distinction matters.

She had been trying to communicate one very small thing:

> **“Please stop.”**

She did not attack anyone.

She did not injure anyone.

She simply used the only response available to her at that moment.

And then she was thrown across a room.

The resulting squeak was loud enough for everyone nearby to hear.

The crying afterward was much quieter.

That was considerably worse.
