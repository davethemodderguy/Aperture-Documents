### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — DO NOT ASK ABOUT HER MOTHER**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICALLY SURVIVED / PROTECTED RESIDENT**

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

C.O.R.E. medical personnel have reached an uncomfortable conclusion:

**Alice should not have survived eight months alone.**

Not under those conditions.

Not at her age.

Not with her physiology.

For more than eight months, Alice survived on an inadequate combination of:

- Snow
    
- Grass
    
- Wild flowers
    
- Whatever other edible vegetation she could find
    

She endured prolonged cold exposure, malnutrition, isolation, injury, and repeated illness while possessing naturally weak muscles, fragile bones, and a severely vulnerable immune system.

From a conventional medical perspective, her survival is extraordinarily unlikely.

Yet she survived.

She was still there when Corin found her.

Small.

Weak.

Hungry.

Cold.

Alive.

---

# **THE MEDICAL TEAM'S CONCLUSION**

Alice's survival should not be interpreted as evidence that the conditions were survivable.

They were not.

Her body was operating far beyond what should have been sustainable.

The fact that she remained alive is considered an exceptional physiological anomaly.

The medical department has therefore issued a standing order:

> **Alice is never to be placed in a situation where her survival depends upon her ability to endure those conditions again.**

---

# **MEMORY PROTECTION**

There is another matter considered equally important.

**Do not remind Alice of her mother.**

Do not ask her about her.

Do not casually mention her.

Do not attempt to make Alice remember.

Do not ask:

> "What was your mother's name?"

> "Do you remember what she looked like?"

> "Where did your mother live?"

> "What happened to your mother?"

Do not use the surviving memory of:

> **"Mom killed."**

as a conversational prompt.

Alice's memories of her mother are already extremely fragmented.

Attempts to access them cause severe emotional distress.

---

# **WHY**

Alice does not remember her mother's face.

She does not remember her name.

She does not remember her voice.

She does not remember enough of her life to reconstruct the person she lost.

She remembers only that:

> **Mom was there.**

Then:

> **Mom was hurt.**

Then:

> **Mom was killed.**

Everything surrounding those memories is largely gone.

Bringing the subject back repeatedly does not restore Alice's childhood.

It only causes her pain.

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE RULE**

A new rule has been added to Alice's personnel file:

> ### **DO NOT REMIND ALICE OF HER MOTHER.**

If Alice herself brings up the subject, personnel should:

1. Listen.
    
2. Remain with her.
    
3. Allow her to decide how much she wants to say.
    
4. Do not pressure her for additional memories.
    
5. Do not attempt forced memory recovery.
    
6. Do not tell her that she _should_ remember.
    
7. Do not tell her to stop crying.
    

If Alice changes the subject, the subject is considered closed.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin understands the rule better than anyone.

She never asks Alice about her mother.

Not because Corin doesn't care.

Because she does.

One afternoon, Alice was sitting beside Corin.

She looked toward the snow.

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then:

> **"...Mom."**

Corin didn't ask what she remembered.

She simply reached out.

Alice leaned against her.

After a while, Alice whispered:

> **"Don't know."**

Corin replied:

> **"That's okay."**

Alice nodded.

And they stayed there.

---

# **THE IMPORTANT MEMORY**

C.O.R.E. personnel have begun treating one particular fact as more important than recovering Alice's past.

Alice does not need to remember everything.

She needs to know what exists **now**.

She has:

**Corin.**

**Orphie.**

**Belle.**

**The SAFEHOUSE.**

**A bed.**

**Food.**

**Warmth.**

**People who will come looking for her.**

The past cannot be repaired.

The present can.

---

# **ALICE'S CURRENT ROUTINE**

Alice is slowly being introduced to things she never had the opportunity to experience normally.

Proper meals.

Warm baths.

Soft blankets.

Toys.

Books.

Games.

Gardening.

Sleeping somewhere warm.

Being told that she doesn't need to search for food.

Being told that tomorrow's food will still exist.

Being allowed to be a child.

---

# **CORIN'S MOST IMPORTANT LESSON**

Corin has taught Alice one thing repeatedly:

> **"You don't have to survive today."**

Alice looked confused the first time.

> **"Why?"**

Corin answered:

> **"Because you're safe."**

Alice thought about this.

> **"...Safe?"**

> **"Safe."**

Alice smiled.

A genuine one.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice survived eight months that should have killed her.

She survived because she had no choice.

She ate snow because she needed water.

She ate grass because she needed food.

She slept in the cold because there was nowhere else.

She kept walking because stopping meant dying.

She was five years old.

She should never have had to do any of it.

Now she doesn't.

Nobody needs to remind her of the person she lost.

Nobody needs to force her to remember.

Nobody needs to make her relive the snow.

Her past can remain quiet.

For now, Alice needs something much simpler.

A warm bed.

A full stomach.

Someone beside her.

And the certainty that when she wakes up tomorrow...

**someone will still be there.**
