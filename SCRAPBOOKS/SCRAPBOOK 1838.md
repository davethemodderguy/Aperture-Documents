### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana and Camelia Candleheart do not sleep at night.

Although both residents maintain humanoid forms resembling children, neither has demonstrated a conventional nighttime sleep cycle during observed periods. The reason for Jake's wakefulness has not been established. Camelia, however, has developed a habit of imitating sleep.

She lies down, closes her eyes, and remains still beneath her blankets. To a casual observer, she appears to be resting.

She is not.

Camelia's severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia has not prevented her from experiencing intermittent fragments of her past life. These fragments occur without warning and can manifest as nightmares when she attempts to sleep. The content is inconsistent, incomplete, and often impossible for her to identify afterward.

The episodes leave her frightened and distressed. On some occasions, she wakes—or abruptly abandons the attempt to sleep—without understanding what she has experienced. She may remember a sensation, a sound, a fleeting image, or nothing that she can explain. The fear, however, remains.

Her inability to remember her past does not protect her from the emotional consequences of it.

Jake has increasingly remained nearby during these nighttime episodes. His own speech impairment limits what he can say, but he appears to recognize when his older sister is frightened.

This report documents several observations and establishes welfare recommendations for both residents.

---

### II. NIGHTTIME OBSERVATIONS

#### Observation A — 00:43, Residential Wing

Camelia was lying in bed with her eyes closed. Her notebook was on the bedside table, positioned within reach.

Jake was sitting on the floor beside her bed.

Neither resident showed signs of conventional sleep.

After approximately twenty minutes of silence, Camelia opened her eyes.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake?”

**JAKE:** “Here.”

**CAMELIA:** “You're awake.”

**JAKE:** “Always... awake.”

_Camelia looks at the ceiling._

**CAMELIA:** “Right.”

_Silence._

**CAMELIA:** “I thought if I kept my eyes closed long enough, it might work.”

**JAKE:** “Sleep?”

**CAMELIA:** “Pretending to sleep, at least.”

_Jake tilts his head._

**CAMELIA:** “Everyone else does it. They lie down, close their eyes, and eventually they stop thinking about things.”

**JAKE:** “You... think?”

**CAMELIA:** “Too much.”

_She pulls the blanket closer around herself._

**CAMELIA:** “Sometimes I almost feel like I'm going to fall asleep. Then something happens.”

**JAKE:** “Bad?”

**CAMELIA:** “I don't know.”

_She hesitates._

**CAMELIA:** “That's the worst part. I don't know what I'm seeing. I don't know where I am. Sometimes I can't even tell whether it's something that happened or something my mind made up.”

**JAKE:** “Scared?”

**CAMELIA:** “Yes.”

_Jake reaches toward the edge of the bed and rests one hand against it._

**JAKE:** “I'm... here.”

**CAMELIA:** “I know.”

_She closes her eyes again, but keeps one hand outside the blanket, resting close to where Jake is sitting._

Neither resident sleeps during the observation period.

---

#### Observation B — 02:17, Residential Wing

Camelia appeared to be asleep when a sudden movement disturbed her blanket.

She recoiled against the headboard, her three magenta flames flickering erratically. Her breathing was rapid, and she repeatedly looked toward the far corner of the room.

Jake was beside her within seconds.

**JAKE:** “Camelia?”

_She does not answer._

**JAKE:** “Sister?”

_Camelia flinches at the sound of his voice, then turns toward him._

**CAMELIA:** “Don't—”

_She stops abruptly._

**JAKE:** “No one... here.”

**CAMELIA:** “I saw—”

_She presses both hands against her head._

**CAMELIA:** “I saw something.”

**JAKE:** “What?”

**CAMELIA:** “I don't know!”

_Her voice rises sharply. She looks frightened by her own inability to explain herself._

**CAMELIA:** “I couldn't see it properly. There was something, and I— I thought I knew where I was, but I didn't, and then—”

_She stops speaking. Her expression becomes distant._

**CAMELIA:** “I can't remember.”

_Jake remains beside her. He does not touch her without permission._

**JAKE:** “Can... I?”

_He raises one hand slightly, asking whether he may hold hers._

_Camelia looks at him, then nods._

_Jake takes her hand._

**CAMELIA:** “Why does it keep happening?”

**JAKE:** “Don't... know.”

**CAMELIA:** “What if I remember something terrible?”

_Jake is quiet for several seconds._

**JAKE:** “Not... alone.”

_Camelia grips his hand more tightly._

**CAMELIA:** “You promise?”

**JAKE:** “Promise.”

_The observation records no further attempt by Camelia to close her eyes. She remains seated with Jake until her distress subsides._

**Post-event note:** The fragment's content could not be established. No conclusion can be drawn regarding whether it represented an actual past event, a distorted recollection, or another phenomenon. Camelia's distress was observable regardless of the fragment's origin.

#### Observation C — 03:06, Residential Wing

Camelia had opened her notebook and was writing.

Jake sat on the floor nearby, his spectral teddy bear resting in his lap.

**CAMELIA:** “I'm writing it down.”

**JAKE:** “What?”

**CAMELIA:** “That I had another nightmare.”

_She looks at the page._

**CAMELIA:** “I can't write down what happened. I don't know what happened. But I can write down that I was scared.”

**JAKE:** “Good.”

**CAMELIA:** “I hate this.”

_Jake looks up at her._

**CAMELIA:** “I don't remember my name. I don't remember my life. I don't remember the people I might have known. But apparently, whatever I can't remember can still frighten me.”

_She puts the pencil down._

**CAMELIA:** “How is that fair?”

_Jake has no answer._

**CAMELIA:** “Sorry. I shouldn't ask you that.”

**JAKE:** “Can... ask.”

_Camelia looks at him._

**JAKE:** “Don't... need answer.”

_She lets out a small, unsteady breath._

**CAMELIA:** “You know, for someone who can barely talk, you sometimes say exactly what I need to hear.”

**JAKE:** “Lucky... words.”

**CAMELIA:** “Is that what they are?”

**JAKE:** “Maybe.”

_For the first time that night, Camelia gives a faint, brief smile. It disappears quickly, but Jake notices._

_Neither attempts to sleep again._

### III. REPEATING PATTERN: THE PRETENCE OF REST

Across subsequent observations, Camelia's behavior followed a recurring pattern:

1. She prepares for bed at an ordinary hour, sometimes even changing into her usual nighttime clothing.
    
2. She places her notebook within reach and lies down.
    
3. She closes her eyes and attempts to imitate the behavior she observes in sleeping residents.
    
4. If a memory fragment emerges, she may startle, recoil, become disoriented, or sit upright in visible distress.
    
5. She struggles to explain what she experienced. Even when she cannot recall the fragment itself, the fear may persist.
    
6. She generally abandons the attempt to sleep and returns to her notebook or sits with Jake.
    

The severity and content of these episodes vary. Not every attempt at rest results in a nightmare, and C.O.R.E. has not established whether the fragments occur exclusively when she is near sleep.

Her notebook helps her record the fact that an episode occurred, along with any details she can safely identify. It does not reliably restore the missing memories.

**C.O.R.E. assessment:** Camelia's imitation of sleep should not be mistaken for successful rest, nor should her failure to sleep be treated as deliberate noncompliance.

She is attempting to participate in a routine that other residents take for granted. The attempt repeatedly exposes her to an experience she cannot predict or understand.

---

### IV. JAKE'S ROLE

Jake remains awake throughout the observed nighttime periods. His reason for doing so remains undetermined, and C.O.R.E. will not assume that his condition is identical to Camelia's.

He has nevertheless become a consistent presence during her episodes.

His support generally consists of a few words, staying nearby, asking before initiating physical contact, and allowing Camelia to decide whether she wants company.

This approach appears to help her regain a sense of orientation.

However, C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Jake is also a child and a resident with significant trauma of his own. He must not become Camelia's sole nighttime support simply because he is awake when other residents are sleeping.

His fragmented speech should not be interpreted as an inability to understand distress. Equally, his willingness to comfort Camelia must not be taken as proof that he is unaffected by these events.

During one later observation, a staff member asked Jake whether he wanted to leave Camelia's room and spend time elsewhere.

**STAFF MEMBER:** “You don't have to stay here every night, Jake.”

**JAKE:** “I know.”

**STAFF MEMBER:** “You can get someone if she needs help.”

**JAKE:** “Know.”

_He looks toward Camelia, who is writing in her notebook._

**JAKE:** “Want... stay.”

The staff member accepted his answer and reminded him that he could change his mind at any time.

No further pressure was applied.

### VI. WELFARE DIRECTIVE

Effective immediately, the following recommendations are entered into the SAFEHOUSE resident-care record.

**1. No forced sleep routines.**  
Camelia is not to be pressured into maintaining a conventional sleep schedule merely for the appearance of normality. Her attempts to imitate sleep should be approached with patience rather than correction.

**2. Preserve her sense of control.**  
If Camelia becomes distressed, staff should identify themselves, speak calmly, and avoid crowding or touching her without permission. She should be allowed to choose whether she wants company, space, or assistance.

**3. Maintain access to her notebook.**  
Her notebook remains an essential accessibility aid. It must be kept within reach when she requests it, and staff must not alter its contents without permission.

**4. Do not force recollection.**  
Staff must not encourage Camelia to reconstruct frightening fragments, speculate about their meaning, or treat them as verified accounts of past events. Her distress warrants care even when the underlying memory cannot be identified.

**5. Provide support independent of Jake.**  
Jake may choose to accompany his sister, but he is not responsible for managing her condition. C.O.R.E. personnel must remain available so that both children can request help without feeling obligated to care for one another continuously.

**6. Respect both residents' nighttime needs.**  
Neither resident is to be treated as a convenient overnight monitor because they remain awake. They should have access to quiet activities, companionship, and private space without being required to justify their wakefulness.

---

### VII. FINAL OBSERVATION

The SAFEHOUSE is full of people who sleep at night.

Doors close. Lights dim. Conversations stop. Eventually, most residents retreat into dreams they will forget by morning.

Jake and Camelia remain awake.

Jake has his own reasons, most of which remain outside the scope of this observation. Camelia, meanwhile, lies beneath her blankets and tries to imitate something she cannot reliably experience.

Sometimes she succeeds in remaining still for hours.

Sometimes a fragment arrives.

A sound without a source. An image without a context. A feeling of danger without an explanation.

She wakes frightened, unable to identify what frightened her. She searches for words, but the memory has already slipped away. All that remains is the feeling that something terrible has happened—or is about to happen.

Then Jake asks if she is all right.

Sometimes she answers. Sometimes she cannot.

He does not have enough words to explain her past to her. He cannot tell her what the fragments mean. He cannot promise that she will remember, or that she will forget.

He can only offer the truth he knows.

He is here.

And Camelia, who has forgotten nearly everything about the person she used to be, can still recognize her little brother.

For now, C.O.R.E. will not measure progress by whether Camelia eventually manages to sleep through the night. Nor will it measure Jake's well-being by how often he stays awake beside her.

The immediate objective is smaller, and more important.

To make the dark hours less frightening.

To ensure that neither child has to face distress without help.

And to remind them, whenever they need reminding, that they do not have to pretend to be all right just because everyone else is asleep.
