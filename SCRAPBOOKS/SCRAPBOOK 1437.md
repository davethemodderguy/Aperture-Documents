# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake is gone.

The search for him has failed.

Not because his location cannot be reached.

Not because his floor cannot be reconstructed.

Not because he is hiding.

Because, at approximately 20:43, **Jake ceased to exist within the collective memory and records of the SAFEHOUSE.**

The process was not observed directly.

There was no explosion.

No rift.

No Null Shell.

No stellar collapse.

No detectable energy discharge.

Instead, information concerning Jake began disappearing.

Then memories disappeared.

Then recognition disappeared.

Then the people who had known him simply...

**forgot.**

Neko was reviewing the SAFEHOUSE resident directory.

Nothing.

Only an empty space.

# 3. THE TEDDY BEAR

The teddy bear remained.

This was the first anomaly.

It was sitting exactly where Jake had left it.

Nobody recognized it.

Bao picked it up.

She stared at it.

> **BAO:**  
> "Why do we have this?"

Neko examined it.

> **NEKO:**  
> "I don't know."

The teddy bear's core was inactive.

Its fabric showed signs of extensive wear.

Nobody knew how they had happened.

Nobody knew why Bao suddenly felt like crying while holding it.

She could not explain the feeling.

> **NEKO:**

# 5. ANBY'S BIRTHDAY

Anby's birthday celebration continued in the aftermath.

Nobody could remember why it had stopped.

The cake remained untouched.

Several apples remained on the table.

One apple had a bite taken from it.

Nobody knew who had eaten it.

Anby eventually asked:

> **ANBY:**  
> "Why did we stop the party?"

Silence.

No one knew.

Then she noticed something on the table.

A birthday card.

She picked it up.

She read it.

> **"Hi Anby, Happy Birthday."**

She frowned.

> **"I'm better now, can we go somewhere sometime?"**

Anby stared at the handwriting.

> **"Have fun!"**

Her expression became confused.

Then she read the final line.

> **"Knew apples was for your favorite so I brought some."**

Anby looked at the apples.

She did not know who had written the card.

She did not know who had brought them.

She did not know why the handwriting felt familiar.

She simply knew that the card made her chest hurt.

The cafeteria.

The medical bay.

The elevator.

The library.

A quiet hallway.

She could not explain why.

She simply kept looking.

One evening, she picked up the teddy bear again.

She held it against her chest.

Her eyes filled with tears.

> **BAO:**  
> "...I'm sorry."

She immediately froze.

She looked around.

Nobody was there.

> **BAO:**  
> "Who am I apologizing to?"

She had no answer.

# 11. COMPLETE ERASURE

The phenomenon has been classified as:

**SELF-ERASURE.**

Jake did not merely hide his location.

He removed himself from the information structure connecting him to this reality.

His name disappeared.

His records disappeared.

His photographs disappeared.

His room disappeared from the directory.

His history disappeared.

And finally—

**the memories disappeared.**

The process appears to have affected everyone who knew him.

There is currently no evidence that anyone retained a complete memory of Jake.

---

# 12. THE CRUELTY OF THE RESULT

Jake had once feared being forgotten.

That fear was repeatedly documented.

He believed that if nobody remembered him, then his existence had no meaning.

During the previous Nullwalker incident, the consequences of being forgotten became literal.

This time, Jake appears to have made the decision himself.

He did not wait for everyone to forget him.

He made himself forgettable.

He removed every reason for anyone to remember.

And it worked.

Not because they hated him.

Not because they wanted him gone.

But because there was no longer anyone left to remember that he had ever been there.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The greatest tragedy is not that Jake disappeared.

It is that the world accepted his disappearance perfectly.

No alarms.

No memorial.

No missing-person posters.

No grieving residents.

No one asking where he went.

Because there is nobody left who remembers that he was supposed to be here.

Jake wanted to know whether anyone would care if he died.

Now he has created a reality in which nobody can even remember that he lived.

The party ended.

The apples were eaten.

The card was kept.

The elevator continued operating.

Everyone went back to their lives.

And somewhere beyond the reach of every coordinate C.O.R.E. possesses—

There is a little star who remembers all of them.

While none of them remember him.
