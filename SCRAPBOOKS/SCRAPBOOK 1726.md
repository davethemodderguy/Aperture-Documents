# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake's first birthday celebration was successfully conducted.

The event was organized by Clover Luminare, with contributions from residents throughout the SAFEHOUSE. The centerpiece was a large collaborative painting that had been gradually completed over the preceding days.

The painting depicted a beautiful nighttime landscape beneath an expansive, star-filled sky.

Its setting was a place where the residents could gather together and go stargazing.

Every resident who contributed to the painting left a signature somewhere within the artwork.

Some signatures were prominent.

Others were small enough to require careful inspection.

All were genuine.

Jake was unaware of the preparations until the moment of the reveal.

Upon entering the cafeteria, he discovered the decorations, the cake, the gathered residents, and the completed painting.

He initially appeared confused.

Then he understood that the celebration was for him.

**Jake began crying almost immediately.**

The tears were consistent with his established anomalous physiology, crystallizing into small diamonds as they fell. However, the quantity of tears produced during the event was substantially greater than during an ordinary emotional response.

Jake did not appear to be in physical distress.

He was overwhelmed.

For the first time in his existence, Jake received a birthday celebration organized specifically to celebrate him.

He could not hold back his tears.

Nobody asked him to.

---

# 1. THE REVEAL

The cafeteria was prepared before Jake arrived.

The tables had been rearranged.

Decorations had been placed around the room.

Bao's birthday cake occupied the central serving table, surrounded by food and refreshments.

The completed painting had been positioned where it could be seen clearly from the entrance.

The original note from Clover had been removed.

The art supplies had been put away.

Only the finished canvas remained.

Residents gathered throughout the cafeteria, making a deliberate effort to appear as though they were engaged in ordinary activities.

The attempt was not entirely successful.

Alice was standing suspiciously close to the cake.

Neko had checked the painting's position three times.

Frost had already told Bao to stop asking whether everyone was ready.

Clover was standing beside the entrance.

Aeterna waited near the painting.

Then Jake arrived.

He entered the cafeteria carrying his teddy bear.

He had expected an ordinary visit.

Instead, several dozen people looked toward him.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then—

**EVERYONE:**

"Happy birthday, Jake!"

Jake stopped.

He looked at the residents.

Then at the decorations.

Then at Bao.

Then at Clover.

His expression suggested that he was trying to determine whether he had entered the correct room.

**JAKE:**

"...Huh?"

Clover smiled.

**CLOVER:**

"Happy birthday, Jake."

Jake looked around again.

**JAKE:**

"What's this?"

**BAO:**

"Your birthday party, sweetheart."

Jake blinked.

Once.

Twice.

**JAKE:**

"My... birthday?"

**NEKO:**

"November first. Year seventy-five. We verified the date."

Jake stared at her.

Then he looked at the cake.

Then at the people who had gathered for him.

His mouth opened slightly.

No words came out.

His eyes began to fill with tears.

---

# 2. THE FIRST TEARS

At first, Jake seemed confused by his own reaction.

He lifted one hand and touched beneath an eye.

His fingers came away wet.

A tiny blue-white diamond rested against his skin.

He looked at it.

Another tear fell.

Then another.

The small diamonds landed on the floor with delicate, almost inaudible clicks.

Jake tried to wipe his face.

It did not help.

He looked toward Clover.

**JAKE:**

"You... did this?"

Clover nodded.

**CLOVER:**

"All of us did."

Jake turned toward the other residents.

Some were smiling.

Some were watching him with quiet affection.

Bao had already started crying herself.

Frost stood beside her, trying very hard to pretend she was not emotional.

Alice looked genuinely concerned.

**ALICE:**

"Jake? Are you hurt?"

Jake shook his head.

He tried to speak.

**JAKE:**

"No, I—"

His voice broke.

He lowered his head.

The tears continued falling.

**JAKE:**

"I've never..."

He stopped.

Nobody interrupted him.

Jake held his teddy bear tighter against his chest.

**JAKE:**

"I've never had one before."

The cafeteria became completely silent.

Clover's smile faltered.

Bao covered her mouth.

Neko looked down at the floor.

Aeterna closed her eyes briefly.

Jake was still crying.

**JAKE:**

"Nobody ever..."

He could not finish the sentence.

He did not need to.

Bao crossed the room and knelt in front of him.

**BAO:**

"Oh, sweetheart."

She opened her arms.

Jake immediately leaned into her.

Bao held him while he cried.

The little diamonds accumulated on the floor around them, catching the cafeteria lights.

Nobody attempted to count them.

Nobody asked him to stop.

And nobody told him that he was too old, too young, too powerful, or too unusual to cry.

For once, the tears were allowed to mean exactly what they meant.

Jake was happy.

Jake was overwhelmed.

Jake had been given something he had never expected to receive.

And he could not hold it all inside.

---

# 3. THE PAINTING

After Jake had been given time to settle, Clover gently directed his attention toward the canvas.

**CLOVER:**

"There's something else."

Jake wiped his eyes.

He looked at her.

**JAKE:**

"More?"

Clover nodded.

She led him toward the painting.

The canvas was enormous.

Its surface had been transformed into a nighttime landscape beneath a sky filled with stars.

The scene depicted a quiet, beautiful place beneath an immense celestial canopy.

There were rolling hills, a still lake, and a distant horizon illuminated by the faintest traces of blue and silver.

The sky occupied most of the composition.

Stars covered it in extraordinary detail.

Some were bright points of white.

Others were soft blue lights.

Thin trails of light crossed the darkness like shooting stars.

The lake reflected the sky so clearly that it was difficult to distinguish where the stars ended and their reflections began.

The landscape was peaceful.

There were no battles.

No laboratories.

No containment chambers.

No alarms.

No doors separating anyone from anyone else.

It was simply a place where people could sit together and look at the stars.

Small figures had been painted throughout the landscape.

Some sat beside the lake.

Others lay in the grass.

A few sat together on a low hill.

Near the center of the scene, a small white-haired boy sat with a teddy bear beside him.

His face was turned toward the sky.

He was surrounded by tiny points of blue light.

Jake stared at the painting.

He did not move.

For several seconds, he seemed to forget that anyone else was standing beside him.

Then he stepped closer.

**JAKE:**

"...That's me."

**CLOVER:**

"Yep."

Jake looked at the little figure.

Then at the sky above it.

**JAKE:**

"That's really pretty."

Clover smiled.

**CLOVER:**

"We thought you'd like it."

Jake's eyes filled with tears again.

Clover quickly amended her statement.

**CLOVER:**

"Okay, maybe we made you cry twice."

Jake let out a tiny, shaky laugh.

It immediately dissolved into another sob.

**JAKE:**

"I'm sorry."

Bao answered from behind him.

**BAO:**

"You have absolutely nothing to apologize for."

---

# 4. THE SIGNATURES

The painting contained something Jake had not initially noticed.

Names.

They were everywhere.

Neko's signature appeared beside the mechanical star she had painted into the sky.

Miss Neko had signed beside her flowers.

Niko's name was written among the constellations.

Bao had signed near the cake resting on a small picnic blanket.

Frost's signature appeared beside a snowflake.

Alice had signed near the rabbit riding a scooter.

The rabbit was still carrying an axe.

Jane's signature was tucked beside the little rat and seal sitting together near the lake.

Aeterna had signed beneath the shooting-star trails.

Clover's name appeared beside the four-leaf clover in the foreground.

Other signatures were scattered throughout the landscape.

Some were written in neat handwriting.

Others were stylized.

A few were accompanied by little drawings.

There were names beside flowers, names beneath stars, names hidden among the grass, and names written so delicately that Jake had to lean closer to read them.

Every contributor had signed somewhere.

No one had been forgotten.

Jake slowly moved along the canvas.

He read one name.

Then another.

Then another.

He recognized them all.

**JAKE:**

"Everyone did something?"

**CLOVER:**

"Everyone who wanted to."

Jake looked at the signatures again.

**JAKE:**

"For me?"

**CLOVER:**

"For you."

He pressed his lips together.

His eyes filled again.

A tiny diamond dropped from his cheek and landed near the base of the canvas.

Then another.

**JAKE:**

"All of you..."

He could not finish.

Neko stepped forward.

**NEKO:**

"You don't have to say anything, Jake."

Jake looked at her.

**NEKO:**

"You can just enjoy it."

He nodded.

Then he carefully touched the edge of the canvas, avoiding the painted surface.

For several seconds, he simply stood there.

A six-year-old boy looking at a picture made by people who cared about him.

A picture full of stars.

A picture full of names.

A picture that proved, in a way words sometimes could not, that he belonged among the people who had made it.

---

# 5. THE REASON FOR THE LANDSCAPE

Jake eventually asked the question that Clover had been waiting for.

**JAKE:**

"Why did you draw stars?"

Clover glanced toward the painting.

**CLOVER:**

"Because you're a star."

Jake looked at her.

**CLOVER:**

"And because we wanted to make somewhere you could take us."

Jake's expression changed.

He looked at the painted lake.

The grass.

The distant hills.

The open sky.

**JAKE:**

"...Take you?"

Clover nodded.

**CLOVER:**

"Wouldn't it be nice if we could all go stargazing together?"

Jake looked back at the painting.

For a moment, he was very still.

Then he turned toward the canvas and raised one hand.

The air shimmered.

A thin line of azure light traced the edge of the painted landscape.

The painted stars brightened.

The lake's reflections began to move.

The distant grass stirred in a breeze that had not existed a moment before.

Neko immediately looked up.

**NEKO:**

"Wait. Jake—"

The canvas expanded.

Not physically.

Its painted world unfolded outward, the landscape extending beyond the limits of the linen without tearing or distorting the original artwork.

A gentle blue light spread across the cafeteria.

The residents watched as the painted horizon opened into a real, accessible space.

The sky beyond the canvas was filled with stars.

The lake reflected them.

The grass moved softly in the night breeze.

The painted setting had become a place they could enter.

Jake looked back at Clover.

His eyes were still wet.

**JAKE:**

"We can go?"

Clover stared at the newly opened landscape.

Then at Jake.

**CLOVER:**

"...Apparently."

Neko was already examining the boundary between the painting and the cafeteria.

**NEKO:**

"I am going to need a very long report."

**ALICE:**

"Can we go now?"

**NEKO:**

"That is not the point."

**ALICE:**

"Can we go now?"

Neko looked at the open landscape.

She sighed.

**NEKO:**

"Stay together. Do not wander beyond the visible landscape. And nobody touches anything that looks anomalous without permission."

Alice immediately stepped through the opening.

**ALICE:**

"RABBIT ADVENTURE!"

**BAO:**

"Alice!"

Alice stopped.

**ALICE:**

"...Careful rabbit adventure."

The residents began entering the painted world.

Jake stood at the threshold, watching them go.

Clover gently took his hand.

**CLOVER:**

"Come on, birthday boy."

Jake smiled through his tears.

Then he stepped into the painting.

---

# 6. STARGAZING

The landscape was even more beautiful from within.

The grass was soft beneath their feet.

The lake reflected the sky with impossible clarity.

The stars seemed brighter than those visible from the Blacksite's ordinary observation areas.

Some formed familiar constellations.

Others formed patterns no resident could identify.

Thin blue trails moved slowly across the heavens.

The entire landscape appeared to exist in a quiet, perpetual night.

There was no artificial lighting.

No ceiling.

No distant machinery.

Only the soft sounds of the breeze, the water, and the residents settling into the grass.

Jake sat on the hillside near the lake.

His teddy bear rested beside him.

Bao sat on one side.

Clover sat on the other.

Frost settled nearby.

Alice lay on her back and immediately began pointing at stars.

Neko remained standing for several minutes, examining the environment before finally sitting down.

Aeterna stood a short distance away, looking toward the horizon.

Jane sat beside the little painted rat and seal, which had somehow become part of the landscape.

The others gradually gathered around them.

Nobody had arranged the seating.

Nobody needed to.

They had all come to look at the stars.

Together.

Jake tilted his head back.

Above him, the sky stretched into an endless field of light.

He recognized some of the patterns.

Others were unfamiliar.

He watched a blue streak move across the heavens.

Then another.

He had seen stars for longer than most of the people around him had been alive.

He had existed among them.

He had been one of them.

But he had never experienced this particular sight.

Not like this.

Not surrounded by people who had come simply because they wanted to be with him.

He looked toward Bao.

Then Clover.

Then the others.

**JAKE:**

"Thank you."

His voice was very quiet.

Clover looked at him.

**CLOVER:**

"You're welcome."

Jake lowered his gaze.

**JAKE:**

"All of you."

Bao reached over and gently rested a hand on his shoulder.

**BAO:**

"Happy birthday, sweetheart."

Jake's eyes filled again.

He laughed softly at himself.

**JAKE:**

"I'm crying again."

**CLOVER:**

"We noticed."

**JAKE:**

"I can't stop."

Bao drew him closer.

**BAO:**

"Then don't."

Jake leaned against her.

The little diamonds fell into the grass, glittering among the dew.

No one tried to collect them.

No one asked how many he had produced.

For once, they were not an anomalous material requiring an inventory.

They were simply tears from a little boy who had been given a beautiful birthday.

And the stars above them continued to shine.

---

# 7. THE MOMENT THAT WAS NOT PLANNED

Clover had organized the canvas.

She had arranged the surprise.

She had coordinated the residents.

She had made certain that Jake would not discover the project beforehand.

But she had not planned for the painting to become a place they could all visit.

That had been Jake's doing.

The discovery prompted a brief discussion between Clover and Neko.

**NEKO:**

"Did you know he could do that?"

**CLOVER:**

"No."

**NEKO:**

"Did you intend for the painting to become physically accessible?"

**CLOVER:**

"No."

**NEKO:**

"Do you know why it happened?"

Clover looked toward Jake.

He was sitting in the grass with Bao, watching the stars.

**CLOVER:**

"No."

Neko followed her gaze.

After a long pause, he closed his notebook.

**NEKO:**

"We will investigate it later."

**CLOVER:**

"Thank you."

**NEKO:**

"You're welcome."

Neko returned to the group.

The investigation could wait.

The birthday could not.

---

# 8. THE CAKE

Eventually, Bao reminded everyone that there was still a birthday cake waiting in the cafeteria.

Jake looked up immediately.

**JAKE:**

"Cake?"

Bao laughed.

**BAO:**

"Yes, sweetheart. Cake."

Jake looked toward the painting's horizon.

Then toward the others.

**JAKE:**

"Can we bring it here?"

Neko slowly turned toward him.

**NEKO:**

"You want to transport the cake through an anomalous painting?"

Jake nodded.

Neko looked at the sky.

Then at the lake.

Then at the painting's boundary, which remained visible in the distance.

**NEKO:**

"...I will permit one cake."

Alice raised her hand.

**ALICE:**

"Can we bring the entire cafeteria?"

**NEKO:**

"No."

**ALICE:**

"Half the cafeteria?"

**NEKO:**

"No."

**ALICE:**

"The cake, then."

**NEKO:**

"That is what I just said."

The cake was brought through.

It was decorated with blue icing, white stars, and small silver details.

There were no candles representing Jake's chronological age.

Neko had specifically vetoed that idea after someone suggested using one candle for every year.

The resulting fire hazard would have been absurd.

Instead, there were six small candles representing Jake's current human age.

Jake stared at them.

**JAKE:**

"Six."

**BAO:**

"Six."

Jake looked toward the sky.

Then at the cake.

Then at his friends.

**JAKE:**

"That's a lot."

Clover laughed.

**CLOVER:**

"You have no idea."

Jake did not understand the joke.

He was already looking at the frosting.

---

# 9. THE WISH

The residents gathered around the cake.

Jake stood before it, holding his teddy bear in one arm.

The six candles burned gently.

Everyone began singing.

The voices were imperfect.

Some residents were off-key.

Alice sang much louder than necessary.

Neko appeared to be reconsidering every decision that had led to this moment.

Bao sang with a smile.

Clover sang while wiping her eyes.

Aeterna's voice was quiet but clear.

Jake listened.

He looked around the circle.

At every face.

Every person who had contributed to the painting.

Every person who had come to celebrate him.

When the song ended, Bao leaned down.

**BAO:**

"Make a wish, sweetheart."

Jake looked at the candles.

He thought for a long time.

Then he closed his eyes.

He made his wish.

And blew.

All six candles went out.

The residents cheered.

Jake laughed.

A small diamond fell from his cheek.

Then he looked at the cake.

**JAKE:**

"Can I have the first piece?"

Bao immediately began cutting it.

**BAO:**

"Of course."

The wish remained private.

No one asked him to reveal it.

# 10. C.O.R.E. POST-EVENT ASSESSMENT

The birthday celebration was completed successfully.

The collaborative painting remains intact and continues to provide access to its depicted landscape.

No injuries were reported during the stargazing event.

The environment appears stable, although its origin and operating principles remain unknown.

The painting's ability to become an accessible location has been recorded as a new anomalous property associated with Jake's artwork.

A formal investigation will be scheduled separately.

Jake's emotional response was substantial but consistent with the circumstances.

He displayed no signs of physical distress requiring medical intervention.

His tears crystallized into diamonds, as expected from his established physiology.

No attempt was made to quantify or collect them during the celebration.

The event's primary objective was achieved.

Jake was able to experience his first birthday celebration surrounded by people who cared about him.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The painting began as a blank canvas.

Clover hung it in the cafeteria with a note and a collection of art supplies.

One by one, residents came and left something behind.

A flower.

A star.

A rabbit.

A cake.

A constellation.

A little rat sitting beside a seal.

They signed their names throughout the painting.

They filled the empty spaces.

They made a place where everyone could belong.

Then Jake saw it.

He cried.

He thanked them.

And when he discovered that the painted world could be entered, he invited everyone inside.

The residents went stargazing together.

They sat beside the lake.

They watched shooting stars cross the sky.

They ate birthday cake beneath a landscape made from their own hands.

And, for a while, nobody needed to think about Jake's anomalous nature, his chronological age, the material he shed, or the things he had survived.

He was simply a little boy celebrating his birthday with his friends.

A little boy who had never had a birthday party before.

A little boy who could not stop crying because so many people had chosen to celebrate him.

A little boy who looked up at the stars and found himself surrounded by people who wanted to stay.

Perhaps the most important part of the painting was not the sky.

Not the lake.

Not even the thousands of tiny stars.

It was the signatures.

Every name was a small promise that someone had been there.

Someone had taken the time.

Someone had wanted to leave something for him.

And when Jake looked up from the grass, he did not have to look at the stars alone.

He had never needed to earn this.

He had never needed to be useful.

He had never needed to become anything other than himself.

For his first birthday, the SAFEHOUSE gave him a painting.

Jake turned it into a world.

And then he invited everyone inside.
