# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has begun experiencing occasional episodes of fainting associated with exhaustion.

The episodes are not entirely unexpected given his current physical condition. However, their frequency and the circumstances surrounding them warrant continued observation.

Although Jake is a literal star, his present human manifestation is **considerably weaker and more fragile than his cosmic nature might suggest.**

His restored wings, celestial abilities, and anomalous physiology have not eliminated this vulnerability.

He can still fly.

He can still manipulate stellar energy.

He can still manifest celestial weapons.

He can still turn ordinary sand into remarkably clear glass without applying heat.

And he can still become so exhausted that he loses consciousness while walking down a hallway.

C.O.R.E. considers this contradiction medically significant.

More importantly, Jake's teddy bear introduces an additional complication.

The bear is not an ordinary comfort object.

It is Jake's sensory extension.

**Anything the bear physically experiences, Jake experiences as well.**

Jake can attenuate this connection, but the ability does not make the bear irrelevant to his welfare.

When Jake is weak, tired, or otherwise physically compromised, the bear must be handled with particular care.

---

# 1. CURRENT PHYSICAL CONDITION

Jake's present condition is best described as **physically frail despite substantial anomalous capabilities.**

His human manifestation has limited endurance.

Ordinary activities may be manageable individually but become exhausting when performed consecutively.

Current observations indicate that Jake may experience:

- rapidly developing fatigue;
    
- reduced stamina after prolonged activity;
    
- unsteadiness when tired;
    
- difficulty remaining upright after exertion;
    
- an increased need to sit or lie down;
    
- occasional fainting associated with exhaustion.
    

These symptoms do not occur at every instance of physical activity.

Jake may appear perfectly comfortable one moment and become visibly exhausted shortly afterward.

This inconsistency makes it difficult for residents to judge his remaining stamina by appearance alone.

His enthusiasm is an especially poor indicator.

Jake may insist that he is fine because he wants to continue reading, playing, stargazing, or spending time with his friends.

He may also fail to recognize how exhausted he has become until his body forces him to stop.

### Medical clarification

Jake's cosmic identity does not guarantee that his human manifestation can tolerate unlimited physical or emotional stress.

His abilities should not be used as evidence that he is physically healthy.

Likewise, his ability to recover from previous anomalous incidents should not encourage personnel to dismiss new symptoms.

---

# 2. EXHAUSTION-RELATED FAINTING

The current working classification for Jake's episodes is **exhaustion-associated syncope**.

This remains a provisional description of the observed events, not a complete explanation of their underlying mechanism.

A typical episode may develop as follows:

1. Jake continues an activity despite accumulating fatigue.
    
2. His movements become slower, and he may become unusually quiet.
    
3. He begins to struggle to remain standing.
    
4. He loses consciousness if the episode progresses.
    
5. He requires assistance and medical assessment before being considered recovered.
    

Not every episode follows this exact sequence.

Some may occur with little warning.

Consequently, residents should not assume that Jake will always have enough time to announce that he feels unwell.

Nor should they assume that his silence means he is merely sleepy.

Jake is naturally quiet when comfortable. His ordinary behavior can therefore conceal early deterioration.

### Response protocol

If Jake begins to lose consciousness, personnel should prevent a fall where safely possible and help him lie down.

They should assess his breathing and responsiveness, summon medical assistance, and follow established emergency procedures. If he remains unresponsive, has abnormal breathing, sustains an injury, or does not recover promptly, emergency intervention is required.

Food, cake, or sugary drinks must not be given to an unconscious person.

Even if Jake appears to recover quickly, a fainting episode warrants medical assessment rather than an automatic return to normal activities.

**A fainting episode is not simply Jake taking an involuntary nap.**

---

# 3. THE TEDDY BEAR COMPLICATION

Jake's teddy bear remains his cardiac vessel and sensory extension.

The relationship between Jake and the bear is not merely emotional, although its emotional importance is considerable.

The bear's physical condition has direct implications for Jake.

If the bear is struck, Jake can experience the impact.

If the bear is handled roughly, Jake can experience the resulting sensation.

If the bear becomes trapped beneath a heavy object, the pressure may affect Jake as well.

Jake has learned to attenuate the sensory connection, reducing the intensity of incoming sensations.

However, this ability should not be treated as a substitute for careful handling.

C.O.R.E. has identified three principal concerns.

### A. Physical vulnerability

Jake's human manifestation is already fragile.

An unexpected impact on the bear may produce an additional source of discomfort or stress at precisely the moment when Jake has limited capacity to tolerate it.

### B. Exhaustion

When Jake is tired, personnel cannot assume that he will always be able to manage the sensory connection as effectively as he might when well-rested.

The extent to which exhaustion affects his attenuation ability remains unconfirmed.

### C. Emotional distress

The bear is also Jake's most familiar source of comfort.

Separating him from it unnecessarily may create additional distress, while mishandling it risks both physical and emotional consequences.

The correct response is therefore straightforward:

**Keep the bear safe, keep Jake comfortable, and do not separate them without a compelling reason.**

# 4. INCIDENT LOG — CORRIDOR COLLAPSE

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, RESIDENTIAL CORRIDOR  
**TIME:** 16:18  
**SEVERITY:** MEDICAL EVENT

Jake had been walking back toward his room after spending time in the common area.

He was carrying his teddy bear.

A resident passing in the opposite direction noticed that Jake had slowed considerably.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "Jake? You okay?"

Jake nodded.

He continued walking.

Several steps later, he stopped.

He looked down at the floor as though trying to orient himself.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Tired."

The resident moved closer.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "Let's sit down for a minute."

Jake attempted to take another step.

His knees buckled.

The resident caught him before his head struck the floor.

Jake was unresponsive for several seconds.

His teddy bear remained secured against his chest.

Medical personnel were summoned, and Jake was assessed after regaining consciousness.

When he became responsive, his first reaction was to look for the bear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Bear?"

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "Right here. You're both okay."

Jake pulled the bear closer.

He remained quiet for the remainder of the assessment.

When asked whether he had felt exhausted before collapsing, he nodded.

When asked why he had continued walking, he gave a small shrug.

No further explanation was offered.

### Assessment

The incident was consistent with Jake's existing pattern of exhaustion-associated fainting.

The absence of a serious fall injury was fortunate.

Personnel were reminded that catching Jake is preferable to allowing an uncontrolled fall, but that his fragile body still requires careful support during and after an episode.

# 5. INCIDENT LOG — THE BEAR FALLS

**LOCATION:** JAKE'S RESIDENCE  
**TIME:** 19:06  
**SEVERITY:** MINOR INCIDENT WITH POTENTIAL MEDICAL CONSEQUENCES

Jake was sitting on his bed with his teddy bear beside him.

He was already visibly tired.

While reaching for a book, he accidentally nudged the bear off the edge of the mattress.

The bear landed on the floor.

The impact was not substantial.

Jake nevertheless flinched.

He immediately leaned forward and retrieved it.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Ow."

Clover looked up from her desk.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Did you hurt yourself?"

Jake shook his head.

Then he held up the bear.

Clover understood.

She moved closer and checked the bear for visible damage without squeezing or manipulating its limbs unnecessarily.

There was none.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'm sorry, sweetheart."

Jake shook his head again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Accident."

He hugged the bear against his chest.

Clover watched him for a moment.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Are you feeling tired?"

Jake nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Then let's get you comfortable."

Jake did not object.

He lay down, keeping the bear beside him.

Clover placed the book within reach and dimmed the lights.

No further incident occurred.

### Assessment

The event demonstrated that even a minor impact involving the bear may produce a noticeable reaction from Jake.

It does not establish that every impact will cause serious harm.

It does establish that the bear should not be treated as an ordinary stuffed toy.

# 6. POST-BIRTHDAY OBSERVATION

The recent birthday celebration provides a useful example of Jake's tendency to continue activities despite accumulating exhaustion.

Throughout the celebration, Jake was emotionally engaged.

He wanted to remain with his friends.

He wanted to look at the stars.

He wanted to experience as much of his first birthday as possible.

His eventual exhaustion was unsurprising.

However, the fact that Jake was happy did not mean he was physically capable of continuing indefinitely.

His current condition creates a difficult distinction:

**Jake may desperately want to participate in an activity that his body can no longer comfortably sustain.**

Residents should not interpret this as stubbornness or deliberate noncompliance.

For Jake, stopping may mean missing something he considers important.

He may therefore need gentle assistance recognizing when an activity has become too demanding.

The objective is not to prevent him from having fun.

It is to make sure he remains well enough to enjoy it.

# 7. UPDATED RESIDENT CARE GUIDELINES

The following measures are now recommended for Jake's daily routine.

## Physical activity

- Encourage regular rest periods during prolonged activities.
    
- Provide seating and easy access to his residence.
    
- Avoid asking him to push through obvious exhaustion.
    
- Allow him to stop participating without having to justify the decision.
    
- Do not use his flight capability as a reason to expect greater physical endurance.
    

## Transportation

When Jake becomes too tired to walk safely, a resident may assist him back to his room.

His body should be supported carefully, with particular attention to his head, limbs, and wings.

His teddy bear should be carried securely rather than allowed to swing, fall, or become trapped during transportation.

## Teddy bear handling

- Ask Jake before moving the bear whenever circumstances permit.
    
- Avoid dropping, squeezing, throwing, or unnecessarily manipulating it.
    
- Keep it within reach when Jake is resting.
    
- Inspect it gently if damage is suspected.
    
- Do not use the bear to test Jake's sensory response.
    

## Medical monitoring

- Record fainting episodes and their circumstances.
    
- Note any preceding fatigue, unsteadiness, or other symptoms.
    
- Assess Jake after each episode rather than assuming he has recovered because he wakes up.
    
- Escalate unusual, prolonged, or worsening episodes for medical evaluation.
    

## Emotional welfare

Do not tell Jake that he is too fragile to participate in ordinary activities.

Do not imply that his weakness makes him less capable of being a child.

Do not make him feel guilty for requiring assistance.

And do not assume that accepting help is something he must compensate for afterward.

# 9. C.O.R.E. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's current weakness represents a meaningful limitation on his day-to-day life.

His anomalous abilities remain present, but they do not eliminate the risk of exhaustion or fainting.

His teddy bear introduces a further vulnerability that must be considered during routine care.

At present, the appropriate response is not to restrict Jake to his room or prohibit physical activity.

It is to adapt his surroundings and routine to his actual physical condition.

Jake should be able to read, draw, play, visit friends, watch the stars, and participate in SAFEHOUSE life.

He should also be able to stop whenever he becomes tired.

He should not have to collapse before someone believes him.

He should not have to prove that he is unwell before receiving assistance.

And he should never be expected to demonstrate how much discomfort he can tolerate simply because he possesses extraordinary abilities.

**His power is not a measure of his endurance.**

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

At the time this report was completed, Jake was resting in his room.

His teddy bear was beside him.

Clover was reading quietly at her desk.

The lights were dim.

No one was asking Jake to do anything.

He had spent the day attempting to keep up with the world around him.

Now the world could wait.

Jake was still a star.

He was still capable of extraordinary things.

He was still the same child who had cried over a birthday painting because so many people had taken the time to make something for him.

But he was also a little boy whose knees sometimes gave out when he became too tired.

A little boy who could faint in a hallway.

A little boy who felt the impact when his teddy bear fell.

A little boy who needed other people to notice when he was no longer strong enough to keep going.

None of those things made him less extraordinary.

They simply meant that, for now, he needed to be treated with a little more care.

Not because he was a star.

Not because he was anomalous.

Not because he was capable of wielding celestial power.

Because he was Jake.

And Jake deserved to be looked after, too.
