### **ANOMALOUS PHYSIOLOGY / VISUAL ANOMALY REPORT**

# **CORIN — FOLDED WING CONCEALMENT**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Phenomenon:** **Passive Wing Concealment**  
**Status:** Confirmed

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin's wings possess an additional anomalous property when fully folded.

Rather than remaining visibly extended from her back, the wings can flatten against her body into a compact **panel-like configuration**.

At the same time, the wings become visually invisible.

The result is an almost completely normal-looking back.

---

# **FOLDED CONFIGURATION**

When Corin retracts her wings, they do not simply disappear.

They physically fold into a highly compact arrangement against her back.

The folded wings resemble a thin panel or layered structure positioned along her spine.

Despite their enormous size when extended, the wings occupy surprisingly little visible space when fully folded.

---

# **INVISIBILITY**

Once the wings reach their fully folded position, their visibility rapidly decreases.

Within moments, they become effectively invisible to normal human vision.

The underlying structure remains physically present.

Corin can still feel the wings.

She can still move them.

They can still interact with the environment.

They are simply no longer visually detectable.

---

# **CLOTHING INTERACTION**

This ability works particularly well with Corin's previously observed wing-phasing capability.

When fully folded:

- The wings rest against Corin's back.
    
- The wings become visually invisible.
    
- Clothing can pass over them normally.
    
- No visible protrusions are apparent.
    
- Corin can wear ordinary clothing without revealing that she possesses wings.
    

From a distance, Corin can therefore appear completely wingless.

---

# **DEPLOYMENT**

The concealment can be reversed immediately.

When Corin wishes to deploy her wings, the folded structure unfolds from her back.

The wings regain full visibility as they extend.

The transition is rapid enough that Corin can go from appearing completely ordinary to fully winged within moments.

---

# **FIELD APPLICATION**

The ability has obvious practical advantages.

Corin can enter populated areas without immediately drawing attention to her wings.

She can wear ordinary clothing.

She can conceal her anomalous anatomy during routine visits.

She can then deploy her wings when flight is required.

This has made her considerably less conspicuous during trips to the Blacksite.

---

# **SAFEHOUSE OBSERVATION**

Belle initially thought Corin had simply stopped having wings.

She stared at Corin's back.

> **Belle:**  
> "Where did your wings go?"

Corin shrugged.

> **"They're here."**

Belle stared harder.

> **"...Where?"**

Corin unfolded one wing.

Belle jumped.

> **"Oh!"**

Corin laughed.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie is now accustomed to the behavior.

She can usually tell when Corin is carrying concealed wings because Corin's posture changes slightly when the folded structures are resting against her back.

To everyone else, however, there is almost nothing to see.

---

# **TACTICAL LIMITATIONS**

The concealment is primarily visual.

It should not be interpreted as complete removal of the wings.

The wings remain physically present.

Advanced anomalous detection systems may still detect them.

Ether-sensitive entities may also be able to perceive their presence.

Therefore:

> **Invisible does not mean nonexistent.**

---

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin's wings are surprisingly considerate when they are not being used.

When folded, they compress against her back like a thin panel.

Then they simply stop being visible.

No complicated equipment.

No illusion device.

No Architect intervention.

The wings themselves appear to decide that being noticeable is unnecessary.

Corin can therefore walk around wearing an ordinary outfit with seemingly nothing unusual on her back.

Then, whenever she wants to fly:

The invisible panel unfolds.

The feathers return to view.

And Corin has her wings again.
