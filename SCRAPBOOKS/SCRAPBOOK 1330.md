---

# 1. SUMMARY

Following the restoration of Jake's wings, **Corin** volunteered to teach him how to fly.

Corin possesses functional wings and therefore had considerably more relevant experience than most SAFEHOUSE residents.

The initial expectation was that Jake would require repeated instruction.

He did not.

Jake learned extremely quickly.

---

# 2. INITIAL ATTEMPT

The first training session took place in a controlled SAFEHOUSE area with sufficient vertical clearance.

Corin began with the fundamentals:

- opening and folding the wings;
    
- maintaining balance;
    
- generating lift;
    
- controlling direction;
    
- slowing down;
    
- landing safely.
    

Jake listened carefully.

He did not interrupt.

He watched Corin demonstrate.

Then he tried.

His first attempt lasted approximately three seconds.

Jake lifted approximately one meter from the ground.

He immediately tilted sideways.

Corin caught him.

Jake blinked.

> **Jake:** “Again.”

Corin smiled.

> **Corin:** “Again.”

---

# 3. SECOND ATTEMPT

The second attempt lasted considerably longer.

Jake extended both wings.

A brief flutter.

A small adjustment.

Then—

He stayed airborne.

Corin stepped backward.

Jake remained in place.

His wings moved with increasingly controlled motions.

He looked down.

Then up.

Then back toward Corin.

> **Jake:** “I'm flying.”

> **Corin:** “You are.”

Jake looked genuinely surprised.

> **Jake:** “Oh.”

He continued.

---

# 4. RAPID ADAPTATION

Within a short period, Jake began correcting his own balance.

He adjusted his wing position instinctively.

He learned how to turn.

He learned how to slow himself.

He learned how to descend.

Most notably, Jake did not appear to require extensive conscious calculation.

Once he understood the relationship between wing movement and movement through the air, he adapted rapidly.

Corin eventually stopped giving instructions.

She simply watched.

Jake continued practicing.

---

# 5. CORIN'S OBSERVATION

After approximately twenty minutes, Corin was asked how Jake was progressing.

Her response:

> **Corin:** “He's already got it.”

The observing technician asked:

> **“Already?”**

Corin nodded.

> **Corin:** “He learns fast.”

Jake flew past them.

He made a wide turn.

Then another.

He descended.

Touched the ground.

Folded his wings.

And walked back over.

> **Jake:** “Again?”

Corin laughed.

> **Corin:** “Sure.”

---

# 6. FIRST UNSUPERVISED FLIGHT

The first unsupervised flight was not planned.

Corin had briefly stepped away.

Jake looked toward the open flight area.

Then looked at his wings.

Then flew.

No assistance.

No hesitation.

He rose slowly, crossed the room, turned around, and landed.

Corin returned several seconds later.

> **Corin:** “Did you just fly without me?”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:** “Yes.”

> **Corin:** “You weren't supposed to do that yet.”

Jake looked down.

Pause.

> **Jake:** “Sorry.”

Corin sighed.

> **Corin:** “...Just tell me before you do it next time.”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:** “Okay.”

---

# 7. JAKE'S FLYING STYLE

Jake's flight is noticeably different from Corin's.

Corin flies with confidence and deliberate control.

Jake is still learning.

However, he appears naturally comfortable in the air.

His movements are small.

He rarely makes dramatic wing movements.

He tends to glide once he has gained enough height.

He also appears to enjoy simply hovering.

This has resulted in several observations of Jake floating approximately half a meter above the floor while holding his teddy bear.

When asked why:

> **Jake:** “It's easier.”

---

# 8. THE TEDDY BEAR PROBLEM

Jake refuses to leave his teddy bear behind while flying.

This was initially considered a potential complication.

Jake disagreed.

He simply holds the teddy bear against his chest.

The teddy bear's heartbeat remains normal.

No adverse effect has been observed.

Corin eventually stopped attempting to convince him otherwise.

---

# 9. NORa's RESPONSE

Nora witnessed Jake's first successful extended flight.

Her response was immediate.

> **Nora:** “JAKE CAN FLY!”

Jake descended.

Nora stared at his wings.

Then at him.

> **Nora:** “Do it again!”

Jake did.

Nora cheered.

Jake flew another circle.

Nora followed him across the room.

> **Nora:** “Faster!”

Jake increased his speed.

> **Nora:** “HIGHER!”

Jake climbed.

Corin immediately intervened.

> **Corin:** “Not that high.”

Jake stopped.

Nora looked disappointed.

> **Nora:** “Aww.”

---

# 10. AETERNA'S RESPONSE

Aeterna observed Jake from below.

She did not interfere.

When Jake eventually landed, she looked at his wings.

Then at him.

> **Aeterna:** “You learned quickly.”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:** “Corin taught me.”

Aeterna looked toward Corin.

> **Aeterna:** “Then she taught well.”

Corin smiled.

Jake quietly moved closer to Aeterna.

His wings folded against his back.

---

# 11. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jake's rapid acquisition of flight capability is consistent with his anomalous nature.

His body is simultaneously:

- a human manifestation;
    
- an angelic form;
    
- and the manifested body of a literal star.
    

The exact biological or anomalous mechanism behind flight remains unresolved.

Further experimentation is **not currently recommended.**

Jake already knows how to fly.

There is no operational reason to turn a simple childhood milestone into a laboratory project.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Corin taught Jake how to fly.

She expected to spend a while teaching him.

Jake apparently decided that twenty minutes was sufficient.

First he learned how to stay in the air.

Then how to turn.

Then how to land.

Then he started flying around with his teddy bear.

The strangest part wasn't how quickly he learned.

It was how natural it looked.

For a child who spent so long trapped on the ground—

Jake took to the sky very quickly.

And when he finally landed, he looked at Corin and asked:

> **“Again?”**

Corin said yes.
