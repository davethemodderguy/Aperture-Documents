**SUBJECT:** Jake — Voluntary Distribution of Tear-Derived Diamonds

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has accumulated approximately **5 kilograms of diamonds** produced naturally from his tears.

He subsequently gave them away.

No coercion was involved.

No exchange was requested.

No compensation was expected.

When asked why he did not keep the diamonds, Jake provided a remarkably simple answer:

> **JAKE:**  
> "What's the benefit if I keep them?"

The statement appears to accurately represent Jake's attitude toward his tear-derived diamonds.

He does not consider them wealth.

He does not consider them valuable possessions.

He considers them something he can produce naturally and therefore something other people might as well have if they want them.

---

# 1. DIAMOND PRODUCTION

Jake's tears naturally crystallize into diamonds upon separation from his body.

The resulting diamonds vary considerably.

Observed forms include:

- conventional crystalline formations;
    
- unusually clear stones;
    
- irregular geometric crystals;
    
- star-like formations;
    
- small polished-looking stones;
    
- and several naturally occurring decorative shapes.
    

The diamonds do not require conventional cutting or processing to become visually distinctive.

Their formation is anomalous and remains unrelated to conventional geological diamond formation.

---

# 2. ACCUMULATION

Jake did not initially intend to accumulate 5 kilograms.

The material simply accumulated over time.

Several containers within his room eventually contained tear-derived diamonds.

At some point, C.O.R.E. personnel became aware of the quantity.

The initial response was concern that Jake might be treating the diamonds as a personal reserve of wealth.

This assumption proved incorrect.

---

# 3. JAKE'S RESPONSE

When asked whether he wanted the diamonds stored securely, Jake appeared confused.

> **C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
> "You have quite a lot of them."

Jake looked at the containers.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

> **C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
> "Do you want to keep them?"

Jake thought for several seconds.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Why?"

> **C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
> "They're valuable."

Jake looked at the diamonds again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I know."

Pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "What's the benefit if I keep them?"

No further argument was presented.

---

# 4. DISTRIBUTION

Jake subsequently began giving the diamonds away.

Recipients were selected personally.

He did not establish a formal system.

He did not assign monetary values.

He did not negotiate.

If someone expressed interest in a particular diamond, Jake simply gave it to them.

In several cases, he specifically selected unusual shapes that he believed suited the recipient.

---

# 5. CLOVER

Clover received several of the more unusual stones.

She initially refused.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Jake, these are yours."

> **JAKE:**  
> "I have more."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "That's not really the point."

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Do you like it?"

Clover looked at the diamond.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Yes."

Jake held it out.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then keep it."

Clover eventually accepted.

She later incorporated one of the stones into a small display beside several of her four-leaf clovers.

---

# 6. BAO

Bao was given a clear diamond approximately the size of a small coin.

She immediately attempted to return it.

> **BAO:**  
> "Jake, you don't have to give me this."

> **JAKE:**  
> "I know."

> **BAO:**  
> "Then why?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Because you're Mom."

Bao stopped.

She kept the diamond.

---

# 7. NERI

Neri received a small irregular diamond.

She examined it under the light.

The crystal produced a faint blue reflection.

> **NERI:**  
> "Why me?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You like blue."

Neri looked at the diamond again.

She quietly put it in her pocket.

No further explanation was requested.

---

# 8. NORA

Nora received a star-shaped diamond.

Her reaction was immediate.

> **NORA:**  
> "IT'S A STAR!"

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

Nora hugged him.

Jake nearly fell over.

The diamond was subsequently placed in Nora's family journal.

A note was written beneath it:

> **"Jake's star."**

---

# 9. AETERNA

Aeterna was offered a diamond.

She initially declined.

Jake held it out again.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "You should keep it."

> **JAKE:**  
> "I have enough."

> **AETERNA:**  
> "It came from you."

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "That's why I gave it to you."

Aeterna accepted the diamond.

She later placed it within a small recess in her armor.

C.O.R.E. has not determined whether this was sentimental or practical.

No one has asked.

---

# 10. NIKO'S QUESTION

Niko eventually asked Jake whether he understood the potential value of what he was giving away.

Jake nodded.

> **NIKO:**  
> "You understand that these could be worth an enormous amount?"

> **JAKE:**  
> "Probably."

> **NIKO:**  
> "And you're just giving them away?"

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

> **NIKO:**  
> "Why?"

Jake looked at the remaining diamonds.

Then at Niko.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I can't eat them."

Niko paused.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I can't sleep on them."

Another pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I don't need five kilograms."

Niko considered this.

> **NIKO:**  
> "...Fair."

---

# 11. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

The diamonds should **not** be treated as an exploitable resource.

Jake's ability to produce them does not constitute authorization to request, encourage, or incentivize further production.

In particular, personnel are prohibited from:

- asking Jake to cry for diamonds;
    
- attempting to make Jake cry;
    
- negotiating for diamonds;
    
- offering compensation in exchange for tears;
    
- treating Jake as a source of material production;
    
- storing excessive quantities without Jake's consent;
    
- or describing the diamonds as company property.
    

Jake's tears are a biological/anomalous phenomenon.

They are not an industrial process.

---

# 12. RESIDENT WELFARE CONSIDERATION

The fact that Jake gives the diamonds away so casually should not be interpreted as evidence that he does not value them.

His reasoning appears to be different.

Jake has little attachment to conventional monetary value.

He values things because they have meaning.

His teddy bear matters.

His drawings matter.

His flowers matter.

His friends matter.

A large pile of identical diamonds does not appear to carry the same emotional significance.

Consequently:

> **Jake would rather someone he cares about enjoy one than have five kilograms sitting in a box.**

---

# 13. FINAL OBSERVATION

After distributing the majority of the diamonds, Jake returned to his room.

Clover asked him:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You really don't want to keep any?"

Jake looked around his room.

There were already several diamonds remaining.

He picked up one.

A small, imperfect crystal.

He placed it beside one of his blue flowers.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I kept this one."

Clover smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Why that one?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It's pretty."

That appears to be the actual distinction.

Jake does not dislike diamonds.

He simply does not need five kilograms of them.

He kept the ones that meant something to him.

He gave the rest to people who might appreciate them more.

No profit.

No transaction.

No ulterior motive.

Just a six-year-old star looking at a mountain of crystallized tears and deciding:

> **"Someone else might as well have them."**
