# SAFEHOUSE MEDICAL INCIDENT REPORT

## INCIDENT: FOUR HOURS UNNOTICED

**Classification:** CRITICAL MEDICAL EVENT  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Cafeteria  
**Incident Type:** Severe Anemic Shock / Cardiac Arrest  
**Discovery Delay:** Approximately 4 hours  
**Emergency Treatment:** Defibrillation + Compatible Blood Transfusion  
**Outcome:** Survived / Stabilized

---

# 1. Summary

Neri was sitting in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria reading a book.

As usual.

There was nothing unusual about her appearance.

She was seated normally.

Her chemistry book was open.

Her glasses were on.

Her eyes were open.

From the perspective of anyone passing through the cafeteria, Neri appeared to simply be doing what Neri did almost every day:

**reading.**

She was not reading.

She was unconscious.

She had suffered an anemic shock.

Nobody knew.

For approximately four hours, Neri remained at the cafeteria table while people passed around her.

Nobody deliberately ignored her.

Nobody knew she was in medical distress.

Her outward appearance gave them no reason to intervene.

---

# 2. Jane's Encounter

Approximately five minutes after Neri's anemic shock began, Jane Doe entered the cafeteria.

Jane was only there to get a quick bite.

She noticed Neri sitting at her usual table.

Jane began walking toward her.

There was nothing about Neri's appearance that immediately indicated an emergency.

She appeared to be reading.

Then Jane's communicator rang.

Jane received a call requiring her immediate attention.

She stopped.

The conversation took priority.

Jane eventually left the cafeteria.

Neri remained where she was.

Jane had no reason to believe she had just walked past a medical emergency.

---

# 3. Two Hours

Approximately two hours after the onset of the episode, Neri remained in the same position.

The cafeteria continued operating normally.

People came and went.

Conversations continued.

Meals were served.

Neri did not move.

Her book remained open.

Her eyes remained open.

Nobody noticed.

This was not because the SAFEHOUSE had consciously decided not to care.

It was because Neri's condition had become exceptionally difficult to distinguish from her normal behavior.

She was quiet.

She was still.

She was reading.

Except she wasn't.

---

# 4. Four Hours

Approximately four hours after the initial onset, a cafeteria worker accidentally bumped into Neri while passing her table.

Neri's body shifted.

Then she fell from her seat onto the floor.

The book fell with her.

The worker immediately realized something was wrong.

> **Worker:** “Neri?”

No response.

The cafeteria rapidly changed from an ordinary meal period into an emergency scene.

Medical personnel were called.

Residents nearby began gathering.

Neri was found to be in **severe anemic shock**.

Her cardiovascular system had deteriorated to the point of cardiac arrest.

---

# 5. Emergency Response

Neri required immediate resuscitation.

A defibrillator was brought to the scene.

Medical personnel initiated emergency cardiac treatment while Bao was contacted.

Bao arrived with compatible blood.

The response required both:

**Defibrillation**

and

**Bao's compatible blood.**

The defibrillator restored cardiac activity.

Bao's blood provided the compatible replacement required to bring Neri's cardiovascular system back above the critical threshold.

The emergency response was successful.

Neri survived.

---

# 7. The Jane Problem

Jane was informed afterward that she had seen Neri approximately five minutes after the episode began.

Jane reportedly became very quiet.

> **Jane:** “I saw her.”

Medical personnel confirmed that Neri had already been experiencing the episode when Jane entered the cafeteria.

Jane reportedly stared at the floor for several seconds.

> **Jane:** “She looked like she was reading.”

That was the problem.

She **did** look like she was reading.

There was no obvious collapse.

No cry for help.

No visible struggle.

No indication that her heart was failing.

Jane had not knowingly walked away from an emergency.

She had simply encountered a child who looked completely normal while being critically ill.

---

# 8. Neri's Response

After stabilization and recovery, Neri remained characteristically unconcerned about her own condition.

When informed that she had suffered cardiac arrest, Neri's response was notably subdued.

She did not appear particularly frightened.

She did not become angry.

She did not seem to understand why everyone was so distressed.

Her primary concern was whether she had interrupted anyone.

This remains consistent with her established behavior.

Neri continues to regard her own health as something that can be placed behind everyone else's needs.

---

# 9. Continuing Behavioral Concern

The medical team considers this the most dangerous aspect of the incident.

Neri's condition is becoming increasingly difficult to manage because the medical emergency itself is not necessarily accompanied by behavior that attracts attention.

Her mindset makes the problem worse.

Neri is still reluctant to interrupt people.

She still assumes other people are busy.

She still tends to remain where she is rather than seeking help.

She still does not reliably treat her own symptoms as urgent.

And she continues to regard herself as expendable.

The emergency blood-response pens prepared by Bao remain available.

However, they are only useful if someone realizes Neri needs help.

---

# 10. SAFEHOUSE Assessment

The incident has prompted a difficult realization among SAFEHOUSE personnel.

The problem is not simply that Neri needs better medical equipment.

She already has access to emergency treatment.

The problem is that:

> **Neri can be dying in a crowded room and still look like she's reading.**

Four hours passed.

People walked by her.

Jane saw her.

Nobody knew.

It took an accidental bump to make her fall before anyone realized there was an emergency.

That cannot be treated as an ordinary medical incident.

---

# 11. Jane Doe — Private Note

Jane later wrote a short note regarding the incident.

> **“I saw her.”**

> **“I was going to check on her.”**

> **“Then my phone rang.”**

> **“I keep telling her she can bother me.”**

> **“She doesn't.”**

A final line was added beneath it:

> **“Next time, I'm checking anyway.”**

---

# 12. Final Assessment

Neri survived.

That is the good news.

The bad news is that survival required an emergency response that came **four hours after the episode began**.

She required defibrillation.

She required Bao's blood.

She came dangerously close to dying without anyone realizing she was even unconscious.

And afterward, Neri still demonstrated the same fundamental problem:

She does not consider herself important enough to protect.

The SAFEHOUSE cannot solve that problem with a defibrillator.

It cannot solve it with emergency blood.

It cannot solve it by simply telling Neri:

> **“Take better care of yourself.”**

Because Neri's problem is not that she doesn't know people care.

It is that some part of her still believes:

> **Everyone else matters more.**

Until that changes, every quiet afternoon in the cafeteria remains potentially dangerous.
