## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — THE INCIDENT WITH THE DOOR**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — PERSONNEL CARE  
**Status:** Resolved  
**Outcome:** Employee Regret / Policy Reinforcement

---

# **INCIDENT**

Alice was following an employee through one of the Blacksite's administrative corridors.

She was riding her scooter slowly.

Her white beanie was slightly crooked.

Her rabbit ears hung naturally beneath it.

She wasn't causing any trouble.

She was simply following.

The employee was busy.

They had paperwork to deliver and several things on their mind.

Eventually, they turned around.

> "Alice, go somewhere else."

Alice stopped.

The employee gestured toward the opposite hallway.

> "Please. I'm busy."

Alice stared at them.

She didn't argue.

She didn't complain.

She simply nodded.

> "...Okay."

Then she turned her scooter around.

And left.

---

# **THE REGRET**

The employee continued walking.

Several seconds passed.

Then they stopped.

Something felt wrong.

They remembered the new SAFEHOUSE guidelines.

They remembered why they existed.

Alice didn't follow people because she wanted to cause problems.

She followed people because she liked being around them.

And she had just been told to leave.

---

# **THE SEARCH**

The employee turned around.

Alice was already gone.

They looked down the hallway.

Nothing.

They checked the nearby intersection.

Nothing.

They called softly:

> "Alice?"

No answer.

Then they remembered.

Alice could speak now.

But her voice was still extremely quiet.

If she didn't want to bother anyone, she might not answer.

---

# **FOUND**

The employee eventually found Alice sitting beside a wall.

Her scooter was parked next to her.

She was drawing something on a small piece of paper.

The employee approached carefully.

> "Alice?"

Alice looked up.

> "Hm?"

> "What are you drawing?"

Alice showed them.

It was a picture of herself riding her scooter beside the employee.

Both of them had enormous smiles.

The employee felt terrible.

---

# **THE APOLOGY**

> "Alice..."

Alice looked up.

> "I'm sorry."

Alice tilted her head.

> "For what?"

> "For telling you to go away."

Alice thought about it.

Then shrugged.

> "It's okay."

The employee frowned.

> "No. It wasn't."

Alice looked confused.

> "You were busy."

> "That doesn't mean I should have chased you away."

Alice lowered her eyes.

> "...Alice was bothering you?"

The employee immediately shook their head.

> "No."

> "You weren't bothering me."

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Alice smiled slightly.

> "Okay."

The employee hesitated.

> "Do you still want to come with me?"

Alice's ears lifted for perhaps two seconds.

Then dropped again.

> "Yes."

The employee smiled.

> "Then come on."

Alice climbed onto her scooter.

> "Okay!"

She followed them again.

This time, the employee made sure she knew she was welcome.

---

# **CORIN'S REACTION**

When the employee later explained what happened to Corin, Corin sighed.

> "Thank you for going back for her."

The employee nodded.

> "I shouldn't have sent her away."

Corin replied:

> "Nobody is perfect."

Then she added:

> "But Alice needs people who remember that she doesn't know how to make herself noticeable."

The employee nodded.

---

# **POLICY REMINDER**

This incident was added to the personnel training material.

If Alice follows you:

**Do not automatically assume she is a nuisance.**

If you are busy:

**Redirect her gently.**

If she leaves:

**Confirm where she went.**

If you tell her to leave:

**Do not simply assume she found somewhere safe.**

And above all:

> **Never use Alice's quietness as evidence that she doesn't care.**

---

# **ALICE'S ADDITION**

Later that evening, Alice found the incident report.

She added one sentence beneath the final paragraph.

Her handwriting was small.

> **"Alice still like them."**

Then, underneath:

> **"They say sorry."**

And finally:

> **"Alice forgive."**

A tiny smiling face was drawn beside it.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

The employee had chased Alice away.

They regretted it almost immediately.

Not because they were reprimanded.

Not because anyone threatened them.

But because they remembered who Alice was.

A little girl who had spent too much of her life being quietly left behind.

She hadn't protested.

She hadn't become angry.

She had simply said:

> **"...Okay."**

And left.

The employee realized that sometimes the most frightening response Alice could give wasn't anger.

It was acceptance.

Because Alice was far too willing to believe that everyone would be happier if she simply disappeared from their way.

So they went back.

They found her.

They apologized.

And Alice came back with them.
