# **KRAMPUS COMPLIANCE AUTHORITY**

## **CONFIDENTIAL PSYCHOLOGICAL CASE NOTE**

### **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – IDENTITY GRIEF**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 – Medical Director & Executive Personnel Only

---

## **OVERVIEW**

One recurring theme has emerged throughout Corin Lockwood's treatment.

It is not simply grief over what happened.

It is grief for **who she believes she used to be.**

Corin does not mourn only her childhood.

She mourns the child she remembers being.

---

## **RECOVERED FAMILY ACCOUNTS**

Accounts from Arcturus Lockwood, Norma Lockwood, and surviving acquaintances consistently describe Corin before her abduction in remarkably similar terms.

She was remembered as:

- Cheerful.
    
- Curious.
    
- Energetic.
    
- Exceptionally kind.
    
- Quick to laugh.
    
- Quick to forgive.
    

She was known for running through the house despite repeatedly being told not to.

She loved helping her mother in the kitchen, even if it usually meant making more mess than progress.

She had a habit of asking endless questions.

According to Arcturus:

> "She smiled so much that it was difficult finding a photograph where she wasn't."

Norma remembered something different.

> "She'd drag me outside to look at clouds."

> "...Then she'd make up stories about what they looked like."

---

## **THE PHOTOGRAPHS**

Corin rarely keeps photographs of herself from before the experiments.

When relatives or coworkers discover one, it often disappears days later.

Some are torn apart.

Some are burned.

Others are quietly shredded.

When asked why she destroys them, Corin eventually answered.

> "...Because she deserved better."

The psychologist asked gently:

> "Who?"

Corin looked at the photograph.

A smiling little girl.

Bright eyes.

A carefree grin.

Then she whispered:

> "...Her."

---

## **CLINICAL INTERPRETATION**

Counselors believe Corin has developed what they describe as **identity grief**.

Rather than seeing childhood photographs as memories of herself...

She increasingly views them as photographs of someone else.

Someone whose future was stolen.

During one session, Corin admitted:

> "...I don't look at those pictures and think..."

> "'That was me.'"

She closed her eyes.

> "...I think..."

> "'That little girl never got to grow up.'"

---

## **ARCTURUS**

When Arcturus discovered another torn photograph in the office shredder, he quietly collected every piece.

That evening, he sat beside Corin.

Neither spoke for several minutes.

Eventually he placed the pieces on the table.

> "Do you remember this day?"

Corin looked away.

"...No."

Arcturus smiled faintly.

> "You chased butterflies."

> "You caught exactly zero."

A tiny laugh escaped Corin before she could stop it.

Arcturus noticed.

He smiled.

> "That's the first time you've laughed about that picture."

---

## **NORMA**

Norma stopped trying to save every damaged photograph.

Instead...

She began writing memories on the back of the ones that remained.

Simple things.

_"Mom laughed because you put flour everywhere."_

_"You fell asleep holding my hand during a thunderstorm."_

_"Dad carried you home because you refused to stop collecting flowers."_

When Corin asked why, Norma shrugged.

> "...Pictures fade."

> "...Stories don't."

---

## **DIRECTOR LUCIOUS' CLINICAL NOTE**

Director Lucious reviewed the latest counseling report.

His annotation read:

> "Trauma has convinced Corin that the child in those photographs no longer belongs to her."

> "Treatment should not attempt to erase that grief."

> "Instead, help her understand that the child she mourns and the young woman she has become are not strangers."

> "They are the same person."

---

## **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin continues to avoid viewing photographs from before her abduction.

When confronted with them unexpectedly, she may experience intense sadness or attempt to destroy them during periods of severe emotional distress.

Her treatment team is working gradually toward helping her reconnect with those memories at her own pace, without forcing her to revisit them before she is ready.

To the Blacksite, Corin is a brilliant accountant.

To Arcturus, she is still the little sister who chased butterflies.

To Norma, she is still the sister who made up stories from the clouds.

And though Corin cannot yet see it herself, those memories all belong to the same person.

**Corin Lockwood.**

**End of Confidential Psychological Case Note**
