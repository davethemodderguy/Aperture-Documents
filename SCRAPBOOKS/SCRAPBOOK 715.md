### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERMANENT INJURY ASSESSMENT**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — IRREVERSIBLE DAMAGE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / PERMANENT PHYSICAL DAMAGE CONFIRMED**

---

# **REASSESSMENT**

Following months of observation, medical personnel have determined that several of Alice's injuries are no longer considered temporary.

Her regeneration has reached a stable equilibrium around certain damaged areas.

The injuries are not actively worsening.

They are not completely healing either.

Two permanent conditions have been confirmed.

---

# **I. PERMANENT CRANIAL INJURY**

Alice has sustained extensive trauma to her head from repeated falls.

The damage has resulted in a persistent opening in the affected area of the skull and surrounding tissue.

Her regenerative system has stabilized the injury enough to prevent immediate catastrophic deterioration.

It has **not restored the area to its original state.**

The injury therefore requires permanent protection.

Alice continues to wear head bandaging.

The bandage is not simply precautionary.

It is part of her ongoing medical care.

---

# **REGENERATIVE PRIORITY**

The medical department believes this is another example of Alice's regeneration prioritizing survival rather than restoration.

Her body appears to have determined:

> **"This condition is survivable."**

Therefore, it maintains it.

It does not spend the enormous resources necessary to completely reconstruct the damaged area.

This is consistent with previous observations of her regenerative system.

---

# **II. RIGHT EYE**

The right eye remains permanently blind.

The ocular damage is irreversible.

No measurable regenerative activity has restored its function.

Alice therefore has permanent loss of vision on her right side.

She continues to wear an eyepatch.

Her left eye remains functional but must now be treated as her primary visual organ.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Alice has largely accepted both conditions.

She rarely asks about them.

When asked why she wears the eyepatch:

> **"Right eye doesn't work."**

When asked about her head bandage:

> **"Head got hurt."**

She doesn't ask whether they will heal.

She seems to have already accepted that they won't.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has difficulty looking at the injuries.

Not because they frighten her.

Because she remembers how they happened.

Every mark represents another time Alice tried to disappear.

Another fall.

Another injury.

Another attempt to convince herself that everyone would be better without her.

Corin sometimes changes Alice's bandage without saying anything.

Alice simply sits still.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle has become extremely careful around Alice's head.

She avoids touching the bandaged area unless necessary.

When Alice's eyepatch needs adjusting, Belle asks first.

> **"Can I?"**

Alice nods.

Belle fixes it gently.

Then she smiles.

> **"There."**

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi's reaction was quiet.

She had hoped Alice's regeneration would eventually undo the damage.

The medical report removed that possibility.

Lumi simply stared at the report for several seconds.

Then closed it.

She did not tell Alice.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has ensured that Alice's permanent injuries are treated as part of her normal life rather than something that defines her.

The medical team is instructed not to repeatedly remind Alice that she is permanently damaged.

She knows.

There is no reason to make her think about it constantly.

---

---

# **THE GREATER CONCERN**

These injuries are permanent.

But the injuries themselves are not what frightens the SAFEHOUSE most.

Alice survived them.

She adapted.

She learned to navigate with one eye.

She learned to live with the bandage.

She continues eating Corin's soup.

She continues breathing.

The greater concern is **how they happened.**

Alice's body can survive an extraordinary amount of damage.

That does not mean Alice should have to.

Her regeneration has prevented countless injuries from becoming fatal.

It cannot undo everything.

And it cannot protect Alice from herself forever.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice's body has healed many things.

Some wounds disappeared.

Some scars faded.

Some fractures repaired.

But not everything could be restored.

Her head will never completely return to what it was.

Her right eye will never see again.

Those injuries are now part of Alice's permanent physical condition.

The SAFEHOUSE does not see her as broken.

They see a little girl who survived.

And now everyone is determined to make sure she never has to earn survival by hurting herself again.
