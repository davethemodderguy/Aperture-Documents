### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — MEMORY DETERIORATION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / PROGRESSIVE COGNITIVE DECLINE**

---

# **THE CARROT MEMORY IS GONE**

Alice has forgotten carrots.

This was confirmed during breakfast.

Belle placed a carrot beside Alice.

Alice looked at it.

She did not react.

Belle smiled.

> **"Your favorite."**

Alice stared at the carrot.

> **"...Favorite?"**

Belle's smile disappeared.

> **"Carrots."**

Alice looked confused.

> **"What's carrot?"**

No recognition.

No familiarity.

Nothing.

---

# **CONFIRMATION**

Multiple attempts were made to determine whether Alice had simply forgotten the word.

She did not recognize:

- The word **carrot**
    
- The appearance of a carrot
    
- The taste of a carrot
    
- The smell of a carrot
    
- Her previous preference for carrots
    
- Her explanation that rabbits liked carrots
    

When shown photographs of herself eating carrots, Alice showed no recognition.

When told:

> **"You used to really like these."**

Alice answered:

> **"Did Alice?"**

She sounded genuinely confused.

---

# **THE MEDICAL EXPLANATION**

The medical department considers this extremely serious.

Alice's nutritional state has deteriorated far enough that her cognitive function is now being affected.

The brain requires continuous energy and nutrients to maintain normal function.

Alice has been receiving insufficient nutrition for an extended period.

Her body has been prioritizing immediate survival.

Higher-order processes are beginning to suffer.

Memory formation and retention are particularly vulnerable.

The medical team cannot determine how much of Alice's remaining memory can be preserved.

---

# **WHAT SHE IS LOSING**

The deterioration is no longer limited to distant childhood memories.

Alice has begun forgetting relatively recent information.

She may forget:

- Conversations from earlier in the day
    
- Names of objects
    
- Familiar routines
    
- Previously learned words
    
- Small personal preferences
    
- Events from the previous day
    

Some memories remain.

Others disappear.

There is no predictable pattern.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Alice does not appear particularly upset by the loss.

That may be the most concerning part.

She simply accepts it.

When Corin explained that she used to love carrots, Alice tilted her head.

> **"Okay."**

Corin asked:

> **"You don't remember?"**

Alice shook her head.

> **"No."**

A pause.

> **"Sorry."**

Corin immediately replied:

> **"You don't have to apologize."**

Alice looked at the carrot again.

> **"Is it good?"**

Corin nodded.

> **"Yeah."**

Alice took a small bite.

She did not smile.

But she swallowed it.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle had to leave the room shortly afterward.

She had spent months bringing Alice carrots.

She had always thought that if Alice could remember one thing she loved, it would be enough.

Now Alice didn't even remember that.

Belle returned several minutes later.

She brought another carrot.

She placed it beside Alice.

She said nothing.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has become increasingly protective of Alice's remaining memories.

She no longer asks Alice:

> "Do you remember this?"

unless absolutely necessary.

Repeatedly testing Alice's memory only risks turning every forgotten thing into another reminder of what she has lost.

Instead, Corin tells her.

Not as a test.

Not as an interrogation.

Simply as information.

> **"Your name is Alice."**

> **"You live with us."**

> **"You're a Lockwood."**

> **"You like flowers."**

> **"You have a bunny nose."**

And sometimes:

> **"You used to love carrots."**

Alice doesn't always remember.

That's okay.

Someone else remembers for her.

---

# **THE FAMILY MEMORY**

C.O.R.E. has begun maintaining a separate document.

Not a medical record.

Not a diagnostic report.

A memory archive.

It contains things Alice may eventually forget.

Her favorite foods.

Her favorite places.

The people she trusts.

The games she played.

The first time she met Corin.

The first time she met Belle.

The Treehouse.

Bunny.

Carrots.

Every small thing that made Alice **Alice**.

The archive is not intended to force memories back into her mind.

It exists so that if Alice forgets something...

someone else will remember.

---

# **NUTRITIONAL EMERGENCY**

The medical department has elevated Alice's nutritional condition to an immediate emergency.

Her continued refusal or inability to consume adequate nutrition is now directly threatening:

- Cognitive function
    
- Physical development
    
- Immune function
    
- Organ function
    
- Existing memory retention
    
- Long-term neurological health
    

Alice is still a growing child.

Her body cannot continue indefinitely without adequate nutritional resources.

The previous assumption that she could simply recover gradually is no longer sufficient.

Intervention is now required.

---

# **IMPORTANT PSYCHOLOGICAL DIRECTIVE**

Alice must not be told:

> **"You're forgetting everything."**

She must not be made frightened about her memory.

She must not be told:

> **"You need to remember."**

Memory is not an assignment.

If Alice forgets something, someone else remembers it for her.

If she forgets a person:

Someone can introduce themselves again.

If she forgets a place:

Someone can show her.

If she forgets a favorite food:

Someone can offer it again.

If she forgets that she is loved:

Someone can tell her.

Again.

And again.

And again.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

For months, the residents had one small certainty.

Alice loved carrots.

It was silly.

It was simple.

It was something a five-year-old rabbit could remember even when almost everything else was gone.

Today, Alice looked at one and asked:

> **"What's that?"**

The carrot itself was never important.

The memory was.

And now that memory is gone.

But Corin still remembers.

Belle still remembers.

Orphie still remembers.

Everyone remembers.

So if Alice forgets tomorrow...

They will tell her again.

If she forgets next week...

They will tell her again.

If she forgets the people around her...

They will introduce themselves again.

Because Alice does not have to carry every memory by herself.

Not anymore.
