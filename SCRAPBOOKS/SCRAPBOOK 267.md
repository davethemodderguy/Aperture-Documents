# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – MEDICAL & PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION DOSSIER**

### **SUBJECTS: DIALYN & COREY LOCKWOOD**

**Classification:** LEVEL 8 – Medical, Psychological Services & Executive Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Following the courtyard incident, Medical and Psychological Services documented two significant developments affecting separate personnel.

Although unrelated biologically or anomalously, both responses illustrate the long-term effects of severe psychological trauma.

---

# **SUBJECT A — DIALYN**

## **TRANSIENT ACHROMATOPSIA**

Medical examinations have confirmed that Dialyn experiences episodes of temporary color blindness during periods of extreme psychological distress.

The condition is not constant.

It occurs only when she is subjected to overwhelming emotional trauma, severe stress, or intense psychological shock.

---

# **SYMPTOMS**

During an episode, Dialyn reports that the world gradually loses its color.

The transition is progressive rather than instantaneous.

Bright reds become gray.

Blue skies become pale.

Green foliage fades into muted shades.

Eventually, she perceives only variations of black, white, and gray.

Neurological scans have found no structural damage to her eyes.

Medical Division believes the phenomenon is a stress-induced neurological response, likely influenced by her prolonged Ether exposure during childhood.

---

# **OBSERVED BEHAVIOR**

Personnel have learned to recognize the warning signs.

Dialyn becomes noticeably quieter.

She blinks more frequently while looking around her surroundings.

She may gently rub one eye before quietly asking questions such as:

> "...Is that flower red?"

or

> "...What color is your scarf today?"

She never panics.

She simply waits.

The colors eventually return once she has recovered emotionally.

# **PERSONAL RECORD**

Administrative records intentionally list Dialyn's gender as:

> **Not Applicable**

This designation exists at Dialyn's request.

She has stated that she does not identify with a conventional gender classification.

For everyday conversation and official communication, personnel are instructed to refer to Dialyn using **she/her** pronouns unless she requests otherwise.

Human Resources considers the matter closed.

---

# **SUBJECT B — COREY LOCKWOOD**

## **POST-INCIDENT WITHDRAWAL**

Following the courtyard accident, Corey's recovery has progressed more slowly than initially anticipated.

Although the maintenance worker survived through the Banlands' Soul Contract, Corey continues to experience persistent psychological distress.

---

# **OBSERVED BEHAVIOR**

Since the incident, Corey has increasingly isolated herself.

Documented changes include:

- Avoiding crowded areas.
    
- Declining social gatherings.
    
- Spending prolonged periods inside the treehouse.
    
- Reduced verbal communication.
    
- Difficulty sleeping.
    
- Startling easily at unexpected movement.
    

She consistently avoids discussing the accident.

---

# **EXCEPTION**

Only one person has been able to approach Corey without hesitation.

Corin.

Corey continues to remain near Corin throughout the day.

If Corin moves to another room, Corey quietly follows.

If Corin sits, Corey often sits nearby.

Personnel believe Corin currently serves as Corey's primary emotional anchor.

---

# **PERSONNEL COMMENTS**

**Corin Lockwood**

> "...You don't have to talk."

> "...I'll stay."

---

**Star**

Star quietly left homemade paper stars outside the treehouse door.

She did not knock.

---

**Nightshade**

Nightshade now patrols the surrounding forest more frequently.

She has offered no explanation.

---

**Bao**

Bao continues preparing two meals each day.

One for Corin.

One for Corey.

Even on the days Corey forgets to eat.

---

**Lumi**

Lumi has elected not to pressure Corey into counseling sessions.

Instead, she visits periodically with tea, books, or simply quiet company.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Medical Division expects Dialyn's color vision to return after periods of recovery, as it has in previous episodes.

Psychological Services continue to monitor Corey, whose healing remains ongoing.

Neither woman is considered beyond help.

Both have survived circumstances that would have broken many others.

For now, their colleagues understand that recovery is not measured only by medicine or anomalies.

Sometimes it is measured by quieter things.

Someone sitting beside you.

A meal left at your door.

A familiar voice.

Or, in Corey's case...

Knowing that wherever she goes, Corin will be there too.

**End of Medical & Psychological Observation Dossier**
