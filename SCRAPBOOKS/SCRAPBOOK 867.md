# SAFEHOUSE PSYCHOLOGICAL ADDENDUM

**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL — RESIDENT WELFARE  
**Subjects:** Neri / Alice  
**Alice Status:** MISSING — 5 WEEKS  
**Neri Status:** SAFEHOUSE RESIDENT  
**Primary Concern:** Persistent interpersonal rupture resulting from fundamental misunderstanding  
**Conversation Contents:** Restricted

---

## 1. Revised Understanding

Further information regarding the conversation between Neri and Alice has clarified the circumstances surrounding Neri's emotional reaction.

The statement that upset Neri was **not an attempt by Alice to belittle, dismiss, or weaponize Neri's past.**

Alice had been attempting to communicate something considerably different.

During their conversation, Alice disclosed that she had previously hated Corin and/or Orphie for reasons unrelated to Neri.

The statement was intended as part of a broader attempt to explain that relationships can be complicated.

Alice's intended message was effectively:

> **People can have complicated histories with one another and still care deeply about each other.**

She was attempting to reassure Neri that the people around her were trying their best to take care of her.

That Neri was not alone.

That having complicated feelings did not mean that someone had stopped caring.

Neri did not understand this.

---

# 2. Neri's Interpretation

Neri interpreted Alice's statement through the only family framework she had ever known.

Her conclusion was substantially different:

> **Alice hated her own family.**

To most residents, this would be an understandable statement about a complicated family relationship.

To Neri, it carried an entirely different meaning.

Neri does not possess a comparable childhood experience of a loving family having disagreements, arguments, resentment, forgiveness, and continued affection.

Her experience was fundamentally different.

Her family taught her that people could be discarded.

Her sister was taken from her.

Neri could not understand how someone could say:

> “I used to hate my family.”

and simultaneously mean:

> “I still care about them.”

To Neri, those concepts had never been demonstrated to coexist.

---

# 3. The Missing Context

This is the central tragedy of the interaction.

Alice was attempting to tell Neri:

> **“You have people who care about you.”**

Neri heard:

> **“Even people who are supposed to love each other can hate each other.”**

Alice was attempting to tell Neri:

> **“You are not alone.”**

Neri heard:

> **“Family doesn't necessarily mean safety.”**

Alice was attempting to normalize complicated relationships.

Neri interpreted the statement as evidence that even apparently loving families could eventually reject one another.

Neither resident was necessarily acting irrationally.

They were speaking from completely different lived experiences.

---

# 4. Why Neri Became Heartbroken

Neri's reaction was therefore not primarily anger toward Alice.

It was grief.

The statement inadvertently forced Neri to confront something she had never possessed:

**a family worth having complicated feelings about.**

Alice could talk about having once hated Corin or Orphie because, underneath that complicated history, there was still a family.

There was still attachment.

There was still something worth repairing.

Neri's younger sister was different.

Neri loved her.

Her sister loved her.

And Neri was powerless to stop what happened to her.

There was no later reconciliation.

No argument followed by forgiveness.

No family waiting on the other side of the conflict.

Only absence.

Consequently, Neri could not understand Alice's intended meaning.

She did not realize that Alice's example was actually evidence that family could survive conflict.

---

# 5. The Five-Week Absence

Alice has now been missing for approximately **five weeks.**

Search efforts remain ongoing.

Neri has been informed of Alice's disappearance.

Her response remains notably indifferent.

She has not requested updates.

She has not participated in searches.

She has not expressed concern regarding Alice's safety.

She has not asked when Alice will return.

This does not appear to be because Neri believes Alice deserves to suffer.

Rather, Neri appears to have emotionally removed Alice from the category of people for whom she feels responsible.

---

# 6. The Cruel Irony

Alice's last meaningful interaction with Neri may have been an attempt to tell her:

> **“You are never alone.”**

Five weeks later, Alice herself is gone.

Neri has not interpreted this as proof that Alice's message was correct.

She has instead seemingly accepted Alice's absence.

This is concerning.

Neri's childhood taught her that when someone disappears, eventually there is simply nothing she can do about it.

Her current reaction may therefore represent the reactivation of an old coping mechanism:

> **If someone leaves, stop expecting them to come back.**

That mechanism protects Neri from repeatedly experiencing the helplessness associated with loss.

It also prevents her from maintaining attachments that she believes have become unsafe.

---

# 7. The Part Neri Never Realized

There is no indication that Neri ever understood Alice's actual intention.

She never realized that Alice was trying to reassure her.

She never realized that the example about Corin and Orphie was meant to demonstrate that **love can survive conflict.**

She never realized that Alice was attempting to tell her:

> **“People here care about you even when things aren't perfect.”**

She never realized that Alice was trying to say:

> **“You don't have to protect everyone by yourself.”**

And she never realized that Alice was, in her own way, trying to tell Neri that she had finally found something she had been missing her entire life:

**a family that would keep trying.**

---

# 8. Personnel Recommendation

Do not tell Neri this interpretation.

At least, not now.

The information is an interpretation of Alice's intent rather than something Neri has independently asked to understand.

Attempting to explain Alice's words on Alice's behalf could cause another emotional injury, particularly while Alice remains missing.

If Alice returns, the matter should be left to the two residents.

If Neri eventually asks what Alice meant, she should be allowed to arrive at that understanding herself, with appropriate support if necessary.

---

# 9. Final Assessment

This incident should not be classified as:

**Alice hurt Neri.**

Nor should it be classified as:

**Neri rejected Alice for no reason.**

The more accurate description is:

> **Two traumatized children attempted to understand one another using completely different definitions of family.**

Alice spoke from the experience of having a family that could argue, resent one another, and still care.

Neri listened from the experience of having a family in which people could be deemed disposable.

Alice tried to tell Neri that she was surrounded by people who cared.

Neri heard a warning that even families could hate one another.

And then Neri walked away.

Alice disappeared.

Five weeks passed.

Neri never learned what Alice was actually trying to say.

---

### ARCHIVIST'S FINAL NOTE

The saddest part is not that Neri misunderstood Alice.

It is that **Alice was probably saying exactly what Neri needed to hear.**

Neri simply did not yet have the experiences necessary to understand it.

She knows what it means to lose family.

She knows what it means to be abandoned.

She knows what it means to be helpless.

She knows what it means to watch someone disappear.

She does not yet know what it means to have a family that argues with you and still comes back.

That is the luxury she never had.

And Alice was trying, perhaps clumsily, to show her that such a thing existed.

**Neri never realized.**
