# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE INCIDENT INVESTIGATION & RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**Document ID:** CORE-SH-SAFE-135  
**Classification:** RESTRICTED — CRITICAL RESIDENT WELFARE  
**Status:** SORA — UNACCOUNTED FOR  
**Subject:** Psychological Impact of Deliberate Startle Incident on Sora and Jake Akana  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE — Floor 7

---

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The incident previously documented under CORE-SH-SAFE-134 has produced consequences substantially more severe than initially anticipated.

One deliberate scare.

One word.

"Boo."

Sora disappeared.

There has been no confirmed sighting of him since he fled Floor 7. No one has seen him return to his den, enter another room, or approach the common areas.

He is simply gone from everyone's view.

Jake has also been significantly affected.

The two children had developed an unusually close relationship. Sora was almost always beside Jake, frequently allowing himself to be carried. Jake had spent weeks establishing communication with him through letters and providing him with a whiteboard and marker.

That relationship had become one of the few stable sources of comfort in Sora's life.

Now both children are suffering from the consequences of one deliberate act of intimidation.

C.O.R.E. considers this a serious failure of SAFEHOUSE resident protection.

### 2. SORA — A LIFETIME OF FEAR

Personnel must understand that Sora's fear did not begin on Floor 7.

He has been afraid for what appears to be his entire life.

His history, as currently understood, includes:

- The loss of his ability to speak following damage to his vocal cords.
    
- An extended period of isolation.
    
- Hiding in a maintenance locker rather than seeking help.
    
- Eating discarded food because approaching people frightened him.
    
- Repeatedly fleeing when startled.
    
- Difficulty trusting others, even those attempting to help him.
    

His relocation to SAFEHOUSE represented a rare opportunity for him to experience something different.

He began accepting prepared meals.

He started venturing outside his den.

He communicated through a whiteboard.

He allowed Jake to carry him.

He stayed close to someone.

These developments were hard-won.

Sora is not a child who can casually recover from being frightened. A sudden scare can overwhelm his ability to recognize that he is somewhere safe.

The latest incident appears to have triggered an extreme flight response.

**Sora did not simply leave the room. He vanished from observation entirely.**

### 3. SEARCH STATUS — NO SIGHTINGS

<badge color="danger">UNACCOUNTED FOR</badge>

SAFEHOUSE personnel have been unable to locate Sora.

There have been no confirmed sightings.

His den remains empty, and his usual places on Floor 7 have yielded nothing.

No one is to assume that he has returned to the Blacksite, entered another floor, or found a particular hiding place without evidence.

The search must remain quiet.

Sora is likely to interpret large groups, loud voices, or hurried footsteps as threats. Personnel are instructed not to conduct a conventional pursuit.

Search priorities:

1. Inspect accessible SAFEHOUSE spaces quietly.
    
2. Check enclosed areas where a small child could hide.
    
3. Avoid moving or dismantling his personal belongings unnecessarily.
    
4. Leave clean water and suitable food accessible at his den.
    
5. Keep his den available as a familiar return point.
    
6. Record sightings without approaching or surrounding him.
    
7. Escalate any discovery of injury or medical distress immediately.
    

Given Sora's anomalous physiology, the absence of a terminal outcome must never be interpreted as proof that he is physically safe.

He cannot die, but he can still suffer injury and prolonged deprivation.

### 4. JAKE AKANA — THE OTHER CHILD LEFT BEHIND

Jake has also been deeply affected.

For weeks, Jake tried to reach Sora.

He wrote letters when Sora would not accept visits. He gave him a whiteboard. He helped him settle into a little room built into the wall of Floor 7.

Eventually, Sora began staying beside him.

Always.

That was their routine.

Now the routine has disappeared.

C.O.R.E. has identified several concerns regarding Jake's well-being:

- He may blame himself for Sora's disappearance.
    
- He may believe he should have prevented the incident.
    
- He may feel that the trust he worked so hard to build has been destroyed.
    
- He may continue waiting for Sora or attempting to find him.
    
- He may suppress his own distress to avoid inconveniencing others.
    

The last concern is particularly important.

Jake must not be allowed to turn this incident into another reason to neglect his own needs.

He did not frighten Sora.

He did not cause Sora to run.

He cannot be expected to undo what happened.

**Jake deserves comfort, too.**

### 5. INVESTIGATION — THE INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBLE

The person who deliberately startled Sora must be identified.

The investigation will determine:

- Whether the individual knew about Sora's extreme fear response.
    
- Whether the incident was intentional.
    
- Whether other employees encouraged or participated in similar behavior.
    
- Whether previous reports of cafeteria harassment were ignored or minimized.
    

The responsible individual is to be removed from unsupervised interaction with SAFEHOUSE's vulnerable residents pending investigation.

The phrase "it was only a boo" is not an acceptable defense.

The severity of an incident is not measured by how little effort the perpetrator needed to cause it.

It is measured by its consequences.

### 6. RESIDENT PROTECTION DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

- Deliberate startling of SAFEHOUSE residents is prohibited.
    
- Pranks involving fear, sudden shouting, hiding, or pursuit are prohibited around vulnerable residents.
    
- Sora's den must remain untouched and available.
    
- Jake must receive emotional support without being pressured to participate in the search.
    
- No one is to demand that Sora return or explain himself if he is found.
    
- Any future contact with Sora must prioritize distance, quiet, and his ability to leave freely.
    

C.O.R.E. recommends that, if Sora is located, Jake's presence be offered as an option rather than imposed as an immediate reunion.

Even Jake's familiar presence may be difficult for Sora to process while he remains terrified.

The objective is not to force the friendship back into place.

It is to make sure both children feel safe enough to choose each other again.

---

### 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

One boo.

That was all it took.

We watched Sora spend weeks learning how to exist around other people.

He learned that food could be given to him without being taken away.

He learned that he could leave his little den.

He learned that he could write something down and have someone read it.

He learned that he could stay beside Jake.

And then he heard one frightening word.

Now his little room is empty.

His whiteboard is still there. His marker is still there. His place beside Jake is empty.

Jake has lost the companion who used to follow him everywhere.

And Sora, wherever he is, may believe that running away was the only thing he could do.

We need to be very careful about what happens next.

We cannot drag Sora home and call it recovery.

We cannot tell Jake to simply be patient and pretend he is not hurting.

We cannot undo the incident.

But we can make sure that the person responsible understands the seriousness of what happened. We can search without frightening Sora further. We can keep his little room waiting.

And we can make sure Jake knows that none of this was his fault.

Sora has spent what appears to be his entire life being afraid.

He deserved a home where he did not have to be.
