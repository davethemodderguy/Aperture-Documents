## I. THE TRACKING SYSTEM THAT COULD NOT FIND HIM

**09:34:11 — ANOMALOUS MONITORING CENTER**

The search team activated Jake Akana's stellar radiation tracking protocol.

The protocol was not experimental.

It had been used before. Jake naturally emitted a harmless stellar radiation signature that could be detected and tracked without physical contact, invasive procedures, or any risk to his health. His signature was sufficiently consistent that C.O.R.E. had incorporated it into his missing-person contingency procedures.

It existed for precisely this reason.

If Jake wandered away, became lost, hid somewhere inaccessible, or could not answer when called, the Blacksite could locate him.

The tracking system did not require Jake to understand the situation.

It did not require him to remember where he was.

It did not require him to ask for help.

**The system only required Jake's stellar signature to be detectable.**

At 09:34, the first sweep returned no signal.

The operator checked the receiver.

The receiver was functioning.

The operator recalibrated the instrument and initiated another sweep.

No signal.

A second receiver was brought online.

No signal.

A third monitoring station was instructed to independently verify the result.

No signal.

At 09:41, the incident commander ordered a complete system diagnostic.

At 09:46, the diagnostic confirmed that the equipment was operational.

At 09:48, the tracking system was tested against other known stellar signatures. The receivers detected them within expected parameters.

At 09:51, the team repeated the search for Jake.

Nothing.

Not a weak return.

Not a partial return.

Not a distant signature that could be isolated with additional equipment.

Nothing.

The system was not reporting that Jake was far away.

It was not reporting that he was underground, inside a shielded room, or concealed behind the Blacksite's infrastructure.

It was reporting that it could not detect him.

**NO SIGNAL HERE.**

## II. VERIFICATION

**09:53 — LIVE TECHNICAL REVIEW**

> **MONITORING OPERATOR:** “Repeat the sweep.”
> 
> **TECHNICIAN:** “Already running.”
> 
> **MONITORING OPERATOR:** “Full coverage. All available receivers.”
> 
> **TECHNICIAN:** “Coverage is confirmed.”
> 
> **MONITORING OPERATOR:** “Then why am I looking at an empty field?”
> 
> **TECHNICIAN:** “I don't know.”

The technician attempted to isolate the expected frequency range associated with Jake's stellar radiation.

No identifiable signature was returned.

The system's sensitivity was increased within its approved operating parameters. The search range was widened. The team examined the raw data rather than relying exclusively on the tracking software's interpretation.

The result remained unchanged.

No detectable signal attributable to Jake Akana was present.

At 10:02, a supervisor requested that the system's historical records be checked to ensure that the expected signature had not been misidentified.

The records confirmed that the tracking protocol had reliably detected Jake before.

The tracking protocol had not failed during previous searches.

There was no documented indication that Jake had deliberately suppressed his stellar radiation.

There was no established precedent showing that ordinary changes between his human and cat forms would make him undetectable.

None of this explained the current result.

A technician suggested that the subject might be outside the effective detection range.

The incident commander asked for the range calculations.

The calculations did not establish that conclusion.

Another technician suggested that the subject might be behind an unknown form of interference.

No such interference had been detected.

Both possibilities remained open, but neither could be used to describe Jake's location with confidence.

At 10:08, the incident commander ordered the following statement entered into the official record:

> **“Do not report that Jake is too far away to detect. We do not know that. Report only what the instruments establish: his stellar signature is not being detected.”**

The statement was repeated to every search team.

The difference mattered.

If Jake was merely far away, the team could expand its search radius.

If his signal was being blocked, the team could search for the source of interference.

If something had happened to his anomalous state, the response would need to change entirely.

At present, C.O.R.E. could not determine which possibility, if any, was correct.

## III. THE BEAR STILL HAS A HEARTBEAT

**10:14 — CONTROLLED ANOMALOUS WELFARE LABORATORY**

Jake's teddy bear remained under continuous supervision.

The recovery team confirmed that the heartbeat was still present.

The bear had been found abandoned in a ventilation shaft. Jake had not been located at the discovery site or in the immediately connected areas.

The stellar tracking system was now unable to detect him anywhere within its available coverage.

The monitoring team compared these observations.

They could not establish a causal relationship between them.

The fact that the bear's heartbeat continued did not establish Jake's location. Nor did the absence of Jake's stellar signature establish that his heart had stopped, that he had died, or that the bear had ceased to function as part of his anomalous physiology.

The team was explicitly prohibited from making any of those conclusions.

However, the combination of observations created a serious and unprecedented welfare concern.

Jake was missing.

His heart-bearing teddy bear had been found separated from him.

His usual stellar signature could not be detected.

The three facts did not form an explanation.

They formed a problem for which C.O.R.E. had no established procedure.

At 10:17, a researcher asked whether the bear could be used to locate Jake through some form of sympathetic stellar response.

The proposal was logged for review.

No experimental procedure was authorized. The bear was not to be modified, exposed to untested equipment, or subjected to anomalous activation attempts merely because the standard tracking method had failed.

The priority was to preserve the recovered artifact while continuing to search for the child.

**Research Division directive:** The bear is evidence. It is not a substitute for Jake, and its continued heartbeat is not grounds for reducing search urgency.

## IV. ARIES RECEIVES THE UPDATE

**10:21 — EMERGENCY RESPONSE CORRIDOR**

Aries was reviewing a list of searched locations when the incident commander approached him.

He had been expecting another location.

Another corridor.

Another possible sighting.

Instead, he received the tracking report.

> **ARIES:** “What do you mean, there's no signal?”
> 
> **INCIDENT COMMANDER:** “The system cannot detect Jake's stellar signature.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “Then it's broken.”
> 
> **INCIDENT COMMANDER:** “We've verified the equipment.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “Verify it again.”

The commander did not argue.

Aries stared at the report.

> **ARIES:** “He always gives off that radiation. You can track him with it. That's why the system exists.”
> 
> **INCIDENT COMMANDER:** “We understand.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “Then find him.”

His voice was beginning to shake.

> **ARIES:** “You can find a signal from a star. You can find things that aren't even supposed to exist. He's right here. He's supposed to be right here.”

He looked toward the monitoring room.

> **ARIES:** “Where is my brother?”

The incident commander had no answer.

Aries turned away and pressed one hand against the wall.

For several seconds, he did not move.

The people around him were careful not to crowd him. He was not preventing the search from continuing. He was trying to comprehend the failure of a system that had always represented one simple promise: if Jake disappeared, they would be able to find him.

That promise had now failed.

Aries lowered his hand.

> **ARIES:** “Keep looking.”

Then he returned to the search.

## V. CLOVER: “HE CAN'T JUST DISAPPEAR.”

Clover was informed of the tracking failure at 10:27.

His first response was to ask whether the system had been checked against the bear.

The researchers explained that the teddy bear's heartbeat was detectable through its established monitoring procedures, but that this was not the same as detecting Jake's stellar radiation.

Clover listened.

Then he shook his head.

> **CLOVER:** “No. That's not right.”
> 
> **PHOENIX:** “Clover—”
> 
> **CLOVER:** “He can't just disappear.”

He looked toward the monitoring displays.

> **CLOVER:** “He's Jake. He's always there. You can find him. Even if he goes somewhere weird, you can find him.”

He stopped.

The words had come out too quickly. He was trying to convince himself as much as anyone else.

> **CLOVER:** “That's what the tracking system is for.”

Phoenix did not tell him that everything would be fine.

There was no evidence to support that reassurance.

Instead, Phoenix placed a hand on Clover's shoulder.

> **PHOENIX:** “They're still searching.”
> 
> **CLOVER:** “But they can't find him.”
> 
> **PHOENIX:** “I know.”
> 
> **CLOVER:** “Then what are we supposed to do?”

Phoenix looked back at the report.

> **PHOENIX:** “Keep looking for him.”

Clover wiped his face with his sleeve and returned to the search coordination area.

He asked for the search to include every place that had not yet been physically checked, regardless of whether the tracking system indicated a likely location.

The system had returned nothing.

Clover refused to let that become the same thing as having nowhere left to look.

## VI. PHOENIX AND BAO

### Phoenix

Phoenix ordered a second review of the tracking infrastructure.

Not because the first review had been inadequate, but because a missing child was now unlocatable through the very anomalous signature that had been established to find him.

The second review would examine possible blind spots, data corruption, unexpected interference, and failures in the assumptions underlying the tracking protocol.

The investigation would not presume that Jake had intentionally caused the failure.

Nor would it presume that he was physically present somewhere inside the Blacksite.

Phoenix authorized the continuation of the physical search regardless of the technical findings.

> **PHOENIX:** “We don't get to stop because our best tool has failed.”
> 
> **SEARCH COORDINATOR:** “Agreed.”
> 
> **PHOENIX:** “Then don't let the teams start treating the empty map as an answer.”

### Bao

Bao received the tracking update while speaking with a search team.

She asked the operator to explain it again.

The operator did.

Bao looked down at the floor.

> **BAO:** “So you can't tell me where he is.”
> 
> **OPERATOR:** “No, ma'am.”
> 
> **BAO:** “You can't tell me whether he's inside or outside.”
> 
> **OPERATOR:** “Not from this data.”
> 
> **BAO:** “And you can't tell me whether he's safe.”
> 
> **OPERATOR:** “No.”

Bao nodded once.

She thanked the operator.

Then she went back to searching.

She did not ask the same questions again.

She had understood the answers the first time.

There was simply nothing in those answers that she could accept as a reason to stop.

## VII. CAMELIA'S NOTEBOOK

**10:46 — SAFEHOUSE COMMON AREA**

Camelia had been told that the tracking system could not locate Jake.

She did not understand all the technical details. She understood that the system had previously been able to find him and that it could not do so now.

She wrote this in her notebook:

**JAKE'S STELLAR SIGNAL CANNOT BE FOUND.**

Beneath it, she wrote:

**THE SYSTEM WORKS. THEY CHECKED.**

Then, after a long pause:

**THEY STILL CANNOT FIND HIM.**

She read the three lines.

Her fingers tightened around the pen.

> **CAMELIA:** “If he wanted to hide, could he do that?”

The staff member sitting nearby explained that there was no evidence Jake had deliberately suppressed his signature and that the reason for the missing signal was unknown.

Camelia looked at the notebook again.

> **CAMELIA:** “Could something have happened to him?”

The staff member did not lie.

> **STAFF:** “We don't know.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Could he be scared?”
> 
> **STAFF:** “We don't know that either.”

Camelia nodded slowly.

She wanted to ask whether Jake might have left because he was upset with her.

She wanted to ask whether he remembered what she had said.

She wanted to ask whether he had gone somewhere because he no longer wanted to be her brother.

But she could not establish that any of those things had happened.

And she could not remember the argument that might have helped her understand why the questions mattered so much.

She wrote one final line.

**IF JAKE COMES BACK, DO NOT MAKE HIM TALK BEFORE HE IS READY.**

She stared at it.

Then she added:

**TELL HIM I'M HERE. IF HE WANTS ME.**

She closed the notebook and held it against her chest.

For once, she did not ask the staff member to explain the situation again.

She was afraid that if she kept asking, the answers would become no more useful than the empty tracking display.

## VIII. ZERO-SIGNAL PROTOCOL

**11:00 — EMERGENCY COMMAND DIRECTIVE**

C.O.R.E. has designated the current incident **ZERO-SIGNAL**.

This designation describes the failure to detect Jake's normally trackable stellar radiation. It does not imply that Jake has ceased to exist, that his anomalous properties have been lost, or that he has entered any particular state.

Until evidence establishes otherwise, all possible explanations remain open.

The following actions are mandatory:

**A. Continue physical searches.** Every accessible sector must be examined without relying on the tracking system to identify likely locations.

**B. Maintain independent monitoring.** Receivers will continue scanning for Jake's stellar signature in case it becomes detectable again.

**C. Investigate the signal failure.** Technical teams will examine the tracking system, possible interference, and any unexplained changes in the Blacksite's anomalous environment.

**D. Preserve the bear.** The teddy bear's heartbeat and physical condition will continue to be monitored. No experimental attempt to force a response from Jake will be made without appropriate review.

**E. Expand the search cautiously.** Search areas will be expanded based on available evidence and practical access, not on an unsupported assumption that Jake is distant.

**F. Protect the family.** Aries, Clover, Phoenix, and Bao will receive regular updates. Their involvement in the search will be coordinated to avoid leaving critical areas unchecked.

**G. Do not presume intent.** Jake's absence is not proof of running away, deliberate concealment, self-harm, or rejection of his family.

**H. Do not presume safety.** The absence of a detectable signal is not proof of death, but it is not reassurance, either.

All incident reports must use the following wording unless new evidence justifies a more specific conclusion:

**“Jake Akana remains missing. His usual stellar radiation signature is not currently detectable. His location and condition are unknown.”**

## IX. THE EMPTY FIELD

**11:18 — ANOMALOUS MONITORING CENTER**

The monitoring display remained open.

The search continued across every available receiver.

The system continued to detect other known signatures.

It continued to report the status of the recovered bear.

It continued to function.

The field associated with Jake Akana remained empty.

A junior operator asked whether the search should be paused to conserve resources while the technical investigation continued.

The senior operator turned toward them.

> **SENIOR OPERATOR:** “No.”
> 
> **JUNIOR OPERATOR:** “But we don't have a location.”
> 
> **SENIOR OPERATOR:** “Then we search without one.”

The junior operator returned to the console.

Another sweep began.

The display refreshed.

No signal.

Across the Blacksite, Aries was still searching. Clover was still calling. Phoenix was still coordinating the response. Bao was still checking places where a frightened child might have stopped.

Camelia sat with her notebook open, trying not to lose the few facts she could still hold onto.

And Jake—

Jake remained nowhere they could find him.

Not on the tracking display.

Not in the ventilation shaft.

Not beside the teddy bear that contained his heart.

Not in any confirmed sighting.

There was no evidence that he had been destroyed. There was no evidence that he had chosen to vanish. There was no evidence that he was safe.

There was only an empty field where his signature should have been.

### X. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

We created a tracking protocol for Jake because he is a child who can become lost.

We made it reliable because we understood that he might not always know how to ask for help.

We made it harmless because his safety should never require him to endure pain simply so that we could find him.

We trusted the system because, until now, it had given us a way to reach him.

Today, it gave us nothing.

The bear is here.

Its heartbeat continues.

The child is not here.

His signal is not here.

And the people who love him are being forced to confront a possibility none of them had prepared for: that the methods we built to find Jake may not be sufficient to reach him now.

We will not turn that possibility into a conclusion.

We will not call him dead because we cannot detect him.

We will not call him safe because his heart-bearing bear continues to beat.

We will not decide that he has abandoned us because we cannot see where he went.

We will keep searching for the child, not merely for his radiation signature.

We will keep asking where he might be, not merely where the instruments tell us to look.

And if the signal returns, we will follow it.

If it does not, we will continue without it.

**Because Jake Akana is not a signal.**

**He is a six-year-old boy.**

**And he is still missing.**
