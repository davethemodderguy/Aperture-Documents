### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the restoration of his physical body by Guiding Light, Jake Akana has increasingly chosen to remain in his Cat Thiren form rather than his humanoid form.

His preferred appearance is a small white cat with blue eyes and visible cat ears.

Although Jake initially remained in this form for an entire day to allow his older sister, Camelia Candleheart, to play with him, the behavior has since become a regular part of their daily lives.

Jake frequently assumes his cat form to comfort Camelia during episodes of distress, particularly when she experiences nightmares or becomes confused by her severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia.

On some occasions, Camelia forgets information she has previously learned. On others, she temporarily fails to recognize Jake himself.

These incidents can be especially distressing because Jake is not merely a familiar person in her life. He is her little brother, and their relationship is one of the few stable parts of her current existence.

Jake has responded by making himself a familiar, quiet presence.

He curls up beside her. He lets her stroke his fur. He nudges her hand when she hesitates. When she cannot remember his name, he does not demand that she recall it.

Instead, he stays close until she feels safe enough to ask questions.

**C.O.R.E. assessment:** Jake's use of cat form has become an important source of comfort for Camelia. The behavior appears voluntary and mutually welcomed. However, Jake must not be made solely responsible for managing his sister's memory-related distress, and Camelia must never be shamed for forgetting him.

### II. INCIDENT LOG A — THE NIGHTMARE

**Time:** 02:31  
**Location:** Camelia's bedroom

Camelia was found sitting upright in bed after a nightmare.

Her three magenta flames were flickering irregularly. She appeared frightened and repeatedly looked toward the bedroom door, seemingly uncertain of her surroundings.

Jake, already in his cat form, was resting nearby.

When Camelia began to cry, he approached the bed.

**CAMELIA:** “No. No, no, no...”

_Jake jumped onto the mattress._

**CAMELIA:** “Something was there.”

_Jake meowed softly._

**CAMELIA:** “I don't know what it was. I can't remember.”

_Jake moved closer and pressed his head against her hand._

Camelia flinched initially, then looked down.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake?”

_Jake meowed again._

**CAMELIA:** “You're here?”

_He climbed into her lap and curled up._

Camelia placed both hands around him, gently stroking his back.

**CAMELIA:** “You're warm.”

_Jake purred._

**CAMELIA:** “I thought I was alone.”

Jake remained still while Camelia's breathing gradually steadied.

After several minutes, she leaned forward and rested her forehead against his fur.

**CAMELIA:** “Stay with me?”

_Jake's tail curled around her wrist._

He did not leave until Camelia was ready to settle back against her pillows.

**Post-incident assessment:** Jake's presence appeared to help Camelia reorient herself after the nightmare. The content of the dream remained unknown. No attempt was made to force her to remember it.

### III. INCIDENT LOG B — THE NAME SHE COULDN'T REMEMBER

**Time:** 11:08  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE common room

Camelia was sitting on the floor when Jake approached her.

She looked at the white cat, tilted her head, and waited.

Jake sat in front of her.

**CAMELIA:** “Hello, little cat.”

_Jake blinked._

**CAMELIA:** “Are you someone's pet?”

Jake gave a small meow and nudged her knee.

Camelia smiled and began stroking his head.

**CAMELIA:** “You're very friendly.”

_Jake remained beside her._

**CAMELIA:** “I wonder who you belong to.”

Jake lowered his head into her palm.

Camelia's smile faded slightly.

**CAMELIA:** “I'm sorry. I think I should know you.”

_Jake looked up at her._

**CAMELIA:** “Do I know you?”

Jake meowed softly.

He did not run away.

Camelia continued petting him for a while, apparently comforted by the cat's presence even though she could not identify him.

A few minutes later, she noticed the notebook beside her.

She opened it and began reading.

The relevant entry stated:

_Jake Akana is my little brother. He can become a white cat with blue eyes. If I forget him, I can ask for help. I must not be frightened or ashamed._

Camelia read the entry twice.

Then she looked down at the cat.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake?”

_His ears turned toward her._

**CAMELIA:** “You're Jake.”

_Jake meowed._

Camelia's expression crumpled.

**CAMELIA:** “I forgot you again.”

_Jake stepped forward and pressed his forehead against her chest._

**CAMELIA:** “I'm sorry.”

_Jake purred._

**CAMELIA:** “You knew I was going to forget, didn't you?”

Jake could not answer in words.

He simply stayed.

Camelia wrapped her arms around him.

**CAMELIA:** “Thank you for waiting for me.”

**Post-incident assessment:** Camelia's failure to recognize Jake was temporary. The notebook helped her recover the information, but the emotional reassurance came primarily through Jake's continued presence.

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Camelia did not deliberately forget her brother. Her condition can disrupt access to information despite previous familiarity and repetition.

Jake did not display any behavior suggesting anger or resentment toward her.

### IV. INCIDENT LOG C — THE CAT THAT NEVER CORRECTS HER

**Time:** 16:44  
**Location:** Residential lounge

During a routine observation, Camelia referred to Jake as “the white cat” several times.

She was not distressed during this period. She appeared relaxed and spent approximately half an hour stroking his fur while reading her notebook.

At one point, she looked down at him.

**CAMELIA:** “You're a good cat.”

_Jake's tail moved._

**CAMELIA:** “Very polite, too.”

_Jake rested his chin on her knee._

**CAMELIA:** “I wonder if your owner knows how sweet you are.”

Jake closed his eyes.

**CAMELIA:** “If I ever find them, I'll tell them.”

_Jake continued resting._

Camelia eventually turned to another page in her notebook. When she saw Jake's name, she looked back at him.

**CAMELIA:** “Oh. You're not just a cat, are you?”

_Jake opened his eyes._

**CAMELIA:** “You're my brother.”

_He meowed._

**CAMELIA:** “I really need to stop forgetting that.”

Jake rubbed his cheek against her hand.

**CAMELIA:** “You don't mind?”

_Jake settled against her._

**CAMELIA:** “I suppose cats aren't very good at holding grudges.”

Jake's ears flicked.

**C.O.R.E. note:** The interaction appeared lighthearted. However, the repeated pattern remains significant: Jake does not force Camelia to remember him before accepting her affection. He allows her to interact with him as a familiar cat until she can reconnect the appearance with the person she knows.

His behavior is not evidence that he is unaffected by being forgotten. It is evidence only that, during these observed incidents, he has chosen patience rather than confrontation.

### V. OBSERVATIONAL FINDINGS

#### 1. Cat form is Jake's preferred means of offering comfort

Jake's white cat form provides a familiar, nonverbal way to interact with Camelia.

Unlike a conversation, which may become difficult when Camelia is disoriented or frightened, his presence does not require her to remember names, relationships, or explanations.

She can stroke his fur without knowing why she feels comforted by him. She can sit beside him while trying to reconstruct a lost memory. She can recognize the sound of his meow before she recalls his name.

The form appears particularly useful during periods when Camelia is overwhelmed by her inability to remember.

#### 2. Recognition and reassurance are not the same thing

Camelia does not always recognize Jake immediately.

Nevertheless, she often responds positively to his presence before she remembers who he is.

C.O.R.E. cannot establish whether this reflects emotional familiarity, learned associations, or another mechanism. The observation is limited to the behavior itself: Camelia sometimes accepts comfort from Jake before she can identify him.

Personnel must not treat this as proof that her memory has recovered or that her distress is resolved.

#### 3. Jake does not demand recognition

During the recorded incidents, Jake did not insist that Camelia identify him correctly.

He did not withdraw affection when she called him a stray cat, ask her why she had forgotten him, or force her to read her notebook.

He remained nearby and allowed her to rediscover the relationship at her own pace.

This approach is consistent with the established need to avoid pressuring Camelia into reconstructing memories she cannot reliably access.

#### 4. Jake remains a child who needs support, too

Jake's actions are compassionate, but C.O.R.E. will not convert his compassion into an obligation.

He may choose to comfort Camelia. He may also choose to play elsewhere, spend time with other residents, or return to his humanoid form.

SAFEHOUSE personnel remain responsible for supporting both siblings.

If Camelia experiences a prolonged or severe episode, staff should assist rather than assume that the presence of a white cat is sufficient intervention.

### VII. C.O.R.E. WELFARE DIRECTIVE

The following measures remain in effect:

- Jake may continue using his cat form for comfort and companionship at his discretion.
    
- Camelia must be allowed to approach, pet, or sit with Jake when he is receptive to interaction. Jake's boundaries must also be respected.
    
- When Camelia does not recognize Jake, personnel should gently orient her if appropriate, using her notebook and clear, nonjudgmental explanations.
    
- Staff must never shame Camelia for forgetting Jake or demand that she prove her affection by recalling his identity.
    
- Jake must not be expected to remain in cat form continuously or to act as Camelia's sole source of reassurance.
    
- Both residents must retain access to staff support, private space, and activities independent of one another.
    

**Priority:** Preserve the siblings' bond without turning either child's condition into the other child's responsibility.

### VIII. FINAL OBSERVATION — A FAMILIAR LITTLE CAT

There are times when Camelia remembers everything she needs to know about Jake.

She knows his name. She knows he is her little brother. She remembers that Guiding Light returned his body and that he can become a white cat with blue eyes.

And then there are times when she looks at him and sees only a cat.

A pretty cat. A friendly cat. A cat who seems to know her.

Sometimes the realization returns quickly. Sometimes she has to consult her notebook. Sometimes the first thing she remembers is simply that she feels safe when he sits beside her.

Jake has learned to accommodate these moments in the way he knows best.

He curls up next to her.

He nudges her hand.

He lets her stroke his ears.

He purrs when she holds him close.

He does not demand that she remember him before he offers comfort.

Perhaps the most painful part of Camelia's condition is that forgetting is not always accompanied by a clear boundary between what she knows and what she has lost. A person can be familiar one moment and unrecognizable the next. A relationship can remain important even when she cannot retrieve the information that defines it.

Jake cannot prevent those episodes.

He cannot repair her memory by purring.

But he can make the moment less frightening.

And sometimes, after a nightmare or a lost name, Camelia looks down at the little white cat in her lap and begins to remember.

“Jake,” she whispers.

His blue eyes look up at her.

His ears turn toward her voice.

He answers with a soft meow.

And, for that moment, she knows him again.

**C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE:**

_Jake does not always wait for Camelia to remember that he is her brother._

_Sometimes, he simply comes and sits beside her until she does._
