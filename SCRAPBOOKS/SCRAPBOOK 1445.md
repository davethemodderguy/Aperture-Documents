# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### ANOMALOUS RESEARCH & RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-ARC-4C-COG-033

**STATUS:** ACTIVE / CRITICAL  
**SUBJECT:** SAFEHOUSE Residents — Collective Exposure  
**LOCATION:** UNKNOWN GRASSLAND / MISSING ELEVATOR FLOOR

---

# 1. INCIDENT SUMMARY

Following Bao's previous exposure to the anomalous elevator destination, she returned to the SAFEHOUSE elevator.

This time, she did not enter alone.

Bao brought the SAFEHOUSE residents with her.

The missing floor button was pressed.

The elevator opened.

The same grassland was present.

The same music.

The same distant birds.

The same apparently infinite horizon.

Bao led the group forward.

---

The following residents were present:

Bao simply told them:

> **"You need to see this."**

---

# 3. THE GRASSLAND

The environment remained unchanged.

The group walked for several minutes.

No structures appeared.

No roads.

No boundaries.

No visible source for the music.

Birdsong occasionally sounded from somewhere beyond the horizon.

The environment was peaceful.

Almost unnaturally so.

Eventually, Bao stopped.

Everyone else stopped with her.

Ahead was the same circular region.

The grass did not grow inside it.

Small two-dimensional doodles resembling flowers surrounded the perimeter.

And in the center—

A child.

---

# 4. COLLECTIVE RECOGNITION

The group initially reacted differently.

Some stared.

Some stepped forward.

Some immediately became confused.

Neri stopped moving entirely.

Jane's expression changed.

Alice tilted her head.

Neko stared at the child for several seconds.

Bao whispered:

> **BAO:**  
> "That's him."

Nobody answered.

Then the child became visible in greater detail.

White hair.

Folded wings.

A shattered halo.

Azure eyes.

And an arrow through his chest.

The reaction was immediate.

---

# 5. COGNITIVE CASCADE

The anomaly activated simultaneously across the group.

There was no warning.

No gradual onset.

No opportunity to resist.

Every exposed resident experienced an abrupt influx of autobiographical information.

Memories returned.

Conversations.

Rooms.

Meals.

Hallway encounters.

Medical incidents.

Arguments.

Quiet moments.

Questions.

Promises.

Mistakes.

Small interactions that had once seemed insignificant.

And one child.

The same child.

---

# 6. INDIVIDUAL RESPONSES

### NERI

Neri immediately collapsed to her knees.

Her glasses fell into the grass.

She stared at the child.

Her breathing became uneven.

She remembered.

Not fragments.

Everything.

She remembered the drawings.

The cafeteria.

The conversations.

The times she had sat beside him.

The times she had spoken to him.

Her expression became completely blank.

Then she whispered:

> **NERI:**  
> "...Jake."

---

### JANE DOE

Jane did not speak.

She simply stared.

Her memories returned with extraordinary clarity.

She remembered carrying him.

Talking to him.

Watching him read.

The small conversations.

The quiet companionship.

The things she had promised.

She remembered the birthday.

The apples.

The card.

Then she saw the arrow.

Jane's face went pale.

> **JANE:**  
> "...No."

She took one step forward.

Then stopped.

---

### ALICE

Alice began crying before she understood why.

She remembered the child.

She remembered forgetting him.

She remembered the elevator.

She remembered all the strange gaps in her own memories.

Her hands began shaking.

> **ALICE:**  
> "I remember."

She looked toward Bao.

> **ALICE:**  
> "I remember him."

---

### NEKO

Neko's reaction was different.

He immediately began attempting to reconstruct the missing data.

His mind rapidly connected:

The missing elevator floor.

The anomalous Stardust.

The missing records.

The astronomical absence.

The unexplained emotional anomalies.

The missing resident.

The engineering discrepancies.

His expression changed.

> **NEKO:**  
> "...Oh."

He stopped speaking.

For perhaps the first time in years, Neko had no technical explanation ready.

---

### MISS NEKO

Miss Neko remembered.

She looked at Neko.

Then at the child.

Then back at Neko.

Neither spoke.

She quietly took Neko's hand.

---

### NIKO

Niko remained motionless.

After several seconds:

> **NIKO:**  
> "He was here."

No one answered.

> **NIKO:**  
> "He was actually here."

---

### FROST

Frost remembered every interaction.

Every time she had seen him.

Every time she had spoken to him.

Every time she had assumed she would see him later.

She covered her mouth.

> **FROST:**  
> "...Bao."

Bao looked at her.

Frost began crying.

---

### NORA

Nora's memories returned all at once.

She remembered her relationship with him.

The conversations.

The food.

The quiet moments.

The times she had been around him without realizing how important those moments were.

She looked at the child.

Her voice became very small.

> **NORA:**  
> "...He was my friend."

A pause.

> **NORA:**  
> "Why did I forget?"

---

### AETERNA

Aeterna was not a permanent SAFEHOUSE resident and was not present during the initial group entry.

However, the memory phenomenon affected those physically present independently of anomalous status.

No resistance mechanism was observed.

---

The remaining residents experienced the same fundamental phenomenon.

---

# 8. THE MOMENT OF UNDERSTANDING

For several minutes, nobody moved.

Twenty residents stood around the circle.

Nobody needed to ask who the child was anymore.

Nobody needed to explain the missing floor.

Nobody needed to explain the Stardust.

The empty region in the sky.

The missing records.

The unexplained gaps.

The strange drawings.

The forgotten conversations.

The emotional weight that had remained after the memories disappeared.

Everything connected.

Neko looked toward Bao.

> **NEKO:**  
> "You were right."

Bao did not respond.

She was crying.

---

# 9. SECONDARY EFFECT

The cognitive exposure did not merely restore memories.

It restored **context.**

Residents remembered not only the existence of the child, but their relationships with him.

They remembered:

- what he liked;
    
- what frightened him;
    
- what he asked for;
    
- who he trusted;
    
- who he followed;
    
- who he called family;
    
- the times he needed help;
    
- the times nobody realized he needed it.
    

The memories were personal.

There was no single shared recording.

Each resident remembered their own version of him.

Together, those memories formed a complete picture.

---

# 10. THE CHILD

The child did not move.

He remained seated inside the grassless circle.

His wings remained folded.

His halo remained shattered.

His azure eyes remained open.

The arrow remained embedded in his chest.

He did not speak.

He did not acknowledge the residents.

He simply sat there.

Watching.

---

# 11. COGNITIVE OVERLOAD

The collective exposure eventually became too intense.

Residents began collapsing one after another.

Bao was the first.

Then Frost.

Then Neri.

Then Alice.

Then Jane.

The remainder followed.

No physical injury occurred.

The anomaly appeared to forcibly terminate consciousness once the recovered memories exceeded an unknown threshold.

The final resident to remain conscious was Neko.

He looked toward the child.

His voice was barely audible.

> **NEKO:**  
> "I'm sorry."

The child did not answer.

Neko collapsed.

---

# 12. RETURN TO SAFEHOUSE

All residents subsequently regained consciousness inside the SAFEHOUSE.

They were found in various locations.

Bedrooms.

Hallways.

The cafeteria.

The lounge.

Several were still unconscious when medical personnel arrived.

No resident reported remembering being transported back.

No elevator movement was recorded.

The missing floor button remained unchanged.

---

# 13. POST-EXPOSURE STATUS

The memories remained.

They did not disappear after waking.

For the first time since the disappearance, the residents possessed a complete recollection of the child.

They knew his name.

They remembered his presence.

They remembered what happened.

And, most importantly:

They remembered **themselves.**

What they had done.

What they had failed to do.

What they had said.

What they had not said.

The memories were no longer isolated fragments.

They were complete.

---

# 14. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

The missing floor is no longer classified solely as a spatial anomaly.

It is now confirmed to possess a **collective mnemonic function.**

The environment appears capable of restoring autobiographical memories associated with the missing individual.

The mechanism remains unknown.

The blue Stardust remains unexplained.

The grassland remains unexplained.

The doodled flowers remain unexplained.

The arrow remains unexplained.

Most importantly:

The child remains physically present within the anomalous environment.

Whether the child is alive, dead, dormant, conscious, or otherwise cannot currently be determined.

---

# CLOSING OBSERVATION

When Bao woke, she immediately looked toward the elevator.

Frost was already standing there.

Neri stood beside her.

Jane joined them.

Then Alice.

Then everyone else.

Nobody spoke.

The missing button was still there.

Blue Stardust still clung to its edges.

For once, nobody questioned what it meant.

They remembered.

All of them.

And somewhere beyond the elevator doors, in an endless field beneath an impossible sky, a small child remained sitting alone.

His halo was broken.

His wings were folded.

His eyes were still blue.

And the arrow was still there.
