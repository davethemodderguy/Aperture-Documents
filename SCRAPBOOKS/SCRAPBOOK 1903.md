## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora has recovered from the physical injuries sustained during the stairwell incident.

His cervical injury has healed sufficiently for him to resume ordinary movement, subject to medical clearance. He is no longer required to remain immobilized.

However, his communication behavior has not returned to its previous baseline.

Sora continues to refuse the replacement whiteboard.

Despite being provided with functional writing equipment, he will not use it as a substitute for his original board.

Instead, he repeatedly searches for the missing item.

Personnel have observed him checking his den, nearby furniture, shared spaces, and locations where he previously spent time.

He frequently repeats these searches, even when informed that the board has not yet been found.

The original whiteboard remains missing.

Its rear side bears Jake Akana's signature.

C.O.R.E. considers the continued search behavior significant. The board is both Sora's primary familiar communication aid and a sentimental possession associated with someone he trusts.

**Physical recovery has not resolved the distress associated with its disappearance.**

---

## 2. CURRENT MEDICAL STATUS

Sora's injuries from the stairwell fall have healed.

He is no longer subject to the previous strict immobilization instructions.

Nevertheless, his established conditions remain:

- Muteness resulting from severe vocal cord damage.
    
- Intense amnesia affecting personal identity and recognition.
    
- Extreme hemophobia.
    
- Dependence on nonverbal communication.
    
- Strong attachment to his original whiteboard.
    

The recovery of his neck injury must not be confused with a complete recovery from the incident.

His physical condition has improved.

His communication difficulties remain.

---

## 3. REPEATED SEARCH BEHAVIOR

Personnel have documented repeated instances of Sora looking for his original whiteboard.

His search pattern is generally consistent.

He checks his personal den first.

He then searches nearby areas, often looking beneath furniture or inspecting places where he previously left his belongings.

If the board is not found, he may move to another familiar location and repeat the process later.

Sometimes he appears to forget that he has already searched a particular area.

This is especially relevant given his severe amnesia.

The repeated searches may be influenced by both his attachment to the board and his difficulty retaining information about previous attempts.

C.O.R.E. cannot currently determine how much of the behavior is driven by emotional attachment, memory impairment, or both.

Personnel must not assume that repeated searching is deliberate stubbornness.

---

## 4. THE REPLACEMENT BOARD – CONSISTENT REFUSAL

The replacement whiteboard remains available.

It is clean, functional, and suitable for ordinary written communication.

Sora does not use it.

He may look at it briefly, but he does not appear willing to adopt it.

When personnel offer it, he generally turns away or resumes searching for his original.

The distinction is important.

Sora has not forgotten how to use a whiteboard.

He is refusing to use this particular replacement.

The original board has a history that the replacement cannot reproduce.

Jake gave it to him.

Jake signed its rear.

Sora carried it everywhere.

It accompanied him through the period in which he gradually began trusting SAFEHOUSE and its residents.

The replacement has the same practical function, but none of that shared history.

C.O.R.E. will not force Sora to accept it as equivalent.

## 5. SELECTED OBSERVATIONS

### Observation A – The Den

**Location:** Sora's personal den  
**Time:** 09:26

Sora was observed searching his den.

He checked the area where he normally kept his whiteboard.

He looked beneath his bedding, inspected the nearby floor, and checked the entrance.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Sora, are you looking for your board again?

Sora nodded.

**STAFF MEMBER:** We haven't found it yet.

Sora looked toward the staff member.

The replacement board was offered.

Sora immediately turned away.

He continued searching.

The staff member did not insist.

### Observation B – Jake's Signature

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE common area  
**Time:** 13:14

Jake approached Sora while he was searching.

**JAKE:** Still looking?

Sora nodded.

**JAKE:** We'll find it. I promise we'll keep looking.

Sora pointed toward Jake, then made a writing gesture.

Jake understood.

**JAKE:** You want the one I signed?

Sora nodded repeatedly.

Jake became quiet.

**JAKE:** The one with my signature on the back?

Sora nodded again.

Jake crouched beside him.

**JAKE:** I remember giving it to you.

Sora looked at him.

Jake smiled gently.

**JAKE:** I'm not going to forget that.

Sora continued searching afterward.

### Observation C – Mia's Reassurance

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7 corridor  
**Time:** 16:08

Mia found Sora checking an area he had already searched earlier.

**MIA:** Sora? Again?

Sora nodded.

**MIA:** You really miss that board, huh?

Sora looked at her.

Mia gestured toward the replacement board.

**MIA:** You could use this one until we find yours.

Sora shook his head.

**MIA:** Okay. We won't force you.

She sat beside him.

**MIA:** I'll help you look.

Neither child spoke for some time.

## 6. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

The original whiteboard appears to have become a significant emotional anchor for Sora.

Its importance extends beyond communication.

It represents continuity, familiarity, and a personal connection with Jake.

For someone with severe amnesia, objects associated with trusted individuals may carry particular significance.

However, C.O.R.E. cannot establish that this is the sole explanation for his behavior.

His memory impairment may also contribute to the repeated searches, particularly if he forgets where he has already looked or what personnel have told him.

The following conclusions are supported by observation:

1. Sora strongly prefers his original whiteboard.
    
2. He consistently refuses the replacement.
    
3. He repeatedly searches for the missing board.
    
4. Jake's signature is an important identifying feature of the original.
    
5. His inability to communicate verbally makes the loss especially consequential.
    

His refusal must not be treated as defiance.

---

## 7. UPDATED DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

**A. Recovery of the original**

The search for Sora's original whiteboard remains active.

Personnel must inspect relevant shared spaces and storage areas.

**B. No forced replacement**

Sora is not to be pressured into using the replacement board.

It may remain available without being repeatedly offered.

**C. Communication alternatives**

Staff should continue using simple gestures, familiar visual references, and patient observation to understand Sora's immediate needs.

A secondary communication system may be developed with his participation when he is comfortable.

**D. Search assistance**

Personnel may accompany Sora during reasonable searches, but should prevent unsafe behavior or unnecessary exhaustion.

**E. Memory support**

If Sora forgets that a location has already been checked, staff should gently redirect him without expressing frustration.

**F. Personal significance**

If the original board is recovered, it must be returned to Sora after inspection and cleaning, provided it is safe to do so.

Jake's signature on the rear must be preserved.

## 8. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE – THE ONE HE WANTS BACK

Sora is physically better.

His neck has healed.

He can walk around SAFEHOUSE again.

He can spend time with Jake and Mia.

He can return to his little den.

But he still looks for his whiteboard.

Everywhere.

He checks places where it might be.

He checks places where he has already looked.

He refuses the new one.

We have offered him something that performs exactly the same function.

He doesn't want it.

Perhaps someone unfamiliar with Sora would consider this unreasonable.

Why refuse a perfectly good replacement?

Because the old one isn't just a whiteboard.

Jake gave it to him.

Jake signed it.

Sora carried it around like a little bag, taking it wherever he went.

It was one of the few possessions that remained familiar even when his memories failed him.

And now it is gone.

We cannot tell him that the new one is just as good and expect him to agree.

We cannot demand that he move on.

And we certainly cannot treat his inability to speak as permission to make decisions for him without considering what he wants.

So we will keep looking.

Jake has offered to help.

Mia has offered to help.

Bao has made certain that Sora is not left searching alone.

Perhaps we will find it tomorrow.

Perhaps it is somewhere nobody has thought to check.

But when we do find it, we should remember something.

The first thing Sora may want to do is turn it over.

Not to write.

Not to draw.

Just to see the signature on the back.

**Sometimes, getting something back means getting back a little piece of familiarity that nothing else can replace.**

— **C.O.R.E. Residential Welfare Division**
