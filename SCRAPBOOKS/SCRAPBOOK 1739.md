# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake no longer requires intravenous support.

His immediate need for IV-administered fluids and other prescribed interventions has subsided sufficiently for the medical team to discontinue the treatment.

This is a positive development.

It is not, however, evidence of psychological recovery.

Jake remains profoundly withdrawn, persistently fearful, physically subdued, and largely uninterested in activities that previously brought him comfort.

Two additional changes have been documented.

**First:** Jake's eyes have stopped glowing.

**Second:** His teddy bear's heartbeat has become exceptionally weak.

The relationship between these two observations, Jake's current emotional state, and his anomalous physiology remains unknown.

C.O.R.E. cannot currently establish whether the changes indicate continued recovery, a reduction in anomalous activity, or a new medical concern.

The subject remains under observation.

---

# 1. DISCONTINUATION OF IV SUPPORT

The medical team discontinued Jake's IV after reassessing his immediate clinical needs.

The pole that had accompanied him through the SAFEHOUSE is no longer part of his daily routine.

For the first time in several days, Jake can move around without dragging medical equipment behind him.

He can sit at a table.

He can walk to the cafeteria.

He can drink water without needing to maneuver around an IV line.

He can return to his room if he chooses.

In practical terms, several small inconveniences have disappeared.

Jake's response has been almost nonexistent.

He did not celebrate.

He did not ask whether he was better.

He did not appear particularly relieved.

When a medical staff member explained that the IV was no longer necessary, Jake listened.

Then he nodded.

That was all.

The equipment was removed.

Jake remained seated.

For several minutes, he continued looking at the empty space where the pole had been.

Eventually, he stood up and walked away.

There was no indication that he understood the development as a milestone.

Or that he cared.

### Medical clarification

The discontinuation of IV support means only that the treatment is no longer required under the current clinical assessment.

It does not establish that Jake has returned to his previous physical baseline.

His recent severe hypothermia, anomalous internal cold, persistent tremors, and unusual cardiac-vessel behavior remain relevant to his ongoing care.

Any recurrence of concerning symptoms requires reassessment.

**Treatment has been reduced. Monitoring has not ended.**

---

# 2. OCULAR OBSERVATION — AZURE EMISSION ABSENT

Jake's eyes no longer emit visible light.

The azure glow previously observed in his irises has disappeared.

The shooting-star patterns that once moved through them are no longer visibly luminous.

His eyes retain their blue coloration.

However, the celestial illumination that had made them resemble Aeterna's is absent.

No abrupt behavioral reaction accompanied the change.

Jake did not appear frightened when it happened.

He did not seek assistance.

He did not ask whether something was wrong.

The first confirmed observation occurred when Neri encountered him sitting at the cafeteria table.

She stopped beside him.

Looked at his face.

Then leaned slightly closer.

Jake looked up.

Neri pointed at his eyes.

Jake blinked.

**NERI:**  
"What?"

Neri pointed again.

**JAKE:**  
"My eyes?"

She nodded.

Jake looked toward the darkened window beside the table.

His reflection looked back at him.

White hair.

Blue eyes.

No glow.

He stared for several seconds.

Then he lowered his gaze to his book.

**JAKE:**  
"Oh."

Neri remained beside the table.

She appeared to be waiting for him to say something else.

He did not.

Eventually, she sat down at the opposite end.

Neither spoke again.

### C.O.R.E. Assessment

The absence of ocular emission is confirmed.

Its significance is not.

Jake's eyes previously displayed an anomalous visual phenomenon, but the disappearance of that phenomenon does not independently establish that his underlying stellar nature has changed.

No conclusion should be drawn regarding the loss of his abilities, the stability of his manifestation, or his continued existence as a literal star.

C.O.R.E. has authorized non-invasive observation and clinically indicated examinations.

Unnecessary testing is prohibited.

Jake is not to be subjected to repeated examinations simply because his appearance has changed.

---

# 3. THE TEDDY BEAR

**Finding: Cardiac activity remains detectable but is markedly diminished.**

Jake's teddy bear continues to function as his cardiac vessel.

Its heartbeat has become very weak.

The rhythm is difficult to perceive without appropriate monitoring, and the amplitude of the detected activity is substantially below Jake's previously recorded baseline.

This finding has prompted renewed concern.

The bear is not an ordinary plush toy.

Its heartbeat corresponds to Jake's own cardiac activity.

The connection is not merely symbolic.

Jake feels through the bear.

The bear's condition therefore cannot be dismissed as a superficial change in an otherwise replaceable possession.

During the latest examination, the medical team assessed the bear without separating it from Jake.

The heartbeat remained present.

Weak.

But present.

No confirmed explanation has been established.

Potential interpretations include:

- a change in Jake's anomalous cardiac physiology;
    
- an alteration in the relationship between his physical manifestation and his cardiac vessel;
    
- a consequence of his recent physiological crisis;
    
- or an unknown mechanism unrelated to his emotional state.
    

The current evidence does not establish which explanation is correct.

In particular, C.O.R.E. refuses to classify the diminished heartbeat as a direct consequence of Jake's depression without supporting evidence.

The observation is concerning enough on its own.

It does not require an emotional explanation to justify further attention.

---

# 4. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake has not volunteered an explanation for the weakened heartbeat.

When asked whether he has noticed a difference, he looks down at the bear.

Sometimes he touches one of its ears.

Sometimes he rests a hand against its body.

On one occasion, he held it against his chest for nearly twenty minutes.

He did not appear to be attempting to activate anything.

He simply held it.

A medical staff member eventually approached and asked whether he wanted the bear examined again.

Jake's arms tightened around it.

**JAKE:**  
"No."

The staff member stopped.

**MEDICAL STAFF:**  
"All right. We won't take it away."

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:**  
"Promise?"

**MEDICAL STAFF:**  
"We'll examine it with you here. We won't separate you from it without a medical necessity."

Jake watched the staff member for several seconds.

Then he looked down.

He did not say thank you.

He did not relax.

But he stopped pulling the bear closer.

The examination was postponed until the clinician could explain the proposed procedure and obtain his cooperation.

No one attempted to take the bear from him by force.

### Welfare note

Jake's refusal must not automatically be interpreted as defiance.

The bear is his cardiac vessel, a sensory extension, and one of his most enduring sources of familiarity.

Given his recent experiences, the prospect of someone taking it away may represent a threat substantially greater than personnel realize.

Medical necessity may occasionally require intervention.

Where possible, such intervention should be explained in advance, performed with Jake present, and designed to preserve the connection between him and the bear.

---

# 5. PHYSICAL CONDITION WITHOUT THE IV

Jake's movements have become easier to observe now that he is no longer attached to an infusion line.

Unfortunately, the absence of the equipment has not made him appear substantially more like himself.

He still moves slowly.

He still spends long periods sitting in one place.

He still rarely initiates interaction.

He still appears frightened when someone approaches without warning.

He still has difficulty accepting assistance.

And he still behaves as though remaining unnoticed is safer than asking for anything.

There have been no meaningful signs of renewed interest in his previous hobbies.

Books remain available.

Drawing materials remain available.

His plush toys remain where he left them.

The cafeteria continues serving food he likes.

Nobody has removed his belongings.

Nobody has prohibited him from returning to his room.

Yet he often sits without using any of them.

When asked whether he wants a book, he shakes his head.

When asked whether he wants to draw, he looks away.

When asked whether he wants company, he sometimes does not respond.

These observations do not prove that he has permanently lost interest in the things he once loved.

They demonstrate that, at present, those things are not reaching him in the way they once did.

The distinction matters.

Personnel are not to turn every former interest into an assignment.

Jake does not need to demonstrate recovery by reading a book, drawing a flower, playing with a toy, or smiling at someone.

---

# 6. THE CAFETERIA

Jake continues to spend substantial amounts of time in the cafeteria.

He does not necessarily sit with anyone.

Sometimes he occupies the same table he used while receiving IV support.

Sometimes he chooses a different seat.

Sometimes he falls asleep with his head resting on his arms.

The difference is that he is no longer attached to an IV pole.

He is simply there.

A recent observation recorded Jake sitting at the table with his teddy bear beside him.

His eyes were no longer glowing.

The bear's heartbeat was barely detectable.

Jake watched the other residents eat and talk.

He did not join them.

At one point, Alice passed his table carrying a plate of food.

She slowed.

**ALICE:**  
"Hey, Jake."

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:**  
"Hi."

Alice glanced at the bear.

Then back at him.

She did not ask about his eyes.

She did not ask whether he was feeling better.

She placed a small portion of food on the table, leaving enough room for him to move it if he did not want it.

**ALICE:**  
"I'm sitting over there."

She pointed toward another table.

**ALICE:**  
"You don't have to come."

Jake stared at the food.

Alice walked away.

He did not eat immediately.

Several minutes later, he moved the plate closer.

He ate a little.

Alice did not turn around to check whether he had done so.

No one recorded this as a breakthrough.

It was a meal.

At present, that was enough.

# 7. CLOVER'S OBSERVATION

Clover was informed of the changes.

She asked whether the IV had been discontinued because Jake was getting better.

The medical team explained the limited meaning of that decision.

She then asked about his eyes.

Finally, she asked about the bear.

She was quiet for a long time after receiving the answer.

Later, she found Jake sitting on a bench outside the medical ward.

She did not sit immediately.

She stopped where he could see her.

**CLOVER:**  
"Can I sit here?"

Jake looked at the empty space beside him.

Then nodded.

Clover sat down, leaving a small gap between them.

For several minutes, neither spoke.

Eventually, she glanced at the bear.

**CLOVER:**  
"Does it hurt?"

Jake's fingers moved over one of the bear's paws.

**JAKE:**  
"I don't know."

Clover waited.

**JAKE:**  
"It's just quiet."

Clover did not ask what he meant.

She did not tell him that the bear was still beating, that his eyes might glow again, or that everything would return to normal.

She could not promise any of those things.

Instead, she looked ahead.

**CLOVER:**  
"Okay."

Jake remained beside her.

His eyes did not glow.

The bear's heartbeat remained weak.

Neither of them spoke again.

Clover stayed until Jake stood up.

She did not ask him to return to their room.

She did not ask him to stay with her.

She simply stood when he did and allowed him to decide where to go.

# 8. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE FINDINGS

C.O.R.E. has specifically reviewed the possibility that Jake's psychological deterioration, absent ocular glow, and diminished cardiac activity represent a single underlying phenomenon.

The possibility cannot be excluded.

It has not been demonstrated.

The three observations must therefore remain distinct in the medical record.

|Observation|Confirmed finding|Unknown|
|---|---|---|
|IV support|No longer required under current assessment|Whether overall physical recovery is complete|
|Ocular emission|Azure glow and visible shooting-star illumination absent|Whether the change is temporary, permanent, or functionally significant|
|Teddy bear|Cardiac activity remains detectable but markedly weak|Cause, prognosis, and relationship to Jake's other changes|
|Psychological state|Severe withdrawal and diminished engagement continue|Extent of recovery and underlying clinical formulation|

Personnel are reminded that Jake is both a child-shaped physical manifestation and an anomalous stellar entity.

Neither aspect invalidates the other.

His cosmic nature does not make his physical condition irrelevant.

His physical appearance does not make the teddy bear an ordinary object.

And his psychological state must not be used as a substitute explanation for unexplained physiological changes.

---

# 9. MEDICAL RESPONSE

The Medical Division has authorized continued monitoring.

The current response includes:

- monitoring the teddy bear's cardiac activity against Jake's previous baseline;
    
- reassessing Jake if the heartbeat becomes undetectable, weakens further, or changes abruptly;
    
- monitoring for recurrent hypothermia, unusual cold, tremors, weakness, altered responsiveness, or other concerning symptoms;
    
- documenting changes in his eyes without repeatedly exposing him to unnecessary examinations;
    
- maintaining access to food, water, rest, and medical attention;
    
- and explaining clinically necessary procedures before they are performed whenever circumstances permit.
    

Jake's IV has been discontinued.

His medical follow-up has not.

Any sudden deterioration requires immediate clinical evaluation.

If the teddy bear's heartbeat becomes undetectable, personnel must not assume Jake is safe merely because he remains conscious or appears physically unchanged.

Given the established connection between the bear and Jake's cardiac activity, this would constitute an urgent anomalous medical event.

---

# 10. RESIDENT WELFARE DIRECTIVE

Personnel must not tell Jake:

> "At least you don't need the IV anymore."

They must not tell him:

> "Your eyes are normal now."

They must not tell him:

> "The bear is still beating, so everything is fine."

Each statement would imply a degree of certainty that C.O.R.E. does not possess.

They must also avoid treating the disappearance of the glow as a reason to stop paying attention to him.

Jake's distress was never established by the brightness of his eyes.

His wellbeing cannot be measured by whether they illuminate a room.

Likewise, the bear's weak heartbeat must not become a reason to surround Jake with personnel, demand answers, or subject him to constant questioning.

Monitoring should be medically appropriate, discreet, and consistent.

He should be informed about changes that affect his care.

He should be allowed to refuse nonessential interaction.

He should not have to repeatedly prove that he is frightened before people take his condition seriously.

Most importantly, **the removal of medical equipment must not become the removal of medical attention.**

---

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake has made one measurable change in his medical care.

He no longer requires IV support.

That is real progress in one specific area.

But he remains deeply withdrawn.

His eyes no longer glow.

His teddy bear's heartbeat is dangerously weak by comparison with its previous baseline, although activity remains detectable.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet determine whether these changes are connected.

It would be irresponsible to declare that Jake is recovering simply because one treatment has ended.

It would be equally irresponsible to conclude that the loss of his glow means he is losing his identity or his existence.

For now, the medical findings remain uncertain.

The welfare findings are less ambiguous.

Jake is still frightened.

Still quiet.

Still reluctant to ask for help.

Still carrying the same teddy bear.

And still behaving as though he must endure whatever happens to him without expecting anyone to intervene.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The IV pole is gone.

The tubing is gone.

The small machine that accompanied Jake around the SAFEHOUSE is no longer there to announce that he needs medical attention.

To someone passing through the cafeteria, he may now look like an ordinary little boy sitting alone with a teddy bear.

White hair.

Blue eyes.

No celestial glow.

No equipment.

Nothing visibly extraordinary.

Until someone notices how carefully he holds the bear.

Until someone listens closely enough to realize that its heartbeat is barely there.

Jake used to carry a little piece of the night sky in his eyes.

Now those eyes are quiet.

His bear still beats.

Weakly.

And Jake, who has spent so long being afraid that everyone will forget him, has not asked anyone to listen.

He simply keeps the bear beside him.

Perhaps because he knows it is still there.

Perhaps because he is afraid of what it would mean if it stopped.

C.O.R.E. does not know.

For now, the heartbeat remains detectable.

For now, Jake remains here.

**Neither fact is a guarantee. Both deserve our attention.**

— **C.O.R.E. Medical & Resident Welfare Division**
