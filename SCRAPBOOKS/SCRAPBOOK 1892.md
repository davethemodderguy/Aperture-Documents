---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana spent the entire night crying.

The distress followed the discovery of Sora outside the Blacksite, where the latter was found beneath a tree, shivering and sobbing while holding a collar bearing the name "Mia."

The discovery provided further insight into Sora's traumatic history, including the loss of his grandmother, the death of his younger sister, and years of mistreatment.

Jake was particularly affected by two things:

- The realization of how much Sora had endured before arriving at the Blacksite.
    
- The apparent collapse of the trust Sora had gradually developed with him.
    

Jake had spent considerable time building a relationship with Sora. He repeatedly visited him when Sora was still living in his maintenance locker, communicated with him through letters, and eventually helped him settle into SAFEHOUSE.

For a considerable period, Sora had become almost inseparable from Jake.

Now, Sora runs away when people approach him.

Jake appears unable to reconcile these two realities.

---

## 2. OVERNIGHT OBSERVATIONS

Jake cried throughout the night.

His distress was not limited to the circumstances of Sora's recent discovery. He was also grieving the things Sora had experienced long before either of them met.

The information concerning Mia appears to have been especially difficult for him.

Sora had lost his grandmother, then his little sister, and subsequently the home and life they had shared.

Jake now understands that the frightened boy he met had already spent years surviving without the people he loved.

The second source of distress concerned their relationship.

Jake had watched Sora gradually become more comfortable around him.

There had been letters. There had been visits. There had been a shared home on Floor 7.

Sora had eventually become comfortable enough to stay by Jake's side almost constantly.

Then one frightening incident caused him to disappear.

And when the search team finally found him, Sora ran again.

Jake's distress appears to center on a question that has no simple answer:

_How could something we spent so long building break so quickly?_

---

## 3. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. identifies several overlapping sources of distress.

### 3.1 Grief for Sora's Past

Jake is grieving experiences he did not personally witness.

He is imagining the childhood Sora and Mia lost, the cruelty inflicted upon them, and the years Sora spent surviving without his family.

This is an understandable emotional response, but personnel must ensure that Jake does not become consumed by the belief that he must somehow compensate for every terrible thing Sora experienced.

He cannot give Sora his grandmother back.

He cannot bring Mia back.

He cannot rewrite the years before their meeting.

That does not make his presence meaningless.

### 3.2 Perceived Collapse of Trust

Jake appears to interpret Sora's flight as evidence that the trust between them has been broken.

This interpretation requires careful correction.

Trust is not necessarily erased when someone becomes frightened.

Sora's history includes the destruction of his family by someone who had access to the very place he considered home. His experiences may have conditioned him to associate unexpected human approaches with danger.

The incident on Floor 7 may have triggered an overwhelming response that temporarily prevented him from distinguishing safety from threat.

Sora running away does not establish that he stopped caring about Jake.

It does not establish that he regrets their friendship.

It does not establish that their time together meant nothing.

**Fear can overpower the ability to approach someone without eliminating the affection that person has developed.**

### 3.3 Jake's Own Emotional Needs

Jake has a tendency to prioritize other people's wellbeing above his own.

In this situation, that tendency presents a particular risk.

He may begin believing that he should have done more, noticed more, or somehow prevented Sora's disappearance.

C.O.R.E. explicitly rejects this conclusion.

Jake was Sora's friend. He was not responsible for repairing the consequences of years of abuse.

He should not be expected to become Sora's sole source of safety.

He is six years old.

He deserves to be cared for, too.

---

## 4. CONCERNS RAISED BY SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTS

Aries, Tom, Clover, Phoenix, and Bao remain concerned about Jake.

Their involvement should focus on providing emotional support without making his every interaction revolve around Sora.

**Aries Akana:** Should reinforce that Jake is not responsible for the outcome of the search or for Sora's emotional recovery.

**Tom Akana:** Should remain attentive to Jake's physical wellbeing, particularly his sleep, hydration, and meals.

**Clover:** Should offer ordinary companionship and familiar activities without requiring Jake to discuss his feelings.

**Phoenix:** Should assist with coordinating support while avoiding unnecessary pressure or promises concerning Sora's recovery.

**Bao:** Should provide maternal reassurance and ensure Jake does not neglect his own basic needs.

Personnel are reminded that Jake should not be told to stop crying, move on, or simply wait patiently.

He is allowed to miss his friend.

---

## 5. RESIDENTIAL GUIDANCE

The following measures are authorized:

1. Jake must not be pressured to participate in Sora's recovery operation.
    
2. Sora's Den must remain available.
    
3. Jake may leave personal messages for Sora, provided he is not encouraged to wait indefinitely for a response.
    
4. Personnel must not promise Jake that Sora will return on a particular date.
    
5. Jake should be offered meals, warm drinks, rest, and familiar companionship.
    
6. His distress should be acknowledged rather than dismissed.
    
7. If Jake wishes to talk about Sora or Mia, personnel should listen without trying to immediately resolve his feelings.
    

A particularly important instruction:

**Do not tell Jake that he needs to earn Sora's trust again.**

He has not committed an offense.

He does not need to prove that he is safe.

The responsibility for recovery must not be placed upon a six-year-old friend.

---

## 6. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake spent the night crying for someone who wasn't there.

He cried about a little boy who lost his grandmother.

He cried about a little girl named Mia, whose collar was the only thing we saw Sora holding when we found him beneath that tree.

And he cried about himself.

About the letters.

About the visits.

About the little den on Floor 7.

About how Sora used to follow him everywhere.

About how something that took so long to build seemed to disappear in a single frightened moment.

Perhaps Jake believes that if he had been kinder, more patient, or more careful, Sora would not have run.

We need to make certain he understands something.

**Sora's fear is older than their friendship.**

Jake did not create that fear. He did not cause the years of suffering that came before him. And he cannot be expected to undo them through affection alone.

The time they spent together was real.

The letters were real.

The trust Sora showed him was real.

One frightened escape does not retroactively turn those moments into lies.

And if Sora eventually comes back, Jake should not have to greet him with an apology for failing to keep him.

He should be allowed to greet his friend.

For now, let Jake cry if he needs to.

Let Tom sit beside him. Let Bao bring him something warm. Let Aries remind him that he is still someone's little brother, and that he does not have to be strong tonight.

We have spent so much time worrying about whether Sora can find his way home.

We must not overlook the little boy who is still waiting for him.

**Jake deserves to feel that he is not being left behind, too.**

— **C.O.R.E. Residential Welfare Division**
