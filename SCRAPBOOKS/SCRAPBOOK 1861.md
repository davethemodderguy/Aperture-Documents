## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

For seven consecutive days, the same distressing sequence occurred.

Tom Akana departed SAFEHOUSE to attend to important commitments. Jake Akana, left without his preferred companion, attempted to find other residents willing to spend time with him. The residents were occupied, and Jake repeatedly found himself alone.

His response followed an increasingly dangerous pattern. After prolonged periods of isolation and emotional distress, Jake deliberately ended his own life. His Transcendence subsequently reconstructed his body, and Tom returned to find him alive.

The first incident prompted an urgent welfare investigation. The following incidents should have prompted a fundamental change in how the household managed Jake's safety.

Instead, the pattern repeated.

Each day, Tom left. Each day, Jake struggled with the absence of companionship. Each day, another fatal incident occurred, followed by resurrection and Tom's return.

By the seventh day, the household's understanding of Jake's Transcendence was challenged in a way nobody had anticipated.

Tom returned to SAFEHOUSE and found Jake apparently dead.

The white fire did not manifest.

Jake remained unresponsive for five hours.

Tom initially believed his brother had finally failed to resurrect. After repeated attempts to get a response, he eventually poked Jake.

That physical contact provided sufficient stimulation to trigger the dormant resurrection mechanism.

White fire appeared. Jake was reconstructed and returned to life.

The discovery established two critical facts:

1. **Jake can remain dead even though his resurrection ability is still available.**
    
2. **His resurrection mechanism may require physical stimulation to activate. It does not necessarily initiate automatically at the moment of death.**
    

The exact threshold, type, and minimum intensity of stimulation required remain unknown. Tom's poke was sufficient on this occasion, but it is not evidence that every touch will trigger resurrection.

The medical implications are significant. Previous observations had led C.O.R.E. to treat Jake's resurrection as an automatic response to fatal injury. That assumption is no longer supported.

Jake's ability may be capable of reconstructing him from death, but it apparently does not guarantee that the process will begin without an external stimulus.

**The seven-day pattern also constitutes a severe and repeated welfare failure.** Jake's resurrection is not a substitute for intervention in an ongoing suicidal crisis, and Tom cannot be expected to prevent it alone.

## 2. INCIDENT CHRONOLOGY — DAYS ONE THROUGH SIX

### 2.1 The pattern becomes routine

The first incident had been documented as an acute emotional crisis. The residents involved expressed regret, and corrective actions were proposed.

However, the underlying circumstances remained largely unchanged.

Tom continued to have obligations that required him to leave SAFEHOUSE. Jake continued to struggle when his brother was absent. The other residents continued to have work, responsibilities, and periods when they could not provide companionship.

The household had learned that Jake was vulnerable to loneliness.

It had not yet built a sufficiently reliable system to keep him safe when that loneliness became overwhelming.

On each of the following six days, the sequence repeated in broadly similar form:

- Tom departed for an important commitment.
    
- Jake sought companionship from other residents.
    
- Residents were unavailable or unable to provide the company he wanted.
    
- Jake withdrew as his distress increased.
    
- Jake deliberately ended his life.
    
- His Transcendence reconstructed his body, and he returned to life.
    
- Tom returned and discovered what had happened.
    

The precise timing varied. The emotional pattern did not.

The incidents were not treated as ordinary accidents. C.O.R.E. recorded them as repeated suicidal behavior requiring immediate, sustained intervention.

Jake's apparent recovery after each resurrection created a dangerous illusion of resolution. Once the white fire subsided and he could speak, residents tended to focus on his physical recovery and the relief of having him back.

But the circumstances that led to the crisis remained present.

**The ability to recover from an attempt does not mean the underlying risk has resolved.**

### 2.2 Tom's increasingly difficult departures

Tom was aware that Jake had become distressed by his absences.

He tried to reassure him before leaving, promised to return, and made efforts to keep their time together predictable.

Those efforts were not enough.

Tom was not deliberately abandoning his brother. His commitments were important, and he could not reasonably be expected to remain at Jake's side every hour of every day.

Nevertheless, learning that Jake repeatedly harmed himself during his absence placed Tom under an immense emotional burden.

By the sixth day, Tom was visibly distressed before leaving.

**TOM:** "I'll be back tonight, okay?"

**JAKE:** "Okay."

**TOM:** "And if you feel bad, you tell someone."

**JAKE:** "I will."

Tom hesitated.

**TOM:** "Jake, I'm serious."

**JAKE:** "I know."

**TOM:** "You can call me. I don't care what I'm doing. If you need me, you call."

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:** "Okay, Tom."

Tom hugged him before departing.

Jake hugged him back.

Neither could have known how the following hours would unfold.

The incident records do not establish exactly what Jake was thinking during the final hours of the sixth day. They do establish that the previous pattern had not been interrupted and that the existing safeguards were inadequate.

C.O.R.E. takes responsibility for that failure of intervention.

## 3. DAY SEVEN — THE ABSENCE OF WHITE FIRE

**Time of discovery:** Approximately 00:00  
**Time of resurrection:** Approximately 05:00  
**Duration of apparent death:** Approximately five hours

Tom returned to SAFEHOUSE at midnight.

He had spent the journey home thinking about Jake. He expected to find him awake, waiting, or perhaps already asleep.

Instead, he found Jake unresponsive.

Tom immediately recognized that something was wrong.

**TOM:** "Jake?"

There was no answer.

He approached and tried to rouse him.

**TOM:** "Hey. I'm home."

Jake did not respond.

Tom checked again, calling his brother's name several times. He attempted to obtain help and followed the available emergency procedures.

There was no white fire.

No visible reconstruction.

No familiar moment when Jake returned and asked what had happened.

Only silence.

**TOM:** "Jake, come on."

He waited.

**TOM:** "I'm back. You can wake up now."

Nothing.

Tom had witnessed his brother die and return more times than he could bear to count. He had come to recognize the white fire as the terrible, miraculous signal that Jake would be with him again.

Now there was no signal.

The minutes passed.

Then an hour.

Then another.

Tom remained beside Jake.

The medical personnel who attended the scene could not identify any sign that the resurrection mechanism had begun. They could not promise Tom that it would.

For five hours, Jake remained dead.

### 3.1 Tom's response

Tom's distress grew as the night continued.

He had been told that Jake's Transcendence reconstructed his body after death. The previous six days had reinforced the belief that resurrection would follow every fatal incident.

He had never been told that the process could remain inactive.

He had never been told that Jake might remain dead for hours despite possessing the ability to return.

And nobody had warned him that he might have to sit beside his brother, uncertain whether the next moment would bring white fire or a final silence.

**TOM:** "You always come back."

He said it more than once.

At some point, the statement stopped sounding like reassurance and began sounding like a plea.

**TOM:** "Jake, please."

He took Jake's hand.

**TOM:** "You don't have to do this. You don't have to stay here. You can come back."

No response.

Tom bowed his head.

**TOM:** "I came home. I'm here."

The room remained silent.

### 3.2 04:58 — The poke

After approximately five hours, Tom was exhausted and frightened. He had tried calling Jake, speaking to him, holding his hand, and gently attempting to rouse him.

Nothing had worked.

Then, almost impulsively, he poked Jake.

It was a small, physical stimulus.

A gesture so ordinary that under different circumstances it might have been used to annoy his brother into getting out of bed.

For a fraction of a second, nothing happened.

Then the air changed.

White fire ignited around Jake's body.

Tom recoiled in shock.

**TOM:** "...What?"

The fire spread around Jake without igniting the surrounding room. Its brightness filled the space, reflecting across Tom's wide, disbelieving eyes.

The reconstruction process began.

Tom stared.

**TOM:** "No way."

The flames intensified, then gradually collapsed inward.

Jake returned.

His body was reconstructed, but he did not immediately sit up. As in previous resurrections, he began coughing and struggled to breathe. Tom immediately called for medical assistance and stayed beside him.

Jake eventually managed to open his eyes.

**JAKE:** "...Tom?"

Tom could not answer at first.

He stared at his brother as though afraid that looking away would make him disappear again.

**JAKE:** "Why are you crying?"

Tom made a broken sound that was almost a laugh.

Then he grabbed Jake in a careful, desperate hug.

**TOM:** "YOU WERE DEAD FOR FIVE HOURS!"

Jake blinked slowly.

**JAKE:** "...Oh."

**TOM:** "FIVE HOURS, JAKE!"

**JAKE:** "I'm sorry."

**TOM:** "I THOUGHT YOU WEREN'T COMING BACK!"

Jake tried to breathe more comfortably before answering.

**JAKE:** "I'm here now."

**TOM:** "THAT IS NOT THE POINT!"

The words came out harshly, but Tom's arms remained wrapped around him.

He had spent five hours preparing himself for a possibility he could not accept.

Now Jake was alive again, coughing against his shoulder, and Tom was too relieved to do anything but hold him.

**JAKE:** "Tom?"

**TOM:** "What?"

**JAKE:** "You poked me."

Tom pulled back just enough to stare at him.

**TOM:** "YES, I POKED YOU!"

**JAKE:** "Why?"

**TOM:** "BECAUSE YOU WOULDN'T WAKE UP!"

Jake considered this.

**JAKE:** "...It worked."

Tom stared at him.

**TOM:** "I AM GOING TO NEED A VERY LONG NAP."

Jake gave a faint, exhausted smile.

**JAKE:** "Can I nap too?"

**TOM:** "After the doctors make sure you can breathe properly."

**JAKE:** "Okay."

Tom held him a little closer.

**TOM:** "Don't ever make me do that again."

Jake's smile disappeared.

He looked down.

**JAKE:** "...I don't know how to stop feeling like that."

Tom's anger vanished.

He drew Jake back into the embrace.

**TOM:** "Then we get help. Together. You don't have to figure it out by yourself."

For the first time that morning, Tom was not asking Jake to promise that everything would be fine.

He was telling him that they would not leave him to face it alone.

## 4. MEDICAL FINDING — RESURRECTION REQUIRES A TRIGGER

The seventh-day incident prompted an immediate review of all previous resurrection records.

Prior investigations had documented a consistent sequence: fatal injury, manifestation of white fire, full-body reconstruction, and return to life.

Because the sequence had occurred repeatedly, investigators had assumed that death itself was sufficient to activate the resurrection mechanism.

The five-hour delay disproved that assumption.

Jake remained dead despite the presumed availability of his Transcendence. The reconstruction process did not begin until Tom applied a physical stimulus by poking him.

The immediate transition from inactivity to visible resurrection strongly suggests that physical stimulation can activate the mechanism.

However, the observation does not establish the precise biological or anomalous pathway involved.

### 4.1 Preliminary activation model

The current model contains three distinct stages:

**Stage One: Death**

Jake's biological functions cease, and his existing cells are killed as part of the fatal event.

**Stage Two: Dormant resurrection**

The reconstruction mechanism remains available but inactive. Jake may remain dead for an undetermined period.

**Stage Three: Stimulus-triggered activation**

A physical stimulus appears to trigger the white fire and initiate reconstruction, after which Jake returns to life.

The model remains provisional. C.O.R.E. has not established whether the trigger must be tactile, whether a particular intensity is required, or whether other kinds of stimuli could activate the process.

Tom's poke is the only trigger directly implicated in the present incident.

It must not be assumed that every touch, sound, movement, or environmental change will produce the same result.

### 4.2 What remains unknown

The following questions remain unanswered:

- Does Jake's resurrection require physical stimulation every time, or only under certain circumstances?
    
- Is there a minimum intensity or duration required?
    
- Can the mechanism be activated by a medical intervention?
    
- Can the mechanism remain dormant indefinitely?
    
- Does the five-hour delay represent an unusual event or a previously overlooked possibility?
    
- Does the emotional or physical state of Jake before death affect the activation process?
    
- Is the reconstruction itself responsible for his recurring respiratory distress?
    

No deliberate lethal testing is authorized to answer these questions.

Jake's death and subsequent reconstruction have already provided sufficient reason for caution.

The investigation must proceed through nonlethal observation, review of existing records, and ethically approved assessments conducted while Jake is alive.

**Critical safety correction:** Staff must not assume that Jake will resurrect automatically after a fatal event. Emergency response must begin immediately, and the absence of white fire must never be interpreted as proof that the mechanism has permanently failed.

Nor may anyone rely on poking Jake as a guaranteed activation method.

It worked once.

That is not a protocol.

## 5. WHY THE DISCOVERY IS SO SERIOUS

The discovery changes the meaning of every previous incident.

Until now, residents had unconsciously treated Jake's resurrection as an inevitable consequence of death. Although C.O.R.E. repeatedly warned that resurrection was not equivalent to safety, the rapid recurrence of the white fire encouraged a dangerous assumption: no matter what happened, Jake would come back.

The seventh day demonstrated that this assumption was false.

Jake can remain dead.

His ability does not necessarily initiate reconstruction without a trigger.

And the people around him may not recognize that anything has changed until they have already spent hours believing he is gone.

The psychological consequences for Tom are substantial. His fear was already shaped by repeated exposure to Jake's deaths. The five-hour delay introduced an entirely new uncertainty: even after previous resurrections, he could no longer be certain that the next one would occur.

Tom must receive dedicated support following this incident. He cannot be expected to serve as Jake's sole caretaker, and he must not be assigned responsibility for activating the resurrection mechanism.

The same applies to every other resident.

**Nobody is to be told that Jake's life depends on being touched by a particular person.**

The available evidence establishes a possible activation requirement, not a new burden that should be placed on his family.

## 6. RESIDENTIAL WELFARE FINDINGS

The repeated incidents over seven days indicate that the original welfare intervention was inadequate.

The residents had been informed that Jake was struggling with loneliness. They had expressed concern and made good-faith efforts to improve his situation.

But the pattern continued.

The household's approach relied too heavily on the assumption that Jake would seek help when distressed, that someone would notice when he stopped asking, or that Tom's eventual return would be sufficient to restore his emotional stability.

None of these assumptions proved reliable.

Jake's repeated self-harm must be treated as an ongoing crisis, not as a sequence of isolated incidents resolved by resurrection.

### Required interventions

**Immediate clinical support**

Jake must receive continued professional assessment and care following the seventh-day incident. His current safety, emotional state, and access to means of self-harm must be addressed directly.

**Reliable companionship**

A structured, voluntary companionship plan must be established. Residents who are busy should help Jake identify another available person rather than leaving him to approach every household independently.

**A dependable absence plan**

When Tom must leave, Jake must know which trusted adults are available, how to reach them, and what to do if he begins feeling overwhelmed. This plan must not depend exclusively on Tom's availability.

**Regular check-ins**

A designated adult must check on Jake at agreed intervals, especially during periods when he is alone. These check-ins should be genuine conversations, not merely confirmation that he is physically present.

**Support for Tom**

Tom must receive emotional support and reassurance that he is not solely responsible for keeping Jake alive. His own needs must not be ignored simply because Jake's crisis is more immediately visible.

**Emergency response**

Any suspected self-harm, fatal injury, or unexpected unresponsiveness requires an immediate emergency response. Staff must not wait for white fire to appear.

**No lethal testing**

The newly discovered activation requirement must not be investigated by intentionally killing Jake, withholding assistance, or deliberately allowing him to remain dead.

The mechanism's uncertainty is a reason to protect him, not an invitation to experiment.

## 7. FINAL INTERVIEW — JAKE AND TOM

The following exchange occurred after Jake had been medically stabilized.

Tom remained beside him throughout the interview.

**INTERVIEWER:** "Jake, do you remember what happened before Tom returned?"

Jake looked down at the blanket covering him.

**JAKE:** "I remember being alone."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do you remember the resurrection?"

**JAKE:** "Not really. I remember waking up with Tom there."

**INTERVIEWER:** "You were unresponsive for approximately five hours."

Jake looked at Tom.

**JAKE:** "You stayed the whole time?"

Tom nodded.

**TOM:** "Of course I did."

**JAKE:** "You didn't sleep?"

**TOM:** "Jake."

**JAKE:** "Sorry."

Tom sighed.

**TOM:** "I'm not angry that you asked. I'm upset that you were hurting so much and I didn't know how to help you before it got this bad."

Jake pulled the blanket closer.

**JAKE:** "I didn't want to bother anyone."

**INTERVIEWER:** "What would you tell another person who felt the same way?"

Jake did not answer immediately.

**JAKE:** "I'd tell them they could play with me."

Tom's eyes filled with tears again.

**JAKE:** "Even if I was busy, I'd make time."

He paused.

**JAKE:** "Because I know how it feels when nobody can."

The interviewer allowed the silence to remain.

Then Tom spoke.

**TOM:** "You don't have to earn time with people by being cheerful, Jake."

Jake looked at him.

**TOM:** "You don't have to keep smiling so everyone knows you're okay. You don't have to say it's fine when it isn't."

**JAKE:** "But what if they're busy?"

**TOM:** "Then we find someone who can help. And if nobody is free right away, we make sure you aren't facing it alone."

**JAKE:** "Promise?"

**TOM:** "I promise we'll keep trying. And we'll get help when we need it."

Jake nodded.

He leaned against Tom's shoulder.

The interview concluded at the medical officer's recommendation.

No further questions were asked.

## 8. FINAL DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

1. Jake's repeated suicidal behavior is to be managed as an ongoing critical welfare concern.
    
2. All existing residential safety measures are to be reviewed for effectiveness, not merely recorded as completed.
    
3. Tom is not to be made solely responsible for Jake's supervision or resurrection.
    
4. Jake's resurrection mechanism is to be classified as **stimulus-dependent or stimulus-sensitive, with activation requirements undetermined**.
    
5. The five-hour delay must be included in all future medical assessments of Jake's Transcendence.
    
6. Medical and security personnel must initiate emergency procedures immediately following a suspected fatal incident, regardless of whether white fire appears.
    
7. No resident may attempt to reproduce the seventh-day event or test the trigger through intentional harm.
    
8. Jake and Tom are both to receive ongoing emotional support.
    
9. SAFEHOUSE must establish a reliable companionship and check-in system that remains functional when individual residents are unavailable.
    
10. The residential welfare review will remain open until the team can demonstrate that the repeated crisis pattern has been interrupted and appropriate safeguards are operating.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

For six days, Jake died and came back.

For six days, Tom returned to find his brother alive again.

For six days, the household was given another opportunity to understand that the crisis had not ended simply because Jake's body had been restored.

On the seventh day, the white fire did not come.

For five hours, Tom had no miracle to wait for.

He had only his brother's motionless body, the memory of every previous death, and the terrible possibility that this time there would be no return.

Then he poked Jake.

And the fire came.

It would be easy to record this as a successful discovery: a new trigger, a new mechanism, another piece of the puzzle surrounding Jake Akana's Transcendence.

That would be an incomplete account.

The discovery was made because a child repeatedly harmed himself after feeling profoundly alone, and because his brother spent five hours believing he might never see him open his eyes again.

No scientific explanation can make that acceptable.

No resurrection can erase what Tom endured.

No number of successful reconstructions can replace the companionship Jake needed before the crisis began.

We must understand the mechanism. We must document the risks. We must prepare for the possibility that resurrection will not begin automatically.

But above all, we must stop allowing a child to reach the point where death feels preferable to another night alone.

**Jake is not a resurrection experiment. Tom is not a failsafe. And the white fire is not a substitute for care.**

The next time Tom must leave, SAFEHOUSE must already have a plan.

The next time Jake feels that nobody wants him around, someone must be ready to listen.

And if Jake ever becomes unresponsive again, no one should have to wait five hours, wondering whether a small poke will bring him back.

_We were given seven days to recognize the pattern._

_We must not need an eighth to take it seriously._

— **C.O.R.E. Anomalous Physiology & Residential Welfare Divisions**
