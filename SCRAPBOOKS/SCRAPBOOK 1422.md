# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has been accidentally forgotten on several occasions.

Unlike the previous Nullwalker incident, these events have not resulted in prolonged exposure, severe deprivation, or significant medical deterioration.

The common factor is considerably simpler:

> **Someone assumes someone else is with Jake.**

Someone else assumes Jake went with somebody.

Someone else assumes Jake is already back.

Someone else assumes he is asleep.

Someone else assumes he is with Bao.

Someone else assumes he is with Anby.

And sometimes—

Nobody assumes anything at all.

Jake is simply left somewhere.

Jake does not appear particularly bothered by this.

In most documented cases, he simply finds somewhere comfortable, waits, reads, watches the surroundings, or entertains himself until someone eventually remembers him.

---

# 1. MOST RECENT INCIDENT — PERIMETER OUTPOST

Jake was accidentally left outside a Perimeter Security Outpost for approximately:

> **2 HOURS.**

The original group had been conducting routine business near the Blacksite perimeter.

Jake had been sitting on a bench outside the outpost.

At some point, everyone returned inside.

Nobody noticed Jake remained outside.

The door closed.

Two hours later, a Security Guard opened it again.

Jake was still sitting on the bench.

He was holding his teddy bear.

The guard froze.

> **GUARD:**  
> “Jake?”

Jake looked up.

He waved.

The guard stared.

> **GUARD:**  
> “You've been out here this whole time?”

Jake nodded.

> **GUARD:**  
> “Why didn't you come inside?”

Jake shrugged.

The guard looked increasingly concerned.

> **GUARD:**  
> “Were you cold?”

Jake shook his head.

> **GUARD:**  
> “Hungry?”

Shake.

> **GUARD:**  
> “Bored?”

Jake considered this.

Then shrugged again.

The guard eventually asked:

> **GUARD:**  
> “Are you okay?”

Jake gave a thumbs-up.

---

# 2. THE EXPLANATION

The guard eventually discovered that Jake had apparently spent most of the two hours reading.

He had brought a book with him.

He had also positioned himself where the afternoon sunlight reached the bench.

Jake had apparently decided that being forgotten outside was not particularly problematic.

When asked why he did not come looking for anyone, he simply pointed toward the book.

The interpretation was straightforward:

> **He was busy.**

# 12. PATTERN IDENTIFIED

C.O.R.E. has identified a consistent pattern.

Jake does not interpret accidental isolation in the same way most residents do.

When left somewhere, he does not immediately assume:

> **“They abandoned me.”**

Instead, he appears to assume:

> **“They'll come back.”**

This distinction is significant.

Jake has become accustomed to being quiet.

He does not always interpret someone's absence as rejection.

If he is comfortable and has something to occupy himself with, he may simply wait.

---

# 13. HOWEVER

This behavior should not be mistaken for evidence that leaving Jake alone indefinitely is acceptable.

His current attitude is considerably healthier than his previous tendency to interpret absence as rejection.

But Jake is still recovering.

He remains:

- physically weak;
    
- unable to speak;
    
- susceptible to fatigue;
    
- dependent on others for assistance;
    
- and vulnerable to medical complications.
    

Therefore:

> **Jake not minding being forgotten does not mean personnel should forget him.**

The two statements can coexist.

---

# 14. JAKE'S CURRENT ATTITUDE

When asked directly whether he minds being forgotten, Jake consistently indicates:

> **No.**

When asked whether he likes being alone:

> **Sometimes.**

When asked whether he wants someone to come back eventually:

> **Yes.**

This appears to be the important distinction.

Jake enjoys solitude.

He does not require constant attention.

But he still likes knowing that people will eventually return.

---

# 15. C.O.R.E. RECOMMENDATION

Personnel are not required to supervise Jake continuously.

Nor should they treat him as incapable of being alone.

Instead:

Before leaving an area, personnel should simply verify:

> **“Jake, are you coming?”**

If he shakes his head:

> **“Okay. We'll see you later.”**

And actually return later.

This allows Jake to maintain independence without repeating the circumstances that previously resulted in prolonged isolation.
