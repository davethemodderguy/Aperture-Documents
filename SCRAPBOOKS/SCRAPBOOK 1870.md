## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At 09:17 hours, Jake Akana resolved the Blacksite's longstanding debate concerning the superiority of waffles versus pancakes.

The debate had persisted for an undocumented period, involving personnel from multiple divisions, several increasingly unprofessional cafeteria polls, and at least one attempt to establish a formal ranking system for breakfast foods.

Previous arguments had relied on such criteria as texture, syrup retention, structural integrity, topping compatibility, preparation time, and whether a breakfast item could reasonably be classified as a dessert.

None had produced a lasting consensus.

Jake Akana resolved the matter in approximately eleven seconds.

His conclusion was:

**“WAFFLES ARE PANCAKES WITH LITTLE SYRUP POCKETS. BUT PANCAKES ARE WAFFLES THAT FORGOT TO GET THE POCKETS. SO THEY'RE BOTH CAKE. AND CAKE IS CAKE.”**

He then ate both.

The cafeteria fell silent.

A subsequent vote was conducted. Although the vote did not establish a unanimous preference for either food, it did establish that Jake's argument was sufficiently ridiculous, internally consistent, and difficult to refute that the original debate effectively collapsed.

C.O.R.E. has classified the incident as a **successful low-risk resolution of a recurring interpersonal dispute**.

No anomalous abilities were detected during the argument.

No deaths were reported.

Given the subject's recent history, Medical has requested that this fact be included in the executive summary.

## II. BACKGROUND — A WAR FOUGHT WITH BREAKFAST

The Blacksite is an institution dedicated to anomalous research, containment, security, and the preservation of personnel lives.

It is therefore understandable that its employees occasionally require an outlet for intellectual disagreement.

Unfortunately, the outlet in question had become breakfast.

The opposing positions were broadly defined as follows.

**THE WAFFLE POSITION**

Waffles were superior due to their crisp exterior, structured interior, and distinctive grid of depressions. Those depressions were considered essential for holding syrup, butter, fruit, and other toppings.

One unidentified Security employee reportedly described pancakes as “flat, structurally disappointing circles.”

**THE PANCAKE POSITION**

Pancakes were superior due to their softness, versatility, ease of preparation, and ability to accommodate toppings without requiring a dedicated architectural surface.

A member of Research described waffles as “breakfast that needs a construction permit.”

This remark resulted in a three-day argument over whether the waffle iron constituted an industrial fabrication device.

It was not the last argument.

The matter spread beyond the cafeteria. Polls appeared on internal message boards. Staff began referring to breakfast meetings as “the neutral territory.” At least one department head attempted to prohibit the discussion during working hours.

The prohibition was ignored.

By the morning of the incident, the debate had become less about food and more about the personal integrity of people who preferred the wrong food.

C.O.R.E. was preparing to issue a formal reminder that breakfast preferences were not grounds for workplace hostility.

Jake arrived first.

## III. INCIDENT CHRONOLOGY

### 09:12 — The Argument Resumes

The cafeteria was moderately occupied.

A table near the center of the room had become the unofficial headquarters of the waffle faction. A second table, several metres away, was occupied by pancake supporters.

Neither faction had chosen these locations deliberately.

Both claimed the other had invaded their territory.

Jake approached carrying a plate.

On it were one waffle and two pancakes.

Tom Akana was walking behind him.

**TOM:** “Why are you carrying both?”

**JAKE:** “Because I'm hungry.”

**TOM:** “That explains the food. It doesn't explain why you look like you're about to start a debate.”

**JAKE:** “I'm not starting a debate!”

Jake looked toward the two tables.

**JAKE:** “I'm going to finish one.”

Tom stopped walking.

**TOM:** “Jake, please don't make breakfast a medical incident.”

**JAKE:** “HOW WOULD I DO THAT?”

Tom considered his younger brother's recent record.

**TOM:** “I'm not answering that.”

Jake continued toward the tables.

### 09:14 — Initial Intervention

The argument was already underway.

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “The pockets are objectively better. Syrup stays where you put it.”

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “The pockets are just holes in your breakfast!”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “They're not holes. They're functional depressions.”

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “You just called breakfast a piece of engineering!”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “I work in Engineering!”

At this point, Jake placed his plate on the table.

Both sides stopped talking.

**JAKE:** “Can I ask something?”

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “Of course.”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “As long as it's relevant.”

Jake pointed at the waffle.

**JAKE:** “Is this a pancake?”

Silence.

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “No.”

Jake pointed at the pancakes.

**JAKE:** “Are these waffles?”

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “Obviously not.”

Jake nodded as though both answers had confirmed a critical hypothesis.

**JAKE:** “Okay. But they're both made of batter, right?”

Neither side answered immediately.

**JAKE:** “And they're both breakfast.”

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “Usually.”

**JAKE:** “And they're both better with syrup.”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “Correct.”

**JAKE:** “So what's the problem?”

The two factions began speaking at once.

Jake raised his hand.

**JAKE:** “WAIT! I HAVE A SOLUTION!”

Tom, who had arrived behind him, quietly sat down.

He knew that tone.

---

## IV. THE SOLUTION

**09:17 HOURS — TRANSCRIPT RECONSTRUCTION**

**JAKE:** “Waffles are pancakes with little syrup pockets.”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “That's an oversimplification.”

**JAKE:** “But pancakes are waffles that forgot to get the pockets.”

A pause.

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “That's not how cooking works.”

**JAKE:** “SO THEY'RE BOTH CAKE!”

The room fell silent.

Jake gestured at the plate.

**JAKE:** “They're both round-ish. They're both made of batter. They're both fluffy. You put sweet things on them. You eat them with a fork. Sometimes you put fruit on them. Sometimes you put chocolate on them.”

He paused to consider his own argument.

**JAKE:** “They're breakfast cake.”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “A waffle has a different texture.”

**JAKE:** “CAKE CAN HAVE DIFFERENT TEXTURES!”

**PANCAKE SUPPORTER:** “They're prepared differently.”

**JAKE:** “CAKE CAN BE PREPARED DIFFERENTLY!”

**WAFFLE SUPPORTER:** “The grid changes the way the syrup distributes.”

**JAKE:** “THEN WAFFLES HAVE EXTRA SYRUP STORAGE!”

Jake lifted the waffle.

**JAKE:** “LOOK! POCKETS!”

He lifted a pancake.

**JAKE:** “NO POCKETS!”

He put both down.

**JAKE:** “BOTH CAKE!”

A lengthy silence followed.

Then Jake delivered the final statement.

**JAKE:** “Waffles are pancakes with little syrup pockets. Pancakes are waffles that forgot to get the pockets. They're both cake. Cake is good. So stop fighting and eat your breakfast.”

He picked up his fork.

**JAKE:** “I'm eating both.”

He did.

The debate ended.

Not because everyone agreed.

Because nobody could find a response that would not require explaining, in a public cafeteria, why they were emotionally invested in the superiority of a breakfast item over another breakfast item that was nearly identical in purpose.

The waffle faction began eating.

The pancake faction began eating.

One employee quietly moved a pancake onto a waffle and called it “the compromise.”

Another employee requested that the cafeteria begin serving both simultaneously.

Within minutes, the tables had merged.

At 09:21, the first joint breakfast order was submitted.

At 09:23, someone asked whether French toast was also cake.

The room immediately became hostile again.

Jake was escorted away before he could answer.

## VI. TECHNICAL REVIEW — IS A WAFFLE A PANCAKE WITH POCKETS?

The Research Division has been asked to determine whether Jake's argument possesses any culinary merit.

The request was initially rejected on the grounds that the Research Division has more important responsibilities.

The request was subsequently resubmitted by three separate employees.

A brief review produced the following findings.

|Criterion|Waffles|Pancakes|
|---|---|---|
|Made from batter|Yes|Yes|
|Commonly eaten for breakfast|Yes|Yes|
|Can be served with syrup|Yes|Yes|
|Can be served with fruit|Yes|Yes|
|Can be served with chocolate|Yes|Yes|
|Possesses a grid of pockets|Yes|No|
|Requires a waffle iron|Generally|No|
|Can be described as “breakfast cake” in casual conversation|Yes|Yes|
|Can settle an argument when eaten together|Apparently|Apparently|

**RESEARCH CONCLUSION:** The items are distinct foods with overlapping ingredients, serving contexts, and culinary uses. Jake's statement is not a literal definition of either dish.

However, it is a surprisingly effective argument against treating the preference as a zero-sum conflict.

The phrase “waffles are pancakes with pockets” is classified as a humorous analogy, not a scientific finding.

The phrase “pancakes are waffles that forgot to get the pockets” has been classified as an insult to the entire culinary discipline.

The Research Division recommends no further investigation.

The cafeteria has ignored this recommendation.

## VII. C.O.R.E. DIRECTIVE — BREAKFAST DIPLOMACY

Effective immediately:

1. Waffles and pancakes are both approved cafeteria breakfast options.
    
2. No employee may claim that the other option is objectively inferior without providing evidence beyond personal preference.
    
3. No department may use breakfast preference as a criterion for recruitment, promotion, containment authorization, or security clearance.
    
4. Employees are encouraged to order both foods if doing so would end the argument.
    
5. Jake Akana is not to be appointed Director of Culinary Affairs.
    
6. Jake Akana is also not to be appointed Director of Culinary Affairs temporarily, unofficially, or “just to see what happens.”
    
7. Tom Akana is not required to supervise every conversation his younger brother begins.
    
8. Aries Akana is not required to mediate subsequent arguments about French toast, crepes, or whether cereal constitutes soup.
    
9. Phoenix is permitted to drink her tea in peace.
    
10. Any future dispute must be resolved without turning the cafeteria into a factional battleground.
    

**Supplementary note:** Should Jake attempt to classify every food as cake, personnel are advised that arguing with him may only encourage further elaboration.

## VIII. POST-INCIDENT OBSERVATION — THE YOUNGEST BROTHER'S VICTORY

Later that morning, Tom found Jake sitting at a cafeteria table with a plate containing one waffle and two pancakes.

Tom stopped beside him.

**TOM:** “You already ate breakfast.”

**JAKE:** “This is second breakfast.”

**TOM:** “That isn't a thing.”

**JAKE:** “It is now.”

Tom pulled out a chair and sat down.

**TOM:** “You know, you could've just told them to stop arguing.”

**JAKE:** “I did!”

**TOM:** “You compared two foods to cake for eleven minutes.”

**JAKE:** “AND IT WORKED!”

Tom glanced around the cafeteria.

The former waffle faction was sharing syrup with the former pancake faction.

Someone had placed a waffle on top of a stack of pancakes and was calling it a “breakfast tower.”

A Security employee had begun taking orders for a combined waffle-pancake platter.

Tom looked back at Jake.

**TOM:** “You know what? Fine. You win.”

Jake beamed.

**JAKE:** “I KNOW!”

**TOM:** “Please don't use this as evidence that you're right about everything.”

**JAKE:** “BUT I AM!”

**TOM:** “You asked whether the moon was made of frosting.”

**JAKE:** “IT LOOKS LIKE IT!”

**TOM:** “You tried to lick a photograph of it.”

**JAKE:** “I WAS SIX!”

Tom stared at him.

**TOM:** “You are six.”

Jake paused.

**JAKE:** “...THAT'S NOT THE POINT!”

Tom laughed.

Jake laughed with him.

For once, the incident required no emergency response, no reconstruction, no medical intervention, and no frantic search for an explanation as to how an ordinary object had become lethal.

It was just breakfast.

Tom leaned back in his chair.

**TOM:** “I'm proud of you, little star.”

Jake stopped chewing.

**JAKE:** “Really?”

**TOM:** “You got people to stop fighting.”

Jake's smile grew enormous.

**JAKE:** “I FIXED THE BLACKSITE!”

**TOM:** “You fixed breakfast.”

**JAKE:** “SAME THING!”

Tom chose not to argue.

It was, after all, a very good breakfast.

## IX. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake Akana's intervention successfully de-escalated the Blacksite's longstanding waffle-versus-pancake dispute.

The event demonstrates that Jake's tendency toward unusual reasoning is not exclusively a source of safety concerns. His perspective can also interrupt entrenched disagreements by reframing them in terms that participants had previously overlooked.

The conclusion itself is not scientifically literal.

Waffles and pancakes are distinct foods.

However, both are valid breakfast options, and no operational advantage has been identified in compelling personnel to choose one over the other.

C.O.R.E. therefore considers the dispute resolved for administrative purposes.

The decision is subject to revision should personnel resume hostilities over breakfast.

This is considered likely.

Nevertheless, the event deserves recognition for one simple reason: Jake brought a room full of arguing adults back to the same table.

He did not need to be the smartest person in the room.

He did not need a complicated theory.

He needed a plate of breakfast, an absurd analogy, and enough confidence to deliver it as though the fate of the Blacksite depended on the existence of syrup pockets.

For once, the result was harmless.

And for once, Tom got to sit beside his little brother without having to call Medical.

C.O.R.E. recommends that the cafeteria continue serving both waffles and pancakes.

## X. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — THE MOST IMPORTANT DISCOVERY

The Blacksite has spent years studying phenomena that defy explanation.

We have catalogued entities that challenge our understanding of life, death, matter, and reality. We have investigated events that cannot be reproduced and developed protocols for circumstances that should not exist.

And this morning, a six-year-old boy resolved a major institutional disagreement by announcing that waffles are pancakes with pockets.

Perhaps there is a lesson here.

Not that every disagreement is trivial.

Not that facts do not matter.

And certainly not that every complicated problem can be solved by comparing it to cake.

The lesson is that people can become so invested in winning an argument that they forget why they were sitting at the same table in the first place.

Jake reminded them.

He reminded them in the loudest, most ridiculous way possible.

Then he ate both sides of the argument.

We have no evidence that Genesis Construction contributed to this event. No anomalous intervention was detected. No reality distortion was recorded.

The only confirmed anomaly was Jake's ability to make several adults question their life choices before 09:30.

C.O.R.E. considers this a relatively benign manifestation of his personality.

**Official cafeteria ruling:**

- Waffles are acceptable.
    
- Pancakes are acceptable.
    
- Both are better when shared.
    
- Jake is not permitted to write the next cafeteria menu without supervision.
    

**Unofficial ruling, attributed to Jake Akana:**

_“WAFFLES ARE PANCAKES WITH POCKETS. PANCAKES ARE WAFFLES WITHOUT POCKETS. BOTH ARE CAKE. EVERYONE EAT.”_

The cafeteria has adopted the final sentence as its unofficial breakfast motto.

Tom has requested that his brother be prevented from making further declarations about food.

The request has been denied.

There is no evidence that doing so would work.
