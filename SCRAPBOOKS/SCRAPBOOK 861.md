# 1. OVERVIEW

Neri has developed a somewhat misleading reputation among SAFEHOUSE medical personnel.

She eats an **exceptionally large quantity of sweets**.

Cake.

Candy.

Pastries.

Pudding.

Ice cream.

Chocolate.

Sweet drinks.

Fruit desserts.

If it contains sugar, Neri will probably like it.

Despite this, repeated dental examinations have found her oral health to be **exceptionally well maintained**.

The reason is simple:

**Neri actually takes care of her teeth.**

---

# 2. DENTAL EXAMINATION

Neri's most recent examination reported:

- No significant cavities.
    
- No notable enamel damage.
    
- Healthy gums.
    
- No persistent plaque buildup.
    
- No significant tooth decay.
    
- No evidence of chronic neglect.
    
- Proper brushing habits.
    
- Regular dental attendance when requested.
    
- Appropriate oral hygiene for age.
    

The examining dentist initially reviewed the dietary report twice.

The dietary report was correct.

The dental examination was also correct.

The two documents were subsequently filed together.

# 3. THE SWEET CONSUMPTION PROBLEM

Neri's sweet consumption initially caused concern among medical personnel.

> **NERI:** "But I brush my teeth."

> **DENTIST:** "You do?"

> **NERI:** "Yes."

> **DENTIST:** "How often?"

> **NERI:** "Every day."

Neri produced dental floss.

The dentist became visibly emotional.

# 5. DENTAL HYGIENE ROUTINE

Neri maintains a regular dental hygiene routine.

Observed practices include:

- Brushing her teeth consistently.
    
- Taking appropriate care around sensitive areas.
    
- Rinsing after particularly sweet foods when practical.
    
- Accepting professional dental examinations.
    
- Maintaining regular meals.
    
- Eating a balanced diet despite her preference for sweets.
    

She has also demonstrated an unusual degree of personal responsibility regarding her dental health for her age.

This has resulted in the following assessment:

> **"She eats like a child who discovered a bakery. She brushes like someone preparing for a dental school examination."**

---

# 6. SEAL THIREN PHYSIOLOGY

Neri's Seal Thiren physiology provides an additional advantage.

Her teeth demonstrate resistance to the normal cavity-forming processes expected from prolonged sugar consumption.

This does **not** mean dental hygiene is unnecessary.

Neri's continued brushing and balanced diet are still considered important.

Her physiology may give her teeth additional protection.

Her personal habits are what make that protection particularly effective.

---

# 7. MEDICAL DIVISION ASSESSMENT

Neri's sweet consumption should therefore **not** be interpreted as evidence of poor nutrition or neglect.

Personnel should not attempt to unnecessarily restrict every sweet she receives.

Instead:

- Maintain balanced meals.
    
- Encourage normal hydration.
    
- Continue regular dental examinations.
    
- Allow reasonable desserts.
    
- Continue supporting her established hygiene routine.
    

Neri already demonstrates that she understands the difference between:

> **"I love cake."**

and

> **"I should eat nothing except cake."**

She chooses the former.
