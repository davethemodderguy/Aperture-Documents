## Executive Summary

Neri has recently begun drinking coffee.

The behavior appears to be voluntary and recreational rather than medically motivated. No indication has been observed that Neri considers coffee a replacement for food, medication, or Bao's blood-support regimen.

The change was initially notable because Neri had previously shown considerably more interest in sweets, particularly cake, pastries, pudding, and other sugary foods.

Coffee now appears to have joined that list of preferred cafeteria beverages.

No significant behavioral change has been attributed directly to the beverage itself.

There is, however, one consistent observation:

**Neri drinks coffee extremely quietly.**

---

## I. Initial Observation

The first confirmed observation occurred in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria.

Neri entered alone, carrying her usual oversized light-azure coat around herself. She collected a cup of coffee, added what appeared to be a substantial amount of sugar, and sat at her usual table.

She did not announce what she was doing.

She did not ask anyone whether they wanted coffee.

She did not ask anyone how coffee was made.

She simply drank it while reading.

Approximately fifteen minutes later, Jane Doe entered the cafeteria.

Jane stopped.

She looked at the cup.

She looked at Neri.

She looked back at the cup.

**JANE:**  
"...Is that coffee?"

Neri looked up.

She nodded.

**JANE:**  
"Since when do you drink coffee?"

Neri shrugged.

She returned to her book.

Jane remained standing for several seconds.

**JANE:**  
"Okay."

Pause.

**JANE:**  
"Apparently I'm the last person to know."

Neri continued drinking.

---

## II. Current Consumption Pattern

Observed coffee behavior is currently characterized by:

- Small-to-moderate servings.
    
- Frequent addition of sugar.
    
- No observed preference for elaborate preparation.
    
- Consumption primarily in the cafeteria.
    
- Occasional consumption while reading.
    
- No observed tendency to use coffee as a social excuse.
    
- No observed attempts to offer coffee to other residents.
    
- No observed interest in discussing the subject extensively.
    

The final point is considered particularly characteristic.

Neri appears to simply like coffee.

There is no indication that the behavior currently represents a major personality change.

---

## III. SAFEHOUSE Reactions

### Jane Doe

Jane initially found the development amusing.

She has since begun referring to Neri's coffee as:

> "Seal fuel."

Neri does not appear to appreciate the terminology.

Jane continues using it.

---

### Bao

Bao's response was considerably more practical.

She asked Neri whether she actually liked the taste.

Neri nodded.

Bao accepted the answer.

No attempt was made to forbid Neri from drinking coffee merely because it was a new habit.

However, given Neri's existing medical instability, Bao continues to monitor what Neri consumes without turning every meal or beverage into an interrogation.

---

### Alice

Alice apparently discovered Neri drinking coffee and immediately asked:

> "Can I try it?"

Neri allowed her to have a sip.

Alice reportedly made a face.

Neri continued drinking hers.

No further discussion occurred.

---

### Frost

Frost initially assumed Neri had somehow acquired an adult beverage.

She was subsequently informed that coffee is, in fact, just coffee.

The misunderstanding has not been formally documented as embarrassing to Frost.

Frost disagrees with this assessment.

---

### Neko

Neko's response was:

> "She's ten."

Followed by:

> "Actually, never mind. I've seen what the rest of you drink."

No intervention followed.

---

## IV. Psychological Observation

The introduction of coffee is not currently considered significant in itself.

More notable is **how Neri incorporates it into her existing routine**.

Neri does not appear to use coffee to attract attention.

She does not announce that she has developed a new preference.

She does not ask people to sit with her while she drinks it.

She does not use the drink as a reason to initiate conversation.

She simply makes coffee, sits down, and continues whatever she was already doing.

This is consistent with the behavioral pattern documented following the implementation of Neri's internal **Non-Interference Rule**.

Neri continues to minimize her social footprint.

The coffee is therefore not considered evidence that Neri is becoming more socially active.

It is simply another thing she now does while being alone.

---

## V. Cafeteria Observation

One later observation provides a more favorable interpretation.

Neri was sitting at the cafeteria table with a book open in front of her.

Her coffee was beside the book.

Jane entered.

Alice entered shortly afterward.

Neither initially spoke to Neri.

Jane eventually sat at the opposite side of the table.

Alice sat nearby.

Neri did not leave.

No conversation was required.

No invitation was formally issued.

No one asked Neri to participate.

She continued reading.

After several minutes, she quietly pushed the sugar container toward Alice.

Alice understood.

She took some.

Neri returned to her book.

Jane observed the interaction and smiled.

No further action was taken.

---

## VI. Assessment

There is currently no evidence that Neri's coffee consumption represents a harmful behavioral development.

The more significant observation is that Neri **remained in the same room as other residents without feeling compelled to leave simply because nobody was directly interacting with her.**

This is considered a minor but positive development.

Neri does not necessarily need to become socially outgoing.

She does not need to initiate conversations constantly.

She does not need to walk alongside someone every time she leaves her room.

She may simply exist around other people.

If coffee becomes part of that routine, C.O.R.E. has no objection to the beverage itself.

---

## VII. Final Note

At approximately 22:14, Jane reportedly asked:

> "So, Neri. Coffee person now?"

Neri nodded.

Jane asked:

> "Want to become a tea person next?"

Neri shook her head.

Jane:

> "Fair."

Neri took another sip.

**Jane:**  
"...You know, for someone who hates being called cute, you're drinking coffee like a tiny office worker."

Neri slowly lowered the cup.

Jane immediately left the cafeteria.

Neri did not pursue her.

C.O.R.E. considers this an appropriate outcome.
