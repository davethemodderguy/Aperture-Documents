# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has a small number of residents toward whom he demonstrates a noticeably stronger preference for companionship.

Three residents stand out:

- **Clover** — his roommate and one of his closest companions.
    
- **Bao** — a trusted maternal figure who provides comfort, care, and a sense of security.
    
- **Phoenix** — a resident Jake appears to like very, very much.
    

The distinction is subtle but consistent.

Jake is comfortable around many SAFEHOUSE residents. He can speak to them, accept assistance, and share common spaces without difficulty.

However, when given a genuine choice about whom to spend time with, Jake frequently gravitates toward Clover, Bao, or Phoenix.

Clover is familiar.

Bao is reassuring.

Phoenix is...

Well.

Phoenix is the resident Jake recently caught while she was falling down the stairs, transported through a spatial rift, and kissed on the cheek because he was relieved she was safe.

C.O.R.E. has determined that this incident was not an isolated indication of affection.

It was merely the most conspicuous one.

Phoenix has not recovered from the discovery.

---

# 1. CLOVER — THE ROOMMATE

Clover occupies a unique position in Jake's life.

They share a residence, which means their companionship is not dependent on arranging visits or deliberately initiating conversation.

They simply exist in the same space.

Clover can read while Jake reads.

She can eat while Jake eats.

She can sit quietly while Jake sleeps.

Neither is required to make the other feel entertained.

This appears to suit Jake particularly well.

### Observed interaction

**LOCATION:** Shared residence

Clover was sitting on the couch when Jake entered the room.

He walked past her.

Stopped.

Turned around.

Then returned and sat beside her.

Clover glanced at him.

**CLOVER:**  
"Something wrong?"

**JAKE:**  
"No."

**CLOVER:**  
"Need something?"

**JAKE:**  
"No."

Clover nodded and returned to her book.

Jake leaned against her shoulder.

Several minutes passed.

**CLOVER:**  
"You're using me as a pillow."

**JAKE:**  
"You're comfortable."

**CLOVER:**  
"...Fair enough."

She continued reading.

Jake fell asleep.

Clover did not move for the next forty minutes because she did not want to wake him.

She later complained that her arm had gone numb.

She also refused to admit that she had found the experience pleasant.

C.O.R.E. considers this typical roommate behavior.

---

# 2. BAO — THE SAFE PERSON

Bao provides a different kind of companionship.

Jake trusts her care.

He does not need to explain every discomfort before she notices something is wrong, and he does not have to demonstrate his abilities to earn her attention.

Around Bao, Jake is often noticeably quieter.

Not because he is uncomfortable.

Because he does not appear to feel the need to fill the silence.

He can sit beside her while she prepares food.

He can rest near her when he is tired.

He can accept a blanket, a meal, or a gentle reminder to sleep without interpreting every act of care as an obligation.

### Observed interaction

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE KITCHEN

Bao was preparing a late-night snack.

Jake appeared in the doorway, carrying his teddy bear.

**BAO:**  
"Can't sleep?"

Jake shook his head.

Bao glanced at him.

**BAO:**  
"Hungry?"

He nodded.

**BAO:**  
"Come here, sweetheart."

Jake approached.

Bao prepared something for him and placed it on the table.

Jake sat beside her.

He ate quietly.

After a while, he leaned against her arm.

Bao looked down at him.

**BAO:**  
"Tired?"

**JAKE:**  
"A little."

**BAO:**  
"You can sleep when you're finished."

Jake nodded.

He continued eating.

Bao did not ask him to explain why he had come looking for her.

She did not need to.

He had wanted to be near someone he trusted.

That was enough.

---

# 3. PHOENIX — THE PROBLEM

Phoenix's situation is considerably more complicated.

Not because Jake behaves badly around her.

Quite the opposite.

Jake appears exceptionally comfortable with Phoenix.

He approaches her voluntarily.

He allows her to carry him when he is tired.

He enjoys sitting beside her.

He sometimes seeks her out when he has nothing particular to say.

He is comfortable enough to fall asleep in her presence.

And, on at least one occasion, he has expressed his affection through a deliberate kiss.

The important distinction is that Jake does not appear to be seeking attention merely because Phoenix is enthusiastic about him.

He appears to genuinely enjoy _her_ company.

Phoenix is not simply a convenient person who happens to like cats.

She is someone Jake wants to spend time with.

Unfortunately, Phoenix has a tendency to become visibly flustered whenever this becomes apparent.

### Observed interaction

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE LOUNGE

Phoenix was sitting on the couch.

Jake approached carrying his teddy bear.

He looked at the empty space beside her.

Then at Phoenix.

**JAKE:**  
"Can I sit here?"

Phoenix immediately moved aside.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Of course!"

Jake sat beside her.

He placed his teddy bear in his lap.

For several minutes, neither spoke.

Phoenix occasionally glanced toward him.

Jake noticed.

**JAKE:**  
"What?"

**PHOENIX:**  
"Nothing!"

Jake tilted his head.

His ears rotated toward her.

**JAKE:**  
"...Okay."

He returned to his book.

Another minute passed.

Then Jake leaned against her shoulder.

Phoenix stopped moving.

Jake turned a page.

**JAKE:**  
"You're warm."

Phoenix's ears—if she had possessed feline ears—would probably have been bright red.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Th-thank you?"

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:**  
"Wasn't a question."

Phoenix pressed both hands against her face.

Jake stared at her for several seconds.

**JAKE:**  
"...You're doing it again."

**PHOENIX:**  
"Doing what?"

**JAKE:**  
"Being weird."

From the other side of the room, Clover began laughing.

# 4. COMPARATIVE BEHAVIOR

C.O.R.E. has identified several differences in Jake's interactions with the three residents.

|Behavior|Clover|Bao|Phoenix|
|---|---|---|---|
|Seeks out voluntarily|Frequent|Frequent|Frequent|
|Comfortable with silence|Very high|Very high|Very high|
|Accepts physical comfort|High|Very high|High|
|Falls asleep nearby|Common|Common|Common|
|Initiates affectionate contact|Occasional|Occasional|Observed|
|Becomes visibly flustered|Rare|Rare|Phoenix does|
|Teases Jake|Frequently|Occasionally|Sometimes|
|Respects his need for space|Yes|Yes|Yes|

These are qualitative observations, not measurements of affection.

C.O.R.E. cannot reliably rank Jake's relationships according to a numerical scale.

Nor is there any reason to require such a ranking.

His attachment to Clover, Bao, and Phoenix serves different emotional purposes.

Clover is the person who shares his everyday life.

Bao is someone who makes it easy for him to accept care.

Phoenix is someone whose presence he actively seeks and toward whom he has demonstrated particularly open affection.

All three matter to him.

In different ways.

# 5. THE QUESTION OF FAVORITISM

During a routine welfare conversation, Jake was asked whether he had a favorite person in SAFEHOUSE.

He looked at the officer.

**JAKE:**  
"Why?"

**OFFICER:**  
"I'm curious."

Jake considered the question.

**JAKE:**  
"...Clover."

The officer nodded.

**OFFICER:**  
"Anyone else?"

**JAKE:**  
"Bao."

Another pause.

**JAKE:**  
"Phoenix."

The officer made a note.

**OFFICER:**  
"You like spending time with them?"

Jake nodded.

**OFFICER:**  
"Why?"

Jake looked down at his teddy bear.

He adjusted its position against his chest.

**JAKE:**  
"Clover lets me be quiet."

A pause.

**JAKE:**  
"Bao takes care of me."

Another pause.

His ears lifted slightly.

**JAKE:**  
"Phoenix makes me happy."

The officer stopped writing for a moment.

**OFFICER:**  
"Happy?"

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:**  
"Yeah."

**OFFICER:**  
"How?"

Jake appeared to find the question difficult.

He thought for several seconds.

**JAKE:**  
"...She's Phoenix."

The officer waited for elaboration.

None followed.

Jake returned to holding his teddy bear.

The interview was concluded.

C.O.R.E. has retained the answer.

No further clarification was considered necessary.

---

# 6. PHOENIX LEARNS THE EXTENT OF THE PREFERENCE

Phoenix was informed of the general finding.

She initially assumed the report meant Jake liked spending time with her.

This was already enough to make her smile.

Then the welfare officer mentioned that Jake had specifically named her when asked about his preferred companions.

Phoenix went quiet.

**PHOENIX:**  
"He said my name?"

**OFFICER:**  
"Yes."

**PHOENIX:**  
"Without anyone suggesting it?"

**OFFICER:**  
"Yes."

**PHOENIX:**  
"He chose to say it?"

**OFFICER:**  
"That is generally how answering a question works."

Phoenix stared at the officer.

The officer began to suspect that the interview was becoming counterproductive.

**PHOENIX:**  
"...Oh."

She sat down.

Then smiled.

Then covered her face.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Oh, that's not fair."

**OFFICER:**  
"What isn't?"

**PHOENIX:**  
"He's so little."

A pause.

**PHOENIX:**  
"And he just says things like that."

She looked down at her hands.

**PHOENIX:**  
"He doesn't even know what it does to me."

The officer carefully clarified that Jake was not responsible for managing Phoenix's emotional reactions.

Phoenix agreed immediately.

She then asked whether she could spend time with him later.

The officer confirmed that she could, provided Jake wanted company.

Phoenix left the office smiling.

She was still smiling when she reached the corridor.

Clover happened to be there.

**CLOVER:**  
"You look suspiciously happy."

**PHOENIX:**  
"Jake likes me."

Clover blinked.

**CLOVER:**  
"...We knew that."

**PHOENIX:**  
"He _said_ it."

Clover paused.

Then smiled.

**CLOVER:**  
"Oh, you're in trouble."

**PHOENIX:**  
"I KNOW."

# 7. C.O.R.E. WELFARE GUIDANCE

Personnel should understand that Jake's preference for certain residents is neither unusual nor concerning.

Children and adults alike develop different kinds of trust with different people.

Jake is permitted to seek companionship with the residents he likes.

He is also permitted to change his mind, decline affection, request solitude, or choose someone else on any given day.

His affection should not be treated as a resource that others are entitled to.

In particular:

- Clover should not be expected to remain physically available whenever Jake wants company.
    
- Bao should not be expected to provide constant maternal care without rest.
    
- Phoenix should not interpret Jake's affection as a promise that he will always seek her out.
    
- Other residents should not pressure Jake to explain or justify his preferences.
    
- Nobody should attempt to turn his relationships into a competition.
    

Jake does not need to prove that he cares about the rest of SAFEHOUSE by distributing his attention equally.

Nor does he need to prove that his affection is genuine by making grand gestures.

Sometimes he simply chooses to sit beside someone.

Sometimes he falls asleep against their shoulder.

Sometimes he asks whether they are okay.

And sometimes, if the situation calls for it, he opens a spatial rift, catches them before they hit the floor, and kisses them because he is relieved they are safe.

The last behavior is not recommended as a routine method of communication.

It is, however, now part of the incident archive.

---

# C.O.R.E. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake likes Clover.

Jake likes Bao.

Jake likes Phoenix very much.

The relationships are not interchangeable.

Clover offers the familiarity of a roommate who can share a room without demanding conversation.

Bao offers the reassurance of someone who cares for him without requiring him to earn that care.

Phoenix offers a kind of companionship that Jake appears to actively seek out, accompanied by a degree of spontaneous affection that has repeatedly left her struggling to behave normally.

None of these relationships needs to replace the others.

Jake is allowed to have several people he loves being around.

He is allowed to express that affection in his own quiet way.

And he is allowed to be a little boy who knows which people make him feel safe.
