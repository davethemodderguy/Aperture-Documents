# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake's hybrid form has demonstrated considerably more extensive feline characteristics than initially anticipated.

Previous observations established the presence of feline ears, a tail, purring, grooming behavior, and a preference for cat beds.

Those observations were incomplete.

Jake has now demonstrated the **full range of ordinary feline physical capabilities and behavioral tendencies**, adapted to his half-human, half-cat anatomy.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- Exceptional balance and body awareness.
    
- The ability to land on his feet after many falls.
    
- Flexible movement and an unusually effective righting reflex.
    
- Rapid acceleration, sudden changes of direction, and short explosive bursts of speed.
    
- Climbing, jumping, squeezing through surprisingly small openings, and balancing on narrow surfaces.
    
- Night-adapted vision and highly sensitive hearing.
    
- Whisker-based spatial awareness, where applicable to his hybrid anatomy.
    
- Kneading soft surfaces.
    
- Loafing, curling up, and sleeping in positions that appear physically unreasonable.
    
- Grooming himself with his tongue.
    
- Purring, chirping, trilling, and producing other feline vocalizations.
    
- Rubbing his face against familiar people and objects.
    
- Hunting and pouncing behavior during play.
    
- Scratching and claw use.
    
- Tail and ear movements that communicate his mood.
    
- A strong preference for warm resting spots.
    
- The ability to become intensely interested in an object for several seconds before immediately losing interest.
    

The last behavior is considered entirely normal.

The most important finding is that these are not merely habits Jake imitates.

His hybrid physiology supports them.

Jake remains a person with his own judgment and preferences. However, his feline instincts and capabilities are now substantially more pronounced.

C.O.R.E. has therefore amended his classification.

> **Jake is not a human who happens to have cat ears and a tail. He is a genuine human–feline hybrid whose body can perform the full range of ordinary feline behaviors.**

Phoenix has been informed.

She has requested a chair.

C.O.R.E. is unsure whether she intends to sit in it or fall out of it.

# 1. PHYSICAL CAPABILITIES

## 1.1 — Balance and the Righting Reflex

Jake has demonstrated a highly developed feline righting reflex.

When his body is tilted or rotated during safe, controlled movement, he can rapidly reorient himself to land feet-first.

His balance is exceptional.

He can walk along narrow ledges, turn around on small platforms, and maintain stability in positions that would be difficult for an ordinary human child.

However, the righting reflex is not an invulnerability mechanism.

A sufficiently high fall, an awkward landing, or an unsuitable surface can still cause injury.

Personnel are expressly forbidden from testing Jake's reflex by dropping him.

This instruction should not have required clarification.

It has nevertheless been added to the formal protocol.

### Observation

Jake was seen stepping off the edge of a low couch.

He rotated in midair and landed on his feet.

He paused.

He looked at the couch.

He jumped back onto it.

He stepped off again.

He landed on his feet.

He jumped back up.

He repeated the sequence four times.

A welfare officer eventually asked whether he was trying to demonstrate something.

Jake looked at the officer.

**JAKE:** "No."

He jumped off again.

**ASSESSMENT:** The activity appeared enjoyable.

---

## 1.2 — Jumping and Climbing

Jake can jump considerably more effectively than his human appearance would suggest.

He can reach elevated surfaces, climb furniture, scale suitable structures, and descend using controlled feline movements.

He has also demonstrated the ability to use his hands and feet together to navigate spaces that would otherwise require specialized climbing equipment.

His tail appears to assist with balance and midair orientation.

It does not function as a prehensile limb.

Jake cannot use it to pick up objects, open doors, or carry his teddy bear.

He has tried to wrap it around the teddy bear anyway.

The result was unsuccessful.

Jake stared at his tail.

His tail moved.

Jake stared harder.

He then picked up the teddy bear with his hands.

The problem was resolved.

---

## 1.3 — Flexibility

Jake's movement has become exceptionally fluid.

He can twist his torso, tuck his limbs close to his body, curl into compact positions, and pass through openings that appear too small for him.

This does not mean that his skeleton is literally boneless.

It means that his feline physiology grants him a greater range of movement than his previous human form.

### The Laundry Basket Incident

A staff member discovered Jake inside an empty laundry basket.

The basket was upright.

Jake was curled into a small ball inside it.

His teddy bear was beside him.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "Jake?"

One ear appeared over the edge.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "How did you get in there?"

Jake blinked.

**JAKE:** "Got in."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "I can see that."

Jake closed his eyes.

The staff member lifted the basket carefully and placed it on the floor.

Jake remained inside.

He appeared comfortable.

C.O.R.E. has declined to investigate further.

The basket is now considered an approved resting option, provided it remains stable, ventilated, and accessible.

# 2. SENSORY CAPABILITIES

## 2.1 — Hearing

Jake's feline ears provide him with highly sensitive directional hearing.

He can detect quiet sounds at a distance, identify the approximate direction of their source, and rotate his ears independently to focus on different noises.

This has produced several unexpected interactions.

A resident may whisper his name from across a room.

Jake's ears turn toward them.

His head does not.

The resident assumes he has not heard.

Then Jake answers without looking up.

**JAKE:** "What?"

The resident jumps.

Jake returns to reading.

Personnel are advised not to deliberately exploit his hearing by making loud noises, testing his reaction, or whispering about him within earshot.

He can hear you.

Frequently, he has heard you for several minutes already.

He simply has not decided that you are worth responding to.

---

## 2.2 — Night Vision

Jake's eyes have adapted to low-light conditions.

He can navigate dim rooms considerably more effectively than an ordinary human child.

His azure irises retain their distinctive shooting-star patterns, and the feline adaptation does not appear to have removed their anomalous characteristics.

He is particularly comfortable in the low lighting of his private residence.

At night, he may move around the room without switching on the main lights.

This is not evidence of insomnia.

Jake continues to require appropriate rest.

His ability to see in dim conditions does not mean that he should be encouraged to remain awake all night.

### Observation

At 02:17, a welfare officer opened Jake's door for a routine visual check.

The room was dark.

Jake was sitting upright in his cat bed.

His eyes reflected the dim illumination.

**OFFICER:** "...Jake?"

**JAKE:** "Yes?"

**OFFICER:** "Why are you awake?"

**JAKE:** "Wasn't sleeping."

**OFFICER:** "Would you like the light on?"

Jake shook his head.

**JAKE:** "Too bright."

The officer left the room dim and allowed Jake to settle again.

No further intervention was required.

---

## 2.3 — Smell and Taste

Jake has demonstrated heightened interest in scents.

He may inspect food before eating, investigate unfamiliar objects with his nose, and recognize familiar people by scent.

He has also become unusually selective about certain smells.

One cleaning product was sufficient to make him leave an entire room.

He returned only after the product had been removed and the area properly ventilated.

His sense of smell should be respected.

Personnel must not deliberately expose him to strong odors to test his reactions.

Additionally, heightened smell does not mean that Jake can safely eat every substance he finds interesting.

**Jerry has been specifically reminded that canned cat food is not automatically appropriate for Jake.**

Jerry has asked whether this restriction also applies to tuna.

It does.

# 3. FELINE COMMUNICATION

Jake's ears, tail, posture, and vocalizations have become meaningful parts of his communication.

Although he can still speak normally, he frequently uses feline body language without consciously deciding to do so.

## Common Observations

|Behavior|Typical interpretation|
|---|---|
|Ears pointed forward|Interest or attention|
|Ears rotated toward a sound|Listening|
|Ears flattened|Discomfort, fear, or irritation|
|Tail held upright|Confidence or friendly approach|
|Tail gently curled|Relaxation or contentment|
|Tail flicking sharply|Agitation or overstimulation|
|Slow blinking|Relaxation or affiliative behavior|
|Rubbing his cheek against someone|Familiarity or affection|
|Kneading a blanket|Comfort or self-soothing|
|Purring|Often contentment, though context matters|
|Chirping or trilling|Excitement, greeting, or attention-seeking|
|Hissing|Strong discomfort or a warning to back away|

These interpretations are contextual, not absolute.

Jake may express several states simultaneously.

For example, he may be comfortable sitting beside Phoenix while still becoming irritated if she touches his ears without warning.

Affection does not cancel boundaries.

# 4. GROOMING

Jake now engages in full feline grooming behavior.

He licks his paws and uses them to clean his face.

He grooms his arms and hands.

He occasionally attempts to smooth his hair with his fingers, apparently applying the same behavioral pattern to his human anatomy.

His tongue and grooming movements appear consistent with his hybrid physiology.

However, ordinary hygiene requirements remain in effect.

Jake is not permitted to substitute grooming for bathing, medical care, or appropriate cleaning when these are necessary.

He has not been pleased by this clarification.

### Observation

Bao found Jake sitting on his bed, licking the back of his hand.

**BAO:** "Jake?"

Jake continued grooming.

**BAO:** "What are you doing?"

**JAKE:** "Cleaning."

Bao looked at his hand.

Then at him.

**BAO:** "You can wash your hands normally, you know."

Jake stopped.

He looked at his hand.

He looked at Bao.

**JAKE:** "...But this works."

Bao sighed.

She brought him a warm, damp cloth.

Jake accepted it.

He used it to clean his hands.

Then he groomed one paw for good measure.

Bao decided that partial success was still success.

# 5. KNEADING, LOAFING, AND SLEEPING

Jake's resting habits have changed considerably.

He now frequently:

- Kneads blankets and pillows with alternating paws.
    
- Sits in a compact loaf position.
    
- Curls into a tight ball when cold.
    
- Sleeps with his face tucked beneath an arm.
    
- Uses his tail as a partial blanket.
    
- Chooses small enclosed spaces over larger beds.
    
- Moves repeatedly between resting spots before settling.
    
- Falls asleep in warm patches of sunlight.
    
- Seeks soft fabric before deciding where to sit.
    

His cat bed remains his preferred resting location.

He also uses his ordinary bed, cushions, blankets, and occasionally Phoenix's lap.

His teddy bear remains an essential companion.

Jake may curl around it while sleeping, tuck it beside himself, or position it beneath one arm.

Personnel are not to remove the teddy bear to make room for other bedding.

The bear stays with him.

---

# 6. PLAY AND HUNTING INSTINCTS

Jake has developed stronger feline play responses.

Small moving objects may attract his attention.

He may crouch, focus intently, wiggle his hindquarters, and pounce.

He may also chase toys, bat objects with his hands, and investigate moving shadows.

These behaviors appear to be ordinary expressions of feline instinct rather than deliberate aggression.

Nevertheless, suitable toys should be used.

Hands and feet should not be presented as prey, especially given Jake's recent tendency to bite when annoyed.

### The Shoelace Incident

A welfare officer was speaking to another staff member when Jake noticed a loose shoelace.

Jake stared at it.

The lace moved slightly.

Jake lowered his body.

His ears pointed forward.

His tail began to twitch.

The officer noticed.

**OFFICER:** "Jake, don't—"

Jake pounced.

The officer stumbled.

Jake landed on the floor with the shoelace between his hands.

He looked extremely pleased with himself.

The officer looked down at him.

**OFFICER:** "...You could have asked."

Jake batted the lace once.

**JAKE:** "Meow."

A suitable toy was subsequently provided.

The shoelace was tied securely.

---

# 7. SCRATCHING AND CLAWS

Jake's feline anatomy includes functional claws.

He can extend and retract them as appropriate to his hybrid physiology.

He uses them for gripping, climbing, scratching, and manipulating certain surfaces.

This has created a minor furniture concern.

Jake has shown a preference for scratching textured materials.

C.O.R.E. has therefore provided a suitable scratching surface in his room.

The scratching surface is not a punishment.

It is an appropriate outlet for a normal feline behavior.

Personnel must not forcibly manipulate his claws or attempt to trim them without appropriate veterinary or medical guidance and Jake's cooperation.

Jake has also been reminded that scratching people is not an acceptable substitute for communicating irritation.

His response was to look at the scratching post.

Then at the officer.

Then back at the post.

He used it.

This was considered a satisfactory outcome.

---

# 8. THE BITE PROBLEM — UPDATED

Jake's full feline behavior has not removed his ability to reason or communicate verbally.

He can still say that he is annoyed.

He can still ask someone to stop.

He can still choose to walk away.

However, his feline instincts may cause him to react physically more quickly than before.

Biting can occur when he is overstimulated, frightened, frustrated, or repeatedly handled against his wishes.

Personnel should therefore respond to early warning signs.

Do not continue touching him when his ears flatten or his tail begins flicking sharply.

Do not corner him.

Do not force him into a lap.

Do not wake him merely because he looks comfortable.

And do not assume that purring means he cannot become annoyed.

Jake may be perfectly happy one moment and decide that he has had enough the next.

That is not permission to ignore his boundaries.

It is a reminder to pay attention.

# 9. PHOENIX'S RESPONSE TO THE COMPLETE FELINE PACKAGE

Phoenix has been briefed on the full extent of Jake's feline capabilities.

She has reacted with enthusiasm.

Considerable enthusiasm.

She has also begun learning which behaviors Jake enjoys and which ones he would prefer people not to encourage.

When Jake kneads a blanket beside her, she lets him.

When he rubs his cheek against her hand, she gently acknowledges him.

When he curls into her lap, she supports him and allows him to settle.

When he starts grooming himself, she does not interrupt.

When his ears flatten, she gives him space.

When he bites at an unwanted touch, she withdraws and reassesses rather than attempting to hold him still.

Her affection remains considerable.

Her understanding has improved.

### Private Observation

Phoenix was sitting on the floor.

Jake approached, circled her twice, and sat beside her.

His tail brushed her leg.

Phoenix looked down.

**PHOENIX:** "Hello, sweetheart."

Jake leaned against her.

A quiet purr followed.

Phoenix gently stroked his head.

After a few moments, Jake moved away and began investigating a cushion.

Phoenix let him go.

A welfare officer observed the interaction.

**OFFICER:** "You didn't try to keep him there."

**PHOENIX:** "He wanted to move."

**OFFICER:** "And you're fine with that?"

Phoenix looked at Jake, who was now attempting to determine whether the cushion could be climbed from the side.

**PHOENIX:** "Of course."

She paused.

**PHOENIX:** "He can come back whenever he wants."

Jake abandoned the cushion and returned to sit beside her approximately thirty seconds later.

Phoenix did not comment.

She simply made room.

---

# 10. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's hybrid physiology supports the full extent of ordinary feline behavior and physical capabilities.

His abilities include movement, balance, climbing, jumping, grooming, communication, play, rest, and sensory responses consistent with a cat.

His behavior is not limited to superficial imitation.

He has genuine feline instincts and anatomy.

However, he remains Jake.

He can reason.

He can speak.

He can make decisions.

He can establish boundaries.

He can enjoy affection without surrendering his autonomy.

He can behave like a cat without being treated as an ordinary pet.

And he can spend an afternoon chasing a toy before returning to his book without anyone demanding an explanation.

C.O.R.E. considers this an acceptable outcome.
