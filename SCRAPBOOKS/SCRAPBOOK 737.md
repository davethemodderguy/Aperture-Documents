## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / ANOMALOUS OBSERVATION LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — GRADUAL TRANSPARENCY**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **SLOW PROGRESSION — CONTINUOUS MONITORING**

---

# **OBSERVATION**

Alice is becoming more transparent.

Very slowly.

So slowly that the change is difficult to notice from one day to the next.

A photograph taken one week apart may show almost no difference.

A photograph taken several months apart tells a different story.

The edges of Alice's body have become increasingly translucent.

Light passes through her hair more easily.

Her arms occasionally appear faint when held against a bright background.

Her ears sometimes become almost transparent at their edges.

Her silhouette remains visible.

But it is becoming softer.

Less definite.

---

# **THE RATE**

The progression is extremely slow.

There have been no sudden changes.

No episodes of rapid disappearance.

No dramatic physical collapse associated with the transparency itself.

It simply continues.

Little by little.

Day by day.

---

# **MEMORY CORRELATION**

The memory loss continues to follow the same general pattern.

As transparency increases, Alice occasionally forgets something.

Sometimes it is insignificant.

Sometimes it isn't.

She may forget a conversation from the previous day.

She may forget why she drew something.

She may recognize a person but forget what they did together.

Sometimes she reads her own journal and realizes she has forgotten something she once considered important.

The progression remains unpredictable.

---

# **ALICE'S REACTION**

Alice doesn't seem particularly frightened by her changing appearance.

She occasionally holds her hand up toward the light.

She watches the light pass through her fingers.

Then she returns to drawing.

When asked if she notices the difference, she sometimes writes:

> **"Alice look weird."**

Then adds:

> **"Pretty maybe?"**

Nobody has the heart to tell her that the phenomenon is considerably more serious than that.

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE**

Everyone has noticed.

Nobody talks about it in front of Alice unless necessary.

Instead, they simply spend time with her.

Corin sits beside her while she draws.

Belle brings her carrots.

Star visits.

Lumi checks on her.

Neko occasionally asks whether she wants to ride her scooter.

Orphie stays nearby.

Nobody wants Alice's final memories to consist of people being afraid of her.

---

# **THE PHOTOGRAPHS**

A private record has been established.

Every few days, a photograph is taken.

Same room.

Same chair.

Same lighting.

Same position whenever possible.

The purpose is to document the progression.

The photographs are not shown to Alice.

Not yet.

The first image shows an ordinary, solid Alice.

The newest images show light beginning to pass through portions of her body.

The difference is small.

But undeniable.

---

# **HER JOURNAL**

Alice continues writing.

Some pages now contain reminders specifically about herself.

> **"Alice is Alice."**

> **"Alice is white bunny."**

> **"Alice live Treehouse."**

> **"Big sis is Corin."**

> **"Bigger sis is Orphie."**

> **"Alice have friends."**

She occasionally adds:

> **"Alice remember."**

Nobody knows whether she writes it because she is afraid of forgetting.

Or because she already forgot something.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has become especially attentive.

Not overbearing.

Not constantly asking Alice if she remembers.

Just present.

When Alice finishes drawing, Corin looks at the picture.

When Alice wants company, Corin sits with her.

When Alice doesn't want to talk, Corin doesn't force conversation.

And when Alice looks at her own increasingly transparent hands, Corin simply holds them.

They are still tangible.

For now.

---

# **CURRENT ASSESSMENT**

The progression remains slow.

There is no immediate indication that Alice is about to disappear.

But the condition has not stopped.

It is still progressing.

A fraction at a time.

A memory at a time.

The Blacksite has learned to measure Alice's transparency in percentages.

Alice herself doesn't care about the number.

She cares about whether everyone is still there.

And everyone is.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Nobody knows how long the process will take.

Weeks.

Months.

Years.

There is no reliable prediction.

So for now, the Blacksite does the only thing it can.

It keeps Alice surrounded by people who remember her.

Every day she remains here is another day she is reminded:

**She is Alice.**

**She belongs here.**

**She is loved.**

And even if the transparency continues—

nobody is going to start treating her like she's already gone.

**End of CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / ANOMALOUS OBSERVATION LOG**
