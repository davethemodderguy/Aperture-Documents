**SUBJECT:** Unidentified Stellar Absence

---

# 1. INITIAL OBSERVATION

The first indication was not detected by the SAFEHOUSE.

It was detected by the sky.

At approximately 02:13, N.O.S.T. astronomical monitoring systems registered an unexpected absence in a previously stable stellar field.

One star was missing.

Not dimmed.

Not obscured.

Not eclipsed.

**Missing.**

The surrounding stars remained unchanged.

The region of space contained no debris.

No explosion.

No gravitational disturbance.

No supernova signature.

No black hole formation.

The star had simply ceased to appear.

---

# 2. HISTORICAL COMPARISON

N.O.S.T. compared the current observation against archived astronomical data.

The missing star had been present in historical records.

It had been catalogued.

Observed.

Photographed.

Mapped.

Its position had remained consistent.

Its disappearance was therefore not attributable to an observational error.

Further analysis produced an unusual result.

The star corresponded to a celestial object previously believed to have disappeared from historical maps decades earlier.

The same star had once been associated with an anomalous terrestrial observation.

The record had no surviving name.

Only an identifier.

---

# 3. THE GAP

N.O.S.T. astronomers initially assumed the missing star had been destroyed.

The evidence contradicted this.

A destroyed star leaves evidence.

A collapsed star leaves evidence.

A displaced star leaves evidence.

This left something considerably more disturbing.

The star had not died.

It had been **removed.**

Not from space.

From observation.

---

# 4. SAFEHOUSE CORRELATION

At 04:27, C.O.R.E. compared the astronomical anomaly against SAFEHOUSE archival irregularities.

A correlation was discovered.

The unidentified star's disappearance occurred within the same temporal window as the complete erasure of an unidentified former SAFEHOUSE resident.

No surviving database entry identified the resident.

No surviving record established a name.

However, the timing was exact.

N.O.S.T. requested authorization to compare the celestial object's historical data against the SAFEHOUSE's anomalous resident records.

Authorization was granted.

The comparison produced one result.

---

# 5. BLUE DWARF

The missing object had characteristics consistent with an extremely rare stellar classification.

**Blue Dwarf.**

N.O.S.T. records indicate that Blue Dwarfs are extraordinarily uncommon.

The missing object was particularly unusual.

Its historical luminosity was consistent with the stellar signature previously associated with the unidentified child resident.

No other known Blue Dwarf within the relevant observation range matched the recorded characteristics.

The correlation remains under investigation.

---

# 6. THE SKY

At 05:02, N.O.S.T. personnel manually observed the region.

The sky looked normal.

Thousands of stars were visible.

The constellations remained intact.

Nothing appeared wrong.

Except for one small patch of darkness.

A place where a star should have been.

An astronomer marked the coordinates.

Then stopped.

She stared at the empty space.

> **ASTRONOMER:**  
> "I know something was there."

Another researcher looked over.

> **RESEARCHER:**  
> "What?"

She pointed.

> **ASTRONOMER:**  
> "That."

> **RESEARCHER:**  
> "There's nothing there."

> **ASTRONOMER:**  
> "I know."

She lowered the telescope.

> **ASTRONOMER:**  
> "That's the problem."

---

# 7. RESIDENT REACTIONS

The anomaly was not immediately disclosed to the SAFEHOUSE residents.

However, several independently began looking toward the same region of the sky.

Anby was among them.

She could not explain why.

She simply found herself staring upward.

She eventually asked:

> **ANBY:**  
> "Was there always a star there?"

Nobody knew.

Alice looked up.

> **ALICE:**  
> "I think so."

Pause.

> **ALICE:**  
> "Maybe."

Neither was certain.

---

# 8. BAO

Bao noticed the absence while standing outside the SAFEHOUSE.

She stared at the sky for several minutes.

Something inside her hurt.

She couldn't identify it.

She eventually whispered:

> **BAO:**  
> "...You were there."

She immediately frowned.

She did not know who she was talking to.

She looked away.

The feeling remained.

---

# 9. NERI

Neri noticed the star field during one of her nighttime walks.

She stopped.

She looked upward.

Then she opened her notebook.

Without understanding why, she drew:

A small child.

White hair.

Round glasses.

A halo.

A teddy bear.

Above him was a single bright star.

Neri stared at the drawing.

She did not remember drawing the child before.

She did not know his name.

At the bottom of the page, she wrote:

> **"Missing."**

---

# 10. THE TEDDY BEAR

The anomalous teddy bear remained in the SAFEHOUSE archive.

Its status changed shortly after the stellar disappearance.

The previously inactive core produced a single measurable pulse.

One.

Then nothing.

No heartbeat.

No energy emission.

No further activity.

Phoenix was called to examine it.

She placed her hand against the fabric.

She became silent.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "...Why does this feel familiar?"

No answer was available.

---

# 11. C.O.R.E. ANALYSIS

The current working hypothesis is that the missing star and the erased resident are connected.

However, the relationship cannot currently be established through conventional evidence.

The available evidence consists of:

- the star's disappearance;
    
- the historical presence of the star;
    
- the resident's complete archival erasure;
    
- the missing SAFEHOUSE floor;
    
- the unidentified teddy bear;
    
- residual emotional responses among former acquaintances;
    
- and the timing of both events.
    

None alone is conclusive.

Together, they are difficult to dismiss.

# FINAL OBSERVATION

The SAFEHOUSE continued.

The residents continued.

The Blacksite continued.

The world continued.

But somewhere above them, the sky had changed.

One star was missing.

Nobody knew its name.

Nobody remembered watching it.

Nobody remembered wishing upon it.

Nobody remembered the little boy who had once been that star.

Nobody remembered the child who had brought apples to a birthday party.

Nobody remembered the teddy bear that had once carried his heart.

Nobody remembered the halo.

Nobody remembered the medical bed.

Nobody remembered the five weeks.

Nobody remembered the door.

Nobody remembered the words.

Nobody remembered the heartbreak.

But the universe remembered one thing.

There had been a light there.

And now there wasn't.

A single star was missing from the sky.

And for reasons nobody could explain,

**the darkness where it had been felt like grief.**
