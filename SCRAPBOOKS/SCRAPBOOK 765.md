# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — CURRENT CONDITION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD WELFARE  
**Age:** 5  
**Status:** Extremely Fragile / Sleep Deprived / Nonverbal  
**Weight:** Lightweight  
**Mobility:** Limited  
**Supervision:** Continuous

---

# **CURRENT STATE**

Alice is currently experiencing **extreme sleep deprivation**.

She is almost constantly fatigued.

Even when awake, she frequently appears as though she could fall asleep at any moment.

Her movements are slow.

Her posture is weak.

Her attention drifts easily.

She may stop in the middle of walking simply because she has become too tired to continue.

She rarely complains.

She cannot reliably communicate discomfort.

---

# **APPEARANCE**

Alice's current appearance has become immediately recognizable throughout the Blacksite.

### **Headwear**

Alice wears a **soft white beanie**.

It is lightweight and comfortable.

Her rabbit ears generally rest underneath or around it.

The beanie has become one of the few pieces of clothing Alice consistently keeps on.

---

### **RIGHT EYE**

Alice's right eye remains protected by a bandage and eyepatch.

The damaged eye is not to be uncovered without medical authorization.

Personnel are instructed not to ask Alice to remove the eyepatch.

She is comfortable with it remaining in place.

---

### **EARS**

Alice's rabbit ears almost always follow gravity.

They rarely remain upright.

When she sits, they fall beside her head.

When she lies down, they rest against the surface beneath her.

When she is carried, they simply flop over naturally.

Occasionally they rise briefly when something catches her attention.

They then fall again.

**Do not force them upright.**

---

### **CLOTHING**

Alice normally wears a combination of:

- Hoodie
    
- Warm coat
    
- Boots
    
- White soft beanie
    

Her clothing is deliberately comfortable and warm.

This is especially important due to her tendency to become cold when exhausted.

---

### **TAIL**

Alice's tail is unusual.

Rather than appearing like a conventional rabbit tail, it resembles a small **fluff of feathers**.

It is soft and highly distinctive.

It is also subject to a strict rule:

# **DO NOT TOUCH ALICE'S TAIL.**

Nobody is exempt.

Not friends.

Not staff.

Not medical personnel except where medically necessary.

Not SAFEHOUSE residents.

Alice does not consent to having it touched.

Her head may be gently petted.

Her tail may not.

---

# **PHYSICAL CONDITION**

Alice is exceptionally lightweight.

She can be picked up with relatively little effort.

However, this should not be interpreted as evidence that she is healthy.

She is physically weak.

Her limbs tire quickly.

She cannot sustain prolonged activity.

Walking for extended periods may leave her exhausted.

Running is strongly discouraged.

---

# **FATIGUE**

Alice appears tired almost constantly.

Common observations include:

- Sitting down unexpectedly.
    
- Leaning against nearby people.
    
- Falling asleep while sitting.
    
- Walking slowly.
    
- Closing her eyes for extended periods.
    
- Becoming unresponsive to conversations because she is too tired.
    
- Resting her head against furniture.
    
- Falling asleep while being carried.
    
- Losing track of what she was doing.
    
- Remaining still for long periods.
    

Her ears frequently become completely limp during these episodes.

---

# **SLEEP DEPRIVATION**

Alice's lack of sleep has become one of the most serious concerns surrounding her current condition.

She may be physically exhausted while simultaneously being unable or unwilling to sleep normally.

This creates a dangerous cycle:

**Fatigue → reduced activity → weakness → exhaustion → inadequate recovery → further fatigue.**

Personnel must not assume that Alice is simply "a sleepy child."

Her level of exhaustion is medically significant.

---

# **BEHAVIOR**

Despite her condition, Alice continues attempting to move around the Blacksite.

She may quietly follow someone.

She may ride her scooter.

She may sit somewhere and observe people.

She may attempt to visit friends.

She does not appear to understand that she should stop when exhausted.

This is particularly dangerous because Alice's natural response to discomfort is usually:

**Do nothing.**

She does not reliably ask for help.

She does not complain.

She does not call attention to herself.

She simply becomes quieter.

---

# **WHEN ALICE BECOMES TOO TIRED**

The most reliable indication is that she stops moving.

She may sit on the floor.

Her ears fall.

Her eyes become half-closed.

Her posture collapses inward.

She may lean against the nearest person.

If someone asks:

> "Are you tired?"

Alice will frequently nod.

If asked:

> "Do you want to sleep?"

She may simply stare.

Sometimes she doesn't answer.

---

# **SAFEHOUSE RESPONSE**

Alice is not to be permitted to isolate herself during severe fatigue.

Someone remains with her.

If she falls asleep somewhere unsafe, she is moved somewhere comfortable.

If she becomes too weak to walk, she is carried.

If she attempts to continue walking despite obvious exhaustion, personnel intervene.

Nobody waits for Alice to ask for help.

She is extremely unlikely to do so.

---

# **THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION**

Alice's quietness should never be interpreted as comfort.

Her silence does not mean:

> "I'm fine."

Her stillness does not mean:

> "I don't need help."

Her refusal to complain does not mean:

> "Nothing is wrong."

Alice has repeatedly demonstrated that she will tolerate severe discomfort without notifying anyone.

Therefore:

> **Personnel must observe her condition rather than wait for Alice to report it.**

---

# **CURRENT VISUAL PROFILE**

When encountered throughout the Blacksite, Alice will most commonly appear as:

> A tiny, lightweight bunny girl wearing a **soft white beanie**, a **hoodie**, a **warm coat**, and **boots**.

Her **right eye remains bandaged beneath an eyepatch**.

Her rabbit ears hang naturally under gravity.

Her feather-like tail remains untouched.

She moves slowly.

She is weak.

She is quiet.

And she almost always looks tired.

---

# **PERSONNEL REMINDER**

Alice is five.

She is not capable of reliably evaluating her own physical condition.

She may believe that being exhausted is simply something she should endure.

She may continue walking when she should rest.

She may refuse help because she does not want to inconvenience anyone.

She may hide when she needs attention.

Therefore:

**Do not wait for Alice to ask.**

Check on her.

Stay with her.

Carry her when necessary.

Let her rest.

Make sure she is warm.

And above all—

**do not leave her alone.**

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice's current appearance is deceptively gentle.

White beanie.

Soft ears.

Oversized hoodie.

Warm coat.

Small boots.

A tiny feather-like tail.

She looks almost peaceful when sitting quietly.

But the reality underneath that appearance is much more concerning.

Alice is exhausted.

She is weak.

She is severely sleep deprived.

And she is still trying to take care of everyone except herself.

So the responsibility has to belong to everyone else now.

Alice doesn't have to ask.

Someone will notice.

Someone will stay.

Someone will carry her home.

And someone will make sure she finally gets to rest.
