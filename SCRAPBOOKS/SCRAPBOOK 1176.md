# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### MEDICAL & RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-MED-4C-NERI-304  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — MEDICAL / SAFEHOUSE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE MEDICAL MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Neri Tachyon  
**INCIDENT:** Severe blunt-force impact during Plush Form

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Neri remains unwell following the recent incident in which she was accidentally thrown into a wall with approximately the force of a baseball.

Although the initial impact injuries were not immediately considered permanently disabling, Neri's recovery has been substantially more difficult than anticipated.

She currently reports:

- persistent physical pain;
    
- generalized weakness;
    
- fatigue;
    
- feeling unwell;
    
- reduced willingness to move;
    
- intermittent nausea;
    
- difficulty becoming comfortable;
    
- and increased sensitivity around the previously injured areas.
    

The most persistent complaint remains:

> **Pain around the cardiac region.**

Neri has not returned to her previous baseline.

---

# 2. CURRENT CONDITION

Neri is currently able to remain conscious and communicate.

Her voice is present, although she is speaking even less than usual.

Most communication continues through:

- nodding;
    
- shaking her head;
    
- pointing;
    
- facial expressions;
    
- brief written responses;
    
- and occasional short verbal statements.
    

When asked how she feels, Neri's responses have been remarkably consistent.

> **Neri:**  
> “Bad.”

When asked whether she is in pain:

> **Neri:**  
> “Yes.”

When asked whether it is getting better:

A pause.

> **Neri:**  
> “A little.”

---

# 3. PERSISTENT PAIN

The pain has not remained confined to the original point of impact.

Neri describes a generalized sensation of being:

> **“Sore everywhere.”**

However, the chest and cardiac region remain the most concerning areas.

She occasionally places a hand against her chest and remains completely still.

During these periods, personnel should not assume that silence means the pain has resolved.

Neri has an established tendency to tolerate discomfort without reporting it.

In this case, however, she has begun verbally acknowledging the pain when directly asked.

This represents a significant behavioral change.

---

# 4. GENERAL ILLNESS

Neri has also developed symptoms resembling a prolonged post-injury illness.

Observed behavior includes:

- sleeping considerably more;
    
- remaining stationary for long periods;
    
- reduced activity;
    
- appearing pale and tired;
    
- occasionally becoming nauseated;
    
- losing interest in conversation;
    
- holding her coffee without drinking it;
    
- and spending extended periods curled up under blankets.
    

At one point, Neri was observed sitting on a couch with her coffee untouched for approximately forty minutes.

This was considered sufficiently unusual that Bao checked on her.

Neri looked at the cup.

Then at Bao.

> **Neri:**  
> “Don't feel good.”

Bao immediately sat beside her.

Neri did not object.

---

# 5. PLUSH FORM

Neri has continued to use her Plush Form intermittently.

The transformation provides significant comfort.

However, personnel are reminded:

> **Plush Form does not erase existing injury.**

The transformation changes Neri's physical properties and greatly reduces ordinary pain perception, but it does not retroactively eliminate damage that occurred while she was vulnerable.

Accordingly, Neri may still appear comfortable while requiring continued medical observation.

Personnel must not interpret:

> **“She's plush again.”**

as:

> **“She's completely fine.”**

---

# 6. MOBILITY

Neri's mobility has temporarily declined again.

She can move independently under favorable conditions.

However, she frequently chooses not to.

This is partly due to weakness and partly because movement can aggravate her discomfort.

Current observations include:

- sitting rather than standing;
    
- lying down rather than walking;
    
- requesting transportation;
    
- accepting assistance from residents;
    
- stopping halfway through a corridor;
    
- and remaining in one location until someone checks on her.
    

When offered a ride, Neri generally accepts.

She no longer appears particularly concerned about imposing on the person helping her.

This is considered preferable to forcing herself to walk while unwell.

---

# 7. BAO'S OBSERVATION

Bao remains closely involved in Neri's recovery.

During one examination, Bao asked:

> **BAO:**  
> “Where does it hurt?”

Neri pointed toward her chest.

Bao's expression changed.

> **BAO:**  
> “Still?”

Neri nodded.

> **BAO:**  
> “Anywhere else?”

Neri pointed toward several other areas.

> **BAO:**  
> “All of that?”

Neri nodded again.

Bao sighed quietly.

> **BAO:**  
> “You've been hurting this whole time.”

Neri looked away.

After several seconds:

> **Neri:**  
> “Didn't want to bother anyone.”

Bao did not answer immediately.

She simply adjusted the blanket around Neri.

---

# 8. JANE DOE

Jane has maintained considerable distance from Neri while allowing Neri to determine whether contact is welcome.

This has been particularly important because Jane was present during several of Neri's earlier recovery periods.

On one occasion, Jane approached only far enough to leave a cup of coffee on the table.

She did not sit down.

Neri looked at the coffee.

Then at Jane.

> **Neri:**  
> “Thank you.”

Jane nodded.

> **JANE:**  
> “You're welcome.”

Jane left.

Neri drank the coffee.

No further interaction was attempted.

---

# 9. MSAC RESPONSE

The MSAC Operator responsible for the original incident remains absent from Neri's immediate environment.

Following the incident, the operator apologized and subsequently resigned.

No reconciliation has been forced.

Neri remains visibly uncomfortable discussing the event.

When asked whether she wanted to talk about what happened:

> **Neri:**  
> “No.”

The matter was dropped.

This is consistent with current welfare guidance.

---

# 10. MEDICAL CONCERN

The most significant concern is not the visible external injury.

It is the persistence of symptoms.

Neri's physiology is already unusually fragile due to her altered blood system and cardiovascular instability.

A severe impact can therefore have consequences beyond what an external examination might initially suggest.

Personnel are instructed to report any significant deterioration immediately.

Particular attention should be given to:

- worsening chest pain;
    
- altered consciousness;
    
- breathing difficulty;
    
- sudden weakness;
    
- inability to remain awake;
    
- unusual pallor;
    
- collapse;
    
- or any sudden change from her current baseline.
    

Neri should not be expected to determine whether a symptom is medically important herself.

---

# 11. NERI'S CURRENT ROUTINE

Her current daily routine has become extremely simple.

> **Sleep.**

> **Wake.**

> **Drink something.**

> **Eat when able.**

> **Rest.**

> **Read occasionally.**

> **Drink coffee when she actually wants it.**

> **Sleep again.**

Chemistry research has been temporarily reduced.

The Limited Merchandise accessory program is being handled administratively while Neri recovers.

No one is asking her to catch up.

---

# 12. OBSERVATION — EVENING

At approximately 21:30, Neri was found lying on the SAFEHOUSE couch.

She was awake.

Her glasses had slipped slightly down her nose.

Her coat sleeves covered most of her hands.

A blanket had been placed over her.

Her coffee sat untouched on the table.

Bao entered.

> **BAO:**  
> “Still hurting?”

Neri nodded.

> **BAO:**  
> “Still feeling sick?”

Another nod.

Bao sat nearby.

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't need to do anything tonight.”

Neri looked at her.

A few seconds passed.

> **Neri:**  
> “Okay.”

She closed her eyes.

Bao remained nearby without asking anything else.

---

# 13. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Neri's current condition represents a **continuing medical concern**.

The fact that she is conscious, capable of communicating, and gradually improving does not mean recovery is complete.

The impact was severe.

Her body is still recovering.

Her current exhaustion and persistent pain should therefore be treated as genuine symptoms rather than behavioral withdrawal.

Most importantly:

> **Neri is not being dramatic.**

She is not seeking attention.

She is not exaggerating.

She is simply still hurting.

And, unusually for Neri, she has started admitting it.

---

# ARCHIVIST NOTE

The original incident lasted approximately several seconds.

The consequences have lasted considerably longer.

Neri was thrown.

She hit the wall.

Everyone initially focused on whether she was seriously injured.

Now the more frustrating question is:

> **Why does she still feel terrible?**

The answer is unfortunately simple.

Because being thrown into a wall with the force of a baseball is a very effective way to make a ten-year-old feel terrible.

Neri's current medical objective is therefore extremely unambitious:

**Recover.**

No chemistry.

No trials.

No Limited Merchandise.

No competitions.

No proving anything.

Just recovery.

She has already spent enough time pretending pain isn't important.

For now, C.O.R.E. would prefer that she simply admit when something hurts.

And, for once, Neri has.

> **“Don't feel good.”**

That's enough information.
