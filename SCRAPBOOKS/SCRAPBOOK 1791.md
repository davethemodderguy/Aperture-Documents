# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake's favorite food is not a specific dish.

It is **anything cooked by Bao, Clover, or Phoenix.**

His preferred meals are generally warm, soft, and easy to eat, particularly:

- stews;
    
- mashed potatoes;
    
- thick soups;
    
- slow-cooked vegetables;
    
- tender meat dishes;
    
- and other homemade comfort foods.
    

Jake demonstrates a particularly strong preference for meals prepared by the three residents he trusts most in this context.

Bao's cooking makes him feel cared for.

Clover's cooking makes him feel at home.

Phoenix's cooking makes him happy in a way she has not yet learned to handle normally.

The distinction between these preferences is subtle but observable.

The food itself matters.

**Who made it matters more.**

C.O.R.E. has therefore concluded that Jake's dietary preferences are not exclusively culinary.

They are also relational.

A bowl of mashed potatoes prepared by Bao is not merely mashed potatoes.

It is Bao making sure he has eaten.

A stew prepared by Clover is not merely dinner.

It is something warm waiting for him in the room they share.

A meal prepared by Phoenix is not merely food.

It is Phoenix thinking about him when he is somewhere else.

Jake does not articulate these distinctions in such terms.

He simply eats, usually finishes his serving, and occasionally asks for more.

For the SAFEHOUSE residents concerned, this has proven considerably more persuasive than any verbal expression of affection.

---

# 1. GENERAL FOOD PREFERENCES

Jake does not require elaborate meals to be satisfied.

His preferred foods tend to share several characteristics:

- warm rather than cold;
    
- soft rather than excessively crunchy;
    
- savory rather than overwhelmingly sweet;
    
- comforting rather than extravagant;
    
- and, most importantly, prepared by someone he likes.
    

Stews and mashed potatoes are his most consistently favored dishes.

He enjoys the way stew combines its ingredients into a single warm meal. He particularly likes tender pieces of meat, soft vegetables, and thick gravy.

Mashed potatoes are even simpler.

They are warm, soft, filling, and easy to eat without much effort.

Jake has never expressed a desire for complicated presentation.

He does not care whether a dish is arranged attractively on the plate.

He does not care whether the cook has used an expensive ingredient.

He does not care whether the meal resembles something served in a restaurant.

If Bao, Clover, or Phoenix made it, he is generally willing to try it.

If it is stew or mashed potatoes, he is likely to enjoy it.

If both conditions are satisfied, personnel have observed an increased probability of Jake requesting seconds.

---

# 2. BAO — THE COMFORT OF A HOME-COOKED MEAL

Bao's cooking occupies a particularly important place in Jake's daily routine.

Her meals are familiar, consistent, and prepared with his needs in mind.

She pays attention to whether he has eaten enough, whether he is tired, and whether he needs something warm after spending too much time in his room.

Jake rarely needs to explain himself.

Bao generally notices before he asks.

### Observation: Mashed Potatoes

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Kitchen  
**TIME:** 18:12

Bao placed a bowl of mashed potatoes in front of Jake.

A small portion of stew sat beside it.

Jake looked at the bowl.

Then at Bao.

**BAO:**  
“Eat while it's warm.”

Jake nodded.

He picked up his spoon.

Several minutes passed.

Bao continued preparing food for the other residents.

Jake finished the potatoes.

He looked at the bowl.

Then toward the kitchen.

**JAKE:**  
“More?”

Bao turned around.

Her expression softened immediately.

**BAO:**  
“Of course.”

She took the bowl.

Jake waited patiently.

When she returned, he accepted the additional serving with both hands.

**JAKE:**  
“Thank you.”

**BAO:**  
“You're welcome, sweetheart.”

Jake began eating again.

Bao watched for another moment before returning to the stove.

### Assessment

Jake's willingness to ask Bao for more food is a useful indicator of comfort.

He does not appear to interpret accepting additional help as an obligation to repay it.

At least, not in this context.

Bao simply feeds him.

Jake simply eats.

For once, the arrangement requires no negotiation.

---

# 3. CLOVER — FOOD WITHOUT EXPECTATIONS

Clover's approach to cooking is less maternal than Bao's.

She does not routinely ask Jake whether he has eaten enough.

She does not hover over him.

She is more likely to put a bowl beside him while he is reading and tell him to move his book before he spills gravy on it.

Jake appears to appreciate this.

Their shared room allows meals to become part of their ordinary routine rather than a formal social event.

Clover can cook.

Jake can eat.

Neither is required to turn dinner into a conversation.

### Observation: The Roommate Stew

**LOCATION:** Jake and Clover's Residence  
**TIME:** 19:04

Clover entered the room carrying two bowls.

Jake was sitting on his bed with his teddy bear beside him.

His wings were folded loosely behind his back.

He looked up.

**JAKE:**  
“What's that?”

**CLOVER:**  
“Stew.”

Jake closed his book.

Clover handed him a bowl.

He accepted it.

**JAKE:**  
“You made this?”

**CLOVER:**  
“No, a mysterious kitchen ghost did.”

Jake looked at her.

Clover grinned.

**JAKE:**  
“Okay.”

He began eating.

Clover sat at her desk with her own bowl.

For approximately ten minutes, the room was quiet except for the occasional sound of a spoon against ceramic.

Then Jake spoke.

**JAKE:**  
“It's good.”

Clover glanced over her shoulder.

**CLOVER:**  
“Yeah?”

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:**  
“Really good.”

Clover smiled to herself.

**CLOVER:**  
“There's more in the kitchen.”

Jake looked into his bowl.

Then at Clover.

**JAKE:**  
“Can I have more later?”

**CLOVER:**  
“Whenever you want.”

Jake returned to eating.

Clover opened her notebook.

Neither considered the interaction particularly remarkable.

That is precisely why it works.

# 4. PHOENIX — THE PROBLEM WITH COOKING FOR JAKE

Phoenix's situation is more complicated.

She likes Jake.

She likes cooking for people she cares about.

She also likes seeing Jake enjoy something she made.

Unfortunately, Jake has a habit of being completely sincere when he enjoys food.

This has caused Phoenix several difficulties.

### Incident: Mashed Potatoes

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Dining Area  
**TIME:** 17:46

Phoenix placed a bowl of mashed potatoes in front of Jake.

She had prepared them herself.

Jake looked at the bowl.

**JAKE:**  
“Is this for me?”

**PHOENIX:**  
“Of course.”

Jake picked up his spoon.

Phoenix sat opposite him, attempting to behave normally.

Jake took a bite.

He paused.

Then took another.

Phoenix watched.

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:**  
“This is good.”

Phoenix's expression immediately changed.

**PHOENIX:**  
“Really?”

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:**  
“Really.”

He took another bite.

**JAKE:**  
“I like it.”

Phoenix looked away.

Her ears had turned red.

Jake noticed.

**JAKE:**  
“Are you hot?”

**PHOENIX:**  
“No.”

**JAKE:**  
“Your face is red.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“Eat your potatoes, Jake.”

Jake considered this instruction reasonable.

He continued eating.

A minute later, he held out his empty bowl.

**JAKE:**  
“More, please.”

Phoenix stared at him.

He stared back.

She took the bowl.

**PHOENIX:**  
“Of course.”

She returned to the kitchen with the expression of someone who had just been entrusted with a highly classified object.

Clover, who had witnessed the entire exchange, followed her.

**CLOVER:**  
“You know, you could just admit that you like feeding him.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“I do like feeding him.”

**CLOVER:**  
“And?”

Phoenix looked down at the bowl.

**PHOENIX:**  
“He asked for seconds.”

Clover smiled.

**CLOVER:**  
“Tragic.”

Phoenix nearly dropped the serving spoon.

# 5. THE STEW PREFERENCE

Jake's fondness for stew has become sufficiently consistent that Bao, Clover, and Phoenix have independently developed their own versions for him.

They do not prepare identical dishes.

Bao favors balanced, nourishing meals with tender ingredients and a rich broth.

Clover prefers practical recipes that can be prepared in larger quantities and reheated without losing their quality.

Phoenix tends to put more effort into presentation, seasoning, and making the meal feel special.

Jake likes all three.

When asked to choose a favorite, he has repeatedly declined to rank them.

### Recorded exchange

**C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
“Which stew do you like best?”

**JAKE:**  
“All of them.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Even if you had to choose one?”

Jake frowned.

**JAKE:**  
“Why?”

**OFFICER:**  
“For the assessment.”

**JAKE:**  
“That's a stupid assessment.”

The officer paused.

Jake continued eating.

**JAKE:**  
“They're different.”

**OFFICER:**  
“How?”

Jake thought for a moment.

**JAKE:**  
“Mom's tastes like Mom's.”

A pause.

**JAKE:**  
“Clover's tastes like Clover's.”

Another pause.

**JAKE:**  
“Phoenix's tastes like Phoenix's.”

**OFFICER:**  
“And which is best?”

Jake looked at the officer as if the answer should have been obvious.

**JAKE:**  
“They made it.”

The officer discontinued the ranking exercise.

# 6. FOOD AS A FORM OF CARE

C.O.R.E. Welfare Division considers Jake's food preferences significant for reasons beyond nutrition.

Jake has historically been more comfortable accepting care when it is offered through ordinary activities.

He may not always ask for company.

He may not always explain what he is feeling.

He may prefer to spend an afternoon in his room rather than participate in a group activity.

But food gives residents a simple way to care for him without demanding anything in return.

Bao can leave a bowl of stew for him.

Clover can make an extra portion while preparing dinner.

Phoenix can ask whether he would like mashed potatoes.

Jake can accept the meal.

No confession is necessary.

No prolonged conversation is required.

No one needs to ask him to explain what the gesture means.

The food is warm.

The person who made it cares about him.

For Jake, that is often enough.

---

# 7. RESIDENT WELFARE GUIDANCE

Personnel should continue respecting Jake's preferences without turning them into a rigid dietary routine.

Recommended practices include:

- Offer familiar meals when practical.
    
- Allow Jake to request additional portions according to his needs.
    
- Avoid using food as a reward for compliance.
    
- Do not pressure him to eat in shared spaces if he prefers a quieter setting.
    
- Do not insist that he verbally express gratitude.
    
- Respect his appetite and any medical dietary guidance.
    
- Do not treat his preference for food prepared by specific residents as evidence that he is incapable of eating anything else.
    

Jake can eat food prepared by other residents.

He simply has particular favorites.

There is a difference between **being able to eat a meal** and **being especially happy that someone made it for you.**

C.O.R.E. considers that distinction relevant to Jake's welfare.

# 8. CLOSING OBSERVATION — THREE BOWLS

At 19:23, Jake was observed sitting in the dining area.

Three bowls had been placed on the table.

Bao had prepared stew.

Clover had made mashed potatoes.

Phoenix had brought a small serving of another homemade dish.

The arrangement had not been planned as a formal meal.

Each resident had simply made something Jake liked.

Jake looked at the food.

Then at the three residents.

**JAKE:**  
“Did you all make these?”

**BAO:**  
“Yes, sweetheart.”

**CLOVER:**  
“Don't look at me. I was hungry too.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“I thought you might like to try mine.”

Jake nodded.

He took a little from each dish.

He tried Bao's stew first.

Then Clover's potatoes.

Then Phoenix's cooking.

He ate quietly.

After a while, he looked up.

**JAKE:**  
“Thank you.”

All three responded almost simultaneously.

**BAO:**  
“You're welcome.”

**CLOVER:**  
“Anytime.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“Of course.”

Jake returned to his meal.

His tail curled around the leg of his chair.

His wings relaxed behind him.

The teddy bear sat on the empty chair beside him, facing the table as though it had also been invited to dinner.

For several minutes, nobody asked Jake anything.

Nobody required him to talk.

Nobody needed him to explain why he liked the food.

They simply stayed.

And Jake, surrounded by three people who had cooked for him, ate until he was satisfied.

## C.O.R.E. FINAL NOTE

Jake's favorite food is stew.

And mashed potatoes.

And whatever else Bao, Clover, or Phoenix decides to cook.

The kitchen staff initially interpreted this as a culinary preference.

Further observation suggests that the food is only part of it.

Jake does not need the meal to be elaborate.

He does not need it to be expensive.

He does not need it to be presented beautifully.

He just needs to know that someone thought of him.

Bao makes him a bowl of stew because she wants him to eat well.

Clover makes extra food because Jake lives with her, and there should be enough for both of them.

Phoenix makes mashed potatoes because she remembers that he likes them, and because making something for him is one of the easiest ways she knows to show affection.

Jake accepts all of it.

Sometimes he asks for seconds.

Sometimes he thanks them.

Sometimes he says nothing at all and simply keeps eating.

And sometimes, when the bowl is empty, he looks up at the person who made it and quietly asks:

**“Is there more?”**

For Bao, Clover, and Phoenix, the answer is almost always yes.
