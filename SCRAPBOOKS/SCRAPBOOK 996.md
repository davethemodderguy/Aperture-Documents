# 1. Medical Development

Following successful resuscitation, Neri was initially considered medically stable enough for continued observation.

A subsequent examination revealed an unexpected complication.

**Neri could no longer produce her normal speaking voice.**

Initial attempts to communicate verbally resulted in little to no audible output.

Neri was conscious.

She understood what was being said to her.

She attempted to respond.

Nothing came out.

---

# 2. Cause

Medical examination indicates that the extreme physiological shock associated with the incident unintentionally resulted in damage severe enough to **sever Neri's vocal cords**.

The damage occurred as a consequence of the critical event.

There is no indication that the injury was intentional.

There is likewise no evidence that Neri caused the injury through any deliberate action.

The exact mechanism remains under investigation.

---

# 3. Prognosis

**ETA FOR RECOVERY: UNKNOWN.**

At present, C.O.R.E. cannot determine whether Neri's vocal function will return naturally, require intervention, or remain permanently impaired.

No reliable recovery timeline can currently be provided.

Medical personnel have therefore been instructed not to give Neri an estimated recovery date.

---

# 4. Initial Psychological Response

Neri's first reaction upon realizing she could no longer speak was reportedly confusion.

She attempted again.

And again.

Eventually, she stopped trying.

When asked whether she understood the question being presented to her, Neri nodded.

When asked whether she was in pain, she shook her head.

When asked whether she wanted to try speaking again, she shook her head.

---

# 5. Jane Doe — Observation

Jane was present during the initial discovery of Neri's inability to speak.

Neri looked toward her.

She attempted to say her name.

No sound was produced.

Jane immediately understood what Neri was attempting to say.

**JANE:**  
"Don't force it."

Neri looked down.

Jane crouched beside her.

**JANE:**  
"You don't need your voice for me to know it's you."

Neri remained silent.

Jane continued:

**JANE:**  
"You asked me for help."

Pause.

**JANE:**  
"I heard you."

Neri looked back at her.

**JANE:**  
"Next time, we'll make sure somebody hears you sooner."

---

# 6. Communication Accommodation

Until further notice, Neri will be permitted and encouraged to communicate through:

- Writing.
    
- Hand signals.
    
- Gestures.
    
- Pre-existing communication devices.
    
- Digital text.
    
- Any other method that does not place strain on the damaged vocal structures.
    

Personnel are specifically instructed **not to pressure Neri into speaking**.

"Just try talking" is not considered an appropriate medical recommendation.

---

# 7. SAFEHOUSE Impact

The loss of Neri's voice represents a significant change for the SAFEHOUSE.

Neri was already a quiet resident.

She frequently communicated through short statements, gestures, facial expressions, and simply sitting beside people.

The difference now is that her silence is no longer merely behavioral.

**She physically cannot speak.**

This distinction must be respected.

Personnel are advised not to interpret her silence as:

- unwillingness to communicate;
    
- anger;
    
- emotional withdrawal;
    
- refusal to cooperate;
    
- or an indication that she does not require assistance.
    

Neri remains fully capable of understanding others.

## C.O.R.E. Closing Note

There is an unfortunate irony in the timing of the injury.

Neri spent months being encouraged to speak when she was hurting.

During the incident, she finally did.

She said:

> **"Jane, help. I can't breathe."**

Jane heard her.

Neri survived.

But now, for an undetermined period of time, she cannot say those words again.

**Medical personnel are reminded that loss of voice does not mean loss of communication.**

If Neri has something to say, **listen to how she says it.**
