**SUBJECT:** Jake Akana — Sleep Absence, Self-Neglect, and Failure to Request Assistance

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana does not sleep.

He cannot.

Since returning in his current spectral manifestation, Jake has spent much of his time outside his private residence, hovering through corridors, occupying quiet corners, and remaining near other residents.

At first, this behavior was interpreted as a preference for being outdoors.

Subsequent observation suggests a more concerning explanation.

**Jake has no ordinary sleep cycle.**

He does not lie down because he is tired.

He does not retreat to his room at night to rest.

He does not experience the familiar transition between wakefulness and sleep that most residents require.

Instead, he remains awake.

And because he remains awake, he remains available.

Available to accompany Aries.

Available to stay near Bao.

Available to follow other residents through the Blacksite.

Available whenever somebody needs company.

The problem is that Jake rarely considers whether _he_ needs anything.

Personnel have repeatedly observed him remaining silent when uncomfortable, concealing distress, and continuing to accompany others despite indications that he is suffering.

He does not reliably call for help.

He does not want to interrupt anyone.

He does not want to inconvenience anyone.

He appears to have developed a pattern in which the needs of others take precedence over his own, even when those needs conflict with his welfare.

C.O.R.E. is particularly concerned that Jake's inability to sleep may conceal the extent of his exhaustion or distress from residents accustomed to using sleep as a visible indicator of someone's need for rest.

Jake never goes to bed.

Therefore, nobody sees him getting up the next morning.

Nobody notices that he has been awake all night unless they happen to notice him.

And Jake has given personnel very little reason to believe he will ask them to notice.

---

# 1. SLEEP ABSENCE

Jake has not demonstrated a conventional sleep cycle since his return as a ghost.

He remains active throughout the day and night.

He may hover through residential corridors, sit beside windows, accompany other residents, or remain outside for prolonged periods.

He has not been observed preparing for sleep.

He has not been observed retiring to bed with the expectation of waking later.

He does not appear to become conventionally drowsy.

The distinction between his behavior and that of other residents is substantial.

Most residents eventually become unavailable because they need to sleep.

Jake does not.

This has created an unusual social dynamic.

When Aries goes to bed, Jake may remain outside his room.

When Bao retires for the night, Jake may continue hovering through SAFEHOUSE.

When other residents stop interacting, Jake may simply find somewhere else to spend his time.

He is almost always somewhere.

He is almost always awake.

And, increasingly, personnel have begun to treat these facts as though they mean Jake is always fine.

They do not.

**The absence of sleep is not evidence of the absence of suffering.**

---

# 2. OUTSIDE

Jake spends a disproportionate amount of time outside his private residence.

He may be found in:

- exterior observation areas;
    
- quiet corridors;
    
- sheltered walkways;
    
- residential entrances;
    
- windowside seating areas;
    
- and other locations where he can remain near people without necessarily joining them.
    

He does not always have a destination.

Sometimes he simply moves.

Sometimes he remains in one place for hours.

Sometimes he accompanies another resident until that person returns to their room, then continues elsewhere.

He rarely explains why.

Personnel have occasionally asked whether he wants to go inside.

His responses are generally brief.

**JAKE:**  
"I'm fine."

**JAKE:**  
"I want to stay here."

**JAKE:**  
"It's okay."

These statements are not inherently concerning.

Jake is entitled to choose where he spends his time.

The concern arises when personnel treat these answers as conclusive evidence that nothing is wrong.

Jake has repeatedly demonstrated that he is reluctant to report discomfort.

His willingness to remain outside therefore cannot be treated as a reliable indicator of his physical or emotional condition.

---

# 3. THE SELF-NEGLECT PATTERN

Jake consistently prioritizes other residents over himself.

This is not necessarily visible in dramatic gestures.

It appears in small decisions.

He stays with Aries because Aries is upset.

He remains nearby when Bao is busy.

He follows someone who appears lonely.

He waits when another person needs time.

He does not complain when he is uncomfortable.

He does not ask others to change their plans for him.

He does not interrupt conversations to announce that something is wrong.

And when somebody asks whether he needs anything, he frequently says no.

The resulting pattern is deceptively simple.

Someone else has a need.

Jake notices.

Jake responds.

Jake's own condition remains unmentioned.

This creates the impression that Jake is unusually accommodating.

In practice, it means his welfare can disappear from consideration even when he is standing directly in front of someone.

Personnel see a child who is always available.

They do not necessarily see a child who has learned to make himself easy to accommodate by asking for almost nothing.

---

# 4. FAILURE TO REQUEST HELP

Jake's reluctance to call for help is one of the most significant findings in this observation period.

He can communicate.

He can speak.

He can identify familiar residents.

He can express preferences.

His silence is not evidence that he lacks the ability to ask for assistance.

Rather, he appears reluctant to do so.

When uncomfortable, he may remain quiet.

When frightened, he may wait for someone else to notice.

When distressed, he may withdraw rather than interrupt another person.

When asked directly whether he is alright, he may give a brief answer that ends the conversation.

This behavior makes informal welfare monitoring unreliable.

A resident who openly complains can be assisted.

A resident who asks for help can be heard.

Jake may do neither.

His needs must therefore be assessed through attentive, respectful interaction rather than waiting for him to announce that he has reached a limit.

This does not authorize personnel to interrogate him or disregard his autonomy.

It does require personnel to stop assuming that a lack of complaint means a lack of need.

---

# 5. OBSERVATION — RESIDENTIAL ENTRANCE

**TIME:** 02:16  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — EXTERIOR RESIDENTIAL WALKWAY

Jake was observed hovering beside the entrance to the residential wing.

The area was quiet.

He had remained there for approximately forty minutes.

A welfare officer approached.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Jake? What are you doing out here?"

**JAKE:**  
"Nothing."

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Are you waiting for someone?"

Jake shook his head.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Do you want to come inside?"

**JAKE:**  
"No, thank you."

The officer remained nearby for several seconds.

Jake did not initiate further conversation.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Is something wrong?"

**JAKE:**  
"No."

The officer considered the answer.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Alright. I'll be inside if you need me."

Jake nodded.

The officer returned indoors.

Jake remained outside.

At 03:04, another resident passed through the entrance.

Jake immediately turned toward them.

**JAKE:**  
"Hi."

The resident stopped.

They exchanged a few words before the resident continued inside.

Jake watched them leave.

He then returned to his previous position.

No request for assistance was made.

No distress was reported.

The observation was initially recorded as uneventful.

On review, the welfare officer amended the assessment.

Jake had spent nearly an hour outside, had no apparent destination, and had declined an invitation to return indoors.

None of those facts establishes distress.

However, the observation also revealed something else.

Jake had remained silent throughout the interaction until another person appeared.

He then initiated contact immediately.

He appeared more concerned with the arrival of another person than with his own circumstances.

The distinction was noted for further observation.

# 6. ARIES AKANA — THE ASSUMPTION OF CONSTANT AVAILABILITY

Aries has occasionally found Jake outside his room at times when most residents would be asleep.

This has become familiar enough that Aries no longer reacts with surprise.

He may tell Jake that he needs to rest.

Jake generally accepts this without protest.

However, Aries has also expressed concern that Jake remains outside for prolonged periods without anyone knowing where he is.

During a private welfare discussion, Aries stated:

**ARIES:**  
"He doesn't sleep."

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"We're aware."

**ARIES:**  
"No, I mean he doesn't sleep. Ever."

The officer allowed him to continue.

**ARIES:**  
"I can tell him I'm tired. I can tell him I'm going to bed. He just says okay."

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Does he ask you to stay?"

**ARIES:**  
"Sometimes."

A pause.

**ARIES:**  
"But mostly he just says it's fine."

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Does that concern you?"

Aries looked down.

**ARIES:**  
"Because sometimes I think he wants me to stay, but he doesn't want to ask."

He was silent for several seconds.

**ARIES:**  
"And I don't know how often that happens when I'm not looking."

The concern is valid.

Jake's apparent acceptance of separation should not automatically be interpreted as emotional indifference.

At the same time, Aries must not be made responsible for identifying every unspoken need his brother may have.

He cannot be expected to read Jake's mind.

Nor should he be expected to remain awake simply because Jake cannot sleep.

The appropriate response is a broader support network, not another obligation placed upon Aries.

**JAKE:**

Whether it had been believed was another matter.

# 8. PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

C.O.R.E. does not consider Jake's behavior sufficient to establish a specific psychological diagnosis.

The available evidence does, however, support concern regarding a pattern of self-neglect and reluctance to seek support.

Several factors may contribute.

### A. Lack of an ordinary sleep cycle

Because Jake does not sleep, he lacks one of the most visible indicators that a person needs to stop and rest.

Other residents naturally become unavailable.

Jake remains available indefinitely.

This may encourage him to continue accompanying others even when he would otherwise benefit from disengaging.

### B. Fear of being a burden

Jake's reluctance to ask for help may reflect concern that doing so would inconvenience others.

This remains a hypothesis rather than an established motive.

However, his repeated tendency to prioritize other residents while minimizing his own needs makes it a reasonable concern.

### C. Grief and attachment

Jake has experienced his own death and a subsequent disappearance that caused severe distress to his brother.

He may be particularly sensitive to the possibility that someone else will be left alone or distressed.

His willingness to remain beside others may partly reflect a desire to prevent them from experiencing the same isolation he has experienced.

This interpretation has not been confirmed directly by Jake.

### D. Difficulty identifying personal needs

Jake's response to Bao's question—"I don't know"—is notable.

He may recognize another person's distress more readily than his own.

Alternatively, he may understand his needs but be reluctant to express them.

C.O.R.E. cannot currently distinguish between these possibilities.

Both warrant a patient, non-coercive approach.

---

# 9. RESPONSE PROTOCOL — DO NOT WAIT FOR JAKE TO ASK

Personnel are instructed to make support available without requiring Jake to initiate every interaction.

This does not mean constantly questioning him.

It means making ordinary offers of companionship and assistance.

Appropriate examples include:

> "I'm going to sit here for a while. You're welcome to join me."

> "Would you like some company?"

> "You can come with me if you want."

> "You don't have to explain anything."

> "You can tell me if something hurts."

> "If you need help, you won't be interrupting me."

Personnel should avoid repeatedly asking whether Jake is alright and accepting a brief "yes" as the end of every welfare interaction.

Instead, they may offer concrete choices.

Would he prefer to remain outside or move somewhere sheltered?

Would he like someone to accompany him?

Would he prefer quiet company rather than conversation?

Does he want help with something specific?

These questions give Jake an opportunity to express a preference without requiring him to formulate an elaborate explanation.

If he declines, his answer should generally be respected unless there is an immediate and credible safety concern.

**The objective is not to force Jake to accept care. It is to make accepting care easier than asking for it.**

---

# 10. THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN KINDNESS AND SELF-ERASURE

Jake's willingness to stay with others is not itself a problem.

Companionship is valuable.

Helping someone through a difficult night is valuable.

Choosing to remain beside a frightened person is valuable.

C.O.R.E. does not intend to discourage Jake from doing these things.

The concern is that Jake may be treating his own needs as less important than those of everyone around him.

There is a difference between:

**"I want to stay with you."**

and

**"I cannot leave because you need me, even if I am suffering."**

There is a difference between:

**"I'm happy to help."**

and

**"I must never ask for help myself."**

Jake should be allowed to care for others.

He should also be allowed to be cared for.

These are not mutually exclusive.

---

# 11. FINAL WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Jake's inability to sleep means that conventional rest schedules cannot be applied to him in the usual manner.

His continued activity should not automatically be interpreted as a sign of good health.

His willingness to accompany other residents should not be treated as proof that he is emotionally well.

His silence should not be treated as proof that he has nothing to say.

And his failure to request help should not be interpreted as evidence that help is unnecessary.

C.O.R.E. recommends continued observation, voluntary companionship, and regular opportunities for Jake to communicate his needs without pressure.

No attempt should be made to force him into a sleep cycle that his current form may not support.

No resident should be expected to supervise him continuously.

The SAFEHOUSE community must instead ensure that Jake has access to support throughout the hours when other residents are asleep or unavailable.

His inability to sleep should not become an expectation that he will spend every night alone.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake does not sleep.

The rest of the Blacksite eventually goes quiet.

Doors close.

Lights go out.

Residents return to their rooms.

Conversations end.

People who spent the evening worrying about one another finally rest.

And Jake remains outside.

Sometimes he stays near Aries's door.

Sometimes he wanders through the corridors.

Sometimes he accompanies somebody who cannot bear the thought of being alone.

He does not complain.

He does not ask for anything.

He does not tell anyone that he might want company, too.

Perhaps he genuinely prefers the quiet.

Perhaps he is content to spend the night moving through the Blacksite.

Perhaps he has never learned how to recognize the moment when his own needs deserve attention.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet say.

But one thing is clear.

Jake can spend hours noticing when somebody else needs help.

He can recognize loneliness in another person.

He can remain beside someone who is frightened.

He can offer companionship without asking for anything in return.

And when the question is turned toward him—

When someone asks whether _he_ needs somebody—

He says he's fine.

Then he goes back outside.

Back to the corridors.

Back to being available.

Back to making sure nobody else has to be alone.

**Perhaps the most concerning thing about Jake is not that he never asks for help.**

It is that everyone may become so accustomed to his kindness that they forget to ask whether he needs help at all.

He does not need to become less kind.

He does not need to stop caring about people.

He does not need to learn to demand attention.

He simply needs to discover that his own suffering matters, even when someone else's suffering is easier to see.

Until then, personnel are reminded:

Do not wait for Jake to ask every time.

Sometimes, sit beside him.

Sometimes, check on him.

Sometimes, let him accompany you without making him responsible for your happiness.

And sometimes, when the Blacksite is quiet and everyone else has gone to sleep, make sure there is someone willing to spend a little time with the child who cannot.

Not because he is useful.

Not because he is a walking candle.

Not because he might be lonely.

But because **Jake deserves companionship even when he has nothing to give in return.**

— **C.O.R.E. Resident Welfare & Psychological Observation Division**
