## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora spent the entirety of the observed day inside his den.

He did not visit the common room.  
He did not join Jake and Mia for meals or activities.  
He did not play.  
He did not seek out anyone.

He remained inside his small, doghouse-like room built into the wall of Floor 7.

There was no significant communication.

No writing. No gestures intended to initiate conversation. No attempt to use the replacement whiteboard.

The white wool scarf knitted by Mia and Jake remained inside the den. Sora had touched it when it was first presented, but no further interaction with it was recorded during this observation period.

The distinction between physical presence and social presence has become increasingly important.

Sora is still on Floor 7. His den is still occupied. His belongings remain where they belong.

But the child who once followed Jake around, sat with Mia, and gradually began to smile is currently absent from the life of the household.

He has not physically disappeared.

He has withdrawn from it.

## 4. PSYCHOLOGICAL AND WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Sora's withdrawal remains consistent with significant emotional distress following the destruction of his original whiteboard and the manner in which he learned of its loss.

The original board was not merely a writing surface.

It was his primary means of communicating. It was familiar, portable, and associated with Jake's initial efforts to establish contact with him.

Its destruction removed more than an object. It removed a communication method that Sora had trusted and relied upon.

The replacement board, despite bearing Jake's signature, remains unacceptable to him.

The scarf has been accepted at least to the extent that Sora touched it. However, this gesture has not yet translated into renewed engagement.

C.O.R.E. identifies the following concerns:

- Prolonged isolation within his den.
    
- Reduced interaction with Mia and Jake.
    
- Lack of reliable communication during distress.
    
- Uncertainty regarding food and fluid intake.
    
- Reduced participation in ordinary daily activities.
    
- Increasing difficulty for staff in distinguishing silence from unexpressed distress.
    

His previous amnesia remains an additional complicating factor. Staff must not assume that every period of silence is caused by memory loss, nor should they assume that he remembers every interaction that occurred earlier in the day.

**The present observation does not establish that Sora has forgotten his siblings. It establishes that he is no longer participating in their shared routine.**

That distinction must be preserved.

---

## 5. REQUIRED STAFF RESPONSE

1. Continue regular welfare checks without crowding the entrance to his den.
    
2. Monitor food and water intake through direct, non-intrusive observation.
    
3. Keep the scarf and other personal belongings accessible.
    
4. Do not force the replacement whiteboard into his hands.
    
5. Continue offering alternative communication methods without demanding that he choose one immediately.
    
6. Maintain medical observation of his neck injuries.
    
7. Ensure that Mia and Jake are not made responsible for his isolation or recovery.
    
8. Record any changes in behavior, including small signs of engagement.
    

Staff are reminded that a child who cannot communicate verbally must not be left without a reliable way to summon help.

The den is a personal space, not a place where welfare oversight may be suspended.
