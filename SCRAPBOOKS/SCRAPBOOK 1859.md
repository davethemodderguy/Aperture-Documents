## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

C.O.R.E. has identified a concerning difference in how certain SAFEHOUSE residents perceive fatal injuries.

For most people, death represents the end of their life.

For Jake Akana and Phoenix, death appears to be an extraordinarily inconvenient interruption to whatever they were doing beforehand.

Jake has repeatedly died in accidental falls and subsequently resurrected through white fire. Phoenix, possessing Transcendence, has demonstrated a similarly casual attitude toward situations in which death would ordinarily be considered a significant consequence.

Both individuals have exhibited reactions that suggest their personal assessment of mortality differs considerably from that of the people around them.

This has created a peculiar household dynamic.

Jake and Phoenix are perfectly capable of discussing their own deaths without becoming particularly distressed.

Tom, meanwhile, appears to be aging approximately forty years every time his brother trips over his own feet.

Aries has requested permission to install padded flooring throughout the entire seventh floor.

Clover has requested that the floor be inspected first, on the grounds that Jake will probably find a way to fall off the padding.

**C.O.R.E. concurs with Clover.**

## 2. OBSERVED INCIDENT — THE CASUALTY REPORT THAT WASN'T

**Time:** 16:18  
**Location:** Floor 7, common room.

Jake was playing with a toy train. Phoenix was sitting on the sofa, apparently attempting to remain awake.

Tom was reading.

Aries was preparing tea.

Clover was doing her best to ensure that everyone in the room remained alive.

This last responsibility proved difficult.

Jake stood up, announced that he needed to retrieve another toy, caught his foot against the leg of the table, and fell.

There was an unpleasant impact.

A pause.

**JAKE:** "Ow."

Tom lowered his book.

**TOM:** "Are you okay?"

**JAKE:** "I think so."

Jake attempted to stand.

He fell again.

This time, the injury was fatal.

Tom's book hit the floor.

**TOM:** "JAKE!"

Aries immediately rushed over. Clover followed, while Phoenix opened one eye from the sofa.

White fire engulfed Jake.

Tom stopped beside the flames, hands trembling.

**TOM:** "Oh, come on. Not again."

Phoenix watched the white fire for a moment.

**PHOENIX:** "Oh. He's doing the thing."

Aries slowly turned toward her.

**ARIES:** "The _thing?_"

**PHOENIX:** "The fire thing."

**ARIES:** "PHOENIX, HE JUST DIED!"

**PHOENIX:** "Yeah, but he's coming back."

**TOM:** "THAT DOESN'T MAKE IT BETTER!"

**PHOENIX:** "I didn't say it made it better."

**TOM:** "YOU SAID IT LIKE HE'D SPILLED HIS DRINK!"

The fire subsided.

Jake reappeared, apparently healthy, and sat up.

**JAKE:** "Did I get the toy?"

Nobody answered.

Jake looked around.

**JAKE:** "...Did I at least fall closer to it?"

Tom stared at him.

**TOM:** "You DIED."

**JAKE:** "I know."

**TOM:** "YOU DIED, JAKE!"

**JAKE:** "Yeah, I remember the falling part."

**TOM:** "YOUR NECK—"

**JAKE:** "It's fine now!"

**TOM:** "THAT IS NOT THE POINT!"

Jake tilted his head.

**JAKE:** "Then what's the point?"

Tom opened his mouth.

He closed it.

He opened it again.

**TOM:** "THE POINT IS THAT YOU SHOULD NOT HAVE TO DIE TO GET A TOY!"

Jake considered this with remarkable seriousness.

**JAKE:** "...Okay, that makes sense."

He stood up.

Tom immediately caught him by the back of his shirt.

**TOM:** "SIT."

Jake sat.

**JAKE:** "Okay."

Phoenix gave a sleepy thumbs-up from the sofa.

**PHOENIX:** "Good talk."

Aries turned toward her.

**ARIES:** "You are not helping."

**PHOENIX:** "I'm supporting him emotionally."

**ARIES:** "YOU HAVEN'T EVEN SAT UP!"

**PHOENIX:** "I'm supporting him horizontally."

Clover had to turn away.

The incident report records that she was laughing.

## 3. THE MORTALITY DISCONNECT

A follow-up interview was conducted with Jake and Phoenix.

They were interviewed together because separating them would have required moving Phoenix off the sofa and persuading Jake not to wander away in search of cake.

C.O.R.E. considered this an unnecessary escalation.

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do either of you understand why the other residents are so upset when you die?"

**JAKE:** "Because they think I'm not coming back?"

**INTERVIEWER:** "Partly."

**PHOENIX:** "Because they care about us."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Correct."

Phoenix yawned.

**PHOENIX:** "But we're fine afterward."

**INTERVIEWER:** "That is not the same as being safe beforehand."

**PHOENIX:** "I know."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do you?"

Phoenix looked at the interviewer.

For once, she appeared completely awake.

**PHOENIX:** "Look, I'm not saying dying is good. It's just... when you know you're going to come back, it stops feeling like the end of everything. It's more like something horrible happened, and then you have to deal with the aftermath."

Jake nodded enthusiastically.

**JAKE:** "Exactly! Like when you break a toy, except the toy is you, and then the toy fixes itself."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Jake, you are not a toy."

**JAKE:** "I know. I'm a person."

A pause.

**JAKE:** "A person who fixes himself."

**INTERVIEWER:** "That is not the lesson I wanted you to take from this."

Phoenix snorted.

**PHOENIX:** "He's got a point, though."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Phoenix."

**PHOENIX:** "What?"

**INTERVIEWER:** "You are supposed to be helping."

**PHOENIX:** "I am helping. I'm agreeing with him."

**INTERVIEWER:** "That is precisely the problem."

Jake swung his legs beneath the chair.

**JAKE:** "Can we have cake now?"

**INTERVIEWER:** "Why does everything lead back to cake?"

**JAKE:** "Because cake is good."

Phoenix nodded solemnly.

**PHOENIX:** "He's right."

The interviewer requested reassignment.

The request was denied.

## 4. TOM AKANA'S STATEMENT

Tom was interviewed separately.

Unlike Jake and Phoenix, he did not appear to regard resurrection as an adequate resolution to the problem.

**INTERVIEWER:** "Jake seems relatively unconcerned."

**TOM:** "Jake is unconcerned about a lot of things."

**INTERVIEWER:** "And Phoenix?"

**TOM:** "She's always tired. I don't think she has enough energy left to be properly concerned about anything."

He paused.

**TOM:** "They both think that because they come back, everything is okay."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do you think they're wrong?"

Tom looked down at his hands.

**TOM:** "I think they're alive afterward. That's different."

Silence.

**TOM:** "Jake doesn't remember how scary it is to watch him die. He remembers falling, then the fire, then waking up. I remember the part where he doesn't move."

His voice became quieter.

**TOM:** "And Phoenix jokes about it because she knows she'll be okay. Maybe that's how she deals with it. I don't know."

He looked toward the door.

**TOM:** "I just wish they'd understand that coming back doesn't mean nobody was scared."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Have you told Jake that?"

**TOM:** "Yes."

**INTERVIEWER:** "What did he say?"

Tom sighed.

**TOM:** "He hugged me and said he was sorry."

**INTERVIEWER:** "That sounds positive."

**TOM:** "Then he tripped over my foot."

The interviewer paused.

**TOM:** "He died again."

**INTERVIEWER:** "...During the conversation?"

**TOM:** "Before I finished the sentence."

Tom leaned back in his chair.

**TOM:** "I love my brother."

A long pause.

**TOM:** "I really, really love my brother."

Another pause.

**TOM:** "I need a vacation."

## 5. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

The available evidence suggests that Jake and Phoenix have developed a comparatively casual attitude toward their own mortality because their observed abilities allow them to return after death.

This attitude is understandable within their individual experiences, but it introduces several risks.

- **Risk normalization:** Fatal incidents may be dismissed because recovery is expected.
    
- **Delayed response:** Other residents may hesitate to treat a serious incident with appropriate urgency if resurrection is assumed to be inevitable.
    
- **Emotional isolation:** Jake and Phoenix may underestimate the distress their deaths cause to people who care about them.
    
- **Unsafe behavior:** A perceived lack of lasting consequences may reduce the urgency of ordinary precautions.
    

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that the last point is a concern, not a conclusion that either resident is deliberately reckless because they can resurrect. Jake's falls have been accidental, and Phoenix's casual comments do not establish that she seeks out danger.

Both residents remain entitled to care, protection, and medical assessment.

**Resurrection is a recovery mechanism. It is not a safety policy.**

The fact that a person can survive a fatal incident does not make the incident acceptable, harmless, or worth repeating.

## 6. FINAL OBSERVATION — 18:42

At the conclusion of the observation period, Jake and Phoenix were sitting together on the sofa.

Jake was eating cake.

Phoenix was asleep.

Tom was sitting on the floor beside them, one hand resting protectively against Jake's ankle to make sure he did not roll off the sofa.

Aries entered the room carrying a blanket.

**ARIES:** "Why is Phoenix asleep on the sofa again?"

**JAKE:** "She's recharging."

**ARIES:** "And why are you sitting on the edge?"

**JAKE:** "I like the edge."

**TOM:** "MOVE AWAY FROM THE EDGE."

Jake moved toward the middle.

**JAKE:** "Okay!"

He accidentally knocked the plate of cake off his lap.

Everyone watched it fall.

The plate hit the carpet.

Jake stared at it.

**JAKE:** "...Oh no."

Tom inhaled sharply.

**TOM:** "Jake. Don't move."

**JAKE:** "But my cake—"

**TOM:** "I WILL GET YOU ANOTHER PIECE."

Jake's eyes brightened.

**JAKE:** "Really?"

**TOM:** "YES. PLEASE. JUST. STAY. THERE."

**JAKE:** "Okay!"

Phoenix stirred.

Without opening her eyes, she mumbled:

**PHOENIX:** "If he dies again, save me a piece."

Aries looked at the ceiling.

Tom covered his face with both hands.

Clover, watching from the doorway, quietly added _cake-related incentives_ to the residential safety plan.

It was, by all available measures, the most effective intervention implemented that day.

### C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

To Jake and Phoenix, death is a minor inconvenience.

To Tom, it is the sound of his little brother hitting the floor.

To Aries, it is one more reason to demand better safety procedures.

To Clover, it is proof that supervising a household requires more patience than any reasonable job description should permit.

And to C.O.R.E., it is a reminder that surviving something does not mean it did not matter.

Jake may wake up from white fire with a smile and a question about cake. Phoenix may wake up from a nap, discover that someone has been discussing her mortality, and immediately fall asleep again.

Neither reaction means they are undeserving of protection.

They are not less precious because they can come back.

They are not less vulnerable because they can recover.

And they are certainly not less loved because the people around them are exhausted.

**Final recommendation:** Continue to protect both residents, even when they do not believe they need protecting.

**Supplementary recommendation:** Move the cake away from the edge of the sofa.
