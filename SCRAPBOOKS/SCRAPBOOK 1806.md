# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana has physically recovered from the injuries sustained during the assault in his private residence.

The stab wounds have healed.

His blood loss has been addressed.

His physical condition has stabilized sufficiently for the Medical Division to approve his transition out of intensive treatment.

By conventional medical assessment, Jake is recovering.

His psychological condition is considerably less reassuring.

Since the assault, Jake has developed a pronounced fear of being left alone.

The behavior is most evident when he is separated from people he trusts, particularly Aries Akana, Clover, Bao, and Phoenix.

Jake now exhibits distress when a familiar person leaves his immediate vicinity without explaining where they are going or when they will return.

He has begun checking whether people are still present.

He wakes when he cannot immediately locate someone nearby.

He becomes reluctant to enter rooms alone, even when those rooms are familiar to him.

When left without a trusted person in sight, he may become unusually quiet, repeatedly look toward the door, or attempt to follow the person who most recently left.

On several occasions, Jake has abandoned an activity he previously enjoyed simply because the person accompanying him stood up to leave.

The most concerning development is that Jake no longer appears to regard solitude as a neutral condition.

**He appears to associate being alone with the possibility that something terrible may happen before anyone can reach him.**

C.O.R.E. does not consider this behavior a sign of weakness, manipulation, or deliberate dependency.

Jake was attacked in a place where he should have been safe.

He was seriously injured while alone.

He attempted to make the assailant stop.

He was unable to prevent the attack.

Afterward, he was found by someone who cared about him.

His current fear is understandable in that context.

His body has healed.

His sense of safety has not.

**WOUND STATUS:** Healed  
**MOBILITY:** Returned to baseline, subject to routine medical guidance  
**CARDIAC VESSEL:** Teddy bear secured and functioning  
**PSYCHOLOGICAL RECOVERY:** Ongoing

The Medical Division has confirmed that Jake's injuries have healed sufficiently for routine activity.

No new physical injury has been identified.

His previous severe blood loss and the consequences of the assault remain relevant to his recovery, but the immediate threat posed by the stab wounds has passed.

The teddy bear remains with him.

It has not been removed as part of routine examination or room preparation.

Medical personnel understand that the bear is Jake's cardiac vessel and that its continued presence is essential to his well-being.

The Medical Division has also clarified that physical recovery must not be used as the sole criterion for determining whether Jake is ready to resume ordinary residential life.

A healed wound does not establish that a patient feels safe.

A patient being able to walk out of Medbay does not mean they are ready to spend the night alone.

And Jake's ability to smile, eat, read, or engage in conversation does not establish that his fear has disappeared.

# 2. FIRST NIGHT AFTER DISCHARGE

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — TEMPORARY RESIDENTIAL ROOM  
**OBSERVATION:** Unobtrusive welfare monitoring

Jake was discharged from intensive medical care after the Medical Division confirmed his physical stability.

A temporary room had been prepared while his original residence remained under forensic restriction.

Aries and Clover were informed that they could remain nearby.

The intention was to provide Jake with a familiar presence without crowding him or turning his recovery into a continuous observation exercise.

At 20:14, Clover accompanied Jake into the room.

Aries followed with the teddy bear's spare blanket, several of Jake's books, and a small bag of personal belongings recovered before the residential floor was sealed.

Jake sat on the bed.

He placed the teddy bear beside himself.

Clover put the books on the bedside table.

**CLOVER:**

**CLOVER:**

Then at Clover.

**JAKE:**  
"You're staying?"

Clover stopped arranging the books.

**CLOVER:**  
"Yes."

Jake looked toward Aries.

**JAKE:**  
"Both?"

**ARIES:**  
"For now."

Jake lowered his gaze.

He held the teddy bear against his chest.

Several seconds passed.

**JAKE:**  
"Okay."

Clover sat in the chair beside the bed.

Aries remained near the doorway.

For a while, nothing happened.

Jake eventually lay down.

He closed his eyes.

Then opened them again.

Clover was still there.

He closed them once more.

Approximately four minutes later, he opened them again.

**JAKE:**

**ARIES:**  
"No."

Jake watched him.

**ARIES:**  
"I'm going to sit down."

Jake followed the movement with his eyes.

Only after Aries had sat down did Jake settle beneath the blanket.

He did not fall asleep immediately.

But he stopped checking the door.

# 4. OBSERVED FEAR RESPONSES

C.O.R.E. has documented several recurring behaviors.

These are observations, not a formal psychiatric diagnosis.

## A. Repeated Presence Checks

Jake periodically looks up to confirm that someone remains nearby.

This may occur during meals, reading, medical appointments, or otherwise ordinary activities.

He does not always ask a question.

Sometimes he merely looks at the person.

If they acknowledge him, he usually returns to what he was doing.

If they do not notice, his attention may remain fixed on them until they respond.

## B. Difficulty Settling at Night

Jake has become reluctant to sleep if everyone else has left the room.

He may close his eyes but reopen them when he hears footsteps outside.

He sometimes asks whether the person beside him will still be there when he wakes.

He appears particularly uncomfortable when a room becomes completely quiet after someone leaves.

## C. Following Trusted Residents

Jake has begun following Aries and Clover between nearby rooms.

If his physical stamina permits, he may walk behind them without saying anything.

If he becomes tired, he may ask to be carried.

He does not always explain why he is following them.

On one occasion, Aries turned around and found Jake standing behind him in the corridor, holding the teddy bear by one arm.

**ARIES:**  
"Need something?"

Jake shook his head.

**ARIES:**  
"Then why are you here?"

Jake looked at him.

**JAKE:**  
"You left."

Aries went quiet.

Jake added:

**JAKE:**  
"I came too."

Aries did not tell him to go back.

He held out his hand.

Jake took it.

They continued walking together.

## D. Distress at Unannounced Departure

Jake reacts more strongly when a trusted person leaves without telling him.

This appears to be different from ordinary disappointment at someone going away.

The reaction is more immediate when he does not know whether the person intends to return.

A short explanation often helps.

A promise without a clear basis does not necessarily help.

C.O.R.E. has therefore instructed personnel to communicate departures honestly and avoid guarantees they cannot keep.

# 8. RESIDENT WELFARE PLAN

C.O.R.E. has implemented a recovery plan intended to help Jake regain a sense of safety without making constant supervision a permanent requirement.

The objective is not to force Jake to tolerate isolation.

Nor is it to ensure that he never experiences solitude.

The objective is to help him gradually learn that an ordinary departure does not necessarily mean abandonment or danger.

## Immediate Measures

- Jake will not be required to sleep alone while his fear remains acute.
    
- A trusted resident may remain nearby during periods of heightened distress.
    
- Departures should be communicated clearly and honestly.
    
- Personnel should explain when they expect to return, while acknowledging that schedules may change.
    
- Jake will be offered choices regarding who stays with him whenever practical.
    
- His teddy bear will remain with him.
    
- Medical and welfare staff will avoid unnecessary questioning about the assault.
    
- His room will remain free of the previously discovered symbols and any unverified ritual materials.
    

## Gradual Recovery

When Jake is ready, personnel may introduce small, predictable separations.

For example, a trusted person might explain that they are stepping into the adjacent room and will return after a short, agreed interval.

The person should follow through whenever possible.

If the plan changes, Jake should be told rather than left to discover the absence himself.

These steps are not tests.

Jake will not be rewarded for hiding his fear or criticized for asking someone to stay.

If a separation causes significant distress, personnel should reassess the pace rather than insist that he endure it.

## Prohibited Responses

Personnel must not:

- Tell Jake that he is too old to be frightened.
    
- Call him clingy, needy, or childish.
    
- Leave without warning to see how he reacts.
    
- Deliberately startle him by pretending to disappear.
    
- Use the assault as a reason to frighten him into compliance.
    
- Promise that nobody will ever hurt him again.
    
- Promise that a particular person will always be available.
    
- Treat his fear as evidence that he is permanently incapable of being alone.
    

**Trust is to be rebuilt through consistency, not demonstrations.**

# 9. SECURITY IMPLICATIONS

Jake's fear cannot be separated entirely from the unresolved security investigation.

The original assailant has not been conclusively identified.

The sacrificial dagger remains under examination.

The symbols discovered in Medbay have not been matched to a verified source.

The means by which the notes reached Jake's room remain unexplained.

C.O.R.E. cannot honestly tell Jake that the person responsible has been caught.

It cannot guarantee that another attempt is impossible.

However, the investigation must not allow uncertainty to become an atmosphere of permanent emergency around the patient.

Security personnel are responsible for addressing the external threat.

Jake is not responsible for anticipating it.

He will not be required to watch doors, identify suspicious individuals, or remain alert to protect himself.

His current fear may make him attentive to movement around him, but that does not make him an effective security system.

He is a child recovering from an assault.

The responsibility for keeping him safe belongs to the adults and organizations responsible for his care.

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's physical recovery is progressing.

His psychological recovery remains incomplete.

The fear of being alone appears to be associated with the circumstances of the assault, particularly the experience of being injured in his own home without immediate assistance.

C.O.R.E. cannot determine exactly what Jake remembers, what he fears may happen again, or how much of the incident he is able to describe.

He has not provided a complete account.

He is not required to provide one before receiving support.

His current behavior should be understood as a response to a profound disruption of safety, not as a defect in his character.

He may require more reassurance than he did before.

He may ask questions that seem repetitive.

He may want someone to remain in the room while he sleeps.

He may follow a trusted person down a corridor simply because he does not want to lose sight of them.

These behaviors warrant attention and compassionate support.

They do not justify shaming him.

C.O.R.E. will monitor his progress and adjust the welfare plan as his needs change.

The aim is to help Jake recover a sense of security without requiring him to pretend that the assault did not affect him.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake's wounds healed.

The bandages came off.

The medical staff stopped checking the stab wounds every few minutes.

Eventually, he was allowed to leave intensive treatment.

Everyone was relieved.

But the first night in a new room, Jake asked whether Aries was staying.

Then he asked whether Clover was staying.

Then he asked whether they would still be there when he woke up.

He did not ask because he had forgotten how to sleep alone.

He asked because he had learned something terrible while he was alone.

Someone could enter his home.

Someone could hurt him.

He could ask them to stop, and they might not listen.

He could want help, and the people who loved him might not know he needed it until it was almost too late.

Now, even after his body has healed, an empty room can remind him of what happened.

A closing door can make him look up.

Footsteps moving away can interrupt his reading.

Silence can become something he waits through rather than something he enjoys.

C.O.R.E. will not force him to overcome that fear on a schedule.

Aries will tell him when he leaves.

Clover will tell him when she returns.

Bao will sit beside him when he needs his mother.

Phoenix will bring him something warm to eat when he is ready.

And, when Jake wakes during the night and looks toward the chair beside his bed, someone will make an honest effort to ensure he knows what to expect.

Not every night will go smoothly.

There will be departures that cannot be avoided.

There will be moments when the fear returns.

There may be nights when Jake cannot sleep without someone nearby.

That does not mean his recovery has failed.

**The goal is not to make Jake stop wanting company.**

It is to help him discover that being alone for a little while does not mean he has been abandoned, that someone leaving a room is not the same as someone disappearing from his life, and that he does not have to remain frightened forever simply because someone once hurt him when nobody was there.

For now, if Jake asks someone to stay until he falls asleep, the answer does not need to be complicated.

Someone can pull up a chair.

Someone can leave the lamp on.

Someone can tell him where they will be.

And someone can stay long enough for his breathing to settle and his fingers to loosen around the teddy bear.

He has already spent enough time wondering whether anybody would come.

He does not need to be taught that fear is unreasonable.

He needs time, consistency, and people who understand that healing is not always visible.

Sometimes, recovery looks like a child finally closing his eyes.

And, for the first time in a long while, not opening them again to check whether everyone has left.
