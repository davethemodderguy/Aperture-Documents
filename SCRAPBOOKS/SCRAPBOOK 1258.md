# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Nora was previously one of the most consistently energetic residents in SAFEHOUSE.

She was loud.

She was curious.

She laughed easily.

She wanted to work at the Tachyon Merch Store.

She wanted to play with Alice.

She wanted to be carried by Aeterna.

She wanted to show Neri things.

She wanted cake.

She wanted to know what everyone was doing.

She wanted to be involved.

Recently, almost all of that has diminished.

The change has been sufficiently rapid that several residents initially described it as:

> **“Nora suddenly became a completely different person.”**

C.O.R.E. assessment disagrees with that interpretation.

The change was sudden in **visibility**.

It was not necessarily sudden in **development**.

Nora appears to have accumulated several sources of psychological and physical stress over a relatively short period.

Eventually, one additional stressor appears to have exceeded her ability to compensate.

The result was not a gradual decline that everyone noticed.

It was a sudden collapse of the behavior she had been using to conceal the decline.

---

# 1. BEFORE THE CHANGE

Nora's normal behavior was exceptionally expressive.

She would:

- run through SAFEHOUSE corridors;
    
- loudly announce observations;
    
- interrupt conversations with unrelated questions;
    
- praise Neri's intelligence;
    
- bother Elsher;
    
- play with Alice;
    
- visit the Merch Store;
    
- ask Aeterna to carry her;
    
- demand cake;
    
- read her family journal;
    
- show people photographs;
    
- talk about Tachyon merchandise;
    
- ask questions about weapons, technology, and Blacksite systems;
    
- become excited over minor discoveries;
    
- and generally make herself extremely difficult to ignore.
    

This behavior was not entirely superficial.

Nora genuinely enjoyed these things.

However, it also served another purpose.

Nora's activity kept her connected to everyone.

As long as she was moving, talking, playing, working, and asking questions, nobody had much reason to stop and ask:

> **“How are you really doing?”**

---

# 2. THE NIGHTMARES

The first major change was sleep.

Nora began experiencing increasingly severe nightmares.

These were not ordinary nightmares.

They repeatedly reconstructed aspects of her death.

Darkness.

Falling.

Cold.

The sensation of being unable to reach Neri.

And, most significantly:

> **Neri's face.**

Nora remembers being killed.

She remembers being discarded.

She remembers Neri seeing it happen.

That memory did not disappear when Nora returned.

It remained.

Initially, Nora could compensate for the nightmares by returning to her normal daytime behavior.

She would wake up frightened.

Then eventually:

> “I'm okay!”

And she would continue.

This was the first warning sign.

---

# 3. SLEEP DEPRIVATION

The nightmares became frequent enough that Nora eventually went approximately one week without meaningful sleep.

Her physical condition deteriorated.

She became exhausted.

She fainted repeatedly.

Her appetite decreased.

Her hydration became inconsistent.

She became temporarily bedridden.

She stopped having the physical energy necessary to maintain her usual level of activity.

This was important.

Nora's personality did not suddenly disappear.

Her ability to **perform her normal personality** became increasingly limited.

---

# 4. THE GET-WELL CARDS

Residents began giving Nora get-well cards.

At first, she loved them.

She read them.

She kept them.

She appreciated that people cared.

But as the number of cards increased, something changed.

Nora began realizing:

> **Everyone is worried about me.**

That observation appears to have become increasingly uncomfortable.

Nora had already developed a strong protective attachment to the people around her.

Now she had evidence that her condition was affecting them.

She was not simply sick.

She was causing concern.

To a healthy child, those are very different statements.

To Nora, they began to become the same thing.

---

# 5. THE MERCH STORE INCIDENT

Nora eventually returned to the Tachyon Merch Store before she had properly recovered.

This was significant.

The store represented independence.

It was something she could contribute to.

Something she could operate.

Something she could be useful for.

She returned.

Then collapsed.

Aeterna carried her back.

There was no punishment.

No anger.

No accusation.

Nobody told her she had failed.

But Nora had now experienced something much more uncomfortable:

She had attempted to prove that she was fine.

And her body had publicly demonstrated that she was not.

---

# 6. THE NIGHTMARES RETURNED

The store incident did not resolve the underlying problem.

Nora continued having nightmares.

The memories became more intrusive.

The more exhausted she became, the less capable she was of emotionally distancing herself from them.

This created a feedback loop:

**Nightmares → less sleep → physical weakness → more worry from others → guilt about causing worry → emotional withdrawal → less support → greater isolation → worse nights.**

Nora did not necessarily understand this process.

She simply experienced:

> **“Everyone is worried because of me.”**

---

# 7. THE EYES

Nora's eyes subsequently changed.

Her normal azure-and-white gradient became hollow black.

The cause remains unknown.

The medical significance has not yet been established.

However, the timing is notable.

The change occurred during the same period in which Nora's physical and psychological condition deteriorated.

C.O.R.E. therefore considers the ocular change a **co-occurring anomaly**, not proof that the behavioral deterioration is caused by it.

Further observation remains ongoing.

---

# 8. THE CRITICAL THOUGHT

Eventually, Nora appears to have reached a conclusion.

Not:

> **“Nobody cares about me.”**

But something considerably more consistent with her recent behavior:

> **“They care about me so much that I am making their lives worse.”**

This distinction is critical.

Nora has not stopped loving SAFEHOUSE.

She has not stopped loving Neri.

She has not stopped loving Elsher.

She has not stopped loving Aeterna.

She has not stopped caring about Alice.

She is attempting to protect them from herself.

---

# 9. THE NERI PATTERN

This is where the similarity to Neri becomes particularly concerning.

Neri's established internal logic is:

> **If I am useful, I deserve to remain.**

Nora's current logic appears to have evolved into:

> **If I am causing worry, I should remove myself from the situation.**

Both systems produce the same behavioral result.

Isolation.

Silence.

Reduced requests for assistance.

Concealing symptoms.

Avoiding people.

Refusing opportunities to be cared for.

The difference is that Neri learned this pattern over a much longer period.

Nora appears to have learned it from observing the consequences of her own illness.

---

# 10. WHY IT LOOKED SO SUDDEN

Nora's previous personality was extremely visible.

Her deterioration was not.

She could laugh while exhausted.

She could play while frightened.

She could talk enthusiastically after a terrible night.

She could insist:

> **“I'm fine!”**

while clearly not being fine.

This created a misleading impression of stability.

Everyone saw:

**Happy Nora.**

They did not necessarily see:

**Exhausted Nora.**

**Frightened Nora.**

**Nora who had not slept.**

**Nora who remembered dying.**

**Nora who woke from nightmares and immediately decided everyone else needed her to be okay.**

Therefore, when the outward behavior finally changed, it appeared instantaneous.

It was not.

The internal decline had already been occurring.

---

# 11. WHY SHE CHOSE ISOLATION

Nora's current behavior has become remarkably consistent.

She does not demand attention.

She does not ask people to leave.

She simply removes herself.

She stops asking for things.

She stops mentioning the Merch Store.

She turns cards face-down.

She spends more time alone.

She avoids admitting when something hurts.

She accepts less assistance.

When someone asks if she needs something, she says no.

When someone offers to stay, she may tell them they don't have to.

When asked why, the answer is usually simple.

> **“It's better this way.”**

This statement should not be interpreted as rejection.

It appears to mean:

> **“If I'm alone, nobody has to worry about me.”**

---

# 12. AETERNA'S OBSERVATION

Aeterna was asked whether she believed Nora had stopped being happy.

Aeterna remained silent for several seconds.

Then:

**AETERNA:**  
“No.”

**C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
“You don't?”

**AETERNA:**  
“She is still in there.”

Pause.

**AETERNA:**  
“She is simply trying very hard not to inconvenience anyone.”

The officer asked whether Aeterna believed Nora understood how much people cared about her.

Aeterna answered:

**AETERNA:**  
“She understands.”

Pause.

**AETERNA:**  
“That is the problem.”

---

# 13. NERI'S OBSERVATION

Neri appears to have recognized the pattern before most other residents.

She was observed sitting near Nora's room.

She did not knock.

She did not enter.

She did not ask Nora what was wrong.

She simply remained nearby.

When asked why, Neri reportedly answered:

> **“I know.”**

No additional explanation was provided.

C.O.R.E. believes Neri recognized something deeply familiar.

Nora is doing what Neri herself once did.

Trying to make herself disappear so nobody has to worry.

---

# 14. CURRENT ASSESSMENT

Nora has not become a different person.

Her previous personality has not necessarily vanished.

Rather, several factors appear to have converged:

**Traumatic memories**

**repeated nightmares**

**severe sleep deprivation**

**physical illness**

**repeated fainting**

**loss of independence**

**awareness that others are worried**

**guilt about receiving care**

**fear of becoming a burden**

=

**withdrawal.**

The apparent suddenness is therefore misleading.

Nora's behavior represents the visible endpoint of several overlapping stressors.

---

# 15. PERSONNEL GUIDANCE

Personnel should not attempt to force Nora back into her previous personality.

Do not tell her:

> “Where did the old Nora go?”

Do not demand:

> “Why aren't you happy anymore?”

Do not repeatedly insist:

> “We're not worried about you.”

Her experience already tells her that people are worried.

Denial may therefore feel dishonest.

Instead, personnel should communicate a different message:

> **“You being sick does not make you a problem.”**

> **“You don't have to entertain anyone.”**

> **“You don't have to make us feel better.”**

> **“You don't have to recover quickly for our sake.”**

And most importantly:

> **“You don't have to disappear to make things easier for us.”**

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

The question currently circulating among SAFEHOUSE personnel is:

> **“What happened to the happy Nora?”**

C.O.R.E. recommends replacing that question.

The happy Nora is still there.

She is simply exhausted.

Frightened.

Sleep-deprived.

Physically unwell.

Carrying memories that a six-year-old should never have had to carry.

And now she has reached the same conclusion Neri once reached:

> **If I make myself smaller, everyone else will hurt less.**

That conclusion is incorrect.

But from Nora's current perspective, it makes sense.

She has spent weeks watching people worry.

Watching Aeterna stay awake.

Watching Neri become concerned.

Watching Elsher return more often.

Receiving cards telling her to get better.

Being carried back to bed.

Being told to eat.

Being told to drink.

Being told to rest.

She has interpreted all of that love as evidence that she is causing trouble.

She has therefore decided to solve the problem herself.

By becoming quiet.

By asking for less.

By staying alone.

By becoming, in her own words:

> **“Easier.”**

C.O.R.E. assessment:

Nora does not need to become the old Nora again.

She needs to learn that she does not have to become **less of Nora** in order to be loved.

And perhaps the most important thing Neri can teach her is the thing Neri herself is still learning:

> **Being cared for is not a debt.**

> **Being loved is not an inconvenience.**

> **And being sick does not make you a burden.**

— **C.O.R.E. Resident Welfare & Medical Division**
