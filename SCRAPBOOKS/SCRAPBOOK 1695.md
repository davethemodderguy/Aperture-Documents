# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Two previously undocumented capabilities have been confirmed in Jake.

First:

**Jake can convert ordinary sand into extremely clear reinforced glass without the application of heat.**

Second:

**Jake has demonstrated mathematical ability sufficient to outperform Neko in a controlled mathematical assessment.**

The second finding is particularly unusual.

Jake did not receive formal mathematical instruction.

His improvement appears to have developed gradually through passive observation.

For several weeks, Jake has frequently sat with Clover while she performed accounting work.

He occasionally watched.

Sometimes he silently followed calculations.

Sometimes he pointed out small discrepancies.

He rarely explained how he reached his conclusions.

C.O.R.E. initially considered these observations incidental.

They are no longer considered incidental.

---

# 1. SAND → GLASS

The first discovery occurred when Jake was playing with sand.

He picked up a handful.

Looked at it.

Then closed his hand.

When he opened it, the sand had become a transparent glass object.

No visible heat was produced.

No melting phase occurred.

No conventional glass-forming process was observed.

The resulting material was remarkably clear.

It possessed significantly greater structural strength than ordinary glass.

---

# 2. MATERIAL CHARACTERISTICS

The resulting glass is visually comparable to extremely high-quality optical glass.

Observed characteristics include:

- exceptional transparency;
    
- minimal visible impurities;
    
- high structural integrity;
    
- resistance to ordinary impact;
    
- resistance to cracking;
    
- anomalously consistent composition;
    
- no detectable thermal history consistent with conventional glass production.
    

The most significant observation remains:

> **Jake does not heat the sand.**

He simply changes it.

---

# 3. DEMONSTRATION

Neko requested a controlled demonstration.

A tray of ordinary sand was provided.

Jake picked up a small amount.

> **NEKO:**  
> "What exactly are you doing?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Making glass."

> **NEKO:**  
> "How?"

Jake looked at the sand.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Like this."

The material changed.

A clear glass block appeared in his hand.

Neko stared at it.

He scanned the material.

Then scanned it again.

> **NEKO:**  
> "...There was no thermal event."

Jake nodded.

> **NEKO:**  
> "How did you do that?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I don't know."

Neko stared at the glass.

Then at Jake.

> **NEKO:**  
> "Of course you don't."

---

# 4. REINFORCED GLASS

Further testing established that Jake's conversion does not merely produce conventional glass.

The resulting material is **reinforced**.

Its exact mechanism remains unknown.

Jake appears to determine the desired physical result instinctively rather than through a conscious chemical or engineering process.

He does not appear to understand the underlying molecular mechanism.

He simply knows:

> **Sand can become strong, clear glass.**

---

# 5. IMPORTANT LIMITATION

C.O.R.E. does not authorize Jake to begin replacing Blacksite windows.

He has been specifically instructed not to convert arbitrary materials without permission.

This restriction exists primarily because nobody knows what happens if Jake decides to turn:

- concrete into glass;
    
- metal into glass;
    
- walls into glass;
    
- someone's lunch into glass;
    
- or something significantly more complicated into something else.
    

Jake agreed.

---

# 6. THE MATHEMATICS INCIDENT

The second discovery was considerably more embarrassing.

For Neko.

Jake had been sitting beside Clover during several accounting sessions.

Clover's work frequently involves:

- addition;
    
- subtraction;
    
- multiplication;
    
- division;
    
- percentages;
    
- ratios;
    
- financial projections;
    
- statistical comparisons;
    
- and increasingly complicated numerical reconciliation.
    

Jake watched.

He occasionally followed along.

He sometimes pointed at a number.

Clover would check it.

Jake was usually correct.

She eventually began letting him silently participate.

---

# 7. THE FIRST WARNING

Clover was reviewing a financial report.

She stopped.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "This total is wrong."

Jake looked over.

He pointed at one line.

Clover recalculated.

Jake was correct.

She fixed the number.

Then checked the surrounding calculations.

Several other values were affected.

Clover looked at Jake.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "How did you notice that?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It looked wrong."

Clover stared at him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Okay."

She continued working.

---

# 8. WEEKS OF OBSERVATION

This continued for several weeks.

Jake never formally announced that he was learning.

He simply watched.

He watched Clover calculate.

He watched her verify figures.

He watched her identify inconsistencies.

He occasionally helped.

Sometimes he would silently point toward an incorrect number.

Sometimes he would rearrange a few figures on a page.

Sometimes he would simply say:

> **"That's wrong."**

And then return to whatever he had been doing.

Clover initially assumed he had developed good numerical intuition.

She eventually realized it was significantly more than that.

---

# 9. THE TEST

C.O.R.E. eventually conducted a controlled assessment.

Neko agreed to participate.

This was a mistake.

The assessment consisted of progressively difficult mathematical problems.

Neko completed the first section rapidly.

Jake completed it quietly.

Neko completed the second section.

Jake completed it.

The third section became substantially more difficult.

Neko continued.

Jake continued.

Neither spoke much.

Then came the final section.

Neko finished first.

He looked confident.

Jake looked at the answers.

He pointed at one.

> **JAKE:**  
> "That's wrong."

Neko stopped.

> **NEKO:**  
> "...What?"

Jake pointed again.

Neko checked.

Then checked again.

He had made an error.

---

# 10. THE RESULT

The assessment was repeated.

Neko's result was excellent.

Jake's result was better.

The assessment was repeated a third time.

Same result.

C.O.R.E. subsequently classified Jake as possessing mathematical competence exceeding Neko's in the tested categories.

Neko was not pleased.

---

Then at Clover.

> "He beat you?"

Neko sighed.

> **NEKO:**  
> "Apparently."

Jake looked back.

> **NERI:**  
> "...Good."

Jake nodded.

Clover believes Jake's mathematical development is primarily observational.

His ability appears highly adaptive.

He may not know formal mathematical terminology.

He may nevertheless arrive at the correct result.
