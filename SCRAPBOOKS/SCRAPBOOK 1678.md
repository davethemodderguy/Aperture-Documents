### RESIDENT WELFARE & MEDICAL DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-WEL-4C-CLOVER-021  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — RESIDENT WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Clover — First Direct Encounter with Jake  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — COMMON ROOM  
**EVENT:** Full-Moon Manifestation

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Clover encountered Jake for the first time during a full-moon manifestation.

The encounter lasted approximately **thirty-seven seconds.**

It ended with Clover fleeing the common room in fear.

No physical harm occurred.

No hostile behavior was observed.

However, the psychological impact on both residents was significant.

Clover is **extremely afraid of ghosts.**

Jake's current manifestation — pale childlike appearance, glowing azure eyes, wings, and partially spectral presentation — was sufficient for Clover to interpret him as a ghost.

Jake was already emotionally fragile prior to the encounter.

He appeared confused when Clover reacted with fear.

When she screamed and ran, Jake remained motionless for several seconds.

He then left the SAFEHOUSE.

He has not returned for subsequent full-moon manifestations.

---

# 1. PRE-INCIDENT CONDITIONS

The encounter occurred shortly after midnight.

Clover was awake.

This was unsurprising.

Her chronic insomnia remains severe, particularly during nighttime.

She had been sitting in the common room with:

- her notebook;
    
- a candle;
    
- several drawing pencils;
    
- and a cup of tea.
    

The SAFEHOUSE was otherwise quiet.

Most residents were asleep.

Clover had been drawing four-leaf clovers.

She was aware that Jake could potentially appear during a full moon.

She had been told:

> **"Don't chase him."**

> **"Don't demand anything."**

> **"Just stay."**

Clover had agreed.

She believed she was prepared.

She was not.

---

# 2. JAKE APPEARS

At approximately 00:18, Clover noticed movement near the entrance to the common room.

She looked up.

Jake was standing there.

His physical manifestation was consistent with previous observations.

White hair.

Pale face.

Azure glowing eyes.

Shooting-star patterns moving through his irises.

Wings.

Small childlike body.

A faint celestial glow.

He was carrying no weapon.

He appeared tired.

His expression was empty.

Not hostile.

Not angry.

Simply sad.

Clover stared.

Jake stared back.

Neither moved.

---

# 3. INITIAL REACTION

Clover's candle shook in her hand.

Jake took one step forward.

Clover immediately stood.

Jake stopped.

His expression changed slightly.

He appeared confused.

Clover backed away.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...You're Jake?"

Jake nodded.

Clover's breathing became noticeably faster.

She looked at his glowing eyes.

Then his wings.

Then the faintly spectral quality of his body.

Her fear response escalated.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You're a ghost."

Jake remained silent.

He could not speak.

Clover took another step backward.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You're a ghost."

Jake shook his head.

Clover did not understand.

---

# 4. THE SCREAM

Jake attempted to approach slowly.

Clover screamed.

The sound woke several residents.

Jake immediately stopped.

Clover dropped her notebook.

She ran.

Jake remained standing in the middle of the common room.

For several seconds, nobody moved.

Jake looked down at the abandoned notebook.

Then toward the hallway where Clover had disappeared.

His expression became visibly more hurt.

---

# 5. RESIDENT RESPONSE

Bao arrived first.

Jane followed.

Neri appeared shortly afterward.

They found Jake standing alone.

Bao immediately recognized the situation.

> **BAO:**  
> "Jake..."

Jake looked at her.

Bao did not approach.

She understood.

Jake looked toward the hallway.

Then back at Bao.

He pointed toward Clover's notebook.

Bao understood that he was asking about her.

> **BAO:**  
> "She's scared."

Jake lowered his hand.

Bao continued:

> **BAO:**  
> "Not of you."

Pause.

> **BAO:**  
> "Of ghosts."

Jake looked down.

He understood.

It did not appear to make him feel better.

---

# 6. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake picked up Clover's notebook.

He looked at the page.

It contained several drawings of four-leaf clovers.

One unfinished drawing had been abandoned halfway through.

Jake held the notebook for several seconds.

Then carefully placed it back on the table.

He did not take anything.

He did not leave a message.

He simply walked away.

At the entrance to the SAFEHOUSE, he stopped.

He looked back once.

Then disappeared into the night.

---

# 7. CLOVER

Clover was later found on Phoenix's floor.

She was sitting against the wall.

Her candle was beside her.

She was shaking.

Phoenix remained nearby without touching her.

Clover eventually spoke.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I thought he was going to..."

She stopped.

Phoenix waited.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "He looked like a ghost."

Phoenix nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "He does."

Clover looked at her.

Phoenix paused.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "But he isn't one."

Clover lowered her head.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

A long silence followed.

Then:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I scared him."

Phoenix did not answer immediately.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You were scared."

Clover looked down.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "He looked sad."

Phoenix nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "He usually does."

---

# 8. THE NEXT FULL MOON

Jake did not return.

The next full moon passed.

No manifestation.

No movement in the common room.

No small figure entering through the hallway.

No blue glow.

No quiet footsteps.

Clover remained awake.

She sat at the same table.

The same candle burned beside her.

The pot of four-leaf clovers remained on the table.

She waited.

Jake did not come.

---

# 9. SUBSEQUENT FULL MOONS

The pattern continued.

Another full moon.

No Jake.

Another.

Nothing.

Another.

Still nothing.

His blue star remained visible in the night sky.

But the childlike manifestation stopped appearing inside SAFEHOUSE.

C.O.R.E. confirmed that Jake had not been physically destroyed.

He had not vanished from existence.

He had simply stopped coming.

---

# 10. CLOVER'S INTERPRETATION

Clover eventually asked Phoenix:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Did I make him stop coming?"

Phoenix considered her answer carefully.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You didn't make him do anything."

Clover remained silent.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "He was already hurt."

Clover looked toward the window.

The blue star was visible.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "But he came here."

Phoenix nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "He did."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "And then I scared him."

Phoenix nodded again.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You did."

Clover's expression fell.

Phoenix continued.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Both things can be true."

Clover looked at her.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You were scared."

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "And he was hurt."

---

# 11. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Neither resident should be assigned sole blame for the incident.

Clover's fear of ghosts is genuine and severe.

Her reaction was involuntary.

She did not know how Jake would appear.

She did not intend to reject him.

She did not know that Jake was already emotionally fragile.

Jake's reaction is also understandable.

He had returned to SAFEHOUSE because it remained one of the few places he associated with belonging.

He encountered a new resident.

He could not explain himself.

She saw him as something frightening.

She screamed.

From Jake's perspective, the encounter may have reinforced an existing belief:

> **"People are better without me around."**

This interpretation is not confirmed.

It is nevertheless considered plausible.

---

# 12. IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Clover did **not** reject Jake because she disliked him.

She was afraid of what she believed he was.

Jake did **not** leave because Clover had done something unforgivable.

He appears to have interpreted her fear through the lens of his existing emotional wounds.

Neither interpretation makes either resident malicious.

Both are frightened.

Both have experienced severe loss.

Both have complicated relationships with memory.

And both have spent years believing that their presence may hurt other people.

---

# 13. RESIDENT WELFARE RECOMMENDATION

C.O.R.E. recommends **no forced reconciliation.**

Clover should not be instructed to apologize directly to Jake unless he chooses to return.

Jake should not be intercepted.

Nobody should attempt to lure him back.

Nobody should use Clover as bait.

Nobody should tell Jake that Clover is "safe now."

Nobody should tell Clover that she is responsible for Jake's absence.

If Jake returns:

Allow him to choose whether to remain.

If Clover encounters him again:

Allow her to choose whether she can remain in the same room.

Neither resident should be forced to overcome their fear or hurt for the convenience of the others.

---

# 14. CLOVER'S NOTEBOOK

Three nights after the incident, Clover returned to the common room.

She opened her notebook.

She turned to the page she had been drawing when Jake appeared.

The unfinished clover remained.

She stared at it.

Then turned to a blank page.

She drew Jake.

Not as a ghost.

Not as a monster.

Not as something frightening.

Just a small child.

White hair.

Blue eyes.

A teddy bear beside him.

Clover stopped drawing before finishing his face.

She stared at the unfinished portrait.

Then wrote underneath it:

> **"I'm sorry I was scared."**

She closed the notebook.

---

# CLOSING OBSERVATION

Jake still visits the sky.

Clover still looks for him.

She has never tried to approach the blue star.

She simply watches.

On full-moon nights, she sometimes sits by the window with a candle beside her.

She draws.

Sometimes four-leaf clovers.

Sometimes stars.

Sometimes a small child with a teddy bear.

She has never finished the face.

One night, Phoenix found her sitting there.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Still drawing him?"

Clover nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Why?"

Clover looked at the blue star outside.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Because I don't want the last thing he remembers about me to be me screaming."

Phoenix said nothing.

Clover continued drawing.

Outside, the blue star remained distant.

Silent.

Beautiful.

And impossibly sad.

Jake had once returned to SAFEHOUSE because he wanted to know whether he still belonged there.

He stopped returning after one frightened scream.

Not because Clover hated him.

Not because anyone told him to leave.

But because sometimes, for someone already carrying too much hurt, one more rejection — even an accidental one — is enough to make staying feel impossible.
