### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — THE BLUE ROSES**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — SAFEHOUSE PERSONNEL  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 5  
**Condition:** Fragile / Nonverbal / High Risk of Unintentional Oversight

---

# **PREVIOUSLY IMPLEMENTED MEASURES**

Following multiple incidents involving Alice being unintentionally left behind, several emergency items were issued to her.

### **Emergency Whistle**

Alice was taught how to use it.

She understood.

She does not use it.

When asked why, Alice wrote:

> **"Whistle loud."**

When asked why that was a problem:

> **"People busy."**

---

### **Flashlight**

Alice was given a small flashlight for emergencies.

She knows how to activate it.

She does not use it.

When asked why:

> **"People see."**

When asked whether she wanted people to see her if she needed help:

Alice hesitated.

Then wrote:

> **"Don't want bother."**

---

# **CONCLUSION**

Providing Alice with methods of attracting attention has proven largely ineffective.

Alice's problem is not an inability to request assistance.

It is her unwillingness to **become a problem that requires assistance.**

If Alice believes someone is busy, she will wait.

If Alice believes someone is tired, she will wait.

If Alice believes asking for help would inconvenience someone—

she will simply remain where she is.

---

# **INCIDENT 17 — THE BLUE ROSES**

The incident began normally.

An employee was heading outside for a short trip around the Blacksite perimeter.

Alice was nearby with her scooter.

The employee invited her to come along.

Alice accepted.

She followed happily.

For once, Alice was not alone.

---

# **THE DISTRACTION**

Near the outer path, Alice noticed something unusual.

Blue roses.

They were growing beside the path.

Alice stopped.

Her ears lifted slightly.

She stared.

Then she pulled out her sketchbook.

The employee continued walking.

They did not notice Alice had stopped.

Alice remained beside the roses.

Drawing.

Carefully.

Quietly.

---

# **THE DOORS**

The employee eventually returned toward the Blacksite.

They entered through the exterior access doors.

The doors closed automatically behind them.

They did not realize Alice was still outside.

Alice remained near the roses.

She finished part of her drawing.

Then looked toward the building.

She could not see the employee anymore.

She did not whistle.

She did not use her flashlight.

She did not knock.

She simply assumed they were busy.

---

# **THE RAIN**

Several minutes later—

it began to rain.

At first, lightly.

Alice continued sitting beneath the tree.

Then the rain became heavier.

She moved closer to the trunk.

Her hat became soaked.

Her white hair became soaked.

Her clothes became soaked.

Her sketchbook became soaked.

The pages began absorbing the water.

The drawings blurred.

Ink ran across the paper.

Lines dissolved.

Faces disappeared.

Names became unreadable.

The pages stuck together.

Alice tried to protect the sketchbook beneath her clothes.

It was already too late.

---

# **FROST**

Frost was watching the rain from inside.

She wasn't looking for Alice.

She simply happened to glance outside.

Something caught her attention.

A small figure beneath a tree.

At first, Frost thought it was debris.

Then she saw the ears.

She immediately recognized her.

> "...Alice?"

---

# **DISCOVERY**

Alice was sitting beneath the tree.

Soaked.

Shivering.

Her hat was dripping water.

Her hair clung to her face.

Her arms were wrapped around her ruined sketchbook.

The drawings were gone.

The pages were saturated.

Alice looked up when Frost approached.

She didn't complain.

She didn't cry.

She simply looked tired.

Frost immediately understood what had happened.

---

# **AFTERMATH**

The events following the discovery are omitted from this record.

They do not require documentation.

Alice was brought inside.

She was warmed.

Her clothes were changed.

Her damaged sketchbook was recovered.

The ruined pages were carefully separated and dried, although the drawings could not be restored.

Alice did not become ill.

Her immune system has strengthened considerably since her earlier condition.

No infection or significant illness developed.

---

# **THE SKETCHBOOK**

Most of the drawings were destroyed.

The blue roses were among them.

Alice had drawn them carefully.

The rain reduced them to blurred patches of ink.

She stared at the ruined page for a long time.

Then closed the sketchbook.

She did not ask anyone to fix it.

She did not blame anyone.

She did not complain.

She simply started drawing again later.

---

# **PERSONNEL REVIEW**

The employee involved was not malicious.

They had genuinely believed Alice had followed them inside.

There was no intention to abandon her.

The incident was another example of the same fundamental problem.

Alice can be invited somewhere.

Alice can follow someone.

Alice can become distracted.

Alice can stop.

And because she does not call out—

the person may never know she stopped.

---

# **ALICE'S WHISTLE**

After the incident, Corin asked Alice why she hadn't used her whistle.

Alice looked down.

She wrote:

> **"Big sis busy."**

Corin replied:

> "I wasn't there."

Alice thought about it.

Then wrote:

> **"Someone busy."**

Corin went quiet.

That was the answer.

Alice hadn't decided that nobody would help her.

She had decided that **someone else probably had something more important to do.**

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

The whistle remains around Alice's neck.

The flashlight remains attached to her scooter.

Both are fully functional.

Neither is considered a reliable emergency measure.

Because Alice doesn't want attention.

She doesn't want to interrupt.

She doesn't want to make anyone stop what they're doing.

She would rather sit in the rain with a ruined sketchbook than risk bothering someone.

The SAFEHOUSE therefore considers the most important emergency device around Alice to be neither a whistle nor a flashlight.

It is another person paying attention.
