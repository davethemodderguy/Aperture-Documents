### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — WITHDRAWAL FROM PERSONAL GIFTS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 6  
**Species:** Full-Body Rabbit Thiren  
**Status:** Active — Psychological Monitoring Required

---

# **OBSERVED CHANGE**

Alice has stopped wearing her soft white beanie.

The beanie had previously been one of her most recognizable possessions.

She wore it frequently.

It was soft.

Comfortable.

Familiar.

And, most importantly, it had been given to her as a gift.

Alice has now stopped wearing it entirely.

The beanie has been found folded neatly among her belongings.

It has not been damaged.

Alice simply no longer puts it on.

---

# **THE CHANGE IS NOT LIMITED TO THE BEANIE**

Further inspection revealed a broader behavioral change.

Alice has stopped using or wearing nearly every gift she has received.

Clothing.

Small toys.

Accessories.

Keepsakes.

Decorations.

Objects made specifically for her.

Even items she previously showed obvious attachment toward are now being left untouched.

When asked why, Alice initially remained silent.

After several seconds, she quietly answered:

> "...Don't need them."

Personnel asked:

> "But you liked them."

Alice looked down.

> "...Alice shouldn't."

She did not elaborate.

---

# **REMOVAL OF THE BEANIE**

Without the beanie covering her head, an old scar became visible.

The scar is located on Alice's scalp and is the result of a previous unfortunate incident.

It is not associated with the recent cafeteria concussion.

Medical records confirm that the injury predates the latest incident.

The scar had simply been hidden beneath Alice's beanie for an extended period.

Personnel had therefore become accustomed to seeing Alice with the beanie and rarely noticed it.

Now it is visible again.

---

# **MEDICAL NOTE**

Medical personnel have confirmed that the existing scar does not currently present a physical danger.

However, the presence of an additional previous head injury is considered relevant when evaluating Alice's recent concussion.

Alice has now experienced multiple incidents involving injury to the head.

Accordingly, medical staff have requested continued observation for any neurological or behavioral changes.

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION**

The abandonment of the beanie is not being treated as a fashion preference.

The timing is significant.

Alice stopped wearing it after her emotional state deteriorated.

She subsequently stopped using other gifts as well.

The current interpretation is that Alice may be attempting to distance herself from objects associated with:

- Affection.
    
- Care.
    
- Attachment.
    
- Dependence.
    
- Positive memories.
    
- Expectations from other people.
    

Alice appears to be trying to make herself require less.

Less food.

Less attention.

Less comfort.

Less space.

Less affection.

Less of everything.

---

# **CORIN'S OBSERVATION**

Corin noticed the missing beanie almost immediately.

She asked:

> "Where's your hat?"

Alice touched one of her ears.

She looked toward the floor.

> "...Don't want it."

Corin asked:

> "Did something happen to it?"

Alice shook her head.

> "...It's okay."

Corin looked at the folded beanie nearby.

> "Then why aren't you wearing it?"

Alice was silent.

Eventually:

> "...It's a gift."

Corin paused.

> "Yes."

Alice whispered:

> "...Alice don't need gifts."

Corin's expression reportedly changed immediately.

She did not argue.

She simply sat beside Alice.

---

# **PERSONNEL GUIDANCE**

Personnel are specifically instructed **not** to force Alice to wear the beanie.

The same applies to every other gift.

Do not say:

> "But I gave that to you."

Do not say:

> "You used to love this."

Do not guilt Alice into accepting affection.

Instead, gifts should remain available without expectation.

If Alice eventually chooses to use something again, she should be allowed to do so naturally.

---

# **THE BEANIE**

The white beanie has been placed in Alice's personal storage area.

It remains clean and undamaged.

No one has taken it away.

No one intends to.

It will simply remain there.

Waiting.

Not as a reminder of what Alice used to be.

Not as something she owes anyone by wearing.

Just a small, soft thing that belongs to her.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

For months, Alice wore the little white beanie almost everywhere.

It covered her head.

It made her feel comfortable.

It became part of the image everyone associated with her.

Now it sits folded in a corner.

And without it, an old scar is visible.

A scar from another incident that happened long before the cafeteria.

Another reminder that Alice has been hurt more times than a six-year-old should have to understand.

But the scar is not what concerns C.O.R.E. most.

The concerning part is why Alice no longer wants the things people gave her.

She used to treasure gifts.

Now she seems to believe that accepting them means accepting that she needs someone.

And Alice has become convinced that needing someone makes her a burden.

So she is trying to need nothing.

No gifts.

No scooter.

No company.

No attention.

Nothing.

C.O.R.E. considers this behavior a significant warning sign.

Alice does not need to be convinced that she deserves expensive things.

She needs to learn something much simpler:

**A gift is not a debt.**

**Care is not something she has to repay.**

**And accepting something made with love does not make Alice a burden.**

The beanie will remain where she left it.

No one will force it onto her head.

But it will still be there whenever Alice decides she wants something soft again.
