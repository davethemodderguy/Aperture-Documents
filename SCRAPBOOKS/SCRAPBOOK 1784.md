# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake's current form is no longer adequately described as a human with feline ears and a tail.

Following continued observation, C.O.R.E. has confirmed that Jake possesses an extensive suite of feline anatomical features, sensory adaptations, reflexes, and physical capabilities.

His appearance remains predominantly humanoid, with white hair and luminous azure eyes containing shooting-star patterns.

However, his physiology now includes:

- Functional feline ears.
    
- A fully mobile feline tail.
    
- Pronounced retractable fangs.
    
- Retractable claws on his hands and feet.
    
- Extraordinary acceleration and short-burst speed.
    
- Enhanced balance, agility, and coordination.
    
- Exceptional jumping and climbing ability.
    
- Highly sensitive hearing and smell.
    
- Improved vision in low-light conditions.
    
- **Miosis:** the ability to constrict his pupils, producing the characteristic narrow vertical pupils associated with cats.
    
- Feline grooming, kneading, purring, and other behavioral tendencies.
    
- A strong righting reflex and unusually flexible movement.
    
- Instinctive responses to sudden movement, unfamiliar sounds, and small moving objects.
    

These characteristics are not merely cosmetic.

Jake can move like a cat, react like a cat, and display the physical abilities expected of a cat—while retaining his humanoid anatomy, intelligence, speech, and individual personality.

His actual performance exceeds ordinary feline limits in several areas, particularly acceleration.

The current working classification is:

> **ANOMALOUS HUMAN–FELINE HYBRID: EXTENSIVE PHENOTYPIC AND FUNCTIONAL EXPRESSION.**

Jake's established identity as a Weeping Angel and the human manifestation of a literal star remains unchanged.

He is still Jake.

He is simply becoming increasingly difficult to catch when he decides he does not want to be caught.

---

# 1. OCULAR PHYSIOLOGY — MIOSIS

Jake can voluntarily constrict his pupils.

Under suitable lighting conditions, his pupils become narrow vertical slits, closely resembling those of a domestic cat.

His azure irises retain their distinctive celestial appearance, including the luminous streaks resembling shooting stars.

The result is unusual.

In dim lighting, his eyes may appear predominantly luminous blue, with the shooting-star patterns clearly visible.

In brighter conditions, his pupils constrict, giving him a distinctly feline expression.

The change appears to occur naturally and does not require Jake to concentrate for an extended period.

### Observation 01 — The Eyes

Phoenix was sitting beside Jake while he read.

She glanced at him.

Then glanced again.

**PHOENIX:** "Jake?"

**JAKE:** "Hm?"

**PHOENIX:** "Your eyes."

Jake looked up.

His pupils were narrow vertical slits.

Phoenix stared.

Jake blinked.

**JAKE:** "What?"

**PHOENIX:** "They're doing the thing."

Jake looked toward the window.

**JAKE:** "It's bright."

**PHOENIX:** "I know."

**JAKE:** "Then why are you staring?"

Phoenix immediately looked away.

**PHOENIX:** "I'm not."

Jake's tail flicked once.

**JAKE:** "You are."

Phoenix said nothing.

Jake returned to his book.

His pupils gradually widened again as the lighting changed.

Phoenix spent the next several minutes attempting to read without looking at him.

She was not particularly successful.

### Medical Note

Miosis is a normal component of Jake's current feline ocular physiology.

His pupils should still be monitored for abnormal or asymmetric responses, persistent discomfort, or other unexpected changes.

The presence of a familiar feline feature does not mean that every possible ocular change is automatically harmless.

# 2. RETRACTABLE FANGS

Jake possesses functional feline-style fangs.

They are more pronounced than ordinary human canine teeth and can become visibly exposed when he opens his mouth, yawns, or deliberately bares them.

The teeth retract or settle into their normal resting presentation according to his current hybrid anatomy.

They are not permanently exposed.

Jake has demonstrated that he can use them to bite.

This ability is not encouraged as a routine means of communication.

Unfortunately, Jake has already discovered that showing his fangs is an effective way to make certain residents reconsider their decisions.

### Observation 02 — Jerry

Jerry entered Jake's room carrying a container.

Jake was sitting in his cat bed.

He immediately recognized the smell.

His ears rotated backward.

His pupils narrowed.

His tail stopped moving.

**JAKE:** "Jerry."

**JERRY:** "I brought you something!"

Jake looked at the container.

**JAKE:** "What is it?"

Jerry hesitated.

**JERRY:** "...Chicken."

Jake stared at him.

**JAKE:** "Normal chicken?"

**JERRY:** "Well—"

Jake's lips lifted slightly, exposing his fangs.

Jerry stopped speaking.

**JAKE:** "Jerry."

**JERRY:** "It's canned cat food."

A long silence followed.

Jake's ears flattened.

**JAKE:** "Out."

Jerry left.

The container left with him.

C.O.R.E. has classified the incident as a successful verbal boundary enforcement event.

No bite occurred.

This is considered progress.

---

# 3. RETRACTABLE CLAWS

Jake's fingers and toes possess retractable claws.

When relaxed, the claws remain largely concealed.

When extended, they provide additional grip and can be used for climbing, scratching, and defensive movements.

The claws are sharp enough to damage fabric, scratch furniture, and injure skin.

They also allow Jake to grip certain surfaces more securely than an ordinary human could.

His hand anatomy remains sufficiently humanoid for ordinary tasks, including writing, holding books, manipulating objects, and using his personal belongings.

This combination is particularly unusual.

Jake can write a perfectly legible sentence, retract his claws, and then climb onto a shelf without changing his overall form.

### Observation 03 — The Bookshelf

A welfare officer entered Jake's room and found him sitting on a low bookshelf.

His teddy bear was on the upper shelf.

The officer stopped.

**OFFICER:** "Jake."

Jake looked down.

**JAKE:** "Yes?"

**OFFICER:** "How did you get up there?"

Jake pointed toward the lower shelf.

**JAKE:** "Climbed."

**OFFICER:** "I can see that."

Jake picked up his teddy bear.

He then jumped down and landed on his feet.

**OFFICER:** "You could have asked someone to get it."

Jake hugged the teddy bear against his chest.

**JAKE:** "Could have."

He walked away.

The officer inspected the shelf.

Several small claw marks were visible.

A suitable climbing and scratching structure has since been installed in Jake's room.

The bookshelf is no longer considered an approved climbing surface.

Jake has not acknowledged this restriction.

# 4. EXTREME SPEED AND ACCELERATION

Jake's speed is among the most significant aspects of his altered physiology.

He can accelerate from a stationary position with almost no apparent preparation.

His initial movement is extremely difficult for ordinary human observers to follow.

He can change direction rapidly, stop within a short distance under suitable conditions, and move through familiar spaces with remarkable efficiency.

The behavior resembles a cat suddenly sprinting across a room.

Except Jake is considerably faster than a normal cat should be.

C.O.R.E. has not established his maximum speed.

Attempts to obtain a precise measurement have been complicated by the following factors:

1. Jake does not consistently cooperate with testing.
    
2. He frequently changes direction before measurement equipment can establish a reliable track.
    
3. He sometimes stops halfway through a trial because he has noticed something interesting.
    
4. He has a tendency to regard formal testing as an unnecessary interruption.
    

### Observation 04 — Corridor Transit

A camera recorded Jake sitting beside a corridor wall.

He was looking at a small toy.

At 14:06:12, he stood.

At 14:06:13, he disappeared from the camera's central field of view.

At 14:06:14, he appeared approximately twenty meters farther down the corridor.

At 14:06:15, he stopped.

He looked toward a window.

At 14:06:17, he returned to his original position.

He sat down.

He resumed playing with the toy.

Total observed movement time: insufficient for a reliable conventional speed estimate.

**C.O.R.E. TECHNICIAN:** "Did he just run down the corridor and back?"

**WELFARE OFFICER:** "Apparently."

**C.O.R.E. TECHNICIAN:** "Why?"

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:** "Wanted to."

He returned to the toy.

### Safety Considerations

Jake's acceleration creates a risk of collisions if he moves through crowded or unfamiliar areas.

Personnel should avoid suddenly blocking his path, chasing him for amusement, or attempting to startle him into running.

Jake is also not to be used as a courier, security asset, or experimental speed platform without appropriate authorization.

He is a resident.

Not a transportation system.

---

# 5. BALANCE, AGILITY, AND THE RIGHTING REFLEX

Jake's coordination has improved considerably.

He can jump onto narrow surfaces, rotate his body during a fall, land feet-first under suitable conditions, and make rapid adjustments to his posture.

His tail appears to assist with balance.

His ears also provide subtle indications of his orientation and attention.

He can climb, crouch, twist, and move through confined spaces with feline efficiency.

These abilities have resulted in several observations of Jake occupying places where nobody remembers seeing him climb.

One example involved a curtain rail.

Another involved the top of a storage cabinet.

The third involved the back of a couch.

C.O.R.E. has concluded that Jake's preferred route through a room is not necessarily the shortest route between two points.

It is the route he finds most interesting.

---

# 6. ENHANCED HEARING AND DIRECTIONAL AWARENESS

Jake's ears are fully functional.

They rotate independently and can orient toward separate sounds.

He can detect quiet noises, distinguish their approximate directions, and respond to sounds that other residents have not noticed.

He frequently hears people approaching before they reach his door.

He also appears capable of identifying familiar voices without needing to look toward the speaker.

### Observation 05 — The Doorway

A staff member approached Jake's room.

Before the staff member knocked, Jake's left ear turned toward the door.

His right ear remained directed toward the book he was reading.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "Jake?"

**JAKE:** "Come in."

The staff member entered.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "How did you know I was there?"

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:** "Heard you."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "From all the way down the hall?"

Jake nodded.

The staff member paused.

**STAFF MEMBER:** "...That's useful."

Jake returned to his book.

**JAKE:** "It's loud."

The staff member immediately lowered their voice.

Jake's ears relaxed.

This response has been noted as a reminder that enhanced hearing may also make ordinary environments uncomfortable.

Personnel should not assume that greater sensory sensitivity is an unqualified advantage.

# 7. FURTHER FELINE CHARACTERISTICS

Jake's current physiology includes several additional traits.

## 7.1 — Whiskers and Spatial Awareness

Jake possesses feline whiskers that appear to contribute to his awareness of nearby objects and confined spaces.

He may pause before entering a narrow opening, adjust his head position, or withdraw when his whiskers contact an unfamiliar surface.

This behavior appears instinctive.

## 7.2 — Purring and Vocalization

Jake can purr, trill, chirp, meow, hiss, and growl.

He continues to speak normally.

The feline vocalizations do not replace his language abilities.

He may answer a question with a word, a meow, a gesture, or no response at all.

The choice depends on his mood and the situation.

## 7.3 — Kneading and Scent Marking

Jake kneads soft blankets and cushions when comfortable.

He also rubs his cheeks against familiar people and objects.

These behaviors may serve affiliative, sensory, and self-soothing functions.

Personnel should not interpret them as evidence of ownership or possession.

Jake is expressing familiarity.

He is not claiming the SAFEHOUSE.

## 7.4 — Grooming

Jake grooms his hands, arms, and face using feline movements.

He may lick his hands and use them to clean his face.

Ordinary hygiene and medical care remain necessary.

## 7.5 — Thermal Preferences

Jake strongly prefers warm resting spots.

He may seek sunlight, warm blankets, or the quietest area near a heat source.

Appropriate temperature monitoring remains necessary.

Jake's preference for warmth does not make him immune to overheating.

## 7.6 — Predatory Play Responses

Jake reacts quickly to small moving objects.

His attention may lock onto a moving toy before he consciously decides to engage.

He can crouch, stalk, pounce, and chase with remarkable coordination.

This is considered ordinary feline play behavior.

Live animals are not to be introduced as toys.

---

# 8. THE TAIL AND EARS — AN UNINTENTIONAL EMOTIONAL DISPLAY SYSTEM

Jake's tail and ears frequently communicate his mood before he speaks.

This has become a source of considerable amusement among residents who know him well.

When interested, his ears point forward.

When sleepy, they relax.

When irritated, they flatten.

When excited, his tail may rise or move rapidly.

When content, it may curl around his body.

When annoyed, it may flick sharply from side to side.

Jake has occasionally attempted to conceal his feelings by maintaining a completely neutral expression.

His tail has not always cooperated.

### Observation 06 — The Unsuccessful Poker Face

Phoenix offered Jake a small piece of cake.

Jake looked at it.

**PHOENIX:** "Want some?"

**JAKE:** "No."

His tail rose.

Phoenix looked at the tail.

Then at Jake.

**PHOENIX:** "Are you sure?"

**JAKE:** "Yes."

His tail curled slightly.

Phoenix waited.

Jake stared at her.

**JAKE:** "...Maybe."

Phoenix offered him the cake.

Jake accepted it.

His tail began moving happily.

**PHOENIX:** "Your tail told me."

Jake stopped chewing.

He looked behind himself.

His tail continued moving.

He looked back at Phoenix.

**JAKE:** "Traitor."

Phoenix laughed.

Jake finished his cake.

# 9. RESIDENT WELFARE — JAKE IS STILL JAKE

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Jake's new abilities do not change his status as a resident.

He must not be:

- Deliberately startled to test his reflexes.
    
- Chased to measure his speed.
    
- Restrained to inspect his claws.
    
- Exposed to loud noises to test his hearing.
    
- Dropped to test his righting reflex.
    
- Fed inappropriate food because of his appearance.
    
- Treated as a weapon or security asset.
    
- Forced to demonstrate his abilities for entertainment.
    

His physiology may be extraordinary.

His needs remain ordinary in several important respects.

He requires food, sleep, appropriate medical care, privacy, and people who respect his boundaries.

He also needs to be allowed to make mistakes, play, rest, and enjoy his own life.

His enhanced speed does not make him responsible for everyone else's errands.

His claws do not make him dangerous by default.

His fangs do not mean that every bite is playful.

His ears and tail do not make his emotions public property.

And his ability to move faster than the security cameras can reliably track does not mean that personnel should keep trying to catch him on camera.

C.O.R.E. has already received too many requests for slow-motion footage.

The answer is no.

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's hybrid physiology now encompasses an extensive range of feline characteristics.

He possesses functional ears, a tail, retractable fangs, retractable claws, miosis, enhanced senses, remarkable balance, extreme acceleration, and numerous other feline capabilities.

His abilities are substantial enough that ordinary assumptions about human movement and sensory limits no longer reliably apply.

However, his form remains stable during ordinary daily activity.

No evidence currently indicates that these characteristics have compromised his intelligence, speech, identity, or ability to make decisions.

Further investigation will continue at a pace that does not interfere unnecessarily with his welfare.

C.O.R.E. considers the current situation manageable.

The only recurring operational concern is that Jake can now reach almost any elevated surface in his room.

And that he has discovered this.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING OBSERVATION

At 21:10, Jake was observed sitting on the highest safe cushion in his room.

His white hair was slightly disheveled.

His ears were upright.

His azure eyes glowed softly in the dim lighting, their pupils narrowed as he watched a toy move across the floor.

His tail swayed slowly behind him.

The toy moved again.

Jake's pupils widened.

His body lowered.

His hind legs tensed.

Then—

**WHOOSH.**

Jake crossed the room in a blur, caught the toy, and landed neatly on his feet.

He stood there for a moment, looking at his prize.

Then he picked it up and carried it back to his bed.

His teddy bear was waiting.

He settled beside it.

A moment later, he began purring.

From the doorway, a welfare officer watched quietly.

**OFFICER:** "You know, you don't have to keep demonstrating your abilities."

Jake looked up.

**JAKE:** "Was playing."

**OFFICER:** "Fair enough."

The officer closed the door gently.

Jake curled up beside his teddy bear.

His tail covered his feet.

His ears relaxed.

His eyes slowly closed.

For all his extraordinary capabilities, all the speed, all the sharp little claws, and all the strange things his body could now do, Jake's final activity for the evening was remarkably simple.

He went to sleep.
