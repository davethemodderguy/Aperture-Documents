# INCIDENT TRANSCRIPT

**TIME:** 21:14

Alice and Anby were seated together in the common area.

The birthday celebration had already ended.

The reason for its early termination was no longer known.

Alice had been unusually quiet.

After several minutes, she suddenly looked toward Anby.

> **ALICE:**  
> "Anby..."

Anby looked over.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Hm?"

Alice frowned.

She rubbed the side of her head.

> **ALICE:**  
> "I feel like I forgot something."

Anby waited.

> **ALICE:**  
> "But I can't remember what it is."

Anby looked at her for several seconds.

Then quietly answered:

> **ANBY:**  
> "If you forgotten it then it isn't important anymore."

Alice considered this.

She looked down.

> **ALICE:**  
> "...Yeah."

A pause.

Alice smiled faintly.

> **ALICE:**  
> "You're probably right."

Anby nodded.

Neither of them mentioned it again.

---

# ARCHIVAL OBSERVATION

The exchange lasted less than thirty seconds.

No anomalous event was visibly associated with it.

No memory recovery occurred.

No alarm was triggered.

Both residents continued with their evening.

However, C.O.R.E. notes that Alice's statement is consistent with the broader memory-loss phenomenon surrounding the unidentified former SAFEHOUSE resident.

The resident is not remembered.

The absence is not recognized.

The loss is not consciously mourned.

Yet several residents continue reporting an indistinct sensation that something is missing.

They cannot identify what.

They cannot describe who.

They cannot even establish whether the sensation is real.

# SUPPLEMENTAL NOTE

At 21:37, Alice passed the elevator.

She stopped.

She looked at the destination panel.

One floor appeared to be missing.

Alice stared at the empty space for approximately eight seconds.

Then she shrugged.

> **ALICE:**  
> "Probably nothing."

She continued walking.

The elevator doors closed behind her.
