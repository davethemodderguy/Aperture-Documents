# Executive Summary

A senior Janitorial employee died in Phoenix Raffedella's presence.

The employee was an original Blacksite worker.

He had been present since the early days of Aperture's Blacksite operations.

He began working for Aperture at approximately **twelve years old**.

He remained with the organization for decades.

He died from internal bleeding.

Phoenix attempted to save him.

She failed.

Under ordinary Aperture circumstances, the death would not necessarily have been permanent.

Through Aperture's established relationship with the Banlands and its Warden, **Lucy Merryweather**, Director Lucious possesses a means of requesting restoration of deceased personnel.

This time, however, Aperture did not use it.

Because the deceased had already made his wishes clear.

He wanted to rest.

He wanted to see his wife again.

And Aperture respected that.

---

# 1. THE PATIENT

The deceased was a long-serving member of Blacksite Janitorial.

His exact employment record is extensive.

He had been present during:

- early Blacksite construction;
    
- multiple facility expansions;
    
- numerous containment incidents;
    
- several relocations;
    
- anomalous events;
    
- changes in Blacksite leadership;
    
- and the establishment of the modern resident areas.
    

He had effectively grown old alongside the facility.

Personnel who joined decades after him still knew him simply as:

> **“One of the old janitors.”**

He rarely discussed his history.

He preferred working.

---

# 2. THE MEDICAL EMERGENCY

The incident occurred during a routine shift.

The employee complained of abdominal pain.

Initially, he attempted to continue working.

His condition deteriorated rapidly.

Phoenix was called.

She immediately began treatment.

The exact internal injury was severe.

There was significant internal bleeding.

Phoenix attempted to stabilize him.

She called for additional medical support.

She attempted every appropriate intervention available to her.

His condition continued to deteriorate.

---

# 3. PHOENIX

Phoenix remained beside him.

She repeatedly checked his condition.

She knew what the signs meant.

She knew what was happening.

And she continued anyway.

> **Phoenix:** “Stay with me.”

The employee remained conscious for part of the intervention.

He looked at Phoenix.

> **Employee:** “I'm trying.”

Phoenix continued working.

> **Phoenix:** “I know.”

---

# 4. THE MOMENT

Eventually, his condition became irreversible.

Phoenix knew it before the monitors confirmed it.

She continued attempting to stabilize him anyway.

There was a final change in his breathing.

Then silence.

Phoenix remained beside him.

She checked again.

And again.

Then stopped.

She knew.

The patient was dead.

---

# 5. PHOENIX'S RESPONSE

Phoenix did not immediately leave.

She remained sitting beside the body.

Her hands were still positioned as though she were about to continue treatment.

She eventually whispered:

> **Phoenix:** “I'm sorry.”

No response.

She said it again.

> **Phoenix:** “I'm sorry.”

---

# 6. THE BANLANDS OPTION

Director Lucious was informed.

The situation created an unusual possibility.

Aperture's relationship with Lucy Merryweather allows deceased personnel to potentially be restored from the Banlands.

The procedure is not hypothetical.

Aperture has previously invoked its agreement with Lucy to restore personnel who were killed during major incidents.

Accordingly, the first question was:

> **“Do we bring him back?”**

The answer was already known.

---

# 7. THE EMPLOYEE'S WISH

The deceased had previously discussed the subject of death with senior personnel.

His position had been recorded.

His reasoning was simple.

He had spent his life working so that his grandson could have a better future.

He knew his grandson might eventually live without him.

He accepted that possibility.

He also wanted to see his wife again.

His exact statement was preserved:

> **“I know, you can revive your employees.”**

> **“But leave me to rest.”**

> **“I've worked my life to get my grandson to a better future.”**

Pause.

> **“He may never see me again.”**

> **“But please...”**

> **“I just want to meet my wife.”**

And finally:

> **“If I die, just leave it so.”**

---

# 8. DIRECTOR LUCIOUS

Lucious reviewed the circumstances personally.

Lucy could potentially restore the employee.

But doing so against his explicitly stated wishes would violate the reason Aperture uses that restoration capability in the first place.

Lucious therefore issued the following instruction:

> **“Don't call Lucy.”**

No revival request was made.

The employee was allowed to remain dead.

---

# 9. PHOENIX LEARNS

Phoenix was informed that the employee had explicitly refused restoration.

She did not react immediately.

Then:

> **Phoenix:** “He knew?”

The attending personnel confirmed that he had.

Phoenix looked down.

> **Phoenix:** “And he still chose it?”

> **C.O.R.E. WELFARE OFFICER:** “Yes.”

Phoenix was silent.

> **Phoenix:** “He wanted to see his wife.”

> **C.O.R.E. WELFARE OFFICER:** “Yes.”

Phoenix nodded.

She understood.

She did not agree with it emotionally.

But she understood.

---

# 10. THE GUILT

Phoenix struggled significantly afterward.

Not because she believed she had caused the death.

She knew she had not.

Not because she had failed to attempt treatment.

She had done everything she could.

The guilt came from something more fundamental.

She had watched someone die.

And this time, she could not bring him back.

Phoenix knows what death looks like.

She has experienced it herself thousands of times.

She has watched people die.

She has watched people fail to return.

But every time she sees another patient die, something in her still reacts as though she should have been able to prevent it.

---

Phoenix later wrote:

> 
> **I knew what his condition meant.**
> 
> **I knew what I had to do.**
> 
> **I did it.**
> 
> **It wasn't enough.**

> 
> **“It wasn't enough.”**
> 
> **I know doctors can't save everyone.**
> 
> **Everyone knows that.**
> 
> **Knowing it doesn't make watching someone die any easier.**

> 
> **Not because he hated living.**
> 
> **He wanted to go home.**
> 
> **He wanted to see his wife.**
> 
> **I don't know how to feel about that.**

---

# 12. THE PROBLEM WITH PHOENIX

C.O.R.E. has identified a recurring pattern.

Phoenix understands intellectually that death is sometimes unavoidable.

She understands medically that some injuries cannot be reversed.

She understands that physicians cannot save every patient.

Yet when a patient dies in front of her, she experiences the event personally.

Her internal question becomes:

> **“What did I miss?”**

Then:

> **“What could I have done differently?”**

Then:

> **“Why wasn't I enough?”**

This pattern is particularly severe because Phoenix herself is incapable of remaining dead.

Her own body has taught her that death can be reversed.

Watching someone else die therefore creates an especially painful contradiction.

---

# 13. THE EMPLOYEE'S FUNERAL

No revival was requested.

No restoration occurred.

The employee was given a private memorial.

His family was informed.

His grandson received the support and resources the employee had spent decades working to provide.

His wife was already deceased.

Phoenix attended the memorial.

She remained at the back.

She did not speak.

---

Phoenix wrote one final statement afterward.

> 
> **He was a person.**
> 
> **He had a grandson.**
> 
> **He had a wife.**
> 
> **He had a life before I ever met him.**
>

>

---

# C.O.R.E. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

Phoenix's distress is consistent with her established inability to emotionally compartmentalize patient deaths.

Her reaction should not be interpreted as evidence that she believes she caused the death.

She understands that she did not.

The guilt instead appears connected to her identity as a Medical Assistant.

Phoenix measures herself by whether she can keep people alive.

Her own Transcendense has complicated this further.

She can return from death.

Her patients cannot necessarily do the same.

Therefore, every failed resuscitation carries an additional psychological weight:

> **“My body gets another chance. Their body didn't.”**

The deceased employee's decision provides an important counterpoint.

Phoenix could not save him because he did not want to be saved.

He wanted rest.

He wanted his wife.

Aperture respected that.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There was a time when Aperture would have been able to answer death with a phone call.

**Call Lucy.**

Ask for the dead to be returned.

Let the Banlands reverse what happened.

This time, the phone never rang.

Because the man on the table had already made his choice.

He had spent his life working.

He had spent his life building something better for his grandson.

He had lost his wife.

And when death finally came for him, he did not ask Aperture to fight it.

He asked them to let him go home.

Phoenix could not understand that immediately.

She wanted to save him.

That is what doctors do.

They fight.

They stabilize.

They refuse to accept the end.

But sometimes the person lying in front of them has already accepted it.

And perhaps that is the lesson Phoenix is going to struggle with most:

**Not every death is a failure.**

Sometimes a patient dies despite everything a doctor can do.

Sometimes a patient dies because they have chosen to.

And sometimes the final act of care is not bringing someone back.

It is letting them rest.

Phoenix still wishes she could have saved him.

She probably always will.

But she did not fail him.

She stayed until the end.

And when he asked to meet his wife again—

Aperture let him.
