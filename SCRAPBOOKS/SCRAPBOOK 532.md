### **BEHAVIORAL / ENVIRONMENTAL PREFERENCE REPORT**

# **CORIN — RAIN AND SNOW**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 — Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Observation Type:** Environmental Preference  
**Status:** Stable

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin has developed a very clear preference regarding weather.

She **hates rain**.

She **loves snow**.

The distinction is largely related to her wings.

Rain soaks through her feathers and leaves them heavy, uncomfortable, and difficult to manage.

Snow, however, produces an entirely different reaction.

Corin finds it beautiful.

---

# **RAIN**

Corin strongly dislikes rainy weather.

When exposed to prolonged rain, she tends to:

- Fold her wings tightly against her back.
    
- Avoid flying.
    
- Seek shelter.
    
- Complain about wet feathers.
    
- Spend less time outdoors.
    

Her most common response is simply:

> **"I hate rain."**

When asked why:

> **"It gets everywhere."**

She particularly dislikes having to dry her wings afterward.

---

# **SNOW**

Snow produces almost the opposite response.

Corin enjoys standing outside during snowfall.

The snow accumulates across her wings and feathers, forming a thin white layer over them.

She usually allows it to accumulate for a while.

Then she shakes her wings.

---

# **THE SHAKE**

When Corin rapidly shakes both wings, the accumulated snow is thrown into the air.

The result is a large cloud of snowflakes surrounding her.

Under sunlight or artificial lighting, the falling snow catches the light and creates a brief shimmering effect.

Corin considers this:

> **"Really pretty."**

She has been observed deliberately waiting for more snow to accumulate simply so she can repeat the process.

---

# **ORPHIE'S OBSERVATION**

Orphie has started recognizing the behavior.

When she sees Corin standing outside during snowfall, she knows what is coming.

> **Orphie:**  
> "You're going to shake them again, aren't you?"

Corin smiles.

> **"Maybe."**

Orphie waits.

Corin shakes her wings.

A cloud of snow falls around her.

Corin laughs.

---

# **SAFEHOUSE REACTION**

Belle particularly enjoys watching Corin do this.

She once described it as:

> **"Like a giant bird made of snow!"**

Corin seemed pleased by the comparison.

Neko, meanwhile, has established one rule:

> **"Don't do that indoors."**

Corin agreed.

She has followed the rule.

Mostly.

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi witnessed the behavior during one winter visit.

Corin had been standing beneath a tree while snow accumulated on her wings.

Lumi watched her shake them.

The snow fell around Corin like a curtain.

Lumi smiled.

> **"You really like snow."**

Corin nodded.

> **"It's quiet."**

Then she looked at her wings.

> **"And it looks nice on them."**

---

# **FLIGHT IN SNOW**

Corin is also comfortable flying during snowfall.

She generally flies at lower speeds than normal because of reduced visibility.

Snow does not appear to interfere significantly with her Ether-integrated wings.

In fact, Corin occasionally flies specifically because she enjoys watching snow trail behind her.

She describes it as:

> **"Flying through glitter."**

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION**

Corin's enjoyment of snow is notable because it is one of the few things she appreciates about her altered body without qualification.

She does not think about whether her wings are strange.

She does not think about the laboratory.

She does not think about being alive or dead.

She simply enjoys the snow.

For a few moments, her wings are not evidence of what happened to her.

They're simply wings.

And snow is simply pretty.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Rain makes Corin miserable.

Snow makes her happy.

Rain leaves her wings heavy.

Snow gives her an excuse to shake them dramatically and create a miniature snowfall of her own.

One weather condition makes Corin hide indoors.

The other makes her stand outside, spread her wings, and smile.

Her current unofficial weather policy is therefore:

> **Rain:** "No."

> **Snow:** **"Absolutely."**

**End of Behavioral / Environmental Preference Report**
