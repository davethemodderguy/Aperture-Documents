# **KRAMPUS COMPLIANCE AUTHORITY**

## **CONFIDENTIAL PSYCHOLOGICAL EVALUATION**

### **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD**

**Classification:** LEVEL 5 – Medical, Psychological & Executive Personnel Only

---

## **CLINICAL SUMMARY**

Following a voluntary counseling session, Corin Lockwood spoke in greater detail about how she understands her own condition.

Medical staff emphasize that Corin's statements reflect **her personal perception**, shaped by severe childhood trauma, and **not** the conclusions of the KCA Medical Department.

Although stabilized Ether now sustains her physiology, KCA physicians recognize Corin as a living patient deserving of the same care and dignity as any other member of personnel.

Corin, however, struggles to see herself that way.

---

## **PATIENT STATEMENT**

When asked why she insists on regular Ether-stabilizing medication despite her condition being medically stable, Corin sat quietly for several moments before answering.

> "Knowing that you're dead..."

> "...and believing you were never supposed to come back..."

She lowered her eyes.

> "You feel hollow inside."

Another pause.

> "Sometimes..."

> "...I wish I could have stayed where it ended."

She quickly clarified.

> "Not because I don't value the life I have now."

> "Because I still remember what it felt like to lose it."

She rested a hand over her chest.

> "Being taken away from death without anyone asking you..."

> "...changes the way you see yourself."

---

## **TRAUMATIC MEMORY**

Corin's recollections remain unusually clear.

Unlike Norma's fragmented nightmares, Corin remembers brief moments with unsettling precision.

She recalls the laboratory becoming increasingly chaotic.

Raised voices.

Equipment alarms.

People arguing over readings.

Then...

One voice that never rose above a calm, detached tone.

> "I don't care if she dies."

A pause.

> "Increase the dosage."

Corin stated that she remembers those words more vividly than almost anything else from that day.

Not because they were shouted.

Because they were spoken as though her life had no value.

---

## **CURRENT MANAGEMENT**

Corin continues to take prescribed medication that helps regulate the stabilized Ether integrated into her physiology.

The medication is intended to reduce discomfort and maintain physiological balance.

She has never missed a scheduled dose.

When asked why she is so meticulous, she simply replied:

> "It's part of staying healthy."

Medical staff noted that her diligence reflects both responsibility and lingering anxiety about her condition.

---

## **SELF-PERCEPTION**

During the session, Corin quietly admitted:

> "...Sometimes I still think of myself as a walking corpse."

The attending psychologist allowed a moment of silence before responding.

> "That may be how the people who harmed you taught you to see yourself."

> "It is not how the people who know you today see you."

Corin did not answer immediately.

Instead, she looked down at the wedding band she wore on a chain around her neck—a personal keepsake she often used for comfort.

After several moments, she gave a small nod.

## **CLINICAL NOTE**

Corin's recurring thoughts about herself are understood by the psychological team as expressions of longstanding trauma rather than objective descriptions of her condition.

She remains engaged with treatment, adheres to medical recommendations, and continues to perform her responsibilities with exceptional professionalism.

Her care plan focuses on helping her reconcile her memories with the life she has built since then, while ensuring she has continued support from both medical staff and those closest to her.
