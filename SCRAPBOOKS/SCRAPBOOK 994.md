## Executive Summary

At approximately **2100 hours**, Neri was observed in the Sector 4-C cafeteria reading books.

Her presence was considered normal.

Neri has a documented history of severe insomnia, and remaining awake between **2100 and 0100 hours** is not considered unusual for her. No immediate medical concern was reported during this period.

At approximately **0230 hours**, however, Neri experienced an acute deterioration associated with her ongoing anemia.

She subsequently **lost consciousness and collapsed in the cafeteria**.

Due to severe weather conditions, the Blacksite had been operating under a weekend rest protocol. Nonessential personnel had been instructed to remain in their quarters, and most SAFEHOUSE residents were taking the day off.

As a result, Neri remained undiscovered for approximately **six and a half hours**.

She was discovered at approximately **0900 hours**.

Medical personnel and Bao were immediately contacted.

---

# 1. Timeline

### 2100 — 0100 HOURS

Neri remains in the cafeteria reading.

Available observations indicate:

* No unusual behavior.
* No reported distress.
* No request for medical assistance.
* Continued reading despite the late hour.
* No indication that Neri intended to sleep.

Personnel familiar with Neri's insomnia did not consider her presence unusual.

**Assessment:** Normal for Neri.

---

### 0100 HOURS

The cafeteria remains occupied by Neri.

No personnel report interaction with her after this point.

Environmental monitoring records no significant disturbance.

---

### 0230 HOURS

Neri develops a severe anemic episode.

Her physiological condition deteriorates beyond the point at which she can remain conscious.

Neri loses consciousness and collapses.

No emergency call is initiated.

No manual alert is activated.

No resident witnesses the collapse.

**Important:** This was not an intentional attempt by Neri to conceal her condition. She was already unconscious.

---

### 0230 — 0900 HOURS

Neri remains unconscious in the cafeteria.

The facility's reduced weekend activity significantly decreases the likelihood of someone entering the area.

Weather conditions further reduce movement throughout Sector 4-C.

No one discovers Neri during this interval.

---

### 0900 HOURS

A SAFEHOUSE resident enters the cafeteria and discovers Neri on the floor.

The resident immediately recognizes that something is seriously wrong.

Medical response is initiated.

Bao is contacted.

Additional SAFEHOUSE residents are alerted.

The cafeteria is temporarily converted into a medical-response area while Neri's condition is assessed.

---

# 2. Medical Assessment

Neri's condition is consistent with her established blood disorder.

Her white blood cells continue to incorrectly target her own red blood cells.

As the red-cell population falls, Neri becomes progressively anemic.

Bao's blood remains the only reliably compatible intervention currently available to stabilize Neri's blood condition.

It can:

* temporarily suppress the abnormal attack on Neri's red blood cells;
* restore a usable population of red blood cells;
* provide enough circulating blood to stabilize her cardiovascular system.

It does **not** correct the underlying disorder.

Consequently, even after recovery from this incident, Neri remains medically vulnerable to another spontaneous anemic episode.

---

# 3. Why Was She Not Found?

The investigation identified several factors.

### 3.1 Weekend Rest Protocol

The Blacksite had granted personnel and residents a day off because of worsening weather.

Normal cafeteria traffic was therefore dramatically reduced.

### 3.2 Neri's Insomnia

Neri being awake at 0200 hours did not immediately appear suspicious.

Her long-standing inability to sleep has made late-night activity relatively routine.

### 3.3 Location

The cafeteria is normally a high-traffic area.

Under ordinary operating conditions, an unconscious resident would likely have been discovered significantly sooner.

The combination of the weekend schedule and severe weather effectively turned the cafeteria into a low-traffic location.

### 3.4 Neri's Behavioral Pattern

There was no evidence that Neri attempted to attract attention before losing consciousness.

This is consistent with an established behavioral concern:

> **Neri frequently attempts to endure physical symptoms without informing others.**

In this particular incident, however, the absence of an alert cannot be interpreted solely as deliberate concealment.

Once she lost consciousness, she was incapable of requesting assistance.

---

# 4. Post-Incident Review

The incident has prompted renewed concern regarding Neri's overnight safety.

The previous assumption that:

> **"Someone will eventually find her."**

is no longer considered an acceptable safety measure.

It was fortunate that Neri was found at all.

The six-and-a-half-hour interval between collapse and discovery represents a significant failure of residential safety coverage, particularly for a resident with a known condition capable of causing sudden unconsciousness.

C.O.R.E. additionally notes that the weather closure was not itself an error.

The problem was that the existing safety system assumed that reduced personnel activity would not materially affect emergency discovery.

That assumption has now been disproven.

---

# 5. Immediate Recommendations

The following measures have been recommended:

1. **Overnight welfare checks** for Neri during periods of severe anemia risk.
2. **Weekend and weather-closure coverage** for SAFEHOUSE residents with known acute medical vulnerabilities.
3. Installation of additional **non-invasive medical monitoring** in common areas.
4. Automatic notification to medical personnel if Neri remains stationary for an abnormal period.
5. Continued availability of Bao for emergency blood stabilization.
6. Review of Neri's tendency to remain alone in common areas during overnight hours.
7. Most importantly, residents are reminded that **Neri being awake is not itself an emergency.**

The objective is not to prevent Neri from reading at night.

The objective is to ensure that if she collapses again, she is found **before six hours have passed.**

---

# 6. Resident Response

The incident produced a strong emotional response among SAFEHOUSE residents.

Several residents reportedly reacted with anger.

Medical personnel determined that much of this anger was directed at the situation rather than at Neri herself.

Neri's established response to such situations remains a concern.

Historically, when others become frightened or angry after discovering that she has been hiding symptoms, Neri tends to interpret their reaction as confirmation that she has caused trouble.

C.O.R.E. therefore recommends that post-incident discussions emphasize:

> **Neri did not cause an inconvenience by becoming sick.**

> **She did not waste anyone's time by requiring medical attention.**

> **The purpose of emergency procedures is specifically to deal with emergencies.**

---

## 7. Final Assessment

Neri survived the incident.

She remains anemic and medically dependent upon continued stabilization measures.

No evidence indicates intentional self-harm.

No evidence indicates that Neri deliberately attempted to remain undiscovered after her condition became critical.

The primary contributing factors were:

**spontaneous anemia + sudden loss of consciousness + reduced weekend traffic + severe weather + insufficient overnight monitoring.**

The incident is therefore classified as a **medical emergency with preventable delay in discovery**.

---

### C.O.R.E. Closing Note

Neri was sitting in the cafeteria at 2100 hours reading a book.

Nobody thought anything of it.

At 0100, she was still reading.

At 0230, she was unconscious on the floor.

At 0900, someone finally found her.

The distinction between those three observations is precisely why this incident has been escalated.

**A resident does not need to look sick for a medical emergency to occur.**

**And Neri does not need to ask for help for us to decide that she deserves it.**
