# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At approximately 08:10, Aries Akana accompanied his younger brother, Jake, outside the Blacksite.

At approximately 08:17, Aries re-entered the facility.

The exterior access door closed behind him.

Jake remained outside.

Due to a sequence of oversights, Aries did not realize that Jake had not followed him inside.

The door did not reopen.

No immediate welfare check was initiated.

For the next **five hours**, Jake remained outside in the snow.

When personnel finally discovered him, he was sitting near the exterior entrance.

Snow had accumulated across his hair, shoulders, clothing, wings, and lap. The accumulation was substantial enough that his small body appeared partially buried beneath it.

He was visibly shivering.

He did not speak.

Personnel initially attempted to guide him indoors.

Jake complied without resistance.

Several minutes after reaching the interior, he collapsed.

Medical personnel identified a critical cold-exposure emergency and immediately initiated treatment.

His condition was serious enough to warrant emergency intervention.

The fact that Jake's skin is normally warm despite his cold-blooded physiology complicated initial visual assessment. His established thermal characteristics did not provide protection against prolonged environmental exposure.

The incident is under formal investigation.

Aries has been removed from operational duties pending review.

No disciplinary determination has been made.

**C.O.R.E. preliminary finding:** The incident was accidental. The resulting danger was nevertheless severe and preventable.

# 1. BACKGROUND — ARIES AKANA'S NEW POSITION

Aries had recently been accepted into the Nine Tailed Fox operational program.

His initial assignment was an evaluation position as an **Operator**.

The purpose of this assignment was to document his existing capabilities, assess his suitability for field operations, and determine which weapon category best matched his demonstrated skills.

Neri was assigned to record his performance.

Her evaluation covered:

- marksmanship;
    
- target acquisition;
    
- accuracy under varying conditions;
    
- consistency across repeated trials;
    
- familiarity with different weapon categories;
    
- weapon handling and safety;
    
- preferred engagement distance;
    
- reaction to changing target conditions;
    
- and overall suitability for the Operator role.
    

Aries demonstrated extraordinary marksmanship.

His first recorded trials were sufficiently accurate that the evaluators initially suspected an instrumentation error.

The equipment was checked.

The targets were checked.

The measurements were repeated.

The results remained consistent.

Neri's preliminary assessment described his marksmanship as **exceptional, repeatable, and substantially beyond the expected baseline for an inexperienced candidate**.

Aries was pleased.

More importantly, he had finally acquired a position of his own.

For once, he was not simply Jake's older brother, a mysterious anomalous entity, or the teenager who had threatened the Director at the Blacksite entrance.

He had a job.

He had a schedule.

He had something to learn.

And, according to Aries, the best part of the assignment was that Nine Tailed Fox operators had considerable downtime between evaluations, training, and scheduled duties.

He had apparently interpreted _downtime_ as _permission to slack off whenever possible_.

Neri had already begun correcting this interpretation.

Unfortunately, the incident occurred before she could finish.

# 4. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

**SUBJECT:** Jake Akana  
**INITIAL CONDITION:** Critical cold exposure with altered responsiveness and subsequent loss of consciousness.

Jake had been exposed to severe winter conditions for approximately five hours.

His condition was consistent with a potentially life-threatening cold-exposure emergency, including concern for hypothermia.

His shivering, reduced responsiveness, and eventual collapse indicated that he could no longer safely compensate for the environmental conditions.

The absence of verbal complaints did not indicate an absence of distress.

Nor did his ability to walk indoors indicate that he was medically stable.

In fact, the interval between recovery and collapse demonstrated why his apparent cooperation should not have been mistaken for recovery.

### Immediate medical priorities

Personnel initiated emergency assessment and treatment.

The response included:

- evaluation of breathing and circulation;
    
- continuous monitoring of vital signs and neurological responsiveness;
    
- careful handling to minimize additional physiological stress;
    
- controlled rewarming under medical supervision;
    
- removal of wet or snow-saturated outer clothing as appropriate;
    
- and assessment for complications associated with prolonged cold exposure.
    

Jake was not left alone during treatment.

His teddy bear was located and kept with him. Because the bear is Jake's cardiac vessel and carries his actual heartbeat, it was treated as medically essential rather than as an ordinary comfort object.

### Anomalous physiology

Jake's cold-blooded nature did not protect him from the cold.

His skin is normally warm despite his blood being cold. Neither characteristic should be used as a substitute for a full medical assessment.

C.O.R.E. specifically rejects the assumption that an anomalous entity is immune to an environmental condition simply because its physiology differs from that of an ordinary human.

Jake's body has limits.

Those limits were approached dangerously closely.

### Prognosis

At the time this report was opened, Jake remained under medical observation.

His collapse made it inappropriate to declare him safe merely because he had been brought indoors.

His eventual recovery, the presence or absence of lasting complications, and the time required to return to his previous baseline remained matters for the medical team to determine.

**No personnel are authorized to describe the incident as harmless or to presume that Jake has recovered without medical clearance.**

# 5. THE TEDDY BEAR

During the initial recovery, one responder asked whether Jake's teddy bear should be transported separately to avoid interfering with medical equipment.

The question was immediately rejected.

The teddy bear is Jake's cardiac vessel.

Its heartbeat is Jake's actual heartbeat.

It is not an optional accessory.

It is not a replaceable comfort item.

It must remain with Jake unless an exceptional medical necessity requires otherwise.

During the incident, the bear had accumulated snow along its outer fabric.

Medical personnel carefully cleared the snow while ensuring that the bear remained with Jake.

The bear was subsequently examined for moisture and physical damage.

No unnecessary separation was permitted.

The medical team also noted that Jake's silence made it especially important not to rely on him to request the bear himself.

He had not requested help.

He had not requested to come inside.

He had not asked anyone to retrieve his belongings.

That did not mean he did not need them.

# 8. THE FIVE-HOUR GAP

The investigation identified multiple missed opportunities to recognize Jake's absence.

Aries's assumption that Jake had followed him was the initial error.

It was not the only failure.

The exterior access system recorded that Aries entered alone.

The system did not automatically identify that Jake, a registered resident, had remained outside.

No automatic welfare alert was generated.

The residential monitoring process did not identify Jake's prolonged absence from his expected locations.

Personnel who knew Jake sometimes spent long periods alone did not consider that his absence might indicate an emergency.

The exterior conditions made visual detection more difficult as snow accumulated.

Each failure contributed to the length of the exposure.

The review therefore distinguishes between two questions:

1. **How did Jake end up outside?**
    
2. **Why did it take five hours for anyone to notice?**
    

The first question concerns Aries's mistake.

The second concerns the Blacksite's safety systems.

Both require answers.

C.O.R.E. will not permit the facility to treat the second question as irrelevant simply because the first has an obvious answer.

Aries's extraordinary marksmanship remains documented.

However, marksmanship is not the entirety of an Operator's suitability.

The position also requires situational awareness, accountability, adherence to safety procedures, and the ability to recognize when another person's welfare depends on one's actions.

Aries's failure to verify Jake's location represents a serious lapse in judgment.

His new position is therefore subject to review.

Pending the outcome:

- live-fire evaluation is suspended;
    
- operational deployment is not authorized;
    
- access to controlled weapons remains governed by standard Nine Tailed Fox procedures;

- and Aries is to cooperate with the incident investigation.
    

His spear remains subject to its existing controlled-inspection and storage requirements.

No decision has been made regarding permanent removal from the Operator program.

**C.O.R.E. does not recommend terminating Aries's position solely as a punitive response.**

The immediate priorities are Jake's recovery, a full investigation, and preventing recurrence.

Any final personnel decision must account for the severity of the error, Aries's response, the circumstances surrounding the incident, and the safeguards that failed.

His skill with a weapon does not excuse this incident.

This incident does not erase his skill with a weapon.

# 10. RESIDENT WELFARE — JAKE'S SILENCE

One of the most troubling details in the incident is that Jake did not call for help.

Investigators have not established why.

He may have expected Aries to return.

He may have believed that he had done something wrong.

He may have been too cold or exhausted to communicate effectively.

He may have simply been waiting.

It is also possible that several of these factors were present at once.

C.O.R.E. will not assign a definitive psychological explanation without speaking with Jake when he is medically stable and willing to do so.

However, his established behavior warrants consideration.

Jake has previously demonstrated reluctance to interrupt others with his needs.

He often seeks comfort through physical proximity rather than direct requests.

He is also particularly attached to Aries, his older brother, whom he spent years separated from before their reunion.

The possibility that Jake remained outside expecting Aries to return must therefore be taken seriously.

This is not proof that Jake believed he had been abandoned.

It is, however, a reason to avoid treating his silence as evidence that he was comfortable.

**A child does not have to ask for rescue before rescue becomes necessary.**

# 13. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS

The incident has prompted an immediate review of exterior access and resident-accountability procedures.

### Exterior access monitoring

C.O.R.E. will assess whether the access system can identify when a resident enters or exits without their accompanying party.

Any proposed system must respect established security restrictions. Residential doors will not be modified to permit unauthorized phone-based, Bluetooth, or application-based access.

### Severe-weather welfare checks

During significant snowfall or other hazardous conditions, exterior access areas must be checked at defined intervals.

Accumulated snow must not be permitted to conceal a person without triggering inspection.

### Resident absence protocol

Jake's preference for solitude must not be treated as a reason to ignore unexplained absences during hazardous conditions.

A resident who normally spends time alone may still require an urgent welfare check.

### Companion accountability

When a resident accompanies a child or another person who may require assistance, the accompanying individual must confirm that the person has reached the intended destination.

This is a safety measure, not a declaration that Jake is incapable of moving independently.

### Emergency response

Personnel are to treat prolonged cold exposure, severe shivering, altered responsiveness, or collapse as urgent medical concerns.

Suspected severe hypothermia requires professional emergency care and careful handling. Personnel must not attempt to force an affected person to walk or rely on apparent responsiveness as proof of safety.

### Personal reassurance

When Jake is medically stable, the welfare team will offer him the opportunity to discuss the incident.

He will not be compelled to reassure Aries.

He will not be required to forgive him.

He will not be asked to minimize what happened in order to protect his brother's feelings.

Jake's recovery is not contingent upon making Aries feel better.

# FINAL OBSERVATION

Aries had wanted a job.

A place where he could prove himself.

A place where people would judge him by what he could do rather than by the strange circumstances surrounding his arrival.

He had been good at it.

Exceptionally good.

His marksmanship had impressed the evaluators. His results had been repeatable. Neri had been preparing to recommend further assessment.

For a few hours, Aries had been able to imagine a future in which he was more than the older brother who had spent years searching for Jake.

Then he walked through a door.

He assumed Jake was behind him.

And he did not look back.

Five hours later, Jake was carried inside beneath a layer of snow.

He was shaking so badly that personnel could see it from a distance.

He did not speak.

He followed them indoors.

And then he collapsed.

There was no dramatic accusation.

No shouted explanation.

No demand that Aries answer for what he had done.

Jake was too unwell to provide any of those things.

That silence is not evidence of forgiveness.

It is not evidence that he understood.

It is not evidence that he was unafraid.

It is simply what happened.

Aries found his brother again after years of separation. He had promised himself, implicitly or otherwise, that he would not lose him.

This time, he did not lose Jake to the sky.

He did not lose Jake to some unknowable force.

He lost track of him because he assumed.

And now he has to face the fact that loving someone fiercely does not make mistakes impossible.

It makes the responsibility to prevent them more important.

But he can take responsibility.

He can listen.

And, when Jake is ready, he can begin earning back the trust that his mistake put at risk.
