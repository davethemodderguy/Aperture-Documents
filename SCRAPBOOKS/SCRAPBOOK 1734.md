**SUBJECT:** Jake — Deterioration of Interpersonal Trust Following Repeated Life-Threatening Incidents

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has begun questioning whether the people around him genuinely intend to keep him safe.

Three statements have been recorded during recent interactions.

> **"Why does everyone forget me behind?"**

> **"Why I am locked outside?"**

> **"Are you trying to kill me?"**

These statements represent a significant change in Jake's perception of his relationships with other SAFEHOUSE residents.

Previously, Jake generally regarded the residents around him as people he could trust.

Bao was someone who looked after him.

Clover was someone who stayed beside him.

Aeterna was someone who understood what it meant to exist as something other than an ordinary human.

Neri was someone with whom he could share quiet moments without being expected to speak.

The other residents were his friends.

They were the people who had celebrated his first birthday, signed his painting, watched the stars with him, and welcomed him into their lives.

Recent events have complicated that understanding.

Jake has been forgotten or left behind on multiple occasions.

He was injured during Anby's sleepwalking episode.

He was nearly poisoned.

He was left outside in heavy snowfall for approximately twelve hours after the entrance was locked.

He subsequently collapsed from severe hypothermia.

His recovery has involved persistent internal cold, unusual respiratory vapor, and an apparent struggle to regulate his own physical condition.

Each incident has had a different immediate cause.

From Jake's perspective, however, they may be beginning to form a single, deeply troubling pattern.

**The people he trusts keep becoming the people he has to survive.**

C.O.R.E. cannot conclude that Jake believes every resident is deliberately attempting to harm him.

The available evidence suggests something more complicated.

Jake may be losing confidence in the distinction between someone who loves him and someone whose actions could cause him to die.

He knows that his friends care about him.

He also knows that their mistakes have nearly killed him.

He is struggling to reconcile those two facts.

---

# 1. THE THREE QUESTIONS

## 1.1 — "Why does everyone forget me behind?"

This question concerns more than physical separation.

Jake has experienced being left behind before.

His absence from the lives of others was once an existential problem: when people forgot him, he became increasingly disconnected from the world that had once given him meaning.

The more recent incidents have given that fear a physical dimension.

Being forgotten no longer means merely that nobody remembers his name.

It can mean that nobody notices he is missing.

Nobody checks whether he has returned.

Nobody realizes that he is still outside.

And nobody knows that he is lying unconscious until someone happens to find him.

Jake's experience has therefore created a potentially dangerous association:

> **If people forget me, I can die before they remember I am gone.**

The birthday celebration may have made this fear more painful.

For the first time, Jake saw an entire community make something specifically for him.

Every signature on the painting was evidence that someone had thought about him.

Every figure beneath the painted stars represented someone who had chosen to be there.

For one evening, Jake was not forgotten.

He was the reason everyone had gathered.

The subsequent incidents may have made that experience feel less secure.

If so many people care about him, why does he keep ending up alone in situations where nobody realizes he needs help?

C.O.R.E. considers this a significant source of distress.

---

## 1.2 — "Why I am locked outside?"

This question is particularly concerning because Jake is asking about an event with a straightforward physical explanation.

Neri believed Jake was returning inside.

Jake wandered away to examine a rose.

The door was locked.

He knocked.

Nobody answered.

The explanation is simple when reconstructed from surveillance footage.

It is considerably less simple from the perspective of a six-year-old who has spent hours freezing outside.

Jake does not experience the incident as a sequence of access-control decisions.

He experiences it as a series of moments.

He was with Neri.

Neri went inside.

The door closed.

He returned.

The door would not open.

He knocked.

Nobody came.

He waited.

He became cold.

He continued waiting.

Eventually, he could no longer remain conscious.

He had no reliable way to know whether the people inside were unaware of his situation, unable to hear him, or simply unwilling to let him in.

He only knew that he needed someone to open the door.

Nobody did.

The fact that the lock functioned correctly does not change the experience.

The fact that Neri did not intend to leave him outside does not change the experience.

The fact that the residents would have helped him had they known does not change the experience.

Those facts matter when determining responsibility.

They do not erase what happened to Jake.

**An accident can be unintentional and still destroy someone's sense of safety.**

---

## 1.3 — "Are you trying to kill me?"

This question is not currently interpreted as a literal accusation against a specific resident.

It appears to reflect Jake's growing difficulty distinguishing deliberate harm from repeated dangerous outcomes.

Consider the incidents from his perspective.

Anby hurt him during a sleepwalking episode.

He was nearly poisoned.

He was left outside in freezing weather until he collapsed.

On separate occasions, people he trusted were involved in circumstances that placed his life at risk.

He may understand that no one intended these outcomes.

However, intent is invisible.

Jake cannot directly observe whether someone means well.

He can observe what happens to him afterward.

Someone can tell him that they love him.

Someone can hold his hand.

Someone can promise that he is safe.

And then something can happen that nearly kills him.

The resulting question is not necessarily:

_Do you consciously want me dead?_

It may instead be:

_How can I know that you are safe to trust when being with you keeps ending with me hurt?_

That distinction is essential.

Jake's question should not be dismissed as irrational, manipulative, or unfair.

It is a frightened child's attempt to understand an environment that has repeatedly become dangerous despite the presence of people who care about him.

# 2. INCIDENT CORRELATION

C.O.R.E. has reviewed the recent events as a cumulative welfare concern.

|Incident|Immediate circumstance|Potential consequence|
|---|---|---|
|Sleepwalking incident|Anby injured Jake while sleepwalking|Serious physical injury; required recovery and assessment|
|Near-poisoning|Jake was exposed to a substance that nearly poisoned him|Potentially fatal poisoning|
|Prolonged cold exposure|Jake remained outside after SAFEHOUSE's entrance was locked|Severe hypothermia and loss of consciousness|
|Post-hypothermic complications|Jake experienced persistent internal cold and abnormal cold-vapor respiration|Continued medical instability; underlying mechanism unresolved|

These incidents should not be treated as interchangeable.

They had different causes, different responsible circumstances, and different medical implications.

Nevertheless, their cumulative effect on Jake may be more significant than any individual event.

Each incident provides another reason for him to question whether his environment is safe.

Each recovery requires him to trust that the next person who approaches him will help rather than hurt him.

Each reassurance must compete with his memory of what happened.

The danger is not simply that Jake may become frightened.

It is that he may begin to regard danger as an ordinary consequence of allowing himself to depend on others.

---

# 3. THE DISTORTION OF TRUST

**Assessment status:** Preliminary psychological formulation. Not a formal diagnosis.

Jake's understanding of his relationships appears to be changing.

Previously, his friends and family represented security.

Now, those same relationships may carry contradictory meanings.

Bao is his mom.

Bao also belongs to a world in which Jake has nearly died.

Clover stays with him.

Clover cannot guarantee that nothing bad will happen while she is nearby.

Aeterna is extraordinarily powerful.

Even Aeterna cannot prevent every incident involving Jake.

Neri is a friend who shares quiet companionship with him.

Neri unknowingly locked the door through which Jake expected to return.

Anby forgave herself after the sleepwalking incident, and Jake forgave her.

But forgiveness does not guarantee that Jake's memory of being hurt has disappeared.

The relationships remain real.

The affection remains real.

The danger he experienced is also real.

Jake may therefore be developing a distorted expectation:

> **The people closest to me are the people I am most likely to be hurt around.**

This does not mean that he has stopped loving them.

It means that love may no longer feel sufficient to establish safety.

He may want to sit beside Bao while simultaneously worrying that he will be left alone.

He may want Clover nearby while remaining uncertain whether she will notice when something goes wrong.

He may want to trust Neri while remembering the locked door.

He may still forgive Anby while becoming frightened whenever she moves unexpectedly.

These reactions can coexist.

A child does not have to stop loving someone before becoming afraid of what might happen around them.

# 4. PRIVATE OBSERVATION — JAKE

**LOCATION:** MEDICAL WARD  
**TIME:** 10:42  
**PARTICIPANTS:** Jake, C.O.R.E. Welfare Officer

Jake was sitting upright in bed.

His teddy bear rested against his chest.

His physical recovery remained incomplete. He occasionally shivered, and his breathing continued to produce visible cold vapor.

The welfare officer sat at a respectful distance.

No recording equipment was placed directly in front of Jake.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"How are you feeling today?"

Jake shrugged.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Are you still feeling cold?"

Jake nodded.

A small cloud of vapor escaped his mouth.

Silence followed.

**JAKE:**  
"Why does everyone forget me behind?"

The officer did not answer immediately.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"I don't think everyone is trying to forget you."

Jake looked down at his teddy bear.

**JAKE:**  
"But they do."

The officer paused.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Sometimes people make mistakes. That doesn't mean they want you hurt."

Jake's fingers tightened around the bear.

**JAKE:**  
"Why I am locked outside?"

The officer had no satisfactory answer that could be delivered in a single sentence.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Neri thought you were coming back inside. She didn't know you were still outside."

Jake looked toward the door of the medical room.

**JAKE:**  
"I knocked."

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"I know."

**JAKE:**  
"I waited."

His voice became quieter.

**JAKE:**  
"It was cold."

The officer remained silent.

Jake drew the teddy bear closer.

**JAKE:**  
"I was really cold."

A longer pause followed.

**JAKE:**  
"Are you trying to kill me?"

The officer did not tell him that he was being silly.

The officer did not tell him to stop thinking that way.

The officer did not immediately list every resident who loved him.

Instead, the officer answered the question directly.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"No. I am not trying to hurt you."

Jake looked at the officer.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"And the people who care about you should be taking better care of you than they have been."

Jake's eyes lowered again.

**JAKE:**  
"...Then why does it keep happening?"

The officer had no answer that would make the incidents disappear.

After several seconds, the officer said:

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"We need to change what happens next."

Jake did not respond.

He continued holding his teddy bear.

The interview ended without further questioning.

### Observation

Jake did not appear to be seeking an argument.

He appeared to be seeking an explanation that could reconcile the affection people expressed toward him with the repeated situations in which he had been placed in danger.

No explanation was sufficient.

# 5. BAO — MATERNAL WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Bao's relationship with Jake makes the situation particularly difficult.

She has cared for him, comforted him, and treated him as a child deserving of protection.

However, maternal affection cannot retroactively prevent a dangerous incident.

Jake may know that Bao loves him.

He may also wonder why that love has not made his circumstances safer.

The distinction between emotional reassurance and practical protection must therefore be maintained.

If Jake asks Bao whether she is trying to kill him, the appropriate response is not to become offended.

It is not to insist that he should know better.

It is not to make him apologize for asking.

Bao should answer him calmly, acknowledge his fear, and allow him to explain what led him to ask.

A suitable response might be:

> **BAO:**  
> "No, sweetheart. I would never want you hurt."

Then:

> **BAO:**  
> "But I know that isn't enough to make you feel safe right now."

And finally:

> **BAO:**  
> "You were hurt. You were left outside. You were frightened. I can't ask you to pretend those things didn't happen."

The objective is not to convince Jake that Bao loves him.

He already has reasons to believe that.

The objective is to help him understand that his safety is a responsibility the adults around him must demonstrate through their actions.

---

# 6. NERI — THE LOCKED DOOR

Neri's involvement requires particular care.

She did not knowingly abandon Jake.

She believed he was returning indoors.

The available evidence supports that account.

Nevertheless, Jake's experience of the incident may be associated with Neri specifically because she was the last person he saw before the door closed.

He may remember her entering the building.

He may remember the lock engaging.

He may remember knocking on the door she had just passed through.

He may remember that she never came back.

Those memories can persist even when he understands that she did not know he was there.

Neri should not be forced into a confrontation intended to obtain immediate forgiveness.

Nor should Jake be required to comfort Neri about the guilt she feels.

If they speak about the incident, Neri should be permitted to explain what she believed was happening while also acknowledging what happened to Jake.

A possible exchange:

**NERI:**  
"I thought you were coming inside."

Jake looks at her.

**JAKE:**  
"I was."

Neri lowers her eyes.

**NERI:**  
"I didn't know you were outside."

Jake holds his teddy bear closer.

**JAKE:**  
"I knocked."

Neri does not attempt to contradict him.

**NERI:**  
"I know."

A long pause.

**NERI:**  
"I'm sorry."

Jake looks away.

He does not answer immediately.

Neri does not demand that he forgive her.

The conversation ends there.

That is acceptable.

An apology can be sincere without immediately restoring trust.

Forgiveness, if it comes, belongs to Jake.

---

# 7. ANBY — THE MEMORY OF BEING HURT

The sleepwalking incident presents a different problem.

Anby did not intentionally decide to attack Jake.

She was sleepwalking.

The circumstances were abnormal, and she subsequently took responsibility for what happened.

Jake forgave her.

Anby forgave herself.

Neither development establishes that Jake has forgotten the experience.

He may still associate sudden movements, unfamiliar sounds, or unexpected physical contact with the possibility of being hurt.

If this occurs, Anby should not interpret it as proof that Jake has withdrawn his forgiveness.

Nor should she insist that he trust her because the incident was accidental.

She can demonstrate that she is safe through consistent behavior, predictable boundaries, and appropriate precautions against another sleepwalking episode.

Jake does not need to decide immediately whether he feels comfortable around her.

**Forgiveness is not amnesia.**

And it is not a requirement to feel safe before safety has been re-established.

---

# 8. THE NEAR-POISONING

The near-poisoning may have contributed to Jake's growing uncertainty about ordinary situations.

Food, drinks, medication, and routine interactions are generally expected to be safe within SAFEHOUSE.

An incident involving poisoning can undermine that assumption.

C.O.R.E. has not established that Jake now distrusts everything offered to him.

However, the possibility must be considered.

If Jake hesitates before accepting food, asks whether something is safe, or watches someone prepare his drink, personnel should not mock him or insist that he is being paranoid.

The appropriate response is to provide clear information and maintain the established safety procedures.

He should not be expected to accept reassurance without evidence simply because the person offering it is a friend.

His trust should not be tested.

It should be earned through reliable conduct.

---

# 9. PSYCHOLOGICAL RISK ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. identifies several potential consequences if Jake's current perception of danger persists.

### A. Hypervigilance

Jake may begin monitoring the movements and behavior of residents who were previously sources of comfort.

He may become unusually attentive to doors closing, people leaving rooms, changes in routine, or signs that someone is about to depart.

### B. Withdrawal

He may avoid activities that require him to depend on others.

This could include accompanying residents outdoors, sleeping while someone else is responsible for him, or accepting assistance when physically weak.

### C. Reluctance to seek help

If Jake begins believing that people will not notice his distress, he may stop calling for assistance.

This is particularly concerning given his history of exhaustion-related fainting and his recent hypothermic collapse.

### D. Distorted threat perception

Jake may begin interpreting accidents as evidence of deliberate hostility.

A locked door may feel like rejection.

An unanswered question may feel like indifference.

An unexpected touch may feel threatening.

These interpretations should be understood in the context of his recent experiences rather than dismissed as arbitrary behavior.

### E. Deterioration of attachment security

Jake may continue loving the people around him while becoming less willing to rely on them.

This is particularly concerning because his current physical condition makes reliable support important.

He needs to be able to tell someone when he is cold, tired, injured, or frightened without first determining whether that person can be trusted not to abandon him.

---

# 10. REQUIRED RESPONSE

C.O.R.E. recommends that Jake's care focus on restoring practical safety before demanding emotional reassurance.

Personnel are instructed to:

- Acknowledge the incidents without minimizing their consequences.
    
- Explain what is known about each event without inventing excuses.
    
- Avoid promising that nothing bad will ever happen again.
    
- Establish reliable check-ins and clear procedures when Jake accompanies someone outside.
    
- Ensure that his location is accounted for when an outing ends.
    
- Maintain medical monitoring during his recovery from hypothermia.
    
- Allow him to decline physical contact or companionship when uncomfortable.
    
- Avoid forcing reconciliation with residents involved in previous incidents.
    
- Respect his need for rest, reassurance, and predictable routines.
    
- Demonstrate through consistent behavior that someone will notice if he is missing.
    

The objective is not to persuade Jake that his fears are incorrect.

The objective is to reduce the circumstances that have made those fears understandable.

---

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's view of his relationships appears to have become distorted by the accumulation of dangerous experiences.

He may still know, intellectually, that his friends and family care about him.

But he may no longer trust that their care will reliably protect him.

That distinction is the central concern.

Jake's current questions are not adequately addressed by telling him that everyone loves him.

He has heard affection expressed before.

He has also experienced situations in which that affection did not prevent him from being hurt.

What he needs now is evidence that the people around him can recognize danger, respond when he needs help, and change the circumstances that placed him at risk.

C.O.R.E. cannot guarantee that accidents will never occur.

It can require that foreseeable risks be taken seriously.

It can ensure that security procedures account for resident welfare.

It can establish clear responsibility when someone accompanies Jake outside.

It can insist that medical vulnerabilities are not ignored because Jake possesses extraordinary abilities.

And it can prevent the people around him from treating his distress as an inconvenience simply because they did not intend to cause it.

Jake is not required to understand every failure of the adults around him.

He is not required to forgive immediately.

He is not required to feel safe because someone tells him that he is.

**Safety must become something he experiences, not merely something he is promised.**

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake once stood beneath a painted sky with everyone he loved.

They had made the painting together.

They had written their names across it.

They had sat beside him beneath the stars.

For a little while, he had been certain that he belonged.

Then came the incidents.

The injury.

The near-poisoning.

The locked door.

The snow.

The hours of waiting.

The cold that settled so deeply into his body that even after waking, he struggled to feel warm again.

Now he asks questions that nobody wants to hear from him.

"Why does everyone forget me behind?"

"Why I am locked outside?"

"Are you trying to kill me?"

They are painful questions.

Not because they are unfair.

Because they reveal how far the distance has grown between what Jake was told about his family and what he has recently experienced around them.

He may still love Bao.

He may still want Clover beside him.

He may still want to trust Aeterna.

He may still forgive Neri.

He may still regard Anby as a friend.

But those relationships no longer exist in his mind without the memories of what happened.

Love and fear have begun occupying the same spaces.

And every time someone tells him that he is safe, a small part of him may remember sitting outside a locked door, waiting for someone who never came.

C.O.R.E. cannot erase that memory.

It cannot ask Jake to forget the cold.

It cannot make the injury disappear by explaining that it was accidental.

It cannot restore his trust through a single conversation.

What it can do is ensure that the next time Jake is outside, someone knows where he is.

That the next time he knocks, someone responds.

That the next time he becomes too weak to stand, someone notices.

And that the next time he asks whether the people around him are trying to kill him, the answer is supported by more than words.

Because Jake has already spent enough time wondering whether the people who love him will notice when he is gone.

**He should never have to wonder whether they will notice while he is still here.**
