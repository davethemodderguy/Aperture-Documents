## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora has been medically cleared from the immediate emergency following his severe injuries and extraordinary blood loss.

He has since returned to his den on SAFEHOUSE Floor 7.

His behavior has changed considerably.

He spends most of his time sitting inside the small, doghouse-like opening built into the wall. He sits quietly, with his head lowered and his body curled inward, resembling a sad, exhausted dog.

He does not appear interested in leaving.

He does not eat.

He does not drink.

He does not seek conversation or companionship.

Staff have attempted to offer food and water, but Sora has shown little interest. The offers themselves appear to make him uncomfortable.

His current condition is not being classified as a continuation of the previous medical emergency. However, medical clearance does not establish that he has recovered psychologically, nor does it eliminate the need to monitor his nutrition and hydration.

The most important observation is that **Sora appears overwhelmingly uncomfortable simply being around other people.**

He is not necessarily frightened of a particular person. He seems uncomfortable with the entire experience of being approached, observed, spoken to, or offered assistance.

He has returned to the one place where he can make himself small and remain undisturbed.

That place is his den.

---

## 2. THE DEN — A FAMILIAR PLACE

Sora's den is not a conventional bedroom.

It is a small, doghouse-like space built into the wall of Floor 7. Its entrance resembles a hole in the wall, large enough for Sora to enter and settle inside.

The arrangement was deliberately made to provide him with a personal space that resembles something familiar.

This detail is important.

Before arriving at the Blacksite, Sora and Mia lived on a farm in suburban Texas. Their grandmother cared for them, and the children spent time around the farm's doghouse.

Sora had a particular role there.

He chased wolves away from the sheep.

While their grandmother was alive, he and Mia had a place in that environment. The doghouse was part of the familiar surroundings of their childhood.

Mia stayed inside.

Sora was the one who went out to keep the wolves away.

The den on Floor 7 was built with that history in mind. It gave Sora a small, enclosed space that felt more familiar than a conventional room.

Now, he has returned to it.

But the circumstances are different.

There are no sheep to protect.

There are no wolves outside.

There is no grandmother waiting for them.

And Mia is not sitting inside with him.

The den remains familiar, but the childhood it recalls is gone.

## 4. INCIDENT A — THE MEAL THAT NEVER LEFT THE ENTRANCE

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, Floor 7  
**Time:** 12:10

A staff member brought a small meal and a cup of water to Sora's den.

The items were placed near the entrance rather than pushed inside.

Sora was sitting in the back of the space.

He looked at the food.

He did not approach it.

**STAFF:** I brought you something to eat.

Sora remained still.

**STAFF:** You don't have to finish it. You can have a little whenever you want.

No response.

The staff member waited.

Sora lowered his head further.

**STAFF:** I'll leave it here, okay?

Sora did not answer.

The staff member left the food and water within reach and stepped away.

Several minutes later, another observation confirmed that neither had been touched.

The meal was eventually removed and replaced with fresh provisions.

### Assessment

The refusal did not appear confrontational.

Sora did not push the food away, throw it, or indicate anger.

He simply did not want it.

The staff member's presence may have contributed to his discomfort, but this cannot yet be established.

Future offers should be quiet and non-demanding. Staff must not turn eating into a test of cooperation.

## 5. INCIDENT B — A SMALL SPACE, A VERY QUIET BOY

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, Floor 7  
**Time:** 16:42

A staff member approached the den to check on Sora.

He was sitting near the back wall, knees drawn close to his body.

His ears were lowered.

He looked toward the entrance when the staff member arrived, then turned his face away.

**STAFF:** Hi, Sora.

No response.

**STAFF:** I'm just checking on you.

Sora shifted slightly farther into the corner.

The staff member stopped approaching.

**STAFF:** I won't come inside.

Sora remained still.

After a short pause, the staff member asked whether he wanted anything.

Sora shook his head.

**STAFF:** All right. I'll give you some space.

The staff member left.

Sora did not follow.

He remained in the same position.

### Assessment

The interaction demonstrates that even a gentle approach can be uncomfortable for Sora.

His den is a personal space, and staff must respect its entrance as a boundary.

Routine checks remain necessary, but they should be conducted with minimal intrusion.

The objective is to let Sora feel that he can remain in his den without having to defend that space from people who are trying to help him.

## 6. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT — WHEN BEING AROUND PEOPLE HURTS

Sora's withdrawal should not be mistaken for simple tiredness.

He has endured a prolonged sequence of traumatic events, including:

- The loss of his childhood home and grandmother.
    
- Years of mistreatment and isolation.
    
- The apparent loss and eventual return of Mia.
    
- Repeated experiences of being frightened or driven away by others.
    
- The destruction of the whiteboard Jake gave him.
    
- A severe argument with Mia.
    
- A period of extreme self-blame and self-injury.
    
- A critical medical emergency involving extraordinary blood loss.
    
- The loss of his voice again.
    

The cumulative effect is significant.

Sora may currently associate interaction with the possibility of disappointment, conflict, embarrassment, or emotional pain.

Even kindness may feel overwhelming when he is unable to trust that he can respond appropriately.

He may not know what to say.

He may not want to be looked at.

He may not want someone to wait for him to respond.

He may simply want to sit somewhere small where nobody expects anything from him.

C.O.R.E. cannot determine exactly what Sora experiences internally.

However, his behavior is consistent with severe emotional exhaustion and social withdrawal.

**He should not have to perform recovery for the people observing him.**

---

## 7. MIA — THE EMPTY SPACE BESIDE HIM

Mia remains an important part of Sora's history.

At the farm, the doghouse was a familiar place for both children. Mia stayed inside while Sora chased wolves away from the sheep.

Their roles were different, but they shared the same childhood.

The present situation is painful because Sora cannot currently tolerate being near her.

Mia must not be brought into the den without Sora's agreement.

No one should tell him that his sister is waiting, that she misses him, or that he should come out for her.

Even well-intentioned reminders may intensify his discomfort.

Mia also requires support. She should not be left to interpret Sora's withdrawal as proof that he no longer loves her.

For now, separation is an accommodation, not a declaration that their relationship is over.

There will be time to address their relationship when both children are ready.

---

## 8. REQUIRED SUPPORT MEASURES

### A. Preserve the den as a safe personal space

- Do not enter without a necessary welfare or medical reason.
    
- Do not block the entrance.
    
- Do not surround the den with staff.
    
- Do not remove familiar belongings without consulting the welfare team.
    
- Allow Sora to leave and return without questioning him.
    

### B. Food and hydration

- Continue offering fresh food and water.
    
- Use small, manageable portions.
    
- Leave provisions within reach without demanding that he eat in front of staff.
    
- Record intake and monitor hydration.
    
- Escalate any medically concerning refusal to the clinical team.
    

If Sora's refusal continues to threaten his health, appropriate medical intervention must be considered. His preference for solitude cannot mean abandoning his basic physical needs.

### C. Communication

Sora is not required to speak.

He is not required to write.

Staff must provide accessible ways to request help without making communication a condition of receiving it.

### D. Social contact

Visits should be brief, predictable, and optional wherever possible.

Staff should avoid repeatedly asking whether he is feeling better.

Quiet company may be offered, but Sora must be allowed to decline it.

### E. Family contact

No forced interaction with Mia.

No expectation that Jake will coax Sora out.

No expectation that either child will repair the other's emotional state.

Adults remain responsible for their welfare.

## 9. CLOSING NOTE — THE BOY WHO USED TO CHASE THE WOLVES

There was a time when Sora had somewhere to belong.

A farm in Texas.

A grandmother who cared for him and Mia.

Sheep that needed protecting.

A little doghouse where Mia could stay while Sora ran outside to chase the wolves away.

He was a little boy with a job to do.

He had a sister beside him.

He had a home to return to.

The den on Floor 7 was meant to give him a little piece of that familiarity back.

And now he sits inside it.

Quiet.

Not eating.

Not drinking.

Not asking for anything.

He looks like a sad little dog who has finally stopped trying to protect anyone.

Perhaps the hardest part is that nobody can simply tell him that the wolves are gone.

Because the things that hurt Sora were not wolves.

They were people. Memories. Losses. Arguments. Things that cannot be chased away by running fast enough.

And the people who care about him cannot fix all of that by bringing him another meal or asking him to come outside.

So we will start smaller.

We will leave food where he can reach it.

We will keep fresh water nearby.

We will knock before approaching.

We will let him sit in his little den without demanding a conversation.

And we will keep checking that he is safe, even when he does not ask us to.

Sora used to chase the wolves away so Mia could stay inside.

He does not have to protect anyone now.

He is allowed to stay inside.

He is allowed to be tired.

But he must not be left to quietly disappear from the world.

**The den is his place to rest. It must never become a place where nobody notices that he needs help.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
