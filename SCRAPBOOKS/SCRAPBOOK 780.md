# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — CAFETERIA INCIDENT & POST-INCIDENT BEHAVIOR**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 6  
**Species:** Full-Body Rabbit Thiren  
**Status:** Active — Medical Clearance Granted / Psychological Monitoring Required

---

# **BACKGROUND**

Alice has developed a consistent habit of eating lunch alone.

Despite the cafeteria frequently being filled with personnel, Alice rarely sits at a table.

Instead, she typically selects a corner of the cafeteria, sits directly on the floor, and eats quietly.

She does not appear distressed by the noise.

She simply prefers not to approach anyone.

Personnel have occasionally invited Alice to sit with them.

Her usual response is either a quiet nod followed by hesitation, or:

> "...I'm okay here."

She does not appear to believe that anyone is deliberately excluding her.

Rather, Alice has increasingly convinced herself that she should not take up anyone else's space.

---

# **INCIDENT — CAFETERIA**

**Time:** Night shift  
**Location:** Blacksite Cafeteria  
**Incident Type:** Misidentification / Accidental Personnel Injury

Later that night, Alice returned to the cafeteria.

The main dining area was largely empty.

Alice was searching quietly for leftover carrots.

She had not requested food from personnel.

She had simply noticed that some food remained and was looking for something she liked.

During this search, a security guard entered the cafeteria.

The guard observed a small figure moving around in the low-lit dining area.

Due to Alice's quiet behavior and the absence of an immediate identification signal, the guard incorrectly interpreted her as an intruder.

The guard struck Alice in the head with his baton.

The impact was significant.

Alice immediately lost her balance.

She fell **face-first onto the floor**.

---

# **IMMEDIATE RESPONSE**

Personnel initially expected Alice to cry out.

She did not.

She did not scream.

She did not call for help.

She simply remained on the floor, visibly disoriented.

After several seconds, Alice raised her head slightly.

Her speech was confused.

> **"Wha?"**

A short pause.

> **"What happened?"**

Then, quietly:

> **"Did Alice did something... wrong"**

These were the only coherent statements Alice made before losing consciousness several seconds later.

Medical personnel were immediately called.

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Alice was transported for examination.

Diagnosis:

**Concussion.**

Additional concern was raised due to Alice's history of a previous head injury.

Medical staff conducted an extended neurological assessment and observation period.

Fortunately, no severe intracranial injury was identified.

Alice eventually regained consciousness and remained medically stable.

Following observation and treatment, Alice was cleared as physically stable.

However, personnel are reminded that:

**"Fine" does not mean "unaffected."**

Alice experienced a significant head impact and must continue to be monitored for delayed symptoms.

---

# **POST-INCIDENT BEHAVIOR**

Alice has not demonstrated anger toward the security guard.

She has not demanded punishment.

She has not complained about the incident.

Instead, she avoids him.

Whenever Alice notices the specific guard nearby, she may:

- Immediately change direction.
    
- Hide behind another person.
    
- Leave the room.
    
- Lower her ears.
    
- Become unusually quiet.
    
- Refuse to approach the area.
    
- Remain close to someone she trusts.
    

When asked whether she was afraid of him, Alice initially answered:

> "...No."

After a pause:

> "...Alice just don't want to see him."

She has not voluntarily approached the guard since the incident.

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL CONCERN**

The most concerning part of the incident was not Alice's lack of crying.

It was her first assumption.

She was struck without warning.

She fell face-first onto the floor.

She was disoriented.

She had a previous head injury.

And when she finally spoke, Alice did not ask:

> "Why did you hit me?"

She asked:

> **"Did Alice did something... wrong?"**

This is consistent with Alice's increasingly negative self-perception.

She continues to interpret harm done to her as something she may have caused.

---

# **CAFETERIA POLICY REVISION**

Effective immediately:

### **1. Alice is not to be treated as an unknown intruder without positive identification.**

### **2. Security personnel must account for Alice's physical appearance and known movement patterns.**

### **3. Lethal and non-lethal force procedures must not be bypassed solely because an individual is unidentified.**

### **4. Low-light cafeteria conditions must be improved during night shifts.**

### **5. Security personnel assigned to cafeteria patrols must receive updated SAFEHOUSE resident identification profiles.**

### **6. Alice's known tendency to remain silent must be considered during searches.**

### **7. Any physical strike to Alice's head requires immediate medical evaluation.**

### **8. Alice is not required to interact with the involved guard.**

---

# **PERSONNEL NOTE**

Alice's cafeteria behavior is also being reassessed.

There is no longer any justification for allowing a six-year-old child to routinely sit alone on the floor simply because she does not ask to sit with someone.

Personnel are encouraged to offer Alice a seat.

Not forcefully.

Not insistently.

Simply offer.

If Alice chooses the corner, someone should at least know where she is.

If she wants company, she should not have to cross an entire crowded cafeteria to find it.

---

# **CORIN'S RESPONSE**

Upon learning that Alice had been struck, Corin reportedly remained silent for several moments.

Her first question was:

> "Is Alice okay?"

After receiving confirmation that Alice was medically stable, Corin asked:

> "Did she cry?"

Medical staff answered no.

Corin reportedly understood immediately.

> "...Of course she didn't."

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice is six years old.

She eats lunch alone in crowded rooms.

She searches for leftover carrots at night rather than asking someone for food.

She sits quietly in corners because she does not want to bother anyone.

And when someone mistakes her for an intruder and hits her hard enough to cause a concussion, her first concern is whether **she did something wrong.**

That response cannot be allowed to become normal.

Alice is not required to justify her presence.

She is not required to be loud enough to be noticed.

She is not required to defend herself before people decide she belongs.

And she certainly does not need to apologize for being hurt.

The involved security guard has been removed from direct interaction with Alice pending review.

Alice's avoidance of him is considered an appropriate protective response.

She is not being pressured to forgive him.

She is not being pressured to speak to him.

For now, the only requirement placed upon Alice is considerably simpler:

**Eat.**

**Rest.**

**Stay safe.**

And if she wants to sit in the corner of the cafeteria—

someone should sit nearby.

Not because Alice is helpless.

Because she should not have to be alone.
