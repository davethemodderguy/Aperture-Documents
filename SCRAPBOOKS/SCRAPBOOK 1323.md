**SUBJECT:** Jake — Generalized Distrust of SAFEHOUSE Residents

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake does not trust anyone.

This includes SAFEHOUSE residents.

This includes residents who have repeatedly helped him.

This includes residents he has known for extended periods.

This includes:

- Nora;
    
- Neri;
    
- Aeterna;
    
- Bao;
    
- Alice;
    
- Neko;
    
- Frost;
    
- Belle;
    
- Endmin;
    
- and other individuals who have demonstrated concern for his welfare.
    

The distinction is important.

Jake does not necessarily believe these individuals are malicious.

He simply does not believe that their presence guarantees his safety.

He expects people to leave.

He expects people to forget.

He expects doors to close.

He expects promises to fail.

And after repeated incidents in which exactly those things happened, Jake has stopped treating reassurance as evidence.

---

# 1. TRUST IS NO LONGER ASSUMED

Jake previously accepted that certain people would return.

That assumption has largely disappeared.

If someone tells him:

> “I'll be back.”

Jake may respond:

> “When?”

If given a time:

> “Exactly?”

If told:

> “Soon.”

Jake may simply stare at them.

He has learned that words such as:

> “soon”

> “later”

> “I'll come back”

> “don't worry”

do not provide him with useful information.

He wants evidence.

---

# 2. NORA

Nora remains the person Jake spends the most time around.

This does **not** mean Jake fully trusts her.

He loves Nora.

He enjoys her company.

He follows her.

He eats with her.

He sits beside her.

He occasionally sleeps near her.

But he still asks:

> **“You're coming back?”**

If Nora answers yes:

> **“When?”**

If she says:

> “After work.”

Jake may ask:

> **“How long?”**

If Nora leaves without giving him enough information, Jake watches the door.

He does not chase her.

He does not cry.

He simply waits.

And if she takes longer than expected:

He assumes she may not return.

---

# 3. NERI

Neri has perhaps the easiest relationship with Jake to maintain.

Neither of them requires constant conversation.

Neri can sit outside Jake's room without entering.

Jake can sit nearby without speaking.

This arrangement is comfortable.

It is not necessarily trust.

When Neri leaves, Jake sometimes asks:

> **“You're coming back?”**

Neri generally answers honestly.

> **Neri:**  
> “Yes.”

> **Jake:**  
> “When?”

> **Neri:**  
> “After I finish this.”

Jake accepts this more readily than vague reassurance.

Not because he completely trusts her.

Because she gave him something concrete.

---

# 4. AETERNA

Aeterna has deliberately avoided asking Jake to trust her.

She understands why.

When Jake asks:

> **“Will you stay?”**

Aeterna does not promise something she cannot guarantee.

Instead:

> **Aeterna:**  
> “I am here now.”

Jake sometimes accepts this.

Sometimes he asks:

> **“For how long?”**

Aeterna answers honestly.

If she must leave:

> **Aeterna:**  
> “I will leave at 04:00.”

Jake knows what that means.

He can watch the clock.

He can prepare.

He does not have to guess.

---

# 5. BAO

Bao's attempts to care for Jake are sometimes met with hesitation.

She may offer food.

Jake looks at it.

> **Bao:**  
> “It's safe.”

Jake does not immediately take it.

> **Jake:**  
> “Did you make it?”

> **Bao:**  
> “Yes.”

Jake eventually accepts it.

This is not rejection of Bao.

It is Jake verifying the situation before acting.

---

# 6. ALICE

Alice has occasionally attempted to approach Jake enthusiastically.

This has produced inconsistent results.

Alice:

> “Jake!”

Jake:

> “Hi.”

Alice:

> “Wanna play?”

Jake:

> “Maybe.”

Alice:

> “Come on!”

Jake takes one step backward.

Alice stops.

This is now understood as a boundary.

Jake does not necessarily dislike Alice.

He simply does not know what will happen when someone approaches him unexpectedly.

---

# 7. HIS ROOM

Jake's room remains his strongest boundary.

The sign remains on the door:

> **Jake's Room**  
> **please knock, I can't take suprises**

The sign is no longer treated as a cute personal quirk.

It is a direct expression of his need for control.

A knock allows Jake to prepare himself.

An opened door without warning removes that control.

Personnel are therefore reminded:

> **Knock.**

> **Wait.**

> **Ask.**

> **Do not enter unless authorized or there is a genuine emergency.**

---

# 8. THE TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear remains his primary source of security.

Not because the bear can protect him.

Because it never leaves him.

The bear contains Jake's stellar core.

It has been with him through:

- his death;
    
- his years of isolation;
    
- his hunger;
    
- his abandonment;
    
- his materialization;
    
- his nightmares;
    
- his panic;
    
- his medical incidents;
    
- and his transition into his current form.
    

Jake does not have to wonder whether the teddy bear remembers him.

It does.

It is physically part of him.

The heartbeat inside it is his.

---

# 9. SOCIAL BEHAVIOR

Jake may still approach people.

This should not be interpreted as evidence that his trust has returned.

He may walk up to someone and say:

> **“Hi.”**

He may ask:

> **“Do you want to be friends?”**

He may sit beside someone.

He may even laugh.

Then the person leaves.

Jake watches them go.

The next time he sees them, he may ask:

> **“Are you staying?”**

The contradiction is consistent.

Jake wants companionship.

He does not trust companionship.

He wants people near him.

He expects them to leave.

---

# 10. THE SAFEHOUSE PROBLEM

SAFEHOUSE was designed to give anomalous residents somewhere safe to live.

Jake understands this concept intellectually.

He does not necessarily believe it emotionally.

To him:

**SAFEHOUSE is safe until something happens.**

A locked door happened.

A fourteen-day exposure happened.

People forgetting him happened.

People entering his room happened.

People leaving happened.

Promises failed.

He has accumulated enough evidence that his brain now treats trust as a liability.

C.O.R.E. therefore rejects the idea that Jake can simply be told:

> **“You're safe here.”**

He has heard versions of that before.

The statement means very little without consistent behavior supporting it.

---

# 11. WHAT PERSONNEL SHOULD DO

Personnel should **not** attempt to force Jake into trusting them.

Do not say:

> “You can trust me.”

Do not say:

> “We're your family.”

Do not say:

> “Nobody here will ever leave you.”

Do not demand:

> “Why don't you trust us?”

Do not become offended when Jake checks a door.

Do not become offended when Jake asks when someone will return.

Do not interpret hesitation as hostility.

Instead:

### Be predictable.

If you say you will return at 21:00, return at 21:00.

If plans change, tell Jake.

If you must leave, tell him.

If you do not know when you will return, say so.

If you cannot promise something, do not promise it.

If Jake asks a question, answer it honestly.

Repeated consistency is currently more valuable to him than reassurance.

---

# 12. OBSERVATION

During one evening, Neri sat outside Jake's room.

She did not knock.

She did not enter.

She simply sat against the wall with a book.

After approximately twenty minutes, Jake opened the door.

> **Jake:**  
> “You're still here.”

Neri nodded.

> **Jake:**  
> “Why?”

Neri shrugged.

> **Neri:**  
> “I was reading.”

Jake looked at the book.

Then at Neri.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

He closed the door.

Approximately ten minutes later, he opened it again.

Neri was still there.

Jake said nothing.

He sat beside her.

Neither spoke.

After another twenty minutes:

> **Jake:**  
> “You can go.”

Neri nodded.

> **Neri:**  
> “Okay.”

She left.

Jake watched her walk away.

He did not look frightened.

He did not look reassured.

He simply watched.

---

# 13. CURRENT PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE

C.O.R.E. currently characterizes Jake's trust as:

**Severely restricted.**

His attachment is not absent.

His capacity for affection is not absent.

His desire for companionship is not absent.

His willingness to interact is not absent.

What is absent is the assumption that another person will remain reliable simply because they care about him.

This distinction is critical.

Jake can love someone without trusting them.

Jake can miss someone without believing they will return.

Jake can want someone nearby while simultaneously preparing himself for their absence.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake is surrounded by people who care about him.

He knows this.

That is not enough.

He has learned that people can care and still leave.

They can care and still forget.

They can care and still close a door.

They can care and still fail to notice he is missing.

So Jake does not ask:

> **“Do you care about me?”**

He already knows the answer might be yes.

He asks something much harder:

> **“Will you still be here?”**

And when someone answers—

Jake watches what they do.

Not what they say.

Because trust, for Jake, is no longer something someone can give him with a sentence.

It has to be demonstrated.

Again.

And again.

And again.

Until eventually—

Maybe—

He believes it.
