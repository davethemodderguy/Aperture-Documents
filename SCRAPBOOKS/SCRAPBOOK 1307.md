# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### RESIDENT WELFARE & MEDICAL DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-MED-4C-JAKE-154  
**CLASSIFICATION:** CRITICAL — RESIDENT WELFARE / MEDICAL  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE INVESTIGATION  
**SUBJECT:** Jake — Prolonged Environmental Exposure  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE PERIMETER / EXTERIOR GROUNDS

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake was unintentionally left outside the SAFEHOUSE for approximately **two weeks**.

The incident began when Nora accompanied Jake outside.

Nora was subsequently called back inside by Neri.

She returned to the SAFEHOUSE without realizing that Jake had remained beneath a tree.

The exterior door was later found locked.

Jake attempted to return inside.

He could not.

He eventually returned to the tree.

He sat beneath it.

Then the rain began.

What followed was approximately two weeks of continuous exposure to severe Icelandic weather conditions.

Rain.

Wind.

Snow.

Freezing temperatures.

Day after day.

Night after night.

Jake did not attempt to force entry.

He did not successfully alert anyone.

He remained beneath the tree.

---

# 1. INITIAL SEPARATION

Nora and Jake had been outside together.

Jake had been physically materialized for only a short period.

He was still adjusting to being able to experience the physical environment.

He sat beneath a tree while Nora wandered nearby.

At approximately 11:20, Neri called Nora back inside.

Nora immediately ran toward the SAFEHOUSE.

Jake remained beneath the tree.

He assumed she would return.

She did not.

---

# 2. THE DOOR

Approximately twenty minutes later, Jake stood up.

He walked toward the SAFEHOUSE entrance.

He reached the door.

He tried the handle.

Locked.

Jake waited.

He looked through the glass.

Nobody was immediately visible.

He waited longer.

Then:

> **"Nora?"**

No response.

Jake tried again.

The door remained locked.

He did not knock loudly.

He did not call for help.

Instead, he eventually walked back outside.

---

# 3. THE TREE

Jake returned to the same tree.

He sat beneath it.

The ground was still dry.

The weather was beginning to change.

Jake looked toward the sky.

Then toward the building.

He appeared to expect someone to notice.

Nobody did.

Eventually, he curled up beneath the tree.

His teddy bear remained wrapped around his shoulders.

He waited.

---

# 4. FIRST RAIN

The first drop landed on his face.

Jake looked upward.

Another followed.

Then another.

Within minutes, rain was falling steadily.

Jake initially remained beneath the tree.

The canopy provided limited protection.

It did not stop the weather.

The rain soaked through his clothing.

The temperature dropped.

Wind began pushing the rain sideways.

The Icelandic weather became increasingly severe.

---

# 5. PHYSICAL RESPONSE

Jake's newly physical body was not designed for prolonged exposure to such conditions.

The rain repeatedly struck exposed skin.

The sensation was described by Jake afterward as:

> **"Like little blades."**

He did not mean literal cutting.

The rain was cold enough, and the wind strong enough, that each impact became painful.

Jake pulled his cloak tighter around himself.

It did little.

He hugged his teddy bear.

The bear remained against his chest.

Its heartbeat became increasingly irregular.

Jake whispered:

> **"It's cold."**

Nobody heard him.

---

# 6. RAIN BECOMES SNOW

The rain continued.

Then temperatures dropped further.

Rain became sleet.

Sleet became snow.

Snow accumulated around the tree.

Jake remained there.

He did not leave.

He did not know where else to go.

He occasionally stood and looked toward the SAFEHOUSE.

The door remained inaccessible.

Eventually he stopped trying.

---

# 7. BLACKSITE WEATHER SEAL

At approximately the same period, C.O.R.E. initiated a weather-related external lockdown.

The Blacksite was sealed.

Exterior access was restricted.

Personnel were instructed to remain indoors unless their duties specifically required perimeter deployment.

No recreational exterior activity was permitted.

This produced an unintended consequence.

Nobody went looking for Jake.

When residents noticed his absence, they assumed:

> **Jake is hiding somewhere.**

This assumption was consistent with his established behavior.

Jake frequently hid.

He disliked being approached.

He sometimes disappeared into quiet spaces.

Therefore, his absence did not immediately trigger an emergency response.

---

# 8. DAY AFTER DAY

The weather continued.

Rain.

Snow.

Freezing wind.

Then more snow.

Jake remained beneath the tree.

He slept intermittently.

He woke repeatedly.

He shivered.

He curled tighter.

His physical body gradually became saturated.

His clothing absorbed water until it was completely soaked.

Snow accumulated on his shoulders.

Then his hair.

Then the ground around him.

The tree provided some shelter.

Not enough.

---

# 9. THE TEDDY BEAR

The teddy bear behaved differently.

Despite Jake's clothing becoming completely saturated, the bear remained remarkably dry.

No measurable water penetration was detected.

The fabric remained dry.

The stuffing remained dry.

The heart inside continued beating.

But the heartbeat changed.

It became slower.

Then irregular.

Then occasionally skipped.

Jake kept one arm around it constantly.

Even while sleeping.

Especially while sleeping.

He appeared unwilling to separate from it.

---

# 10. JAKE'S CONDITION

As the days passed, Jake's behavior became increasingly limited.

He stopped walking around the perimeter.

He stopped approaching the door.

He stopped calling for Nora.

He mostly remained beneath the tree.

Curled up.

Waiting.

At some point, he stopped expecting anyone to come.

This was not because he decided he preferred being alone.

It was because he had gradually concluded that nobody knew he was there.

---

# 11. THE WEATHER CONTINUES

The Blacksite remained sealed.

The snow accumulated.

Personnel continued their normal routines indoors.

Residents occasionally asked:

> "Has anyone seen Jake?"

The answer was usually:

> "He's probably hiding."

This assumption went unchallenged.

No one considered the possibility that Jake had been outside.

Because, according to the internal access logs:

**No resident was expected to be outside.**

---

# 12. TWO WEEKS

Approximately fourteen days after Jake was last seen inside, the weather finally began to improve.

The external seal was lifted.

Perimeter Security resumed routine patrols.

A Security Guard decided to take a short morning walk around the exterior grounds.

At approximately 06:45, the Guard noticed something beneath a tree.

At first, he assumed it was a pile of clothing.

Then it moved.

---

# 13. DISCOVERY

The Guard approached.

He found Jake.

Curled tightly on his side.

His arms wrapped around the teddy bear.

His knees pulled toward his chest.

His clothing was completely saturated.

Snow covered portions of his body.

His hair was wet and frozen together.

He was breathing.

Barely.

The Guard immediately called for medical assistance.

Jake did not respond.

He was conscious, but extremely weak.

His eyes opened briefly.

He looked at the Guard.

Then at the building.

Then back at the bear.

---

# 14. THE HEARTBEAT

The Guard could hear it.

The teddy bear's heartbeat was wrong.

Not merely slow.

Not merely fast.

**Irregular.**

A long pause.

Then several rapid beats.

Then another pause.

The Guard immediately radioed:

> **"Medical emergency. Exterior grounds. We have Jake."**

Medical personnel arrived within minutes.

Jake was lifted from the ground.

He did not resist.

He simply held the bear tighter.

---

# 15. INSIDE

Jake was brought indoors.

His clothes were removed for warming and treatment.

They were saturated with water and partially frozen.

The teddy bear was different.

It was dry.

Extremely dry.

Medical staff examined it.

No external moisture was detected.

The bear's heart continued beating.

Irregularly.

Jake remained unconscious during the initial warming period.

---

# 16. NORA

Nora was informed.

She ran toward the medical area.

The moment she saw Jake, she stopped.

His hair was still damp.

His skin was pale.

His body was wrapped in thermal coverings.

The teddy bear was held against his chest.

Nora stared.

Then remembered.

The tree.

The door.

The weather.

The fact that she had run inside.

And never returned.

Her expression changed.

> **NORA:**  
> "I..."

She stopped.

She looked at Jake.

Then at the bear.

Then back at Jake.

> **NORA:**  
> "I left him outside."

Nobody answered immediately.

Nora's voice became smaller.

> **NORA:**  
> "I locked him outside."

---

# 17. REALIZATION

Nora began crying.

Not loudly.

She simply stood beside the bed.

> **NORA:**  
> "He waited for me."

A pause.

> **NORA:**  
> "He went to the door."

Another pause.

> **NORA:**  
> "He couldn't get in."

She covered her face.

> **NORA:**  
> "And he went back to the tree."

Neri arrived shortly afterward.

She looked at Jake.

Then at Nora.

Neri did not scold her.

She simply walked over and sat beside her.

Nora whispered:

> **"I forgot him."**

Neri shook her head.

Then quietly:

> **"You didn't mean to."**

Nora cried harder.

---

# 18. JAKE WAKES

Several hours later, Jake regained consciousness.

Nora was sitting beside him.

His eyes opened.

He looked at her.

There was no anger.

No accusation.

Only exhaustion.

Nora immediately started crying.

> **NORA:**  
> "Jake..."

Jake looked at her for several seconds.

Then quietly:

> **"Hi."**

Nora broke down.

> **"I'm sorry."**

Jake blinked.

> **"For what?"**

Nora couldn't answer.

Jake looked toward the window.

Then back at her.

> **"It was raining."**

Nora nodded.

> **"I know."**

Jake hugged the teddy bear.

> **"It hurt."**

Nora began crying again.

Jake looked confused.

Then quietly added:

> **"But you're here now."**

---

# 19. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

Jake survived the exposure.

However, the event caused significant physical and anomalous stress.

Current findings include:

- severe hypothermic stress;
    
- exhaustion;
    
- dehydration;
    
- prolonged cold exposure;
    
- respiratory irritation;
    
- physical pain;
    
- cardiac irregularity localized to the teddy bear;
    
- increased manifestation sensitivity;
    
- temporary weakness;
    
- and severe emotional distress.
    

His physical materialization remained intact.

He did **not** revert to a ghost.

This is considered significant.

---

# 20. THE BEAR

The teddy bear remains physically dry.

This has not been explained.

It was exposed to the same environment as Jake.

Jake's clothes became saturated.

The bear did not.

Its fabric shows no evidence of water damage.

Its stuffing is completely dry.

Its cardiac manifestation remains functional.

C.O.R.E. currently considers the bear's unusual environmental resistance intrinsic to the curse binding Jake's heart to it.

It is not to be separated from Jake.

---

# 21. BEHAVIORAL CONSEQUENCES

The incident is expected to have significant psychological consequences.

Jake already hated being alone.

He already hid when frightened.

He already believed that people might forget him.

Now he has experienced an event that appears to confirm his deepest fear:

> **Someone he trusted left.**

Not intentionally.

Not maliciously.

But from Jake's perspective, the distinction may be difficult to process.

He waited.

He returned to the door.

He found it locked.

He went back to the tree.

And nobody came.

For two weeks.

---

# 22. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

This incident must not be described as:

> **"Jake was hiding outside."**

He was not.

He was stranded.

He attempted to return.

The door was inaccessible.

The weather became severe.

The Blacksite was sealed.

His absence was misinterpreted.

His continued silence was mistaken for voluntary isolation.

This distinction is now formally recorded.

---

# 23. NEW SAFETY PROTOCOL

Effective immediately:

**Any unexplained absence of Jake requires physical confirmation of his location.**

Personnel are no longer permitted to assume:

> "He's probably hiding."

A SAFEHOUSE-wide search must be conducted if Jake cannot be located.

Exterior grounds must be checked whenever weather conditions permit.

All external doors are now subject to resident-presence verification.

Additionally:

> **Jake is never to be left outside without another responsible resident or staff member maintaining confirmed awareness of his location.**

---

# FINAL OBSERVATION

Jake was found beneath the same tree where he had sat fourteen days earlier.

He had not wandered away.

He had not tried to leave.

He had not gone somewhere else.

He had simply stayed.

Rain fell on him.

Then snow.

Then more snow.

And through it all, he held the same teddy bear against his chest.

His clothes were soaked.

His body was freezing.

His heartbeat was failing to maintain a normal rhythm.

But the bear was completely dry.

When asked later why he had not tried harder to get back inside, Jake gave a quiet answer:

> **"The door was locked."**

The welfare officer asked:

> **"Why didn't you call for help?"**

Jake looked down.

A long pause.

> **"I thought Nora would come back."**

Silence.

Then:

> **"She always comes back."**

That sentence has been flagged for continued psychological observation.

Because Jake had already spent decades waiting for someone to return.

And for fourteen days,

beneath a tree,

in the rain and snow,

he waited again.

**— END DOCUMENT**
