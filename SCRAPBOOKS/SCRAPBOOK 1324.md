**SUBJECT:** Jake — Maternal Attachment to Bao

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake does not trust the SAFEHOUSE residents.

There is, however, one important complication.

Jake calls Bao:

> **“Mom.”**

He does not use the word casually.

He does not use it as a nickname.

He treats Bao as a maternal figure.

He seeks her out when frightened.

He sits beside her when overwhelmed.

He accepts food and drink from her more readily than from unfamiliar residents.

He sometimes follows her through the SAFEHOUSE.

He has fallen asleep near her.

And when something is wrong, Bao is one of the first people he looks for.

This creates a significant distinction in Jake's current psychological state:

> **Jake does not trust people.**

> **Jake trusts Mom differently.**

---

# 1. THE FIRST "MOM"

The exact moment Jake began calling Bao "Mom" was not formally recorded.

It appears to have developed naturally.

One evening, Bao found Jake sitting alone in the common room.

He had his teddy bear pressed against his chest.

Bao approached slowly.

> **Bao:**  
> “Jake?”

Jake looked up.

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom?”

Bao stopped.

There was a brief silence.

> **Bao:**  
> “...Yes?”

Jake held his arms out.

Bao understood.

She sat beside him.

Jake leaned against her.

No further conversation occurred.

---

# 2. IT BECAME NORMAL

After that, the word appeared increasingly often.

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom, can I have milk?”

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom, where's Nora?”

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom, it's loud.”

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom?”

Sometimes the word was not followed by a request.

Jake would simply say:

> **“Mom.”**

Bao would answer:

> **“I'm here.”**

And that was enough.

---

# 3. BAO'S RESPONSE

Bao does not force the relationship.

She does not insist Jake call her Mom.

She does not ask him to explain why.

She simply accepts it.

When Jake uses the word, Bao answers naturally.

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom.”

> **Bao:**  
> “Yes, sweetheart?”

Jake looks around.

> **Jake:**  
> “Nothing.”

> **Bao:**  
> “Okay.”

He sits beside her.

---

# 4. THE TRUST EXCEPTION

Jake may hesitate when other residents offer him food.

He may ask questions.

He may check where they got it.

With Bao, this behavior is considerably less pronounced.

If Bao places warm milk in front of him:

Jake drinks it.

If Bao offers food:

Jake generally accepts.

If Bao tells him:

> **“Sit here.”**

He may actually sit.

If Bao says:

> **“You're tired.”**

Jake may initially deny it.

But he usually remains nearby.

This is not absolute trust.

Jake still asks questions.

He still checks doors.

He still becomes frightened when people leave unexpectedly.

But Bao has been given a degree of access that most residents have not.

---

# 5. PHYSICAL AFFECTION

Jake rarely initiates physical contact with most residents.

Bao is different.

He may:

- hold her hand;
    
- lean against her;
    
- sit beside her;
    
- rest his head against her;
    
- hug her;
    
- fall asleep against her;
    
- or simply remain physically close.
    

He does not appear to regard this as surrendering control.

Bao is familiar.

Her presence has become predictable.

---

# 6. AFTER THE STELLAR DISCOVERY

The discovery that Jake is a star produced an unusual reaction from Bao.

She did not become frightened of him.

She did not treat him differently.

She simply asked:

> **Bao:**  
> “Did you eat?”

Jake looked at her.

> **Jake:**  
> “Yes.”

> **Bao:**  
> “Drink?”

> **Jake:**  
> “Yes.”

> **Bao:**  
> “Good.”

Jake stared at her.

> **Jake:**  
> “I'm a star.”

Bao nodded.

> **Bao:**  
> “I know.”

> **Jake:**  
> “A real one.”

> **Bao:**  
> “I know.”

Pause.

> **Jake:**  
> “...I'm still Jake.”

Bao reached over and adjusted his hair.

> **Bao:**  
> “Of course you are.”

Jake leaned against her.

---

# 7. THE CORE

Bao understands the importance of Jake's teddy bear.

She understands that the object contains his stellar core.

She understands that separating him from it could kill him.

Yet she does not treat the bear as something frightening.

She treats it as part of Jake.

If Jake falls asleep while holding it, Bao makes sure it stays with him.

If he accidentally leaves it somewhere, she retrieves it.

If someone unfamiliar reaches toward it, Bao intervenes.

> **Bao:**  
> “Don't.”

The tone is enough.

---

# 8. JAKE'S ROOM

Jake occasionally leaves his door open when Bao is nearby.

This is notable.

His room remains his private space.

He still dislikes unexpected entry.

But Bao may sit outside his room.

Sometimes Jake opens the door.

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom?”

> **Bao:**  
> “Yes?”

> **Jake:**  
> “Stay.”

Bao sits down.

> **Bao:**  
> “Okay.”

She does not enter unless invited.

Jake closes the door.

Bao remains outside.

---

# 9. A SMALL INCIDENT

One night, Jake woke from a nightmare.

He left his room carrying his teddy bear.

He walked through the hallway.

He passed several residents.

He ignored them.

He continued toward the kitchen.

Bao was there.

Jake stopped.

> **Jake:**  
> “Mom.”

Bao immediately looked over.

> **Bao:**  
> “Bad dream?”

Jake nodded.

Bao opened her arms.

Jake walked over.

He climbed onto the chair beside her and leaned against her.

> **Bao:**  
> “Want warm milk?”

Jake nodded.

Bao prepared it.

Jake drank quietly.

After several minutes:

> **Jake:**  
> “Can I stay?”

Bao smiled softly.

> **Bao:**  
> “Of course.”

Jake rested his head against her.

The teddy bear's heartbeat gradually slowed.

---

# 10. THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

C.O.R.E. has previously documented Jake's severe generalized distrust.

That remains accurate.

Calling Bao "Mom" does not mean Jake has suddenly developed complete trust.

Instead, Bao occupies a special category in his mind.

She is not merely:

**A resident.**

She is:

**Mom.**

That distinction appears to matter considerably.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake has spent most of his existence without a stable family.

He was found without a known origin.

He was abandoned.

He waited.

He died.

He became a ghost.

He spent decades alone.

He lost his connection to the Starlands.

He learned that promises could fail.

He learned that people could forget him.

He learned to protect himself by expecting people to leave.

And yet, somewhere inside all of that—

Jake still wanted a mother.

So he found Bao.

Not because anyone assigned the role.

Not because C.O.R.E. designated her as his guardian.

Not because she asked him to.

Jake simply looked at her one day and decided:

> **“Mom.”**

And Bao accepted.

That may be one of the few relationships in Jake's life where the title came before the trust.

He does not trust everyone.

He does not even fully trust SAFEHOUSE.

But when Jake walks into the kitchen at night, clutching his teddy bear, looks up at Bao and quietly says:

> **“Mom?”**

Bao always answers:

> **“I'm here.”**

For Jake, that answer matters.
