# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The unidentified white cat discovered within SAFEHOUSE has been confirmed to be **Jake**.

The confirmation was not obtained through conventional biometric testing.

It was established through a combination of behavioral recognition, a unique response to Jake's personal belongings, recognition of familiar individuals, and the eventual discovery of his teddy bear.

Jake has somehow transformed himself into a cat.

He does not know how he accomplished this.

He does not know how to reverse it.

And, owing to his current anatomy, **he cannot speak.**

The transformation appears to have occurred during or after his disappearance from the medical ward. The exact mechanism remains unknown.

His current condition is noticeably improved compared with his final recorded medical state before disappearing. He is more alert, has begun moving independently, and displays a greater degree of physical stability.

However, he remains under observation.

His improvement must not be mistaken for complete recovery.

His psychological condition also remains undetermined.

Jake has not been able to explain what happened to him, what he remembers, or whether he intended to transform himself in the first place.

His current means of communication consist primarily of:

- vocalizations;
    
- facial expressions;
    
- ear and tail movements;
    
- body posture;
    
- pawing at objects;
    
- approaching or avoiding individuals;
    
- and a surprisingly expressive collection of feline behaviors.
    

C.O.R.E. has therefore amended the classification of UFE-178-A.

The entity is no longer unidentified.

It is Jake.

And Jake appears to be having a very bad time being a cat.

# 1. IDENTITY CONFIRMATION

**SUBJECT:** Jake  
**CURRENT FORM:** White domestic-feline morphology  
**PREVIOUS FORM:** Human manifestation of a literal star; Weeping Angel  
**CURRENT VOCAL CAPACITY:** Feline vocalizations only  
**VERBAL COMMUNICATION:** Impossible under present anatomy  
**COGNITIVE STATUS:** Alert; identity and reasoning abilities appear substantially preserved  
**MEDICAL STATUS:** Improved from last recorded condition; continued monitoring required  
**REVERSAL METHOD:** Unknown

The first definitive indication occurred when the cat entered Jake's room.

It did not merely investigate the room.

It went directly to Jake's side of the bedroll.

It climbed onto his old blanket, turned in a circle, and settled precisely where Jake had previously rested.

When Clover approached, the cat watched her carefully.

When she sat down on the floor, it approached her without being called.

It pressed its forehead against her hand.

Clover stopped moving.

For several seconds, she did not appear to breathe.

Then she whispered:

**CLOVER:**  
"...Jake?"

The cat looked directly at her.

Its blue eyes brightened.

The tiny shooting-star streaks within its irises moved slowly across the azure glow.

It made a small sound.

"Mrrp."

Clover covered her mouth.

**CLOVER:**  
"Oh."

A pause.

**CLOVER:**  
"Oh, you're here."

The cat immediately climbed into her lap.

Clover began crying.

Jake, apparently uncomfortable with the sudden moisture accumulating on his fur, shifted several centimeters to the left.

Clover laughed through her tears.

**CLOVER:**  
"You're still you."

The cat tucked its paws beneath its body.

It did not leave.

# 2. THE FIRST PROBLEM: JAKE CANNOT EXPLAIN HIMSELF

For the first several hours after identification, personnel attempted to determine whether Jake could communicate through his usual methods.

The results were mixed.

He could recognize written words.

He appeared capable of understanding spoken conversation.

He responded appropriately to familiar names and instructions.

He also demonstrated clear awareness of his surroundings, including the medical equipment brought into his room.

However, he could not write.

His front paws were not sufficiently dexterous to hold a pen in his previous manner.

He could not operate his usual tablet reliably.

He could not point with the precision of a human hand.

And when asked a direct question, he could only respond with feline vocalizations.

This created an immediate communication problem.

**JANE:**  
"Jake?"

The cat looked at her.

**JANE:**  
"Can you understand me?"

Jake blinked once.

Jane hesitated.

**JANE:**  
"That could mean anything."

Jake blinked again.

Jane crouched to his level.

**JANE:**  
"Do you know how you became a cat?"

Jake stared at her.

His ears gradually rotated backward.

His tail struck the floor once.

Then again.

Jane watched the tail.

**JANE:**  
"...I'm going to assume that's a no."

Jake made a low, frustrated sound.

"Mrrrrow."

**JANE:**  
"Right. Sorry."

He turned away from her.

Jane remained crouched for several seconds.

**JANE:**  
"Okay. We don't have to figure it out right now."

Jake looked back.

Jane held out her hand without moving closer.

After a moment, Jake sniffed her fingers.

He did not retreat.

That was the end of the conversation.

It was also the first indication that his inability to speak might be considerably more frustrating to him than it was to everyone else.

# 3. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. Medical Division conducted a limited, non-invasive assessment.

Jake was not subjected to unnecessary procedures merely because his current appearance was anomalous.

The immediate objectives were to establish whether he was in acute distress, whether the transformation had introduced new physiological instability, and whether his improved condition could be maintained.

## Observed improvements

Compared with his final recorded condition before disappearance, Jake currently demonstrates:

- greater alertness;
    
- improved spontaneous movement;
    
- less visible physical exhaustion;
    
- a more stable resting posture;
    
- improved responsiveness to familiar people;
    
- and an apparent ability to regulate his body temperature more effectively.
    

These changes are encouraging.

They do not establish that his underlying condition has resolved.

His previous medical crisis was severe enough that the absence of obvious symptoms cannot be treated as proof of recovery.

## Remaining uncertainties

C.O.R.E. has not established:

- whether his original human manifestation remains intact elsewhere;
    
- whether the cat is a new physical manifestation or a complete transformation of his existing body;
    
- whether his teddy bear remains physiologically connected to him;
    
- whether his previous cardiac condition has changed;
    
- whether the transformation consumes energy;
    
- whether his star-based abilities remain accessible;
    
- or whether the transformation can be reversed voluntarily.
    

The last question is especially significant.

Jake has repeatedly attempted to return to his previous form.

No successful reversal has been observed.

His frustration increases when these attempts fail.

Medical personnel have been instructed not to encourage repeated attempts that leave him visibly exhausted.

**The fact that Jake is a cat does not make his body indestructible.**

He remains a vulnerable resident recovering from a serious medical event.

# 4. THE CATASTROPHIC FOOD INCIDENT

At 10:16, a member of the welfare team made an understandable but deeply unfortunate assumption.

Jake was a cat.

Therefore, Jake should receive cat food.

A small bowl was placed in front of him.

The staff member stepped back.

Jake looked at the bowl.

Then at the staff member.

Then at the bowl again.

His expression was difficult to interpret through feline anatomy.

Nevertheless, several witnesses independently described it as _offended_.

Jake leaned forward and sniffed the contents.

He immediately recoiled.

His ears flattened.

His entire body seemed to become smaller.

He stared at the bowl as though somebody had placed an insult in front of him and expected him to be grateful.

The staff member attempted reassurance.

**WELFARE STAFF:**  
"It's all right, little guy. It's for cats."

Jake slowly turned his head.

He looked directly at the staff member.

His tail lashed once.

**JAKE:**  
"HSSSSS!"

The staff member withdrew.

Jake moved away from the bowl.

He then walked directly to the table, where a plate containing ordinary cooked chicken had been left for another resident.

He sat beside the plate.

Looked at the chicken.

Looked at the staff member.

Looked at the chicken again.

The implication was unmistakable.

**JANE:**  
"Oh, absolutely not."

The staff member looked at her.

**JANE:**  
"That's Jake."

She pointed at the untouched bowl.

**JANE:**  
"Jake hates that."

Jake gave a short, emphatic meow.

**JANE:**  
"See? He's filing a complaint."

The bowl was removed.

Jake was offered a small portion of plain, boneless cooked chicken appropriate for him, without seasoning or unsafe ingredients. He ate a little, then drank water.

He appeared considerably less offended.

## Revised feeding protocol

Jake is not to be fed commercial cat food simply because he currently resembles a cat.

His preferences, nutritional requirements, and medical condition must be considered together.

Food should be appropriate for his current physiology, with veterinary and medical guidance used to establish a safe diet.

Where suitable, familiar human foods may be offered in safe portions.

Chocolate, onions, garlic, grapes, raisins, alcohol, and other potentially toxic foods remain prohibited.

Jake's personal preferences should be respected wherever medically possible.

**C.O.R.E. NOTE:** His transformation has not erased his personality.

It has apparently preserved his capacity to judge people for giving him things he dislikes.

# 5. THE TEDDY BEAR

At 11:03, the missing teddy bear was located.

It was found in Jake's room, tucked beneath the edge of his old blanket.

No personnel had placed it there.

The bear's presence was the strongest remaining indication that Jake's disappearance and transformation were connected.

When the cat saw it, he stopped walking.

He approached slowly.

Then he pressed his nose against the bear's fabric.

He remained motionless for several seconds.

Finally, he climbed onto the blanket and curled around it.

His front paws rested against the bear's torso.

He pulled it closer.

The behavior was consistent with Jake's previous attachment to the object.

More importantly, subsequent monitoring detected a heartbeat-like rhythm associated with the bear.

The signal was weak but present.

It was not identical to Jake's last recorded cardiac signal.

It was, however, consistent enough to warrant continued observation.

No attempt was made to separate the bear from Jake.

The relationship between the two remains too poorly understood to justify unnecessary interference.

Clover watched from the doorway.

**CLOVER:**  
"You found it."

Jake looked up.

Clover approached slowly and sat on the floor beside him.

**CLOVER:**  
"Good."

She did not reach for the bear.

She did not ask him to surrender it for testing.

She simply remained beside him.

Jake lowered his head onto the bear's stomach.

His eyes closed.

For approximately twenty minutes, he did not move.

His breathing remained steady.

The heartbeat-like rhythm continued.

For the first time since his disappearance, the medical team recorded a sustained period of apparent rest without visible agitation.

# 8. ATTEMPTS AT REVERSAL

C.O.R.E. has documented several spontaneous attempts by Jake to restore his former body.

None have succeeded.

### Attempt A — Concentration

Jake sat on the blanket, closed his eyes, and remained motionless for approximately two minutes.

He then opened his eyes.

He was still a cat.

He looked down at his paws.

His ears drooped.

### Attempt B — Familiar Posture

Jake attempted to stand upright on his hind legs.

He remained upright for approximately three seconds before losing his balance and sitting down abruptly.

No injury was sustained.

The attempt was discontinued after Aeterna intervened to prevent further strain.

### Attempt C — Visual Reference

Jake examined an old photograph of himself.

He looked at the photograph.

Then at his reflection in a small mirror.

Then at the photograph again.

He attempted to reproduce his previous facial expression.

The result was a slow blink.

He stared at the mirror with visible displeasure.

### Attempt D — Vocalization

Jake attempted to say his own name.

The resulting sound was:

"Mrrrow."

He immediately turned away and hid beneath the blanket.

No further attempts were observed for the next two hours.

C.O.R.E. has classified the reversal attempts as **unsuccessful and potentially distressing**.

Jake will not be required to repeat them for documentation.

# 9. COMMUNICATION ACCOMMODATIONS

The transformation has removed Jake's ability to use his usual speech.

His cognitive abilities appear preserved, but his capacity to communicate is now substantially reduced.

C.O.R.E. will therefore provide several accommodations.

### 9.1 Choice-based communication

Personnel will use simple questions that permit clear responses through movement.

Examples:

- "Do you want to stay here?"
    
- "Do you want me to leave?"
    
- "Do you want water?"
    
- "Do you want company?"
    
- "Do you want the door open?"
    

Questions should be presented individually.

Jake should not be expected to respond immediately.

### 9.2 Visual choices

A tablet will be adapted to display large, easily distinguishable symbols.

Jake may be able to select options using his nose or paw.

The interface must be tested with Jake's cooperation and must not require repeated unsuccessful attempts.

### 9.3 Familiar objects

His books, blanket, teddy bear, and other personal belongings may be used to establish preferences.

Personnel must not assume that interacting with an object automatically communicates a specific emotion.

### 9.4 Respect for refusal

Moving away, flattening his ears, hiding, or otherwise withdrawing may indicate discomfort.

These behaviors should prompt personnel to pause rather than pursue him.

They should not automatically be interpreted as hostility.

### 9.5 No forced performance

Jake will not be asked to demonstrate his inability to speak.

He will not be repeatedly prompted to transform back.

He will not be treated as an attraction because his eyes resemble Aeterna's.

He is a resident.

Not an exhibit.

# 10. PSYCHOLOGICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Jake's transformation has occurred during an already severe period of psychological distress.

He has recently experienced repeated situations in which his safety depended on other people, followed by serious harm or perceived abandonment.

He has also experienced a period of critical medical instability.

C.O.R.E. cannot currently determine whether the transformation was:

- a deliberate act;
    
- an involuntary defensive response;
    
- a spontaneous anomalous event;
    
- an attempt to escape his previous physical condition;
    
- or an entirely unrelated phenomenon.
    

Jake's inability to explain the event makes premature conclusions particularly inappropriate.

Personnel must not tell him that he transformed himself because he was afraid.

They must not tell him that the cat form is a blessing.

They must not insist that he should be happy to have survived.

And they must not assume that improved physical stability means he is ready to resume ordinary social contact.

**Jake may be less sick without feeling safe.**

Those are separate conditions.

His current form may even introduce additional distress: he can understand people but cannot answer them in the way he previously could.

He may hear reassurance without being able to say whether he believes it.

He may want help without knowing how to request it.

He may wish to explain something important and be limited to a meow.

The response must therefore emphasize patience, accessible communication, and consistency.

Not pressure.

- He cannot speak.

- He has shown no successful attempt at reversal.

**Jake is not refusing to turn back. He does not know how.**

Personnel must not interpret his continued feline form as a decision to remain a cat.

# C.O.R.E. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake is alive.

He is more physically stable than he was before his disappearance.

He has returned to SAFEHOUSE.

He has his teddy bear.

He has his blanket.

He has people who recognize him.

These are meaningful improvements.

They do not erase what happened before.

Jake is still unable to explain his disappearance. He cannot tell anyone what he experienced while he was gone. He cannot describe why he transformed, whether he remembers doing so, or what he needs to feel safe again.

He cannot even say his own name.

For now, he is a small white cat with blue eyes and shooting stars moving through them.

He eats ordinary food when it is safe for him.

He drinks water.

He sleeps on his old blanket.

He follows people he trusts, sometimes.

He hides from people he does not wish to approach.

He dislikes cat food with a degree of conviction that has already become a matter of official record.

And, when he is frustrated, he still manages to communicate that frustration quite clearly.

He has not become a different person simply because his body has changed.

He is still Jake.

He is still allowed to be frightened.

He is still allowed to be angry.

He is still allowed to refuse contact.

And he is still entitled to care.
