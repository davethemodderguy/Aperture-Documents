# Executive Summary

Phoenix was unable to sleep again tonight.

This time, however, the cause appears different from her usual nighttime insomnia.

Phoenix's established inability to voluntarily sleep remains present.

Tonight's wakefulness was additionally intensified by **guilt concerning the recent incident involving Jake's teddy bear.**

Phoenix accidentally threw Jake's teddy bear into a wall after mistaking it for a nearby ball.

Jake was physically unharmed beyond temporary pain.

His cardiac core remained stable.

The incident was accidental.

Jake forgave her.

Phoenix has not forgiven herself.

# 5. THE PROBLEM

Phoenix's guilt is not primarily focused on the physical damage.

The injuries were negligible.

Jake recovered.

The teddy bear remained functional.

The medical danger passed quickly.

Phoenix's distress instead appears connected to the **trust involved.**

Jake trusted her.

She accidentally hurt him.

Her current reasoning appears to be:

> **“If I can hurt someone I care about by accident, then I need to be more careful.”**

This has developed into:

> **“If I'm not careful enough, I might hurt him again.”**

Which has developed into:

> **“What if I shouldn't be around him?”**

C.O.R.E. considers the final conclusion unwarranted.

# 9. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT

Phoenix's current sleeplessness appears to be a combination of:

- her established nighttime insomnia;
    
- persistent physical fatigue;
    
- guilt;
    
- repetitive recollection of the incident;
    
- fear of accidentally harming Jake again.
    

There is currently no evidence that Phoenix intends to isolate herself from Jake permanently.

There is also no indication that she wishes to harm herself.

Her distress is specifically centered around the possibility that she may hurt someone she cares about.

# C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Phoenix does not appear to need repeated reassurance that Jake is physically unharmed.

She already knows.

What she appears to need is time to accept that an accident does not invalidate her relationship with him.

Jake has already made his position clear.

He forgave her.

He returned to her.

He trusted her enough to sit beside her again.

Phoenix's responsibility now is not to punish herself indefinitely.

It is to learn from the accident and continue being present.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Phoenix has spent years waking up from death.

Tonight, she could not sleep because she kept remembering one moment.

A hand reaching for the wrong object.

A teddy bear flying through the air.

A small voice crying out.

And the realization:

> **“That's his heart.”**

Jake is fine.

His teddy bear is fine.

He forgave her.

But Phoenix is still awake.

Perhaps because she is used to fixing things.

Broken bones.

Wounds.

Bleeding.

Ether instability.

People who stop breathing.

Those problems have solutions.

Guilt is different.

There is nothing to bandage.

Nothing to stitch.

Nothing to revive.

Sometimes the only thing that can repair an accident is time.

So, for tonight, C.O.R.E. recommends something simple.

Let Phoenix stay beside Jake.

Let Jake hold his teddy bear.

And let Phoenix slowly learn that being forgiven does not require her to keep suffering.
