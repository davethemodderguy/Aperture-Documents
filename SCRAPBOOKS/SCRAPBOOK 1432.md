# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake was accidentally left outside the SAFEHOUSE for approximately **three hours**.

The incident occurred during an outdoor activity with Neri.

Neri had taken Jake outside to play.

During the activity, Neri was called back inside.

She returned to the SAFEHOUSE.

She did not remember that Jake was still outside.

Jake subsequently attempted to return.

He could not.

Jake does not currently possess a functional SAFEHOUSE keycard.

The exterior access system therefore did not recognize him as an authorized entrant.

Jake remained outside while exposed to:

- rain;
    
- snow;
    
- low temperatures;
    
- and prolonged environmental moisture.
    

He did not successfully summon assistance.

After approximately three hours, he was discovered and brought inside.

Jake subsequently developed a **fever**.

Given his already severely depleted condition, the exposure is considered medically significant.

---

# 1. INITIAL ACTIVITY

Neri requested that Jake accompany her outside.

The activity was recreational.

No mission, experiment, or operational purpose was involved.

Jake reportedly accepted.

His current physical condition meant that he spent much of the activity sitting or moving slowly.

Neri remained nearby.

The two were described as spending most of the time simply being outside together.

There was no indication of distress at this stage.

---

# 2. NERI IS CALLED INSIDE

Approximately midway through the activity, Neri was called back into the SAFEHOUSE.

The reason was routine.

Neri immediately responded.

She entered the building.

She did not intentionally abandon Jake.

She did not tell him to remain outside.

She did not realize that he was no longer beside her.

This is consistent with Neri's established tendency to become intensely focused on whatever immediate task or request has captured her attention.

Unfortunately:

> **Jake was still outside.**

---

# 3. JAKE ATTEMPTS TO RETURN

Jake eventually decided to go inside.

He approached the SAFEHOUSE entrance.

He attempted to access the door.

The system did not recognize him.

Jake checked himself.

No keycard.

He looked toward the interior.

The door remained inaccessible.

Jake attempted again.

Nothing.

He could not call for assistance.

His voice remains unavailable.

He did not possess a functioning communication device.

He therefore remained outside.

---

# 4. WEATHER CONDITIONS

Weather conditions deteriorated during the following period.

Rain began falling continuously.

Snow followed.

Jake was exposed to both.

His clothing gradually became soaked.

His already limited ability to regulate his physical condition was placed under additional stress.

His stellar glow became increasingly difficult to see through the precipitation.

At some point, Jake stopped attempting to enter.

He sat near the entrance.

---

# 5. PASSIVE WELFARE FAILURE

The existing Jake welfare protocol should have identified his absence.

It did not.

The reason is currently under investigation.

The protocol primarily requires personnel to monitor Jake when he is **within areas of normal SAFEHOUSE activity.**

Jake was outside the normal monitoring environment.

His absence was not immediately interpreted as an emergency because:

- he had previously gone outside voluntarily;
    
- Neri had been accompanying him;
    
- no distress signal was received;
    
- and personnel assumed that he had returned with her.
    

This assumption was incorrect.

---

# 6. THREE HOURS

Jake remained outside for approximately:

> **3 HOURS.**

He did not make significant attempts to attract attention.

He did not bang on the door.

He did not attempt to break the entrance.

He did not leave the immediate area.

He simply waited.

At several points, he reportedly looked through the entrance toward the interior.

No one noticed.

Eventually, Jake sat down.

His head tilted farther to one side.

His already faint glow became almost impossible to distinguish from the surrounding darkness.

---

# 7. DISCOVERY

A resident eventually noticed Jake's absence from the SAFEHOUSE.

A subsequent check of the exterior revealed him.

He was sitting near the entrance.

His clothing was soaked.

Snow had accumulated around him.

His halo was resting heavily against his head.

His eyes were half-closed.

His teddy bear was pressed against his chest.

The bear remained silent.

Jake looked toward the person who found him.

He did not attempt to stand.

He simply raised one hand.

---

# 8. RETURN INSIDE

Jake was immediately brought indoors.

His temperature was assessed.

He was significantly colder than his current baseline.

His clothing was replaced with dry clothing.

He was wrapped in blankets and transferred to medical observation.

Jake remained unusually quiet.

When asked whether he had been outside for a long time, he nodded.

When asked whether he had tried to come inside, he nodded again.

When asked whether he had called for help:

Jake shook his head.

The question was unnecessary.

Everyone already knew why.

He cannot speak.

---

# 9. FEVER

Within the following period, Jake developed a fever.

The fever is believed to be associated with the prolonged exposure and his already compromised physical condition.

This is particularly concerning because Jake entered the incident in a state of severe exhaustion.

His body had already been struggling to recover from:

- five weeks of extreme deprivation;
    
- prolonged cold exposure;
    
- severe nutritional deficiency;
    
- Nullwalker transformation;
    
- and subsequent physiological instability.
    

Three additional hours of exposure were therefore not medically trivial.

---

# 10. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri eventually realized Jake was missing.

She initially assumed he had gone somewhere else inside the SAFEHOUSE.

Then she remembered.

She stopped.

She immediately went toward the entrance.

Jake was no longer outside.

He was already under medical care.

Neri stood in the doorway for several seconds.

She looked down.

She then approached the medical area.

Jake was lying beneath several blankets.

His eyes were half-open.

Neri sat beside him.

She did not immediately speak.

After several seconds:

> **NERI:**  
> “I forgot.”

Jake looked at her.

Neri's voice became quieter.

> **NERI:**  
> “I'm sorry.”

Jake did not respond.

He simply looked at her.

Neri remained beside him.

---

# 11. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake eventually moved one hand.

Neri noticed.

He reached toward his teddy bear.

She placed it closer.

Jake held it.

Then closed his eyes.

No forgiveness was verbally requested.

No forgiveness was verbally given.

There was simply silence.

---

Jake remains febrile.

He is:

- extremely tired;
    
- sluggish;
    
- physically weak;
    
- unusually pale;
    
- minimally responsive;
    
- unable to speak;

His stellar core remains active.

Medical monitoring is ongoing.

# 13. PROTOCOL REVISION

The existing Jake welfare protocol is being immediately amended.

Effective immediately:

> **JAKE IS NOT TO BE ALLOWED OUTSIDE WITHOUT CONFIRMED RETURN ACCESS.**

If Jake leaves the SAFEHOUSE with another resident:

### That resident is responsible for confirming:

1. Jake remains with them;
    
2. Jake possesses authorized access;
    
3. Jake returns inside with them;
    
4. Jake is physically accounted for after re-entry.
    

A resident leaving Jake outside unintentionally remains an incident.

Intent is not the determining factor.

---

# 14. KEYCARD ISSUE

Jake's lack of a keycard has been identified as a direct contributing factor.

A permanent solution is being evaluated.

Until then, personnel must assume:

> **Jake cannot independently re-enter the SAFEHOUSE once outside.**

No resident should take him outside without accounting for this limitation.

No resident should assume:

> “He can just come back inside.”

He cannot.

---

# 15. PASSIVE MONITORING ADDITION

The previous protocol emphasized observing Jake when he was nearby.

That is no longer sufficient.

Personnel must now also maintain awareness of:

> **Where Jake is supposed to be.**

If a resident leaves an area with Jake and returns without him:

**Confirm his location.**

If Jake is expected to be outside:

**Confirm he has returned.**

If Jake's location is unknown:

**Locate him immediately.**

Do not assume another resident has him.

Do not assume he returned to his room.

Do not assume he is resting somewhere.

Confirm.

---

# 16. WHY THIS MATTERS

Jake has an unusually high tolerance for simply remaining wherever he happens to be.

He will not necessarily:

- seek shelter;
    
- call for assistance;
    
- complain;
    
- attempt to attract attention;
    
- or tell someone that he is becoming ill.
    

His current condition makes this behavior especially dangerous.

Three hours outside was enough to produce a fever.

A longer period could have resulted in substantially more severe consequences.

---

# 17. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

This incident was accidental.

Neri did not intentionally leave Jake outside.

However, the outcome remains serious.

Jake had only recently survived a much longer period of isolation and environmental exposure.

The fact that he was subsequently left outside again, even unintentionally, demonstrates a significant weakness in the current welfare system.

The solution cannot be:

> **“Everyone should remember better.”**

The solution must be structural.

Jake should not have to depend entirely on another person remembering that he is there.

---

# 18. IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

Effective immediately:

- Jake remains indoors during recovery.
    
- Outdoor activity requires a confirmed responsible companion.
    
- Jake's re-entry access is being reviewed.
    
- Exterior personnel will be informed of Jake's current appearance.
    
- Missing-resident checks will include Jake.
    
- Personnel must verify Jake's return after outdoor activities.
    
- Medical monitoring continues until the fever resolves.
    
- The existing passive welfare protocol remains active.
    
- No one is to assume Jake will call for help.
