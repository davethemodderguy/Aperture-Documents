## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora has been found.

He was located beneath a tree, several kilometers away from the Blacksite.

He was no longer running.

He was no longer walking.

He had lost the strength to move.

His white clothing was extensively stained red. Blood continued to drip from his injuries, falling onto the snow beneath him. The surrounding area showed evidence of prolonged bleeding.

The estimated cumulative blood loss was described by the initial recovery team as equivalent to the entire blood volume of the population of Texas.

This figure is an extraordinary anomalous measurement, not a conventional clinical estimate. Nevertheless, the medical significance is unmistakable: Sora has lost an incomprehensible quantity of blood, and his condition is critical.

His anomalous immortality has kept him alive.

It has not restored what he has lost.

It has not stopped the bleeding.

It has not relieved his pain.

**Sora was found alive, but survival must not be mistaken for stability.**

## 3. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT — THE LIMITS OF IMMORTALITY

The scale of Sora's blood loss exceeds anything previously documented in his medical history.

The comparison to the total blood volume of Texas's population is not intended as a literal conventional measurement. It conveys the extraordinary magnitude of the anomalous phenomenon observed.

Regardless of the precise quantity, the central medical finding remains unchanged:

**Sora has suffered catastrophic blood loss and continues to bleed.**

His condition is complicated by several established characteristics:

- He cannot die through conventional means.
    
- He can still experience severe physical suffering.
    
- His body does not naturally regenerate.
    
- He does not automatically replace lost blood.
    
- His injuries do not resolve merely because he remains alive.
    
- He has severe hemophobia.
    

His immortality has provided no meaningful protection against the consequences of this incident.

Medical personnel must not assume that his body can tolerate additional blood loss simply because it has survived the previous amount.

### Immediate Medical Directives

1. Stabilization and hemorrhage control take precedence over all nonessential procedures.
    
2. All existing injuries must be assessed and treated.
    
3. Blood loss must be documented using the best available anomalous medical measurements.
    
4. Medical staff must account for his inability to naturally recover lost blood.
    
5. Exposure to the snow and cold must be addressed.
    
6. His psychological condition must be considered throughout treatment.
    

The medical team is explicitly prohibited from describing his condition as safe, stable, or recovered without supporting clinical evidence.

## 4. PSYCHOLOGICAL CONTEXT — WHY HE DID NOT RETURN

The distance between the Blacksite and the recovery site is significant.

Sora did not simply leave the building and hide nearby.

He traveled several kilometers while severely injured and bleeding.

The reason for this departure has not been established through direct testimony. However, the incident occurred immediately after a period of intense self-blame and psychological distress.

His previous statements remain relevant:

> "Just... leave... me... in... the snow.... please."

> "I'm worth nothing."

> "I made Mia mad."

> "How can a big brother do that?"

These statements indicate a deeply distorted perception of his own worth.

Sora appears to believe that making a mistake, upsetting someone, or failing as an older brother makes him undeserving of care.

C.O.R.E. rejects that conclusion entirely.

He is six years old.

He is allowed to make mistakes.

He is allowed to become frightened.

He is allowed to be angry, confused, upset, and imperfect.

He is not required to be a perfect older brother to deserve medical attention.

His injuries are not a punishment he deserves.

His suffering is not an acceptable consequence of an argument.

---

## 5. RECOVERY AND TRANSPORT PROTOCOL

Sora is to be transported directly to the designated medical facility.

The recovery team must minimize unnecessary movement and coordinate all handling with medical personnel.

His condition does not permit a prolonged conversation or questioning session.

Staff are instructed to use short, reassuring statements.

Suggested language:

- "Sora, we've found you."
    
- "You're not in trouble."
    
- "We're taking you somewhere warm."
    
- "You don't have to explain anything."
    
- "You don't need to apologize."
    

Personnel must avoid promising that everything will immediately be fine. False assurances may undermine his trust when he remains in pain.

Likewise, nobody is to tell him that he has frightened everyone, caused trouble, or made the staff worry.

He already carries excessive guilt.

The recovery operation must not add to it.

## 6. FAMILY NOTIFICATION AND WELFARE

Mia is to be informed that Sora has been located and is receiving emergency medical care.

She must not be made responsible for his condition.

The message left by Sora during the previous incident — **"Not your fault."** — remains important. It was a familiar reassurance he used during their childhood, even when Mia was upset with herself.

His recent actions do not erase the affection between them.

However, no immediate reunion is to be demanded.

Both children require support, and Sora's medical condition takes precedence.

Jake is also to be informed in an age-appropriate manner. He must not be expected to act as Sora's rescuer or emotional caretaker.

The responsibility belongs to the adults.

---

## 7. C.O.R.E. INTERNAL REVIEW — A CHILD WHO RAN OUT OF STRENGTH

The recovery team has been asked to submit a full account of the search and transport.

A separate welfare investigation will examine how Sora was able to leave supervision while remaining in such a severe medical condition.

This investigation is not intended to assign blame to individual recovery personnel.

It is intended to identify failures in the support system that allowed a critically injured six-year-old to travel several kilometers without assistance.

There is one conclusion that must remain clear:

Sora should never have had to reach that tree before someone found him.

He should not have had to lose that much blood before his suffering became impossible to overlook.

His inability to die must never become an excuse to underestimate the urgency of his medical needs.
