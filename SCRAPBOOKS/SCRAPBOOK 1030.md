## Executive Summary

Neri exhibits a **high susceptibility to motion sickness**.

The condition is not related to her inability to walk. Neri remains independently mobile when her stamina permits, but movement involving significant acceleration, rotation, sudden direction changes, or repeated vertical motion can cause her to become sick very quickly.

This is particularly relevant when Neri is being transported by another resident.

### Primary restriction

> **DO NOT JUMP OR SPIN WHILE NERI IS ON YOUR BACK.**

This instruction was specifically issued by **Bao** to **Jane Doe** following observations of Neri's motion sensitivity.

---

## Transportation Advisory

When carrying Neri, particularly via piggyback:

- Maintain a steady walking pace.
    
- Avoid sudden acceleration.
    
- Avoid jumping.
    
- Avoid spinning or turning repeatedly.
    
- Avoid unnecessary sharp direction changes.
    
- Do not deliberately bounce her for amusement.
    
- If Neri becomes visibly uncomfortable, stop and allow her to settle.
    
- If she signals that she wants to get down, comply.
    

Neri is capable of tolerating ordinary walking movement. The problem is **excessive or unpredictable motion**, not movement itself.

Her usual piggyback movement is therefore acceptable:

> **Step → small natural movement → step → small natural movement.**

What is _not_ acceptable:

> **Step → jump → spin → jump → “WHEEEE” → medical complaint.**

The latter sequence has been specifically prohibited.
