# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The collaborative canvas project initiated by Clover Luminare has reached its final stage.

The white linen canvas installed in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria is now almost completely covered in artwork contributed by SAFEHOUSE residents.

Clover is the final contributor.

Her remaining task is to complete the composition, conceal the finished work until the appropriate moment, and coordinate the planned surprise.

The original note remains beside the art supplies:

> "Let's make a suprise for Jake, let's see how all of you can make a masterpiece"
> 
> — Clover

The spelling remains unchanged.

The project's true purpose has now been confirmed.

**Clover is organizing Jake's first-ever birthday celebration.**

Following an investigation conducted by Neko, Jake's date of birth has been reliably identified as **1 November, Year 75**.

This will be the first birthday celebration Jake has ever received.

The discovery has prompted a coordinated effort involving Clover, Neko, Bao, Aeterna, and the other SAFEHOUSE residents.

The canvas is intended to serve as the centerpiece of the celebration.

The artwork is not merely a collection of drawings.

It is a record of the people who wanted to celebrate Jake's existence.

And Jake, who has spent the entire project apparently unable to perceive the canvas, remains unaware of the preparations.

---

# 1. THE PERCEPTION MASK

The apparent inability of Jake to see the canvas was initially classified as an unexplained perceptual anomaly.

That classification has now been corrected.

**Clover deliberately masked the canvas and its accompanying note from Jake's point of view.**

The effect does not make the canvas invisible to everyone.

Other residents can see it normally.

They can approach it, draw on it, read the note, and observe the artwork as it develops.

Jake, however, perceives the space as though the canvas and note are not present.

The masking is selective.

It does not obstruct his vision of the cafeteria itself, nor does it interfere with his ability to interact with other residents.

It simply prevents him from noticing the installation.

The exact mechanism remains under review.

Clover has declined to provide a technical explanation.

When Neko asked how she had managed to keep Jake from noticing a large canvas hanging on a cafeteria wall for more than a week, Clover gave the following answer:

**CLOVER:**  
"Luck."

Neko stared at her.

**NEKO:**  
"Your luck does not ordinarily operate by selectively removing objects from someone's perception."

**CLOVER:**  
"Maybe I got creative."

Neko requested a more detailed explanation.

Clover smiled.

She declined to elaborate.

For the purposes of the event, the masking is considered stable enough to maintain the surprise, subject to continued observation.

# 2. THE CANVAS IS COMPLETE

The canvas has been filled.

Not every inch is covered, but the composition has reached the point where additional contributions would risk overcrowding it.

The original blank linen is almost unrecognizable.

Across its surface are dozens of drawings, symbols, and painted scenes.

Some are large enough to occupy entire sections.

Others are small enough to be overlooked at first glance.

There are stars and constellations.

Flowers and clovers.

Snowflakes.

Books.

Cakes.

Animals.

Mechanical designs.

Little portraits.

Small scenes of everyday life.

Several drawings reference Jake's favorite things.

Others reference people who have been important to him.

A few are personal messages that their contributors have chosen not to explain.

The canvas contains work from residents with extensive artistic experience and residents who have no particular artistic ability whatsoever.

The difference is visible.

It is also irrelevant.

Alice's rabbit still has an axe.

Corin's flower still looks suspiciously like a potato.

Neko's mechanical star is precise enough to qualify as a technical illustration.

Aeterna's shooting-star painting is delicate and restrained.

Bao's cake is surrounded by tiny blue stars.

Jane's little rat and seal remain seated together.

Nobody has erased anyone else's contribution.

Nobody has demanded that the artwork meet a uniform standard.

It is a collaborative masterpiece in the only way that matters to this project.

Everyone has left something behind.

# 3. CLOVER'S TURN

Clover has reserved the final section of the canvas for herself.

The space is near the center, surrounded by the contributions of the other residents.

She has not yet painted it.

Several residents have asked what she intends to add.

Clover has provided no direct answer.

She has instead spent the past several days checking the artwork, arranging the surrounding supplies, and making small corrections to the composition.

Her own original four-leaf clover remains in one corner.

The final section is large enough to accommodate a substantial image.

Clover has confirmed that she will finish it before the celebration.

She has also requested that nobody else add anything to the canvas without her permission.

This is the first time she has imposed a firm artistic restriction on the project.

The request has been respected.

When asked whether she was nervous about completing the final section, Clover answered:

**CLOVER:**  
"A little."

**NIKO:**  
"You're the one who organized all of this."

**CLOVER:**  
"I know."

**NIKO:**  
"You already knew you would have to finish it."

**CLOVER:**  
"I know."

**NIKO:**  
"Then why are you nervous?"

Clover looked at the canvas.

**CLOVER:**  
"Because this part matters."

Niko did not press her further.

# 4. THE DATE

The celebration's date was not selected arbitrarily.

Neko has successfully identified Jake's birth date through a combination of temporal records, anomalous chronological analysis, and cross-referencing against the earliest verifiable records of his existence.

The findings were reviewed for consistency.

The result is considered reliable.

```
  <text color="secondary" size="sm">No prior birthday celebration has been documented.</text>
</box>
```

Neko's investigation has established a date.

It has not established that Jake ever knew it.

Jake has no conventional birth certificate, childhood photograph from infancy, or family record documenting a birthday celebration.

For most of his existence, the concept of celebrating his birth was simply absent from his life.

No one had organized such an event for him.

No one had made a cake specifically for his birthday.

No one had gathered his friends to celebrate the day he came into existence.

Until now, nobody had even been able to provide a reliably established date.

That has changed.

---

# 5. THE AGE QUESTION

The date has revealed another unexpected detail.

Jake is considerably older than his human appearance suggests.

He is a literal star manifested in the form of a six-year-old boy.

His apparent age does not correspond to the amount of time that has passed since his birth.

Using the established chronological records, Jake is **almost as old as Aeterna**.

The similarity is striking.

Aeterna is an ancient Invictus whose existence spans an immense period of history.

Jake is similarly ancient by chronology, yet remains a child in his present manifestation.

The two differ enormously in temperament, history, and experience.

Aeterna carries the weight of thousands of years of memory and responsibility.

Jake spends his time reading, drawing, carrying his teddy bear, asking Bao for cake, and occasionally creating administrative problems by naturally shedding several kilograms of glowing blue stardust over the course of a year.

Their chronological proximity does not make them equivalent.

It does, however, make the upcoming celebration rather unusual.

The SAFEHOUSE is preparing to celebrate the birthday of a child who is almost as old as one of its most ancient residents.

C.O.R.E. has determined that this is not a reason to cancel the party.

Neko was asked whether the date had been established with sufficient confidence to proceed.

**NEKO:**  
"It is a birthday party. Not a peer-reviewed publication."

The matter was considered settled.

# 7. THE CELEBRATION PLAN

Clover has coordinated the event with assistance from the residents.

The current plan includes:

- a birthday cake prepared with Bao's assistance;
    
- decorations throughout the cafeteria;
    
- the completed collaborative canvas;
    
- a small gathering of Jake's friends;
    
- food and refreshments;
    
- and a formal reveal of the artwork.
    

The event will be kept deliberately simple.

Jake is not to be overwhelmed with speeches, ceremonial duties, or an extended explanation of his chronological age.

The purpose is to celebrate him, not to turn his birthday into a temporal research symposium.

Bao will oversee the food.

Clover will complete the canvas.

Neko will verify that the perception mask remains in place until the reveal.

Aeterna has agreed to attend.

The other residents have been invited to participate according to their preferences.

Nobody is required to make a speech.

Nobody is required to bring a gift.

Nobody is required to produce another drawing.

The canvas is already enough.

She has not disclosed the final design.

Aeterna has been informed of the confirmed date and the planned celebration.

Her response was quiet.

She examined Neko's findings for several minutes before looking toward the canvas.

**AETERNA:**

No one asked what it represented.

# 10. JAKE REMAINS UNAWARE

Jake continues to recover from his recent illness.

His visits to the cafeteria have gradually returned to normal.

He has not discovered the canvas.

He has not read Clover's note.

He has not asked why so many residents have been spending time with art supplies.

The perception mask remains effective.

On one occasion, Jake entered the cafeteria while Clover was standing beside the canvas.

He approached her.

**JAKE:**  
"Hi."

**CLOVER:**  
"Hi, Jake."

**JAKE:**  
"What are you doing?"

Clover was holding a paintbrush.

She glanced at it.

Then at him.

**CLOVER:**  
"Painting."

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:**  
"Okay."

He went to collect a drink.

He passed the canvas.

He passed the note.

He passed the entire collection of drawings.

He did not react.

Clover remained perfectly still until he left.

Then she leaned against the table and exhaled.

**CLOVER:**  
"How is that even working?"

No one answered.

Neko, who had been observing from the other side of the cafeteria, made a note to investigate the phenomenon after the celebration.

For now, the masking was doing exactly what Clover needed it to do.

# 11. THE LAST SECTION

Clover has finally begun work on the last section of the canvas.

The details are being kept confidential.

She has asked the residents not to photograph the unfinished area or discuss its contents outside the preparation group.

The only confirmed information is that the final image will connect the surrounding contributions into a single composition.

It will incorporate the clover she drew on the first day.

It will also include the date:

**1 NOVEMBER — YEAR 75.**

The inscription is small.

It does not dominate the artwork.

It simply identifies the day the residents have chosen to celebrate.

Clover has spent considerable time making sure the date is written correctly.

When asked why, she explained:

**CLOVER:**  
"If we're doing this, I want to get it right."

She returned to painting.

No further comment was provided.

# 12. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

The cafeteria canvas project has evolved from a spontaneous collaborative art activity into the centerpiece of a planned birthday celebration.

Neko's investigation has reliably identified Jake's birth date as 1 November, Year 75.

The finding has established a basis for celebrating an occasion Jake has never previously celebrated.

The fact that Jake is chronologically almost as old as Aeterna does not diminish the significance of the event.

His present form, preferences, and emotional needs remain those of a child.

The celebration should therefore be approached as a birthday for Jake as he is, rather than a ceremony for an ancient celestial entity.

Clover's perception mask has preserved the surprise.

The canvas is complete apart from her final contribution.

The event remains on schedule.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING OBSERVATION

For more than a week, the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria has quietly accumulated pieces of a gift.

A clover.

A cake.

A rabbit.

A constellation.

A snowflake.

A mechanical star.

A teddy bear.

A rat sitting beside a seal.

Every resident who wanted to participate left something behind.

Some drew beautifully.

Some drew terribly.

Some wrote messages.

Some said nothing at all.

The canvas has become a record of a community expressing something it rarely needs to formalize.

They want Jake to know he matters.

Not because he is a literal star.

Not because he possesses extraordinary abilities.

Not because he can produce anomalous material.

Not because he has survived things that no child should have to experience.

They want to celebrate him because he is Jake.

And now, for the first time, they have a date on which to do it.

**1 November, Year 75.**

Clover is finishing the final section.

Bao is preparing the cake.

Neko is protecting the secret.

Aeterna is waiting.

And Jake is somewhere in his room, probably reading, completely unaware that the cafeteria has been transformed into a gift made by the people around him.

He has spent an immense amount of time existing.

He has never had a birthday party.

That is about to change.
