# SAFEHOUSE MEDICAL DIRECTIVE

## TEMPORARY ANEMIA RESPONSE SYSTEM — NERI

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE Medical / Emergency Use  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Prepared By:** Bao  
**Status:** Temporary Emergency Measure  
**Age:** 10  
**Compatible Donor:** Bao  
**Device:** Administrative Emergency Blood-Response Pen  
**Quantity:** Limited

---

# 1. Situation

Neri's anemic episodes have become increasingly frequent.

The change has occurred alongside another behavioral pattern:

**Neri has been spending more time alone.**

This is not because she has suddenly stopped caring about the other SAFEHOUSE residents.

Rather, circumstances have increasingly separated her from them.

Jane Doe has been exceptionally busy with her own responsibilities.

Alice has periodically been unavailable due to illness or other circumstances.

Neko's chemistry work continues.

Star continues her own activities.

Bao herself has responsibilities that cannot simply be abandoned whenever Neri is nearby.

Guiding Light, Frost, Corin, Orphie, and the other residents likewise have their own duties, projects, or personal activities.

Nobody intentionally abandoned Neri.

But from Neri's perspective, the distinction may not matter.

She sees people being busy.

She sees herself being alone.

And she increasingly concludes:

> **“I shouldn't bother them.”**

This has become medically dangerous.

---

# 2. Increasing Frequency

Neri's anemia is no longer an unusual event that can be treated as an isolated emergency.

Episodes can occur:

- while walking,
    
- while reading,
    
- while eating,
    
- while sitting,
    
- during ordinary conversation,
    
- or while doing absolutely nothing strenuous.
    

This makes relying on physical exertion as a warning sign ineffective.

More concerningly, Neri has demonstrated that she can remain outwardly composed while her cardiovascular system deteriorates.

She may continue sitting normally.

She may continue holding a book.

She may keep her eyes open.

She may not call anyone.

In previous incidents, this has resulted in Neri remaining unconscious for extended periods before discovery.

---

# 3. Bao's Temporary Solution

Because Bao's blood remains the only consistently compatible emergency source identified for Neri, Bao developed a temporary emergency-response device.

The devices are informally referred to as:

> **“Pens.”**

They resemble oversized medical auto-injectors.

Each device contains approximately **250 mL of Bao's compatible blood**.

Their purpose is not to cure Neri's underlying condition.

They are intended solely to provide an emergency intervention when Neri's blood volume/function has reached a dangerous threshold and immediate access to Bao is unavailable.

Bao has emphasized repeatedly:

> **“This is a bridge. It is not a cure.”**

---

# 4. Emergency Procedure

Each pen is individually sealed and intended for emergency use.

When Neri is experiencing a confirmed or strongly suspected severe anemic episode and Bao cannot immediately reach her, an authorized SAFEHOUSE responder may administer the emergency device according to Bao's medical instructions.

The device is designed for administration through the thigh.

**The pen is activated against Neri's thigh.**

The device handles the delivery automatically.

No attempt should be made to manually manipulate or improvise the device.

The objective is simple:

**Stabilize Neri long enough for proper medical intervention and/or Bao's arrival.**

---

# 5. Critical Limitation

The emergency pen does **not** mean Neri is safe to leave alone.

It does not mean:

> “She has a pen, so she'll be fine.”

It does not replace medical personnel.

It does not permanently replenish her blood.

It does not prevent another episode.

It does not correct the underlying abnormal degradation of her blood.

It is an emergency measure intended to prevent an immediately dangerous episode from becoming fatal before help arrives.

---

# 6. Distribution

A limited number of pens have been distributed to locations where Neri is known to spend time.

Personnel have been instructed to keep them accessible rather than locked away in distant medical storage.

However, Bao has explicitly stated that the devices are **not toys, not snacks, and not something Neri should carry around casually.**

Neri has already demonstrated enough medical self-neglect that simply handing her an emergency device and saying _“use this if you're sick”_ is considered insufficient.

She may decide that using it would bother somebody.

That possibility is considered unacceptable.

---

# 7. Personnel Concern

The medical team has identified a secondary problem.

The frequency of Neri's episodes is increasing at approximately the same time that her opportunities for casual social contact are decreasing.

This has created a dangerous feedback loop:

**Neri is alone → Neri becomes unwell → Neri doesn't want to bother anyone → Neri remains alone → nobody notices → episode becomes severe.**

The cafeteria incident demonstrated the extreme version of this problem.

Neri does not need to be surrounded by people every second.

But she does need people who will notice when she has quietly disappeared from normal life.

---

# 8. Jane Doe

**Age:** 28  
**Relationship to Neri:** Close friend / Protective adult figure

Jane's workload has become a significant obstacle.

She is not deliberately neglecting Neri.

In fact, Jane remains one of the people most concerned about Neri's condition.

The problem is that concern does not automatically translate into availability.

Jane can be working.

Neri sees Jane working.

Neri thinks:

> _Jane is busy._

Then:

> _I shouldn't interrupt her._

Then:

> _I'll wait._

And Neri's definition of “wait” has repeatedly proven medically unsafe.

Jane is therefore considered particularly vulnerable to missing an episode despite being one of Neri's closest friends.

---

# 9. SAFEHOUSE Behavioral Assessment

A concerning change has been observed among the wider SAFEHOUSE population.

Residents remain fond of Neri.

However, fewer people are actively checking on her.

Not because they stopped caring.

Because they became busy.

This distinction is obvious to an adult.

It is considerably less obvious to Neri.

To Neri, the practical result is simply:

> **Nobody came.**

That can reinforce her existing belief that she is not particularly important to anyone.

This is considered a greater long-term concern than the emergency pens themselves.

---

# 10. Bao's Statement

When asked why she had created the pens despite already being capable of personally providing compatible blood, Bao reportedly answered:

> “Because I can't be everywhere.”

After a pause, she added:

> “And neither can Jane.”

She then reportedly looked at the completed devices and said:

> “But Neri shouldn't have to die just because we're busy.”

---

# 11. Jane's Reaction

Jane reportedly became quiet when she learned about the pens.

She understood immediately why Bao had created them.

She also understood something more uncomfortable:

She had been telling Neri for weeks that she could interrupt her.

Neri apparently still didn't believe it.

Jane's private assessment was concise:

> **“I keep telling her she can bother me.”**

> **“She keeps deciding I'm too busy.”**

> **“And apparently, she can be unconscious for two hours without making enough noise for anyone to notice.”**

Jane then requested that the emergency pens remain available throughout the SAFEHOUSE.

She did not request that Neri be placed under constant surveillance.

Instead, she asked for something considerably simpler:

> **“Check on her.”**

---

# 12. Final Medical Position

The emergency pens are a temporary solution to an increasingly serious problem.

They address the question:

> **“What happens if Neri crashes and Bao isn't there?”**

They do not address the more important question:

> **“Why does Neri keep ending up alone long enough for nobody to notice?”**

That remains a SAFEHOUSE responsibility.

Neri is ten years old.

She should not have to calculate whether everyone else is too busy to deserve an interruption.

She should not have to decide whether her own heartbeat is important enough to report.

And she should never have to conclude that dying quietly is preferable to bothering someone.

**The pens are emergency equipment.**

**They are not permission for the SAFEHOUSE to become less attentive.**

**END DIRECTIVE**
