# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – ANOMALOUS SPECIES ADDENDUM II**

### **DESIGNATION: M.A.R.T. BLOBS – LIFE CYCLE & SOCIAL BEHAVIOR**

**Classification:** LEVEL 2 – Friendly Anomalous Organism

---

# **LIFE CYCLE**

Further long-term observation has established that individual Marts have remarkably short lifespans.

Average lifespan:

> **Approximately five weeks.**

Despite this, the overall population remains stable due to their regular asexual reproduction every three weeks.

Each Mart will generally reproduce once before the end of its life, ensuring the continuation of the colony.

---

# **END OF LIFE**

As a Mart approaches the end of its lifespan, subtle behavioral changes become apparent.

They become quieter.

More affectionate.

And eventually begin searching for the nearest source of running water.

Most commonly this is:

- A sink.
    
- A wash basin.
    
- A shallow stream.
    
- A fountain.
    

Once found, the Mart simply smiles, tips its tiny top hat, and quietly melts into ordinary water.

No distress has ever been observed.

No pain has been recorded.

The water rapidly loses all Ether activity within minutes.

---

# **ATTITUDE TOWARD DEATH**

Perhaps the most unusual aspect of the species is its complete lack of fear regarding death.

When researchers attempted to explain mortality to a group of Marts, the response was unanimous.

> "We'll be back!"

Another Mart added:

> "See you soon!"

To the Marts, death is not viewed as an ending.

It is simply part of an ongoing cycle shared by the colony.

This perspective appears to be reinforced by their collective consciousness.

---

# **COLLECTIVE HIVEMIND**

All known Marts participate in a distributed Ether-linked hivemind.

The network allows them to:

- Share memories.
    
- Coordinate tasks.
    
- Exchange observations.
    
- Instantly communicate over long distances.
    

Despite this shared consciousness, each Mart retains a playful individual personality.

Researchers often describe them as:

> "One mind having hundreds of tiny conversations with itself."

---

# **BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATIONS**

Marts display a strong instinct to seek out individuals experiencing emotional distress.

Documented behaviors include:

- Quietly sitting beside someone.
    
- Offering tiny hats as "gifts."
    
- Bringing flowers.
    
- Stacking themselves into amusing shapes.
    
- Performing synchronized bouncing.
    
- Playing harmless games.
    
- Curling up nearby during naps.
    

No formal training has been observed.

The behavior appears entirely instinctive.

---

# **RELATIONSHIP WITH MART**

The Marts have developed a particular fondness for **Mart**, Astral's Owl Thiren son.

Whenever he enters a room, nearby Marts eagerly gather around him.

Some perch on his shoulders.

Others ride on his head.

Several simply bounce alongside him wherever he goes.

Mart eventually stopped questioning it.

> "...Guess I've got fans."

The Marts unanimously replied:

> "Mart!"

No further clarification was provided.

---

# **RELATIONSHIP WITH LUMI**

Although Lumi originally viewed their creation as an accident, the Marts reached a different conclusion.

Without exception, every Mart refers to Lumi as:

> **"Mother."**

The first time Lumi heard it, she froze.

She quietly asked:

> "...Me?"

The nearest Mart enthusiastically nodded.

> "Mother!"

Within seconds, dozens of others repeated it.

> "Mother!"

> "Mother!"

> "Mother!"

Witnesses report Lumi smiling through tears.

She has never corrected them.

---

> "...I spent years believing I was the last remnant of my people."

She looked down at the tiny blue blob.

> "...I never imagined I'd become the beginning of someone else's."

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

The Marts continue to thrive throughout Aperture Industries, filling halls, offices, workshops, gardens, and SAFEHOUSE with quiet acts of kindness.

Though each individual lives for only five weeks, none seem to mourn the cycle.

To them, every ending is simply another beginning shared by the colony.

Lumi, once convinced she was the last bearer of a dying legacy, now finds herself surrounded by dozens—sometimes hundreds—of cheerful blue blobs calling her "Mother."

It remains one of the happiest accidents ever recorded within Aperture Industries.
