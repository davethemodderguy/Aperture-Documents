# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A large white linen canvas has been installed in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria.

Beside it are a collection of art supplies and a handwritten note from Clover Luminare.

The note reads:

> **"Let's make a suprise for Jake, let's see how all of you can make a masterpiece"**
> 
> — Clover

The spelling has been preserved exactly as written.

Clover has declined to explain the project further.

The stated objective is to create a collaborative artwork as a surprise for Jake.

At the time of installation, Jake was sick and had temporarily stopped visiting the cafeteria. His absence provided an opportunity to begin the project without immediately attracting his attention.

However, the circumstances surrounding the canvas have raised an unusual question.

**Jake does not appear to be able to see the canvas or its accompanying note.**

He can see the cafeteria.

He can see the tables, chairs, food, residents, and other ordinary objects in the room.

He can even see the wall behind the canvas.

But when he looks toward the installation itself, his attention appears to pass over it as though nothing unusual is present.

No resident has deliberately explained the project to him.

The artwork is still in progress.

And, for reasons C.O.R.E. cannot yet explain, the surprise appears to be working rather better than anyone anticipated.

---

# 1. INSTALLATION

The canvas was installed during a period when Jake was recovering in his private residence.

Clover arranged the following materials:

- one large white linen canvas;
    
- pencils and erasers;
    
- graphite and charcoal;
    
- colored pencils;
    
- watercolor paints;
    
- acrylic paints;
    
- brushes of various sizes;
    
- markers;
    
- a collection of drawing tools;
    
- and several containers for shared supplies.
    

The materials were placed on a table beside the canvas.

A small sign invited residents to contribute.

There were no assigned sections.

No mandatory subject.

No requirement to demonstrate artistic ability.

No restrictions on style.

Clover's only instruction was that the finished work should be a surprise for Jake.

The initial plan was simple.

Residents would add something whenever they visited the cafeteria.

Over several days, the empty canvas would gradually become a collaborative piece of art made by the people who cared about him.

No one would be required to participate.

No one would be told what to draw.

And Jake would not see the result until the appropriate moment.

The plan was approved.

The canvas was hung.

The first contribution appeared before lunchtime.

---

# 2. THE FIRST DAY

The first person to approach the canvas was Clover herself.

She stood before the blank linen for several minutes.

Then she picked up a pencil and drew a small four-leaf clover in one corner.

Beneath it, she wrote her name.

She put the pencil down and stepped back.

The drawing was small.

The lines were simple.

The clover was unmistakably hers.

Clover then returned to her usual activities as though nothing had happened.

Later that morning, Bao entered the cafeteria.

She noticed the canvas.

She read the note.

She smiled faintly.

After a brief moment of consideration, she added a small drawing of a cake.

It was decorated with blue icing and several tiny stars.

She did not write anything beside it.

At lunchtime, Frost added a snowflake.

Alice drew a rabbit.

The rabbit had very large ears and a disproportionately large axe.

Clover looked at the drawing.

Then at Alice.

**CLOVER:**  
"Alice."

**ALICE:**  
"What?"

**CLOVER:**  
"Why does the rabbit have an axe?"

**ALICE:**  
"Because rabbit."

Clover considered arguing.

She decided the drawing was probably fine.

By the end of the first day, the canvas was no longer empty.

It contained a clover, a cake, a snowflake, and an armed rabbit.

The masterpiece had begun.

---

# 3. THE SECOND DAY

The project spread through the SAFEHOUSE without any formal announcement.

Residents began making contributions whenever they passed through the cafeteria.

Some stayed for several minutes.

Others added a single line before returning to work.

Neko drew a small mechanical star surrounded by precise geometric markings.

Miss Neko added a series of tiny flowers.

Niko contributed an elaborate constellation.

Astral painted a crescent moon.

Star added a bright point of light with several delicate trails extending behind it.

Nightshade drew a night sky.

Mart added an owl.

Taiga drew a snow leopard curled into a circle.

Belle contributed a small rabbit.

Endmin added a set of delicate lines that initially appeared abstract until the surrounding drawings gave them context.

No one attempted to organize the contributions into a single style.

The canvas became a collection of individual expressions.

Some drawings were carefully planned.

Some were spontaneous.

Some were technically impressive.

Others were charmingly terrible.

All were allowed to remain.

At one point, Corin spent nearly twenty minutes attempting to draw a flower.

She eventually produced something resembling a potato with petals.

She stared at it.

Then wrote beneath it:

**FLOWER.**

No one corrected her.

The potato-flower remains on the canvas.

---

# 4. THE UNUSUAL PART

The first indication that something unusual was happening came when Jake briefly visited the cafeteria to collect a drink.

He had been recovering in his room and had not participated in his usual daily routine.

Bao accompanied him.

The canvas was already partially completed.

Clover was standing near the art supplies.

Several residents were eating lunch.

Jake entered.

He looked toward the tables.

Then toward the food counter.

Then toward Bao.

He did not look at the canvas.

Not once.

Clover watched him carefully.

Jake collected his drink.

He walked past the canvas.

He looked directly at the wall beside it.

Then he continued toward the exit.

He did not pause.

He did not ask why there were art supplies on the table.

He did not notice the note.

He did not notice the drawings.

The canvas was less than two meters away.

Jake left the cafeteria.

Clover remained still for several seconds.

**CLOVER:**  
"...Okay."

Bao looked at her.

**BAO:**  
"What?"

Clover pointed toward the canvas.

**CLOVER:**  
"He didn't see it."

Bao glanced at the canvas.

Then toward the door.

**BAO:**  
"Maybe he was tired."

Clover considered this explanation.

It was plausible.

At least, it was plausible until the following day.

---

# 5. THE SECOND OBSERVATION

On the next day, Jake returned to the cafeteria.

He was feeling somewhat better.

He walked beside Bao, holding his teddy bear.

The canvas had changed considerably overnight.

There were now dozens of drawings.

The empty spaces between them were gradually disappearing.

Someone had added a small blue star.

Someone else had drawn a book.

A tiny plate of cake had appeared near the bottom.

Jane Doe had drawn a rat sitting beside a seal.

The rat was holding a little sign.

The sign read:

**STAY HERE.**

The seal had no sign.

It simply sat beside the rat.

Jake walked past the canvas.

He stopped near the cafeteria entrance.

He turned toward the wall.

For a moment, Clover thought he had finally noticed the artwork.

Instead, he examined a small mark on the wall several centimeters away from the canvas.

He brushed at it with his sleeve.

Then he continued walking.

He passed within arm's reach of the linen.

His eyes never settled on it.

Clover watched him leave.

Jane approached her.

**JANE:**  
"That was weird."

**CLOVER:**  
"Very."

**JANE:**  
"Does he know?"

**CLOVER:**  
"I don't think he can see it."

Jane looked at the canvas.

Then toward the door.

**JANE:**  
"...Should we be worried?"

Clover thought about it.

**CLOVER:**  
"Not yet."

She picked up a green pencil and added another leaf to her clover.

The project continued.

---

# 6. THE FOLLOWING DAYS

The canvas gradually became part of the cafeteria's ordinary environment.

Residents entered.

They ate.

They talked.

They worked.

They passed the canvas and added something whenever they felt like it.

Then they returned to their normal activities.

No formal schedule was established.

No one was required to contribute.

The project was not treated as an official assignment.

It became something people simply did.

### Bao

Bao added more food.

Cake.

Cookies.

A bowl of soup.

A little steaming mug.

She drew a small whale beside them.

The whale was wearing an apron.

### Frost

Frost added several snowflakes and a tiny figure standing beneath them.

The figure was holding a cup of coffee.

### Alice

Alice drew another rabbit.

This one had a scooter.

It was unclear whether the rabbit was driving the scooter or threatening it.

### Jane Doe

Jane added several small drawings around the rat and seal.

A hand holding another hand.

Two cups of coffee.

A closed door with a small light beneath it.

She did not explain these additions.

No one asked.

### Neko

Neko returned to the canvas several times to refine the mechanical star she had drawn.

She added delicate lines, small symbols, and a few carefully placed blue highlights.

She eventually began sketching a little teddy bear beneath it.

The teddy bear was surprisingly accurate.

### Miss Neko

Miss Neko drew flowers around the edges of the canvas.

They gradually formed a loose border.

### Niko

Niko added more stars.

Some were large.

Some were small.

Some were connected by thin lines.

Together, they formed a constellation that did not correspond to any recognized astronomical chart.

Niko declined to explain whether this was intentional.

### Aeterna

Aeterna stood before the canvas for a long time.

She examined the contributions.

She looked at the empty space near the center.

Then she painted a small blue star surrounded by shooting-star trails.

The lines were delicate.

The glow was suggested through layers of blue and white rather than actual anomalous illumination.

She placed no name beside it.

Everyone knew who had drawn it.

---

# 7. CLOVER'S OBSERVATION LOG

Clover began keeping a private record of the project's progress.

Not because she wanted to control the artwork.

She simply wanted to remember who had contributed what.

The notes became increasingly unusual.

**DAY 1:** Canvas installed. Jake absent due to illness. First drawings completed.

**DAY 2:** Jake entered cafeteria. Did not appear to perceive canvas.

**DAY 3:** Canvas expanded. Jake walked past it without reacting.

**DAY 4:** Jake looked directly at the wall beside the canvas. No apparent recognition.

**DAY 5:** Jake asked why Alice had drawn a rabbit on a piece of scrap paper. He did not ask about the rabbit on the canvas.

**DAY 6:** Jake entered while Jane was drawing. He looked toward Jane, spoke to her, collected a drink, and left. No apparent awareness of canvas.

Clover underlined the final entry.

Then added:

> **Either the surprise is working, or something is very strange.**

She did not yet know which explanation she preferred.

---

# 8. THE CANVAS ITSELF

C.O.R.E. was eventually informed of the apparent perceptual anomaly.

A limited review was conducted.

The canvas was examined for unusual energy signatures, visual distortions, or properties that might interfere with Jake's perception.

No definitive cause was identified.

The linen appeared physically ordinary.

The paints and pencils were ordinary.

The note was ordinary paper.

Clover had not requested any anomalous assistance.

No one had deliberately concealed the canvas through a known anomalous mechanism.

The installation was plainly visible to everyone else.

Personnel could describe individual drawings, identify the contributors, and observe the artwork from different positions.

Jake's apparent inability to perceive the installation remained unexplained.

Importantly, no evidence suggested that the canvas was actively harming him.

The project was therefore permitted to continue, with one restriction:

**No one was to deliberately test Jake's perception by forcing him to look at the canvas.**

The surprise was supposed to be a gift.

It was not an experiment.

---

# 9. A SMALL INCIDENT

On the seventh day, Jake entered the cafeteria while Alice was drawing.

Alice was adding a little blue ribbon to her rabbit.

Jake approached her.

**JAKE:**  
"What are you doing?"

Alice immediately placed her hand over the section she was drawing.

**ALICE:**  
"Nothing."

Jake looked at her.

**JAKE:**  
"You're holding a pencil."

**ALICE:**  
"Yes."

**JAKE:**  
"And paper."

**ALICE:**  
"Correct."

Jake waited.

Alice waited.

**JAKE:**  
"...Okay."

He accepted this explanation with the solemnity of a child who had decided that the matter was not worth investigating.

He went to collect his drink.

Alice watched him leave.

Then she slowly removed her hand from the canvas.

The rabbit's ribbon was crooked.

She corrected it.

From the other side of the cafeteria, Clover covered her mouth to suppress a laugh.

---

# 10. WHY EVERYONE IS KEEPING THE SECRET

The residents have been asked to avoid discussing the project in Jake's presence.

Not because Jake cannot be trusted.

Not because the canvas is dangerous.

And not because the residents believe he should be excluded from their activities.

The secrecy serves one simple purpose.

**They want him to experience the finished artwork without knowing how much effort went into making it.**

The surprise is not merely the canvas itself.

It is the discovery that so many people have chosen to contribute something for him.

Some residents are drawing things they are good at.

Others are drawing things they are decidedly not good at.

Some have written messages.

Others have left only a small symbol.

There is no single artistic standard.

There is no single correct way to participate.

The only shared intention is that, when Jake finally sees the finished work, he will be able to recognize a little piece of each person who helped make it.

And perhaps understand that the canvas exists because the residents wanted to give him something.

Not because he had earned it.

Not because he had been useful.

Simply because they wanted to.

# 11. CURRENT STATUS

As of the latest observation:

- The canvas remains installed in the cafeteria.
    
- Residents continue to contribute.
    
- The note remains beside the art supplies.
    
- Jake has not acknowledged the canvas or note.
    
- No one has deliberately forced his attention toward the installation.
    
- The reason for his apparent inability to perceive it remains unknown.
    
- The surprise has not yet been revealed.
    

Clover has declined to announce a completion date.

Her stated reasoning is that the canvas will be finished when the residents feel it is finished.

This has caused some minor disagreement.

Neko believes the composition would benefit from more deliberate use of negative space.

Alice believes it needs another rabbit.

Niko has suggested adding more stars.

Bao has suggested that everyone stop arguing and eat lunch.

Bao's suggestion was accepted.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING OBSERVATION

The cafeteria continues to function normally.

Residents come and go.

Food is served.

Conversations take place.

People sit together, work, eat, and return to their rooms.

And, beside one wall, a white linen canvas slowly fills with drawings.

A clover.

A cake.

A whale.

A snowflake.

A rabbit with an axe.

A constellation.

A teddy bear.

A little rat sitting beside a seal.

The drawings do not share a single style.

They do not need to.

Every day, someone adds another small piece.

Every day, someone else pauses to look at what has changed.

And every day, Jake walks through the cafeteria without seeing any of it.

Perhaps the most unusual part is that no one has had to coordinate the residents very much.

They simply keep coming back.

They draw something.

They put the pencils away.

They return to their lives.

The canvas grows.

The secret holds.

And Clover, who started the entire thing with a handwritten note and a collection of art supplies, watches the empty spaces slowly disappear.

She has not told Jake.

She has not told him what the residents are making.

She has not told him why everyone seems to have suddenly developed an interest in drawing.

For now, she is content to let the surprise take shape.

Because this is not a research project.

It is not a mandatory SAFEHOUSE activity.

It is not an official C.O.R.E. initiative.

It is a gift.

Made by people who want Jake to have something beautiful.

And, for reasons nobody understands, the universe itself seems willing to help them keep it secret.

**— C.O.R.E. Resident Welfare Division**

**END OF OBSERVATION**
