## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the stairwell incident documented in CORE-SH-INC-152, further observations have identified two additional concerns regarding Sora's condition.

First, Sora's cervical injury requires him to remain still under medical supervision. He has been explicitly instructed not to move his neck or attempt to sit up or reposition himself independently.

Second, Sora is exhibiting an unwillingness to communicate through replacement writing equipment.

Although a new whiteboard has been provided, he does not appear comfortable using it. He remains largely silent, relying on neither writing nor gestures to initiate communication.

The original whiteboard has not yet been recovered.

An examination of the board's rear side, following its eventual recovery, is expected to be particularly important: Jake Akana's personal signature is present on it.

The board is therefore not merely a communication aid. It is also a personal possession carrying a direct connection to someone Sora trusts.

Additionally, the incident revealed that Sora experiences **extreme hemophobia**, an intense fear of blood.

This fear is now considered a significant psychological and medical concern, particularly given the nature of his recent injury.

---

## 2. MEDICAL CONDITION – CERVICAL TRAUMA

Sora's neck sustained a severe fracture during the fall.

Medical personnel have instructed him to remain completely still within the limits of his prescribed treatment and immobilization.

He must not attempt to:

- Turn his head.
    
- Sit up independently.
    
- Adjust his position without assistance.
    
- Remove or interfere with medical stabilization equipment.
    
- Attempt to leave the medical bed.
    

All necessary repositioning and transfers must be performed by qualified medical personnel.

His anomalous immortality does not alter these requirements.

Sora remains alive, but his injury is genuine and potentially disabling.

His inability to die does not guarantee that his spinal cord, nervous system, or other damaged structures will recover.

C.O.R.E. has prohibited personnel from using his anomalous condition as justification for reduced medical precautions.

**Medical instruction: Sora must remain still.**

---

## 3. THE REPLACEMENT WHITEBOARD

A replacement whiteboard was provided shortly after the incident.

It was placed within Sora's accessible field of view, with a marker available.

The expectation was that he could resume communicating through writing once he was medically stable.

This did not occur.

Sora looked at the replacement board but did not reach for it.

When personnel attempted to make it available, he turned his attention away.

He did not write.

He did not attempt to use it.

He did not appear interested in communicating through the replacement equipment.

This behavior has been observed repeatedly.

### Why the replacement is insufficient

The replacement board performs the same practical function as the original.

However, it is not the same object.

The original was given to Sora by Jake.

Sora carried it everywhere, eventually wearing it like a satchel.

It had become part of his daily routine and a familiar possession.

The replacement may be functionally identical, but it does not carry the same personal history.

C.O.R.E. cautions against interpreting Sora's refusal as stubbornness or deliberate noncooperation.

He has lost something familiar during an already frightening experience.

Providing an identical object does not automatically restore his sense of security.

---

## 4. THE SIGNATURE ON THE REAR

During the search for Sora's original whiteboard, personnel confirmed a detail that had previously received little attention.

The rear of the board bears Jake Akana's signature.

This discovery provides additional context for Sora's attachment to the object.

The whiteboard was not simply something he used to communicate.

It was something Jake had personally given him.

Sora had carried it through his recovery, his disappearance, his reunion with Mia, and his growing life on Floor 7.

The signature made it uniquely identifiable as his.

For a child with severe amnesia, familiar possessions may provide a degree of continuity when memories themselves are unreliable.

The board's loss may therefore have been especially distressing.

It is not established whether Sora consciously recognized the signature as the reason for his attachment.

However, C.O.R.E. considers its presence important.

**The original whiteboard must be treated as a sentimental possession, not disposable equipment.**

If recovered, it must be returned to Sora when medically appropriate.

It must not be replaced, discarded, or permanently stored away merely because a newer board is available.

---

## 5. NEWLY IDENTIFIED CONDITION – EXTREME HEMOPHOBIA

During the medical response, personnel identified an additional psychological concern.

Sora has an extreme fear of blood.

The response to visible blood was substantially more severe than ordinary discomfort or apprehension.

He displayed intense distress.

This reaction is consistent with hemophobia, an intense fear of blood.

The condition is particularly concerning because Sora's recent injury involved significant physical trauma.

Medical treatment may require personnel to examine wounds, manage bleeding, and perform procedures involving visible blood.

These procedures are necessary.

However, the psychological impact must not be ignored.

### Medical considerations

Sora's hemophobia must be taken into account during treatment whenever clinically possible.

Personnel should:

- Minimize unnecessary exposure to visible blood.
    
- Explain procedures through accessible, nonverbal means when possible.
    
- Avoid unexpectedly exposing him to blood-soaked materials.
    
- Keep the treatment environment as calm and predictable as circumstances allow.
    
- Monitor for severe distress during examinations and procedures.
    
- Never delay life-saving treatment solely to avoid triggering his fear.
    

His fear must not be treated as misbehavior.

He is not being difficult.

He is frightened.

C.O.R.E. also notes that Sora's inability to speak makes it particularly difficult for him to warn personnel when he is becoming overwhelmed.

His silence must never be interpreted as consent or comfort.

## 6. OBSERVATION – THE CHILD WHO STOPPED WRITING

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Medical Unit  
**Time:** 19:12

Sora was resting under medical supervision.

His neck remained stabilized.

The replacement whiteboard had been placed nearby.

Jake, Mia, and Bao were permitted to visit under medical guidance.

Jake noticed that Sora had not touched the new board.

**JAKE:** You haven't used it?

Sora looked at him.

He did not answer.

Jake moved a little closer.

**JAKE:** It's okay. You can write if you want.

Sora looked away.

Mia sat beside the bed.

**MIA:** Maybe he doesn't like the new one.

Jake looked at the replacement.

**JAKE:** But it's the same...

He stopped.

He remembered something.

The original board had his signature on the back.

**JAKE:** Oh.

Bao gently addressed him.

**BAO:** What's wrong, sweetheart?

**JAKE:** The old one has my name on it.

Mia looked at him.

**MIA:** You signed it?

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:** I gave it to him.

He looked toward Sora.

**JAKE:** He always carried it.

Sora's eyes moved toward Jake.

Jake lowered his voice.

**JAKE:** I'm sorry, Sora. We'll find it.

Sora remained quiet.

He did not reach for the new board.

He did not gesture.

He simply looked at Jake.

Bao gently placed a hand on Jake's shoulder.

**BAO:** He doesn't have to write right now.

**JAKE:** But how will we know what he wants?

**BAO:** We'll pay attention. We'll help him.

Mia looked at Sora.

**MIA:** You don't have to do anything, okay?

Sora blinked.

Mia stayed beside him.

No further communication was attempted.

### Assessment

The interaction demonstrates that the replacement board has not restored Sora's willingness to communicate.

Personnel should not repeatedly encourage him to use it.

He has been through a serious injury, lost a familiar possession, and is currently required to remain physically still.

His silence should be accommodated rather than challenged.

## 9. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE – IT WASN'T JUST A WHITEBOARD

We originally thought the problem was simple.

Sora lost his whiteboard.

We would find it, provide another one, and make sure he could communicate again.

But the replacement is still untouched.

And now we understand why the original matters so much.

Jake signed it.

A little signature on the back of a writing board.

Something that might seem insignificant to anyone else.

But Sora carried it everywhere.

He wore it like a satchel.

It was familiar. It belonged to him. It came from someone he trusted.

For a boy who sometimes forgets his own name, familiar things matter.

And now he is lying in a medical bed with a broken neck, unable to move freely, unable to speak, and unwilling to use the replacement.

He cannot tell us whether he is frightened.

He cannot write that he wants the old one.

He cannot ask Jake to sign another.

And he is terrified of blood.

We have to remember that Sora is not simply an anomalous child who happens to survive injuries.

He is a six-year-old boy who is frightened of something as ordinary as blood, who misses his sister, who cannot speak, and who has just lost something that made communication feel familiar.

His immortality does not make this less tragic.

His silence does not make his distress less real.

And a replacement is not always the same as getting something back.

Find the original whiteboard.

Keep Sora safe.

Give him time.

**He has already lost his voice. We should not make him feel that he has lost the person who gave him another way to use it.**
