# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Jake's return to the Blacksite, C.O.R.E. has observed an unexpected development.

**Clover and Jake get along.**

More specifically:

**Clover likes Jake's room.**

This was not anticipated.

Jake's residence remains one of the least conventional living spaces in SAFEHOUSE.

It contains:

- grass covering much of the floor;
    
- manifested two-dimensional flowers;
    
- plushies;
    
- books;
    
- drawings;
    
- an old mattress;
    
- assorted objects with no immediately identifiable organizational system;
    
- and very little conventional furniture.
    

There is no proper bed.

There is no wardrobe.

There is no desk.

There are no shelves.

There is, however, grass.

Clover appears to consider this a significant improvement.

---

# 1. FIRST VISIT

Clover entered Jake's room approximately three days after his return.

She stopped at the doorway.

Jake was sitting on the grass with his teddy bear.

He looked up.

Clover looked around.

She blinked.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Oh."

Jake tilted his head.

Clover stepped inside.

She looked at the grass.

Then the flowers.

Then the plushies.

Then the drawings.

Then the mattress.

Then back at Jake.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I like it."

Jake stared at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Really?"

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "It's comfortable."

Jake looked around his room.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It's messy."

Clover shrugged.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "So?"

Jake appeared to consider this.

Then he moved slightly to make room.

Clover sat down.

---

# 2. WHY CLOVER LIKES IT

C.O.R.E. initially assumed Clover's preference was related to her botanical anomaly.

This was incorrect.

Clover's explanation was considerably simpler.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "It doesn't feel like a normal room."

She paused.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I like that."

Jake looked at her.

Clover continued.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Normal rooms feel like you're supposed to do something."

Jake nodded slowly.

Clover gestured toward the grass.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Here you can just sit."

Jake smiled faintly.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

---

# 3. THE FLOWERS

Clover was particularly interested in Jake's manifested flowers.

She crouched beside one.

It was a simple two-dimensional drawing.

Yet physically present.

Clover touched one of the petals.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You made these?"

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I draw them."

Clover examined the flower.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "They're cute."

Jake immediately looked away.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They're flowers."

Clover smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

She did not tease him further.

---

# 4. CLOVER'S CLOVERS

Several days later, Clover asked Jake whether she could grow something in his room.

Jake agreed.

Clover found a small patch of exposed soil.

She placed her hand over it.

A green shoot emerged.

Then a stem.

Then four leaves.

A four-leaf clover.

Jake stared.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You can do that?"

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Only four-leaf ones."

Jake crouched beside it.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It looks like you."

Clover smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Maybe."

She left it growing beside one of Jake's blue flowers.

---

# 5. THE ROOM CHANGES

Jake did not remove it.

Instead, he began drawing small blue flowers around Clover's clover.

Clover noticed.

She added another clover.

Jake added another flower.

Neither discussed what they were doing.

Within a week, a small section of the room had become a mixture of:

**Blue sketched flowers.**

and

**Real four-leaf clovers.**

C.O.R.E. has classified the area as:

> **UNOFFICIAL JOINT GARDEN**

Jake has not objected to the designation.

---

# 6. CLOVER'S CANDLE

Clover eventually began bringing her candle when visiting Jake.

This initially concerned him.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Why do you need that?"

Clover looked at the candle.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I don't like dark rooms."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Okay."

He subsequently began leaving a small clear space beside the mattress where Clover could place the candle.

No one instructed him to do this.

---

# 7. QUIET COMPANY

The majority of Clover and Jake's interactions involve very little conversation.

Clover draws.

Jake reads.

Clover grows clovers.

Jake draws flowers.

Sometimes Clover sits on the grass and watches him draw.

Sometimes Jake lies on the mattress while Clover works on her accounting notes.

Sometimes neither does anything.

This arrangement appears comfortable for both.

Neither requires the other to speak.

---

# 8. CLOVER'S FEAR OF GHOSTS

There is one notable difference from their original encounter.

Clover is still afraid of ghosts.

Jake is still technically a ghost-like manifestation of a celestial entity.

But she no longer associates him with the frightening image that initially caused her panic.

She has since learned something important:

Jake is simply Jake.

A small child.

A teddy bear.

A pile of grass.

Too many plushies.

And an unreasonable number of blue flowers.

The fear has not disappeared entirely.

It simply no longer controls the interaction.

---

# 9. JAKE'S OPINION

Jake was asked whether he liked Clover visiting.

He shrugged.

Then answered:

> **JAKE:**  
> "She doesn't make it loud."

This was considered a positive assessment.

Clover later asked him the same question.

Jake thought for a moment.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You can come here."

Clover smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Thanks."

---

# 10. OBSERVED EXCHANGE

Clover entered Jake's room carrying her notebook.

Jake was lying on the grass.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Hi."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Hi."

Clover sat down.

She opened her notebook.

Jake looked at her drawing.

> **JAKE:**  
> "What's that?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "A clover."

Jake looked at it.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It's missing something."

Clover frowned.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "What?"

Jake pointed at the center.

> **JAKE:**  
> "A face."

Clover stared at him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Clover doesn't have a face."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Neither do my flowers."

Clover smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Fair."

---

# 11. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

The relationship appears healthy.

Clover is not treating Jake as a ghost.

Jake is not treating Clover as someone who needs to apologize.

Neither appears preoccupied with their previous encounter.

The relationship is instead developing around a shared preference:

> **Quiet places.**

Clover's insomnia makes nighttime difficult.

Jake's room is quiet.

Clover's candle provides enough light to make the space comfortable.

Jake does not require conversation.

Clover does not require conversation.

Both can draw.

Both can sit.

Neither has to explain themselves.

---

# CLOSING OBSERVATION

Clover once believed Jake's room was somewhere she should never enter.

Now she keeps asking whether she can visit.

Jake keeps saying yes.

The room itself has changed slightly.

There are now more clovers.

There are more blue flowers.

There is a candle holder beside the mattress.

And, occasionally, two sets of footsteps cross the grass.

One belongs to a little star.

The other belongs to a woman who once thought she had scared him away forever.

Apparently, she didn't.

Jake seems perfectly happy to have her there.

And Clover seems perfectly happy to sit in a room that has no proper furniture, no proper organization, and absolutely no sensible interior design.

Her assessment was recorded as follows:

> **"It's nicer than it looks."**

Jake disagreed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It looks nice."

Clover considered this.

Then nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Yeah."
