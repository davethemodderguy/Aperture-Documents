### **TREEHOUSE / RECOVERED PERSONAL PROPERTY**

# **CORIN'S NOTEBOOK — BLACK-EYE CONTENT ANALYSIS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Item:** Corin's Personal Notebook  
**Status:** **Emotionally Hazardous Material — Restricted Review**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Further review of Corin's notebook has established a strong correlation between the **Black Eye** drawings and pages containing intensely negative writing.

The Black Eyes are rarely drawn alongside ordinary entries.

They appear predominantly beside passages concerning:

- Self-harm.
    
- Self-abandonment.
    
- Helplessness.
    
- Depression.
    
- Worthlessness.
    
- Isolation.
    
- Rejection.
    
- Abandonment.
    
- Negative perceptions of Corin herself.
    

The notebook therefore provides evidence of a severe deterioration in Corin's self-perception during Orphie's absence.

---

# **THE BLACK EYES**

The drawings remain consistent with previously documented examples.

A black, viscous-looking circular mass.

No visible iris.

No recognizable pupil.

No expression.

Yet every one appears to stare directly outward.

They frequently surround passages concerning Corin herself.

The visual effect is especially pronounced when multiple Black Eyes are drawn around a single sentence.

---

# **SELF-DIRECTED WRITING**

Many of the associated passages are not simply sad.

They are actively hostile toward Corin herself.

Examples include:

> **"Maybe I'm the problem."**

> **"Everyone would be better without me."**

> **"I make everything worse."**

> **"Why am I like this?"**

> **"I shouldn't have trusted anyone."**

> **"I shouldn't have gotten attached."**

Several pages contain statements repeatedly crossing out positive descriptions of Corin and replacing them with negative ones.

---

# **SELF-ABANDONMENT**

A recurring theme is the belief that Corin should abandon herself before someone else can do it.

One page reads:

> **"If everyone leaves eventually, I should stop waiting."**

Another:

> **"Maybe I should just disappear first."**

The latter sentence is surrounded by seven Black Eyes.

---

# **HELPLESSNESS**

Several entries describe Corin's inability to change the situation.

> **"I can't find her."**

> **"I can't make her come back."**

> **"I can't fix this."**

> **"I can't even make myself feel better."**

One page contains only:

> **"I can't."**

The sentence is surrounded by Black Eyes.

---

# **SELF-HARM REFERENCES**

Some pages contain references to self-harm.

These passages have been classified as **restricted psychological evidence** and are not being reproduced in full.

The entries demonstrate that Corin was experiencing thoughts of harming herself during the prolonged period of isolation.

C.O.R.E. has found no indication that the notebook was intended as an instruction manual.

The writing instead appears to document distress and intrusive thoughts.

---

# **THE BLACK EYES AND SELF-PERCEPTION**

A recurring visual pattern has been identified.

When Corin writes something positive about herself:

**Few or no Black Eyes appear.**

When she writes something negative about herself:

**The number of Black Eyes increases.**

When she expresses hopelessness:

**The Black Eyes frequently surround the entire page.**

The correlation is too consistent to be dismissed as random decoration.

---

# **PAGE 173**

A particularly disturbing page contains a normally drawn eye.

Beside it are several Black Eyes.

Below them:

> **"Which one sees me correctly?"**

Underneath:

> **"The normal one is lying."**

The Black Eyes have been drawn over the surrounding paper repeatedly.

---

# **PAGE 181**

The page contains no normal eyes.

Only Black Eyes.

Beneath them:

> **"You know what I am."**

> **"Don't pretend I'm worth saving."**

No name is written.

The context makes it clear that Corin is referring to herself.

---

# **PAGE 194**

A single sentence appears in the center of the page:

> **"I don't need someone to abandon me."**

Below it:

> **"I can do it myself."**

The sentence is surrounded by Black Eyes.

---

# **PAGE 201**

The handwriting becomes extremely small.

The page contains:

> **"I wish someone would tell me I'm wrong."**

No Black Eyes appear on this page.

This is one of the few later entries that contains an explicit desire for reassurance.

---

# **ORPHIE'S RESPONSE**

Orphie was given access to the relevant pages.

She became visibly distressed.

After reading the entries, she repeatedly whispered:

> **"I didn't abandon you."**

She eventually placed the notebook down.

Then:

> **"I was trying to come home."**

She remained silent afterward.

---

# **C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT**

The notebook demonstrates that Corin's suffering was not limited to missing Orphie.

The prolonged absence appears to have transformed into an internal narrative in which Corin herself became the perceived cause of the abandonment.

The resulting thought pattern became increasingly circular:

> **Orphie is gone.**

↓

> **Orphie doesn't want me.**

↓

> **There must be something wrong with me.**

↓

> **I deserve to be left.**

↓

> **Nobody will come back.**

↓

> **I should leave first.**

This pattern appears repeatedly throughout the later notebook.

---

# **IMPORTANT DISTINCTION**

C.O.R.E. emphasizes:

**These statements are Corin's perception of herself, not objective descriptions of Corin.**

There is no evidence supporting the claims that she is:

- Unworthy.
    
- A burden.
    
- Unwanted.
    
- Responsible for Orphie's disappearance.
    

Those beliefs appear to have emerged from prolonged uncertainty and isolation.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

The Black Eyes do not appear alongside Corin's happiest memories.

They appear when she turns against herself.

They surround the words she writes when she feels helpless.

They watch over the pages where she calls herself worthless.

They fill the margins when she writes about abandonment.

And they become most numerous when Corin stops asking:

> **"Why did she leave?"**

and starts asking:

> **"What's wrong with me?"**

The notebook does not show a girl who wanted to be hated.

It shows a girl who became convinced she deserved to be.

And every Black Eye appears to stare directly at that belief.
