# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **FIELD OBSERVATION REPORT**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – SNOWFALL PERIOD**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 – Executive & Medical Personnel Only

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Subsequent investigation determined that Corin Lockwood's memory impairment was **temporary**.

Although her recollection had become severely fragmented during her disappearance, the episodes gradually resolved over time.

The emotional impact, however, remained.

Corin increasingly sought solitude during periods of heavy snowfall.

Personnel eventually began referring to these disappearances as **"Snow Periods."**

Whenever the snow fell thick enough to conceal footprints...

Corin became almost impossible to find.

---

# **THE SNOW**

Corin did not choose the snow because she enjoyed the cold.

She chose it because it covered everything.

Footprints disappeared.

Sounds became muffled.

The world became quieter.

The falling snow created a place where she felt unseen.

For Corin...

That felt safer than being recognized.

---

# **RECOVERED NOTE**

A single sheet of paper was recovered from beneath a layer of fresh snow after one such period.

The handwriting matched Corin's.

It read:

> "I didn't deserve a family."

> "I didn't deserve to live."

> "My life is just a play of a tragedy."

> "Nothing else."

Psychological Services classify these statements as expressions of Corin's depressive self-beliefs during a period of profound emotional distress.

They do **not** reflect the views of her family, friends, or Medical Services.

---

# **LUMI**

After her return, Lumi eventually found the note.

She read it only once.

She carefully folded it closed.

When asked whether it should be discarded, Lumi quietly answered,

> "No."

She held the paper against her chest.

> "This isn't the truth."

A tear rolled down her cheek.

> "It's what my daughter believed about herself."

She looked toward the snow-covered forest.

> "There's a difference."

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Medical and Psychological Services determined that Corin's temporary memory loss had resolved, but the underlying trauma remained largely unchanged.

Clinicians noted that the return of memory did not immediately relieve her emotional pain.

Instead, remembering often meant reliving experiences she desperately wished had never happened.

Continued treatment remains focused on helping Corin separate the reality of who she is from the harsh judgments she directs toward herself.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin's memories have returned.

The snow still calls to her on difficult days.

Not because it makes her forget.

But because, for a little while, it lets the world grow quiet.

**End of Field Observation Report**
