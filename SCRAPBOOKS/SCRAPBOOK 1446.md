# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### ANOMALOUS RESEARCH & RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-ARC-4C-COG-034  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — COGNITIVE ANOMALY  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE / CRITICAL  
**SUBJECT:** SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTS — COMPLETE MEMORY RESTORATION  
**PRIMARY OBSERVATION:** Twiggy

---

# 1. MEMORY RESTORATION

The second exposure to the anomalous grassland produced a complete restoration of the residents' memories.

There were no longer isolated fragments.

No longer vague impressions.

No longer unexplained emotional responses.

Everyone remembered.

The missing child.

His presence.

His name.

Their relationships with him.

Their conversations.

Their mistakes.

Everything.

But the effect was particularly severe for **Twiggy**.

---

# 2. TWIGGY — COMPLETE RECALL

Twiggy's previously observed cognitive anomaly immediately resolved.

The fragment she had experienced earlier:

> **"I hurt a kid."**

was no longer fragmented.

She remembered the entire sequence.

She remembered the cafeteria.

She remembered the celebration.

She remembered drinking Nitro Fuel.

She remembered becoming increasingly intoxicated.

She remembered speaking without considering the effect her words would have.

She remembered:

> **"Friends, my ass."**

She remembered:

> **"I don't even bother to check on him."**

And, most importantly:

> **"If he die right now nobody will actually care and move on."**

She remembered saying it.

She remembered who was nearby.

She remembered the doorway.

She remembered the bowl of apples.

She remembered the card.

She remembered the sound of ceramic breaking.

She remembered the look on the child's face.

She remembered him leaving.

---

# 3. THE MEMORY SHE COULD NOT ACCESS

The original cognitive fragment had contained only one conclusion:

> **I hurt a kid.**

Twiggy now understood why.

The memory had not been false.

It had been incomplete.

The anomaly had allowed her to remember the emotional consequence before restoring the event itself.

She had been carrying guilt without knowing what she had done.

Now she knew.

---

# 4. TWIGGY'S RESPONSE

Twiggy remained completely motionless after the memories returned.

Everyone else was struggling with their own recollections.

Twiggy did not move.

She stared at the child sitting in the grass.

Her face gradually changed.

Recognition.

Horror.

Grief.

Then understanding.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "...Oh."

She covered her mouth.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "Oh God."

Nobody interrupted her.

Twiggy began shaking.

---

# 5. MEMORY CASCADE

More memories continued returning.

Not just the incident.

Everything.

Twiggy remembered the quieter moments.

She remembered seeing him around the SAFEHOUSE.

She remembered his medical condition.

She remembered his exhaustion.

She remembered how often he sat alone.

She remembered how little he asked for.

She remembered that he rarely complained.

She remembered that when he did ask for help, it was usually because he genuinely needed it.

She remembered assuming he would be fine.

She remembered everyone else doing the same.

The realization became increasingly painful.

The problem had never been that nobody cared.

The problem was that everyone kept assuming there would be another opportunity.

Another day.

Another conversation.

Another meal.

Another time to help.

Another chance.

There wasn't.

---

# 6. TWIGGY LOOKS AT HIM

Twiggy slowly approached the circle.

She stopped several meters away.

The child did not react.

His azure eyes remained open.

The shattered halo hung above his head.

The arrow remained embedded in his chest.

Twiggy looked at him for a long time.

Then:

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "I'm sorry."

Nothing.

Twiggy took another step.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "I remember."

Still nothing.

Her voice broke.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "I remember everything."

She lowered herself to her knees.

---

# 7. NO EXCUSE

Twiggy did not attempt to explain herself.

She did not blame the alcohol.

She did not blame exhaustion.

She did not blame the circumstances.

She did not say that she had not meant it.

She simply remembered.

And accepted that the words had been spoken.

The most painful part was that she now understood exactly what those words meant to the person who heard them.

A child who had already spent much of his existence wondering whether anyone would notice if he disappeared.

And someone he trusted had told him that nobody would care.

---

# 8. THE OTHERS

Around her, the other residents were also processing their memories.

Bao remembered being called Mom.

Neri remembered the child she had drawn.

Nora remembered being his friend.

Alice remembered forgetting him.

Jane remembered the conversations nobody else had heard.

Neko remembered the engineering projects.

Frost remembered the quiet companionship.

Each resident carried their own history.

But Twiggy's memories contained something different.

She remembered the moment that preceded the final disappearance.

She remembered being one of the last people he saw.

And she remembered exactly what she had said.

---

# 9. TWIGGY'S COGNITIVE ANOMALY

The previous designation **CA-031** is now considered resolved.

The anomaly was not an independent false-memory phenomenon.

It was a **partial restoration event.**

Twiggy had begun remembering before the broader mnemonic mechanism became accessible.

Her initial fragment was therefore the first visible symptom of the larger memory suppression.

Her mind retained the emotional consequence before it recovered the associated autobiographical information.

The subsequent grassland exposure restored the missing context.

---

# 10. FINAL OBSERVATION

Twiggy remained kneeling.

She looked toward the child.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "You were real."

A long silence.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "You were really here."

Her hands tightened against the grass.

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "And I hurt you."

No response.

Twiggy lowered her head.

For several seconds, nobody moved.

Then she whispered:

> **TWIGGY:**  
> "I'm sorry."

This time, she knew exactly who she was apologizing to.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Every resident remembered.

But for Twiggy, remembering was not simply recovering someone she had forgotten.

It was recovering the reason she had already been afraid.

The child.

The words.

The doorway.

The apples.

The broken card.

The elevator.

The disappearance.

All of it.

The fragment had been telling her the truth from the beginning.

She had hurt a kid.

She simply hadn't remembered **which kid**.

Now she does.
