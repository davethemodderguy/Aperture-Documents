## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora is gone.

Not missing.

Not hiding.

Not residing somewhere beyond the known search perimeter.

Gone.

The distinction is now considered essential.

For months, Sora existed in a state of progressive isolation. His memories deteriorated following a severe head injury. Faces became indistinguishable beneath the black scribbles he described. Names, relationships, and personal history disappeared one after another.

Eventually, he remembered almost nothing beyond himself, his den, and the fact that he talked to himself.

The people around him gradually stopped looking for him.

Some forgot his existence. Others remembered that someone had once lived on Floor 7 but could no longer identify who. References to his medical history disappeared from routine conversations. His absence became ordinary.

Then even the records stopped being consulted.

Only Mia remembered.

She remembered the farm. Their grandmother. The winters. The wolves. The doghouse. The boy who used to protect her. She remembered having a big brother.

She remembered Sora.

And now, according to the latest confirmation, Sora is permanently gone.

There is no verified location to search, no confirmed destination, and no evidence supporting the possibility of his return.

C.O.R.E. will not describe this as another disappearance.

**This is a permanent loss.**

---

## 2. THE ERASURE OF A PERSON

The initial deterioration was treated as a medical and welfare emergency.

That emergency became a prolonged institutional failure.

Sora had already experienced abandonment, severe physical injuries, malnutrition, loss of his voice, and repeated failures of recognition. His den, originally intended to provide familiarity and safety, gradually became the only place in which he was known to exist.

His condition was documented.

His suffering was documented.

His isolation was documented.

Yet documentation did not translate into consistent human attention.

The reports accumulated while the child himself became increasingly absent from the lives of the people who were supposed to protect him.

The following progression has been reconstructed from available records:

|Stage|Condition|
|---|---|
|Initial isolation|Sora withdrew into his den and stopped seeking company.|
|Physical deterioration|Malnutrition, weakness, and untreated welfare concerns accumulated.|
|Social withdrawal|Staff and residents increasingly avoided approaching him.|
|Memory deterioration|Sora began forgetting people, places, and personal history.|
|Neurological deterioration|Face blindness made every person appear to have black scribbles covering their face.|
|Recognition collapse|Sora could no longer recognize Mia or remember their relationship.|
|Social erasure|Almost everyone forgot Sora existed.|
|Final status|Sora permanently gone.|

This progression must not be interpreted as a sequence of inevitable events.

There were opportunities to intervene.

There were people who could have visited.

There were people who could have remembered.

There were responsibilities that were not fulfilled.

C.O.R.E. acknowledges that the institution failed to maintain the attention necessary to protect a child who had already been repeatedly abandoned.

---

## 3. THE LAST PERSON WHO REMEMBERED

Mia remains the only confirmed individual who remembers Sora.

She remembers him not as a case number, a missing resident, or a child whose medical condition required monitoring.

She remembers him as her brother.

She remembers the way he protected her when they lived on the farm. She remembers their grandmother. She remembers the doghouse where she stayed while Sora watched over the sheep.

She remembers the boy who left reassuring messages whenever she was upset.

She remembers the reunion.

She remembers telling him that she still wanted to be his little sister, even though they were the same age.

And she remembers the last time she saw him.

The asymmetry is absolute.

Mia remembers someone who cannot be found.

There is no corresponding memory on Sora's side to recover, because Sora is no longer here.

## 4. THE DEN

Sora's Den remains on Floor 7.

It is a small, doghouse-like space built into the wall. Its entrance resembles a familiar hole in the wall rather than a conventional bedroom doorway.

It was constructed to give Sora somewhere familiar.

It became the place where he spent most of his remaining time.

The original whiteboard is gone. The scarf knitted by Mia and Jake was previously provided as a comfort item. His medical supplies and remaining belongings have been accounted for only to the extent that surviving records permit.

The den is now unoccupied.

No resident has reported hearing Sora speaking to himself.

No one has reported seeing him playing with his tail.

No one has reported him asking questions in the darkness.

No one has heard him call for someone, only to answer himself that nobody needed to come.

The silence is not evidence of recovery.

It is the absence of the person who used to make those sounds.

**C.O.R.E. DIRECTIVE:** The den is not to be casually repurposed, dismantled, or treated as ordinary vacant space. Its preservation is authorized pending a separate review.

---

## 5. INSTITUTIONAL ACCOUNTABILITY

C.O.R.E. recognizes that the phrase _everyone forgot Sora_ is not an adequate explanation.

Forgetting is an observation.

It is not an excuse.

Sora was a six-year-old child living within an organization whose stated purpose includes protecting vulnerable individuals. His condition was known. His history was documented. His needs were repeatedly identified.

Yet his isolation continued.

Mia was six years old. Jake was six years old. Neither was responsible for keeping Sora alive, monitoring his medical condition, or ensuring that someone remembered to visit him.

Their childhood should never have been treated as a substitute for adult care.

The responsibility belonged to the adults and the institution.

C.O.R.E. will not transfer that responsibility to the children now that the outcome is irreversible.

## 7. CLOSING NOTE — SOMEONE SHOULD HAVE REMEMBERED

There used to be a little white dog boy on Floor 7.

He had a whiteboard because writing was easier than speaking.

He carried it around like a schoolbag.

He liked staying close to Jake.

He called Bao Mom.

He liked the moon.

He had a little sister named Mia, even though they were the same age.

He was afraid of people, but he tried to trust them.

He was afraid of blood.

He was afraid of being a burden.

He spent months alone in a little doghouse built into a wall, talking to himself because there was nobody else he could reliably speak to.

And eventually, people forgot him.

Not all at once.

That might have been easier to notice.

They forgot him gradually, between meetings, meals, assignments, jokes, and ordinary days. His absence became less unusual each time someone failed to ask where he was.

Until there was almost nobody left who could say his name.

Almost nobody.

Mia still can.

She remembers a big brother who once stood between her and the wolves.

She remembers a boy who used to reassure her when she blamed herself.

She remembers someone who tried to protect her even when he was frightened himself.

She remembers Sora.

C.O.R.E. cannot give her brother back.

It cannot promise that he will return.

It cannot ask her to stop grieving, and it cannot make her memories less painful.

But it can refuse to repeat the final indignity.

We will not delete his records.

We will not reclassify him as an unoccupied space.

We will not allow his name to disappear from the SAFEHOUSE archives simply because there is nobody left to ask about him.

Sora existed.

He was six years old.

He had a sister.

He was loved, even when he could no longer remember it.

And someone should have remembered him before Mia became the only one.

**For Mia.**

**For Sora.**

**For the little boy we failed to keep.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
