### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — SOCIAL WITHDRAWAL EVENT**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 6  
**Species:** Full-Body Rabbit Thiren  
**Status:** Active — Immediate Psychological Intervention Required

---

# **CURRENT OBSERVATION**

Alice has stopped spending time around other residents.

Completely.

She no longer follows personnel through the Blacksite.

She no longer visits friends.

She no longer rides her scooter around the facility.

She rarely enters communal areas unless necessary.

She does not sit beside anyone during meals.

She does not seek out Belle.

She does not approach Corin.

She does not wander toward Neko, Miss Neko, Star, Nightshade, Bao, Frost, Astral, or any other familiar personnel.

Instead, Alice has developed a single repeated behavior.

She finds a corner.

She sits down.

And she stares at her hands.

---

# **OBSERVED BEHAVIOR**

Alice may remain in the same position for extended periods.

Her ears remain lowered.

Her posture is withdrawn.

Her hands are usually held together in her lap.

She rarely looks toward passing personnel.

If someone approaches her, she may briefly look up before lowering her gaze again.

She does not appear to be sleeping.

She is simply staring at her hands.

When asked what she is doing, Alice generally does not answer.

On one occasion, a staff member sat several meters away and quietly asked:

> "Alice?"

Alice continued staring at her hands.

After several seconds, she whispered:

> **"Alice bad."**

The staff member asked:

> "Why do you think that?"

Alice's fingers tightened together.

> **"Alice got punished."**

A pause.

> **"Alice is not good."**

Another pause.

> **"Alice make everyone tired."**

She then stopped speaking.

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE**

This represents a significant deterioration from Alice's previous behavior.

Alice has historically struggled with feelings of worthlessness.

However, she previously continued seeking connection despite those feelings.

She would follow people.

She would sit nearby.

She would ride her scooter through familiar areas.

She would draw pictures.

She would visit friends.

She would quietly remain near people even when she was afraid of bothering them.

That behavior has now disappeared.

Alice is no longer merely asking whether she is a burden.

She appears to have accepted it as fact.

The recent cafeteria incident appears to have reinforced this belief.

From Alice's perspective:

**She was somewhere she was not supposed to be.**

**Someone hurt her.**

**She was unconscious afterward.**

Therefore:

> **"Alice was bad, so Alice got punished."**

This conclusion is incorrect.

But Alice currently appears unable to recognize that.

---

# **CRITICAL MISINTERPRETATION**

The medical team has emphasized that Alice's concussion was an injury resulting from a security failure and mistaken identification.

It was **not punishment.**

Alice did nothing that justified being struck.

This distinction must be repeated carefully.

Personnel must not tell Alice:

> "You weren't punished because you were good."

This still frames physical harm as something that can be deserved.

Instead, personnel should communicate:

> **"You were hurt because someone made a mistake."**

> **"You did not deserve to be hit."**

> **"You are not bad."**

> **"You are allowed to be here."**

---

# **CORIN'S RESPONSE**

Corin eventually found Alice sitting alone.

She did not immediately touch her.

She simply sat down nearby.

Alice continued looking at her hands.

After several minutes, Corin quietly said:

> "You don't have to talk."

Alice remained silent.

Corin continued:

> "You can just sit here."

Alice's ears moved slightly.

Corin waited.

Eventually, Alice whispered:

> "...Alice make everyone tired."

Corin answered:

> "You make me worry sometimes."

Alice looked down further.

Corin continued:

> "That's different."

No response.

> "Being cared for isn't the same thing as being a burden."

Alice remained quiet.

But she did not leave.

---

# **PERSONNEL DIRECTIVE**

For the immediate future, personnel are instructed **not to overwhelm Alice with attention.**

Her withdrawal must be respected without allowing her to become isolated.

Recommended approach:

- Sit nearby.
    
- Speak calmly.
    
- Offer food and water.
    
- Offer companionship without demanding conversation.
    
- Allow Alice to choose physical distance.
    
- Remind her that she is welcome.
    
- Check on her regularly.
    
- Do not force her into crowded environments.
    
- Do not demand that she "cheer up."
    
- Do not tell her to stop being sad.
    
- Do not punish withdrawal.
    
- Do not interpret silence as refusal of care.
    

Most importantly:

**Do not leave her alone simply because she does not ask anyone to stay.**

---

# **ADDITIONAL SAFETY CONCERN**

Alice has historically been poor at communicating physical and emotional needs.

Her current withdrawal makes this significantly worse.

Personnel must therefore assume that:

> **Silence does not mean "Alice is okay."**

> **Silence does not mean "Alice wants to be alone."**

> **Silence does not mean "Alice doesn't need help."**

Alice may simply believe that asking for help would make her a burden.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

The cafeteria incident injured Alice's head.

But something else was injured as well.

Her willingness to believe that people would protect her.

Alice used to enter a room and look for someone she knew.

Now she looks for a corner.

She used to ride her scooter because it let her reach people without exhausting herself.

Now the scooter remains untouched.

She used to follow people because she liked them.

Now she avoids everyone.

And when asked what she thinks about herself, she looks at her tiny hands and quietly repeats:

> **"Alice bad."**

> **"Alice got punished."**

> **"Alice is not good."**

> **"Alice make everyone tired."**

C.O.R.E. formally rejects every one of these conclusions.

Alice was not punished.

Alice was hurt.

Alice is not bad.

Alice is a six-year-old child who has been hurt repeatedly and has begun blaming herself for things that were never her fault.

She does not need to prove that she is good enough to come back.

She does not need to apologize for existing.

And she does not need to become useful before anyone is willing to stay.

For now, personnel will sit with her.

Even if she says nothing.

Even if she looks only at her hands.

Even if she believes she does not deserve the company.

Because someone needs to remain long enough for Alice to eventually learn:

**Being cared for is not a punishment.**

**Needing someone is not a failure.**

**And Alice is still allowed to belong.**
