# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake is being treated like a cat.

Not metaphorically.

Not as a temporary nickname.

Not as a joke that residents occasionally make at his expense.

**Jake is, for all practical purposes, being treated like an actual cat.**

He sleeps on blankets, occupies windowsills, receives head scratches, follows people around, and has developed a preference for sitting on furniture that was not designed to accommodate him.

Residents speak to him as though he were a cat.

They offer him water.

They provide appropriate food.

They make room for him on couches.

They open doors for him.

They occasionally ask him questions and accept a meow as an answer.

They also stop what they are doing whenever he walks across their keyboards.

This last behavior has become a serious productivity concern.

The distinction between Jake's current physical form and his retained cognitive abilities remains important to C.O.R.E. Research.

However, SAFEHOUSE residents have developed a much simpler approach.

Jake is a cat.

Jake needs to eat.

Jake needs somewhere comfortable to sleep.

Jake needs people to respect his boundaries.

And Jake apparently needs to be informed that biting charging cables is not an acceptable way to express displeasure.

Whether he understands the last point remains uncertain.

---

# 1. THE NEW NORMAL

The first major change occurred when residents stopped treating Jake's feline body as an emergency that needed to be discussed every time he entered a room.

Initially, every encounter involved some variation of:

> "Jake? Is that really you?"

> "Can you understand me?"

> "Do you know how to turn back?"

> "Are you feeling better?"

The questions were understandable.

They were also exhausting.

Jake could understand every word.

He simply could not answer them.

Eventually, Bao established a rule.

**BAO:**  
"If he needs something, help him. If he's resting, let him rest. And stop asking him the same questions every time you see him."

The rule was adopted almost immediately.

Within forty-eight hours, Jake's daily routine had begun to resemble that of an ordinary household cat.

There were, however, several complications.

The first was that Jake was not an ordinary cat.

The second was that Jake knew this.

The third was that everyone else occasionally forgot the second point.

# 2. MORNING ROUTINE

Jake's current morning routine begins whenever he wakes up.

This is not necessarily the same time as everyone else's morning.

At 06:14, Jake emerged from his blanket.

He stretched his front legs.

Arched his back.

Yawned.

Then sat down and stared at the room.

Clover was already awake at her desk.

She glanced over.

**CLOVER:**  
"Morning, Jake."

"Mrrp."

**CLOVER:**  
"Sleep well?"

Jake blinked slowly.

Clover interpreted this as an affirmative response.

She returned to her work.

Jake sat there for another minute.

Then he walked to the edge of the bedroll and looked down.

He was approximately thirty centimeters above the floor.

He considered the distance.

He looked at Clover.

Then at the floor.

Then back at Clover.

**CLOVER:**  
"...You can jump."

Jake stared at her.

Clover stared back.

**CLOVER:**  
"You're a cat."

Jake's ears rotated backward.

Clover sighed.

She stood up and lowered him to the floor.

**CLOVER:**  
"There."

Jake immediately walked away.

Clover watched him go.

**CLOVER:**  
"You could have jumped."

From the hallway, a small, indignant meow was heard.

Clover returned to her desk.

**C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION:** The subject may be physically capable of an action while remaining perfectly willing to have another person perform it for him.

# 3. FOOD AND WATER

Jake's feeding arrangements have been adjusted to account for his current physiology and established preferences.

Commercial cat food has been removed from his regular meal rotation following the incident documented in C.O.R.E.-ANM-4C-JAKE-179.

Jake's opinion of it remains unchanged.

He hates it.

He has demonstrated no interest in reconsidering his position.

Instead, his meals consist of suitable, safely prepared foods selected with medical and veterinary guidance. Portions are adjusted to his current size and nutritional needs.

Fresh water is always available.

The bowl is placed somewhere he can reach without having to climb.

This arrangement has produced one unexpected complication.

Jake prefers drinking water from the glass beside Clover's desk.

Not the water bowl.

Not the second water bowl.

Not the fresh water Bao placed beside his blanket.

Clover's glass.

At 08:03, Clover returned from the kitchen to find Jake standing on her desk.

His head was inside her glass.

She stopped.

Jake looked up.

Water dripped from his whiskers.

**CLOVER:**  
"Jake."

"Mrrp?"

**CLOVER:**  
"That's mine."

Jake looked at the glass.

Then at her.

He lowered his head and took another drink.

Clover closed her eyes.

**CLOVER:**  
"Bao bought you three bowls."

Jake continued drinking.

Clover carefully moved the glass away.

Jake followed it with his eyes.

**CLOVER:**  
"No."

He placed one paw on her wrist.

**CLOVER:**  
"No."

He placed the other paw on her wrist.

**CLOVER:**  
"...You are unbelievable."

Jake began purring.

Clover gave up.

A dedicated glass of fresh water was subsequently placed on the desk.

Jake drank from it once.

Then returned to Clover's glass.

**C.O.R.E. NOTE:** The provision of appropriate alternatives does not guarantee that the subject will use them.

# 4. PETTING PROTOCOL

Jake has received considerably more physical affection since becoming a cat.

This is partly because his current appearance makes him considerably easier to pick up.

It is also because several SAFEHOUSE residents have discovered that Jake's ears and forehead are particularly receptive to gentle scratching.

Jake has not issued a formal objection.

He has, however, developed preferences.

## Approved areas

Subject to Jake's willingness:

- forehead;
    
- cheeks;
    
- behind the ears;
    
- the area beneath the chin.
    

## Areas requiring caution

- paws;
    
- tail;
    
- stomach;
    
- any area he actively moves away from.
    

His stomach is particularly deceptive.

Jake has occasionally rolled onto his back, exposed his belly, and looked directly at a nearby resident.

The resident then attempted to rub it.

Jake immediately grabbed their hand with all four paws.

No serious injury has resulted.

The resident learned something.

Jake learned that this behavior makes people stop.

The behavior has therefore continued.

# 5. PICKING JAKE UP

Jake is now routinely carried by residents.

This is not because he cannot walk.

He can.

His current mobility appears substantially better than during his final medical crisis.

Rather, residents have discovered that Jake sometimes prefers being carried.

He has developed several methods of communicating this preference.

### Method A — The Ankle Approach

Jake approaches someone's feet and looks upward.

If ignored, he meows.

If still ignored, he places both front paws against their leg.

If the person continues walking, Jake follows.

If the person kneels, Jake allows himself to be picked up.

### Method B — The Desk Invitation

Jake jumps onto a desk.

He walks to the edge.

He looks at the nearest person.

Then he sits down.

This is generally interpreted as a request to be lifted.

### Method C — The Direct Approach

Jake simply climbs onto someone.

If they are sitting, he settles in their lap.

If they are lying down, he may lie across their chest.

If they are working, he may sit on their keyboard.

The keyboard method is strongly discouraged.

Jake has not acknowledged this restriction.

# 6. THE WINDOW PROBLEM

Jake has developed a preference for windowsills.

He likes watching movement outside.

He watches birds.

He watches passing personnel.

He watches the lights in distant corridors.

Sometimes he simply sits there for an hour without moving.

C.O.R.E. has determined that the windows Jake uses are structurally safe and that appropriate barriers are in place.

No open window is to be left accessible without suitable protection.

Jake is still a small, vulnerable body.

He is not to be allowed to fall from a height on the assumption that his stellar nature will protect him.

This rule was established after an incident in which Jake attempted to climb onto a particularly narrow ledge.

Neri found him halfway up the curtain.

She stopped.

Jake looked down.

Neri looked up.

**NERI:**  
"...Jake."

Jake meowed.

**NERI:**  
"Get down."

Jake climbed higher.

Neri closed her eyes.

**NERI:**  
"Of course."

She fetched a chair and carefully helped him down.

Jake immediately walked to the window and sat on the sill.

Neri stared at him.

**NERI:**  
"You cannot be serious."

Jake blinked.

Neri moved a cushion onto the wider, safer windowsill.

Jake inspected it.

He stepped onto it.

Turned around.

Sat down.

Neri nodded.

**NERI:**  
"Thank you."

Then slowly turned his back.

Sometimes he sleeps on his blanket.

Sometimes on Clover's bedroll.

Stretched.

Climbed onto Clover's bedroll.

**CLOVER:**

**CLOVER:**

# 9. THE OTHER RESIDENTS HAVE ACCEPTED IT

The transformation initially caused significant concern.

That concern has not disappeared.

However, daily life has begun to accommodate Jake's current form.

## Alice

Alice has developed a habit of asking Jake whether he wants a ride on her scooter.

Jake cannot safely operate the scooter himself.

He can, however, sit in a secure carrier while Alice transports him when medically appropriate and the route is safe.

Alice considers this an excellent arrangement.

Jake considers the carrier unnecessary.

The first time he saw it, he climbed inside voluntarily.

The second time, he refused to enter until Alice placed his blanket inside.

The third time, he climbed in and immediately sat on the blanket.

Alice smiled.

**ALICE:**  
"See? You like it."

Jake stared at her.

**ALICE:**  
"You do."

Jake turned around and presented her with his back.

Alice considered this a victory.

## Frost

Frost has become the resident most likely to find Jake sleeping in an inconvenient location.

She has discovered him:

- inside an empty laundry basket;
    
- beneath a chair;
    
- between two cushions;
    
- on top of a folded blanket;

## Bao

Bao has become responsible for ensuring Jake's current diet and daily routine remain appropriate.

She also has a habit of checking his ears, paws, and general responsiveness when he seems unusually tired.

Jake tolerates these checks more readily from Bao than from most other residents.

When he has had enough, he walks away.

Bao lets him.

She has also begun preparing small, safe portions of food that he is willing to eat.

Jake now appears beside her whenever she enters the kitchen.

Bao has interpreted this as a request for food.

Sometimes she is correct.

Sometimes Jake merely sits on the floor and watches her cook.

Sometimes he falls asleep while waiting.

Bao has started placing a soft mat in the corner of the kitchen.

Jake uses it regularly.

## Neri

Neri has become unusually good at understanding Jake's body language.

She watches what he does repeatedly and waits for him to make his preferences clear.

When Jake wants to be left alone, Neri leaves him alone.

When he approaches, she allows him to approach.

When he becomes frustrated, she does not demand that he calm down.

She simply lowers herself to his level and waits.

On one occasion, Jake sat beside her while she was reading.

He remained there for approximately twenty minutes.

Then he leaned against her leg.

Neri looked down.

**NERI:**

**NERI:**

# 10. BEHAVIORAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's current behavior is broadly consistent with ordinary feline behavior, although several characteristics suggest that his previous cognitive abilities remain intact.

These include:

- recognizing familiar individuals;
    
- responding to his name;
    
- understanding spoken instructions;
    
- remembering the location of personal belongings;
    
- demonstrating specific food preferences;
    
- deliberately seeking particular people;
    
- and displaying frustration when unable to communicate.
    

His intelligence should not be underestimated simply because he cannot speak.

At the same time, C.O.R.E. must avoid assigning human intentions to every feline behavior.

A purr may indicate comfort, but it is not definitive proof of happiness.

A tail movement may communicate agitation, but its meaning depends on context.

A cat approaching someone may indicate interest, familiarity, or a desire for warmth.

Jake must be treated as an individual whose behavior is observed rather than assumed.

The objective is not to decode every movement.

It is to provide a safe environment in which he can express preferences without being punished for failing to communicate them in words.

# C.O.R.E. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's current form remains anomalous and medically unexplained.

Investigation into the transformation continues.

However, daily welfare procedures have changed.

Residents no longer treat every ordinary interaction as an opportunity to investigate the anomaly.

They feed him appropriately.

They provide fresh water.

They keep his environment safe.

They let him sleep.

They pet him when he wants affection.

They stop when he wants them to stop.

They help him reach places he cannot safely access.

They keep his teddy bear nearby.

And, occasionally, they allow him to sit in someone's lap while the person continues working.

Jake has not recovered his ability to speak.

He has not regained his previous form.

He has not explained why he became a cat.

But he has begun to establish a routine.

That routine matters.

Predictable meals.

Familiar people.

A safe room.

A comfortable blanket.

A teddy bear whose heartbeat continues.

The ability to approach someone without being interrogated.

The ability to leave without being chased.

These are small things.

For Jake, they are also things that were missing when he needed them most.
