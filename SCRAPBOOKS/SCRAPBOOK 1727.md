**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE / ANOMALOUS PAINTING ENVIRONMENT  
**EVENT:** First Birthday Celebration — 1 November, Year 75

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake is exhausted.

This was expected.

The birthday celebration involved an unusually high degree of emotional stimulation, sustained social interaction, crying, cake consumption, and prolonged exposure to an anomalous environment that Jake himself helped access.

The celebration was successful.

The resident in question is now approximately one hug away from falling asleep standing up.

C.O.R.E. has therefore initiated routine post-event welfare monitoring.

No further activities are scheduled for Jake.

**This includes additional surprises.**

Clover has been specifically instructed not to organize an afterparty.

She has stated that she was not planning one.

C.O.R.E. has recorded this statement.

---

# 1. EVENT CONCLUSION

The birthday celebration concluded without serious incident.

The painting remained stable throughout the gathering.

Residents were able to enter and leave the painted landscape without apparent difficulty. The environment remained visually consistent, and no participant reported immediate adverse effects.

The residents spent the remainder of the event stargazing.

Several sat in the grass.

Others gathered beside the lake.

Some compared the painted constellations with those visible in the actual night sky.

Neri sat beside Bao with a cup of warm coffee.

Alice attempted to determine whether the painted grass could be pulled out of the ground.

Neko told her not to.

Alice stopped.

Approximately four minutes later, Neko discovered her examining a different patch of grass.

The investigation was postponed.

Jake, meanwhile, appeared happier than personnel had previously observed.

He also appeared increasingly tired.

Initially, this was not particularly noticeable.

Jake was smiling at the painting.

He was looking at the stars.

He was listening to his friends.

He was occasionally pointing out constellations.

Then his responses became slower.

His head began to droop.

At one point, he rested his forehead against Clover's shoulder and remained there for nearly a minute.

Clover did not move.

Neither did Jake.

Eventually, Clover quietly asked:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Sleepy?"

Jake shook his head.

His eyes closed.

Clover waited.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...A little."

This was considered a substantial understatement.

---

# 2. THE GRADUAL SHUTDOWN

Jake's exhaustion developed gradually.

No single symptom suggested an immediate medical crisis.

Instead, his usual activity declined in several small stages.

|Observation|Recorded behavior|
|---|---|
|Initial|Actively participated in the celebration|
|Mid-event|Became quieter between conversations|
|Later|Remained seated rather than exploring|
|Final stargazing period|Leaned against Clover and stopped initiating conversation|
|Departure|Required assistance returning to SAFEHOUSE|

Jake continued to respond appropriately when spoken to.

He was not confused.

He did not appear distressed.

He was simply running out of energy.

C.O.R.E. notes that Jake's emotional enthusiasm should not be mistaken for unlimited physical endurance.

His ability to manifest celestial phenomena does not prevent a six-year-old-presenting child from becoming thoroughly exhausted after an unusually eventful evening.

Apparently, neither does being a star.

---

# 3. THE FINAL FIVE MINUTES

**LOCATION:** PAINTED LANDSCAPE — LAKESIDE

The following interaction was recorded by a resident's personal device.

Jake was sitting in the grass between Clover and Bao.

His teddy bear rested in his lap.

The cake had been finished.

The candles were extinguished.

The stars continued moving overhead.

Jake looked up at them.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Pretty."

> **BAO:**  
> "Very pretty."

Jake nodded.

A few seconds passed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Can we stay?"

Bao glanced toward Clover.

Clover gently brushed a little grass from Jake's white hair.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "We can come back."

Jake considered this.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Tomorrow?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "If you want."

He nodded.

His eyelids lowered.

Then opened again.

Then lowered.

Clover looked down at him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Jake."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Mm?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You can sleep."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Not yet."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Why not?"

Jake looked toward the other residents.

Everyone was still there.

Jane was sitting near Alice.

Frost and Nora were looking at the stars.

Neko was quietly speaking with Aeterna about the painting.

Bao was beside him.

Clover was beside him.

Jake's fingers curled around his teddy bear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...I don't want to miss it."

Nobody answered immediately.

Clover's expression softened.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You won't miss it."

Jake looked at her.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "The stars will still be here when you wake up."

> **BAO:**  
> "And so will we."

Jake stared at them for several seconds.

Then he leaned against Clover again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Okay."

His eyes closed.

This time, they stayed closed.

---

# 4. RETURN TO SAFEHOUSE

Jake did not wake when the residents began leaving the painting.

He was carried back through the canvas with his teddy bear held securely against his chest.

The transition produced no observable distress.

Upon returning to SAFEHOUSE, Clover and Bao coordinated his care.

His teddy bear was positioned so that it would not become trapped beneath his body or subjected to unnecessary pressure.

Because the bear functions as a sensory extension of Jake, personnel were reminded that handling it roughly would not be harmless simply because it was a stuffed toy.

No one attempted to remove it from his arms.

Clover carried Jake to his room.

Bao followed.

The room was quiet.

His bedding had been prepared earlier that evening.

Clover laid him down carefully and adjusted his pillow.

Jake stirred.

For several seconds, he appeared to be waking.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Clover?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'm here."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Mom?"

> **BAO:**  
> "I'm here too, sweetheart."

Jake relaxed.

He shifted his teddy bear closer.

Then, very quietly:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Best birthday."

Clover stopped adjusting the blanket.

Bao lowered her head.

Neither corrected his grammar.

Neither asked him to repeat himself.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'm glad."

Jake's eyes remained closed.

A few seconds later, his breathing settled into a steady rhythm.

He was asleep.

---

# 5. POST-EVENT MEDICAL OBSERVATION

**ASSESSMENT:** Apparent ordinary exhaustion following prolonged activity and emotional stimulation.

No immediate evidence of a new medical emergency was observed during the return to SAFEHOUSE.

C.O.R.E. nevertheless recommends that residents remain attentive to any unusual symptoms, given Jake's anomalous physiology and fragile human manifestation.

The following measures were implemented:

- All remaining activities were cancelled.
    
- Jake was permitted to sleep without interruption.
    
- His teddy bear remained within reach.
    
- Water was placed nearby for when he woke.
    
- No additional visitors were permitted to enter solely to congratulate him.
    
- No testing of the painting was authorized while Jake was resting.
    
- The cake was stored for the following day.
    

The last measure was particularly important.

Alice had suggested saving Jake an additional slice.

Bao had already done so.

Alice then suggested saving a second slice.

Bao informed her that Jake did not need an entire cake waiting for him when he woke up.

Alice considered this.

> **ALICE:**  
> "What about a small cake?"

> **BAO:**  
> "No."

> **ALICE:**  
> "...A cupcake?"

> **BAO:**  
> "Alice."

The discussion ended.

# 6. THE PAINTING AFTER EVERYONE LEFT

The completed painting remained in the cafeteria.

Its painted landscape had returned to its original dimensions.

The lake was still.

The stars remained scattered across the canvas.

The signatures of the residents were visible throughout the composition.

Some were neat.

Some were crooked.

Some had been drawn beside tiny illustrations.

One signature was accompanied by a flower that still looked suspiciously like a potato.

The words **1 November — Year 75** remained in their subtle corner of the painting.

Clover stood before it for a while after returning from Jake's room.

She had organized the surprise.

She had helped conceal it.

She had watched everyone contribute.

She had seen Jake discover it.

She had watched him cry.

And she had watched the painting become a place where everyone could sit together beneath the stars.

Now, the cafeteria was quiet.

Clover looked at the painting and smiled faintly.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Happy birthday, Jake."

She turned off the cafeteria lights.

The painting remained on the wall.

---

# 7. C.O.R.E. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Jake's exhaustion is not considered a negative outcome of the celebration.

The event involved more activity and emotional engagement than his usual routine.

For a child who had never previously celebrated a birthday, the experience was also likely to have been unusually significant.

He was surprised.

He was overwhelmed.

He cried.

He laughed.

He spent time with people who wanted him there.

He received a painting containing the signatures of people who had chosen to participate.

He ate cake.

He watched the stars with his friends.

And when he became too tired to remain awake, he was able to fall asleep without having to leave everyone behind.

That final detail is worth noting.

Jake's earlier concern was not that he disliked the celebration.

It was that he did not want to miss it.

Personnel reassured him that the people and the stars would remain.

He accepted that reassurance.

He went to sleep.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

At 22:41, Jake was asleep.

His teddy bear rested beneath his chin.

His white hair was slightly untidy.

The faint azure glow in his eyes had disappeared beneath his closed eyelids.

Clover sat on the other side of the room, reading quietly.

Bao checked on him once before leaving.

Jake did not wake.

There were no further celebrations.

No additional surprises.

No speeches.

No requests for him to get up and look at something.

For once, everyone allowed the evening to end.

The birthday had been extraordinary.

The painting had become a world.

The stars had been beautiful.

But the most important thing that happened afterward was considerably less unusual.

A tired little boy went to bed.

His teddy bear was beside him.

His friends were nearby.

And tomorrow, when he woke up, the world would still be there.
