# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana's condition has deteriorated following the latest attack.

Although he survived the immediate medical emergency, the consequences are now extending well beyond his original injuries.

His voice is persistently strained, shaky, and restricted following the penetrating wound to his neck. Speaking appears difficult, and his already limited verbal communication has become even less frequent.

Numerous scars have developed across his body, including at the sites of his previous injuries. Their long-term permanence and the precise mechanisms behind their formation remain under medical review.

More concerningly, Jake has developed an increasingly deliberate pattern of avoidance directed toward his older brother, Aries Akana.

He does not merely leave when Aries enters a room.

He attempts to ensure that Aries will not find him in the first place.

Jake has been observed remaining outdoors in freezing conditions for hours rather than returning to an area where Aries might be present. He has stopped sleeping regularly in his assigned room. He changes his usual locations, moves unpredictably through SAFEHOUSE, and appears to avoid establishing any routine that could make his whereabouts easy to anticipate.

At the same time, Jake has not eaten for an extended period.

The exact duration of his food refusal requires confirmation, but it has persisted long enough to constitute a significant medical concern.

These behaviors must not be treated as ordinary withdrawal.

Jake appears to be organizing his daily life around one central objective:

**Avoid being found by the person he fears, even when doing so places him in danger.**

The person he fears is his own brother.

And the person he fears is not the person who attacked him.

The original assailant deliberately used Aries's appearance to exploit Jake's trust. Jake has not recovered from that deception. Following another violent incident, the resemblance has become a source of continuing fear.

For Aries, the situation is devastating.

For Jake, it is a problem he is attempting to solve through disappearance.

C.O.R.E. considers the combination of physical deterioration, prolonged food refusal, cold exposure, disrupted sleep, and active avoidance a critical welfare concern.

# 1. MEDICAL STATUS — RESIDUAL INJURY

Jake's survival has not constituted a full recovery.

The immediate emergency has passed, but his physical condition remains compromised.

## 1.1 Vocal impairment

Jake's voice is now notably different.

When he attempts to speak, his voice may shake.

His words are strained.

His speech is restricted, and he often appears to expend considerable effort producing even a short sentence.

The wound to his neck is a significant medical consideration. The precise structures affected and the extent of residual damage must be determined by the attending medical team.

C.O.R.E. does not assume that the restriction is exclusively psychological.

Pain, tissue damage, swelling, scarring, and other consequences of the injury may contribute to his current difficulty speaking.

His voice must not be forced through prolonged conversation or repeated questioning.

Personnel should allow him to communicate through gestures, writing, or other comfortable means whenever possible.

If his breathing, swallowing, voice, or level of consciousness deteriorates, immediate medical reassessment is required.

The fact that Jake can still produce words does not establish that speaking is medically effortless or safe.

## 1.2 Scarring

Multiple scars are now visible across Jake's body.

They include the sites of the latest penetrating injuries and other areas affected by previous trauma.

Their appearance has become an additional source of concern during medical assessments.

Jake sometimes looks at them.

At other times, he deliberately avoids looking at his own body while being examined.

He has not consistently discussed what he remembers.

Personnel must not assume that silence indicates a lack of awareness.

The scars are physically visible reminders of events that Jake has struggled to understand.

They may also make the injuries feel more permanent to him, particularly when he encounters them unexpectedly.

Medical staff are instructed to explain examinations before proceeding, obtain cooperation where possible, and avoid unnecessarily exposing or handling injured areas.

Jake must not be required to discuss the circumstances of every scar.

The purpose of treatment is to support his recovery, not to make his body a permanent subject of investigation in his presence.

# 2. THE DISAPPEARING ROUTINE

Jake's previous daily pattern was relatively predictable.

He might spend time in his room.

He might visit the cafeteria.

He might sit with a trusted resident, read, or drink tea.

He might seek out Aries.

That pattern has largely disappeared.

Jake now changes his location frequently.

He has been observed in corridors that he previously rarely used, quiet service areas, unused rooms, and outdoor sections of the Blacksite.

He does not consistently return to his assigned room to sleep.

Sometimes he remains elsewhere for the night.

Sometimes he cannot be found at the location where personnel expected him to be.

The changes are not random in their apparent purpose.

They repeatedly coincide with the possibility that Aries might approach.

If Aries is expected in one area, Jake may leave.

If Aries changes his route, Jake may move again.

If a familiar location becomes associated with Aries's presence, Jake may stop using it.

The result is a pattern in which Jake's whereabouts are increasingly difficult to predict.

### C.O.R.E. interpretation

Jake appears to believe that being difficult to locate is safer than being accessible.

He cannot guarantee that another person will not approach him.

He cannot control who enters a room.

He cannot know whether a familiar face is genuine merely by looking at it.

But he can attempt to control where he is.

He can leave before someone arrives.

He can avoid places where Aries is expected.

He can stop sleeping in the room where people know to look for him.

And if he remains somewhere nobody would reasonably expect him to be, he may feel less likely to encounter the person who frightens him.

This is an interpretation of the observed behavior, not a confirmed statement of Jake's reasoning.

Nevertheless, it is consistent with his repeated attempts to avoid Aries.

**Jake is not merely seeking solitude. He appears to be trying to make himself difficult to find.**

# 3. OUTDOOR EXPOSURE

The most concerning example occurred during a period of severe cold.

Jake was found outside the main residential area after remaining outdoors for an extended period.

He had not returned to his assigned room.

He had not accepted an offer to relocate to a nearby indoor area.

He had remained in the cold despite his compromised physical condition.

The external temperature and precise duration of exposure are being reconstructed from available records.

By the time personnel located him, his condition warranted immediate assessment.

Jake had already been suffering from reduced stamina and had sustained serious blood loss during the previous incidents.

Prolonged cold exposure introduces additional risks, particularly if he has not been eating, drinking, or sleeping adequately.

Personnel approached him carefully.

They did not surround him.

They did not immediately call Aries.

When Jake recognized the approaching staff, he attempted to move farther away.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Jake, we're not going to make you go with Aries."

Jake stopped.

His expression remained guarded.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"We want to get you somewhere warm."

Jake shook his head.

The officer paused.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"You don't have to see him."

Jake looked toward the building.

Then back toward the snow.

He did not immediately move.

A second staff member remained at a respectful distance while the first offered him a warm, sheltered location away from Aries.

After a prolonged pause, Jake accepted assistance.

He was brought indoors for assessment and gradual rewarming as clinically appropriate.

He did not ask for Aries.

He did not ask when Aries would return.

He asked only one question.

His voice was so strained that the officer initially struggled to hear him.

**JAKE:**  
"...He's there?"

The officer understood who he meant.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"No. Aries isn't here."

Jake lowered his head.

Only then did he permit the staff member to guide him inside.

### Assessment

Jake's willingness to remain in freezing conditions rather than risk encountering Aries represents a significant escalation.

His avoidance is now strong enough to override his apparent awareness of immediate physical discomfort.

This is not an acceptable long-term arrangement.

However, forcibly returning him to his room or bringing Aries into the interaction would risk reinforcing the association between seeking help and losing control over his surroundings.

The appropriate response is to provide warmth, medical attention, and a safe location where Aries will not unexpectedly appear.

# 4. FOOD REFUSAL

Jake has not eaten for an extended period.

The exact duration remains under verification.

This is particularly concerning given his recent blood loss, residual injuries, and reduced physical stamina.

The reason for his food refusal has not been conclusively established.

It may be related to distress, exhaustion, disrupted routine, pain, or a combination of factors.

Personnel must not assume that he is refusing food as a deliberate attempt to harm himself.

They must also not assume that the refusal will resolve once he feels calmer.

The medical team is responsible for assessing hydration, nutritional status, swallowing safety, and the need for further intervention.

Given his neck injury, the safety of swallowing must be evaluated rather than presumed.

### Familiar food protocol

Jake has historically responded well to familiar food prepared by people he trusts.

Bao's cooking has been particularly significant to him.

However, food must not become a negotiation.

Personnel must not tell Jake that he can see a particular caregiver only after eating.

They must not threaten to summon Aries if he refuses.

They must not imply that eating will prove he is recovering.

Instead, offer small, medically appropriate options in a quiet environment.

If Jake prefers tea, it may be offered when clinically appropriate, with consideration of hydration and the suitability of the beverage.

Matcha remains a known comfort preference, but it must not be treated as a substitute for adequate nutrition or medical care.

If Jake refuses, record the refusal and continue the medical assessment.

**Jake does not have to eat to earn the right to feel safe.**

At the same time, his physical deterioration cannot be ignored simply because coercion would be harmful.

Care must remain patient without becoming passive.

---

# 5. THE ROOM HE NO LONGER SLEEPS IN

Jake's bedroom was previously one of the few places where he could retreat without needing to interact with anyone.

He could sleep there.

He could read.

He could keep his teddy bear close.

He could expect the room to remain familiar.

Following the attacks, that expectation has deteriorated.

Jake has repeatedly avoided sleeping in his assigned room.

He may remain elsewhere until he becomes exhausted.

He may fall asleep in a quiet corner rather than return to his bed.

He may select a location based on the likelihood that Aries will not be present.

The significance of this change should not be underestimated.

A room that once offered privacy now appears to carry an additional association with danger.

The latest attack occurred while Jake was sleeping.

He was not actively confronting an intruder.

He was not knowingly taking a risk.

He was asleep in a place where he should have been able to rest.

C.O.R.E. cannot reasonably expect him to return to the same room and sleep normally simply because the immediate crime scene has been secured.

### Temporary accommodation directive

Jake is to be offered a different, secured sleeping location if he prefers it.

The location must be:

- medically appropriate;
    
- warm;
    
- accessible to designated caregivers;
    
- secured against unauthorized entry;
    
- equipped to accommodate his teddy bear safely;
    
- and arranged so that Aries will not enter unexpectedly.
    

Jake should be told who has access to the room and how he can request assistance.

Where feasible, he should have meaningful control over the door's accessibility, subject to necessary security and medical safeguards.

The objective is not to hide Jake indefinitely.

It is to provide a place where he can sleep without having to remain alert for someone he fears.

# 6. ARIES AKANA — THE CONSEQUENCES OF IMPERSONATION

The psychological consequences for Aries are also severe.

He is being avoided by the younger brother he loves.

Jake is not merely reluctant to speak to him.

He actively changes his behavior to reduce the possibility of encountering him.

He has remained outside in dangerous conditions.

He has stopped sleeping in his usual room.

He has become difficult to locate.

He has not been eating adequately.

And when asked whether he is afraid of Aries, his behavior strongly indicates that he is.

Aries understands why.

Someone deliberately used his appearance to approach Jake.

That person exploited the trust Jake had placed in his older brother.

The attacker then inflicted severe injuries.

The result is that Jake now reacts to Aries's presence as though it might precede another assault.

For Aries, this creates an unbearable contradiction.

He wants to approach Jake because Jake is injured.

Jake fears his approach because he is injured.

Aries wants to reassure him.

Jake cannot currently accept reassurance from him.

Aries wants to be present.

Jake is trying to ensure that he is not present.

The temptation to resolve this through a direct conversation must be resisted.

Aries cannot make Jake understand that he is safe by insisting on being near him.

He cannot prove his identity through affection that Jake is currently frightened to receive.

He cannot repair the damage by asking Jake to remember their previous relationship.

**The person who impersonated Aries has taken something from both brothers: Jake's ability to feel safe around him, and Aries's ability to comfort Jake without frightening him.**

That harm is real even though Aries is not established to be the perpetrator.

# 8. UNPREDICTABILITY AS A SURVIVAL STRATEGY

C.O.R.E. has identified a concerning pattern in Jake's movements.

He appears increasingly reluctant to establish predictable habits.

He changes locations.

He avoids his room.

He chooses places where Aries is unlikely to look.

He may remain somewhere uncomfortable if it offers greater certainty that he will not encounter his brother.

This behavior may be understood as an attempt to regain control after repeated experiences in which Jake was unable to predict what would happen to him.

The previous attacker controlled the encounter through deception.

The subsequent injuries occurred while Jake was sleeping.

The recent confinement deprived him of control over his own movements.

In each case, Jake was unable to prevent the situation from unfolding.

His current response appears to involve controlling the one factor he can influence:

Where he is.

By remaining unpredictable, Jake may believe he reduces the possibility of another unwanted encounter.

However, the strategy introduces additional risks.

If Jake's location becomes difficult to establish, personnel may not know whether he is resting safely or experiencing a medical emergency.

If he avoids his room, he may sleep in an unsuitable environment.

If he avoids familiar caregivers, he may delay seeking help.

If he remains outdoors, he may expose himself to dangerous cold.

And if he continues refusing food, his physical condition may deteriorate further.

C.O.R.E. must therefore distinguish between two objectives:

**Preventing Jake from being harmed.**

**Preventing Jake from feeling that safety requires him to disappear.**

The first requires active supervision.

The second requires predictable, respectful care.

Neither can be achieved through unrestricted surveillance or indiscriminate pursuit.

Personnel must know where Jake is for medical safety, but they must not turn every movement into a confrontation.

A designated caregiver should maintain contact through calm, non-intrusive means.

Jake should be offered a safe location and told who can access it.

He should not be required to disclose his emotional state every time his whereabouts are confirmed.

The objective is to make safety available without making Jake feel hunted.

# 9. SECURITY AND RESIDENTIAL SAFEGUARDS

The previous incidents have demonstrated that ordinary assumptions about residential security are insufficient.

The following safeguards are recommended:

1. Restrict access to Jake's temporary accommodation to authorized personnel.
    
2. Review door access records and surveillance for every relevant incident.
    
3. Ensure that Jake's sleeping location is known to a designated caregiver without broadcasting it unnecessarily.
    
4. Establish a reliable way for Jake to request assistance without needing to approach Aries.
    
5. Ensure that any caregiver entering his room identifies themselves before approaching.
    
6. Avoid unexpected physical contact.
    
7. Provide a clear procedure for responding if Jake relocates or cannot be found.
    
8. Do not allow security personnel to interpret his avoidance as misconduct.
    
9. Do not force him to return to a location he currently fears without medical or immediate safety necessity.
    
10. Investigate the perpetrator independently of Jake's relationship with Aries.
    

If Jake's whereabouts become unknown, the missing-resident protocol must be activated.

If he is located outdoors in dangerous conditions, personnel should prioritize medical safety and offer an alternative environment without using Aries as the means of retrieving him.

His need for privacy must be respected.

His immediate safety must also be maintained.

---

# 10. MEDICAL AND WELFARE PRIORITIES

Jake's current condition requires coordinated intervention.

The following issues must be addressed concurrently.

### Physical recovery

Continue monitoring the neck wound, chest injuries, thigh injury, and resulting scars.

Assess his voice, breathing, swallowing, pain, and general physiological stability.

### Nutrition and hydration

Establish how long Jake has gone without eating and whether he is receiving adequate fluids.

Assess swallowing safety before offering oral intake.

Use familiar foods and drinks when appropriate, without coercion.

### Sleep

Provide a secure sleeping location that does not require him to return to his previous room.

Do not leave him alone simply because he has fallen asleep.

### Cold exposure

Prevent further prolonged exposure to snow and freezing conditions.

Offer warmth and medical assessment without making acceptance conditional on seeing Aries.

### Psychological support

Use familiar, trusted caregivers.

Do not force verbal explanations.

Do not insist on contact with Aries.

Allow Jake to communicate through gestures or writing if speaking is difficult.

### Supervision

Maintain explicit caregiver handovers.

Do not assume that another resident is watching him.

Do not use his unpredictability as a reason to abandon supervision.

### Security investigation

Identify the attacker and establish how the residence was accessed.

Do not allow the investigation to interfere unnecessarily with Jake's recovery.

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's condition is no longer adequately described as a temporary fear response following an assault.

His fear is affecting where he sleeps, where he goes, what he eats, whether he accepts assistance, and how he responds to the people closest to him.

His voice remains strained.

His body carries numerous scars.

He has not eaten for an extended period.

He has remained outside in dangerous cold to avoid his brother.

He changes his location so that his brother will not find him.

And he has become increasingly difficult to care for because the person whose presence once offered comfort now triggers fear.

This is a serious deterioration in both physical welfare and psychological security.

C.O.R.E. must not interpret the behavior as deliberate defiance.

Jake is not refusing to cooperate with his recovery.

He appears to be attempting to protect himself using the limited means available to him.

The problem is that those means are becoming dangerous.

He cannot safely live outdoors.

He cannot indefinitely avoid sleep.

He cannot recover from serious injuries without appropriate medical care.

And he cannot be expected to restore trust in Aries through willpower alone.

The intervention must address the conditions producing the behavior rather than simply suppressing the behavior itself.

Jake needs a safe place where he can sleep.

He needs appropriate medical care.

He needs reliable supervision.

He needs food and fluids that he can safely tolerate.

He needs to know that he can refuse contact with Aries without losing access to warmth, companionship, or protection.

And he needs the investigation to establish who attacked him.

Aries, meanwhile, must be supported without being given control over Jake's recovery.

He is permitted to grieve the current state of their relationship.

He is permitted to feel frightened by the possibility that Jake may continue avoiding him.

He is permitted to love his brother.

He is not permitted to demand that Jake resolve those feelings for him.

The possibility of reconciliation remains open.

Its timing belongs to Jake's recovery, not to the adults' desire to restore normality.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Imagine being six years old.

Someone takes the face of the person you trust most.

They approach you.

You let them close.

And then they hurt you.

You survive.

You try to understand what happened.

You try to believe that your brother is still your brother.

Then you are hurt again.

Your voice no longer sounds the way it used to.

Your body carries scars you did not have before.

Your room no longer feels safe.

And the person you want to run toward is the person whose face now makes you want to run away.

So you stop sleeping in your room.

You stop going where people expect you to be.

You stop eating.

You find somewhere outside, where the snow falls and the cold settles into your clothes, because being cold feels preferable to seeing the face you are afraid of.

You do not explain yourself.

Perhaps you cannot find the words.

Perhaps speaking hurts.

Perhaps you are afraid that if someone knows where you are, they will bring him to you.

So you make yourself unpredictable.

You make yourself difficult to find.

You try to become someone nobody can surprise again.

And somewhere inside SAFEHOUSE, Aries is trying to understand how his little brother can love him and still be terrified of him.

The cruelest part is that neither brother chose this.

Jake did not choose to be attacked.

Aries did not choose to have his appearance used as a weapon.

But Jake is the one who has to live with the fear.

And Aries is the one who has to watch that fear turn him into a stranger.

C.O.R.E. cannot promise that Jake will trust Aries again soon.

It cannot promise that the scars will disappear.

It cannot promise that Jake will return to his room tomorrow, or eat dinner tonight, or stop checking the doorway whenever someone approaches.

What it can do is refuse to make his recovery another situation in which he has no control.

It can give him a room he feels safe entering.

It can ensure that someone stays nearby.

It can let him refuse a visit.

It can bring him warm tea without demanding an explanation.

It can find the person who hurt him.

And it can make certain that Jake never has to choose between **being safe from the person he fears and receiving the care he needs.**

Because Jake should not have to freeze outside to avoid his brother.

He should not have to starve to remain out of reach.

He should not have to disappear to feel safe.

He is six years old.

He has already survived more than enough.
