# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### MEDICAL RESEARCH & TREATMENT EXECUTIVE

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-MED-4C-JAKE-319  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — RESIDENT WELFARE / PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Jake — Persistent Emotional Distress Following Grassland Incident  
**OBSERVATION PERIOD:** Several months

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake remains emotionally distressed.

Several months have passed since his recovery from the Grassland incident.

His physical condition has stabilized considerably.

His emotional condition has not.

Jake continues to demonstrate:

- persistent sadness;
    
- emotional fragility;
    
- social withdrawal;
    
- prolonged periods of isolation;
    
- frequent crying when alone;
    
- difficulty expressing distress directly;
    
- forced positive affect when approached by people he trusts;
    
- heightened sensitivity to perceived rejection;
    
- and an apparent reluctance to allow others to see him upset.
    

This behavior has remained remarkably consistent.

Jake is not constantly visibly distressed.

He can read.

He can eat.

He can participate in activities.

He can speak.

He can interact with his friends.

He can even laugh occasionally.

However, when left alone for sufficiently long periods, Jake frequently returns to the same behavior:

> **He sits somewhere quiet and cries.**

---

# 2. IMPORTANT CLARIFICATION

Jake does **not** have a birthday.

There is no known date of birth.

He was not born through a conventional biological process.

His earliest known existence is associated with his appearance as a human child after falling from the sky.

There is therefore no meaningful biological birthday available to record.

More importantly:

> **Jake does not want one.**

Personnel are instructed not to pressure him into celebrating a birthday.

Do not attempt to assign him an arbitrary date.

Do not organize surprise birthday celebrations.

Do not repeatedly ask when his birthday is.

Do not attempt to convince him that he "deserves a birthday."

His preference is sufficient.

---

# 3. CURRENT EMOTIONAL STATE

Jake should currently be considered:

> **Emotionally fragile.**

This does not mean that he is incapable of normal interaction.

It means that relatively minor interpersonal events can carry considerably more emotional weight for him than personnel may expect.

Jake is a star.

He possesses an existence vastly beyond that of an ordinary child.

His stellar nature does not make him emotionally mature.

His physical manifestation remains that of a six-year-old child.

His understanding of relationships, abandonment, trust, and rejection therefore remains fundamentally childlike.

This distinction must be respected.

---

# 4. THE CONTRADICTION

Jake's circumstances create an unusual psychological contradiction.

He is, in one sense, an extraordinarily powerful celestial entity.

He has existed as a literal star.

He possesses capabilities far beyond conventional human limits.

He has survived dimensional phenomena that would be impossible for an ordinary child.

Yet he can still become devastated by something as simple as:

> **“Nobody came when I needed them.”**

His power does not protect him from loneliness.

His stellar nature does not make rejection hurt less.

His abilities do not automatically provide emotional resilience.

C.O.R.E. therefore considers it inappropriate to assess Jake's emotional state according to his anomalous capabilities.

---

# 5. ISOLATION PATTERN

Jake frequently seeks isolated locations.

Common locations include:

- his room;
    
- quiet hallways;
    
- balconies;
    
- unused common areas;
    
- secluded sections of the Blacksite;
    
- and locations where he can observe others without being immediately noticed.
    

When alone, Jake may sit for extended periods.

His usual posture is consistent.

He sits with his knees drawn toward his chest.

His arms wrap around his legs.

His head lowers.

His halo becomes noticeably less expressive.

His stellar glow may diminish.

His teddy bear is usually held against his chest.

And he cries.

Quietly.

---

# 6. THE FORCED SMILE

One of the more concerning behavioral patterns is what personnel have informally designated:

> **THE FORCED SMILE.**

Jake can often be heard crying shortly before another resident approaches.

The moment he notices them, his behavior changes.

He wipes his face.

Straightens his posture.

Looks toward the approaching person.

And smiles.

The smile is often immediate.

It is also often visibly unnatural.

His voice may become lighter.

He may say:

> “Hi.”

or:

> “I'm okay.”

or:

> “Nothing happened.”

Sometimes he asks the other person about their day.

Sometimes he attempts to joke.

The transition can occur within seconds.

---

# 7. OBSERVED EXAMPLE

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE CORRIDOR

Jake was sitting alone against a wall.

He was crying quietly.

His teddy bear was held against his chest.

Approximately thirty seconds later, footsteps were detected.

Jake immediately wiped his face.

His expression changed.

By the time Neri appeared around the corner, Jake was smiling.

> **NERI:**  
> “Hi.”

> **JAKE:**  
> “Hi.”

Neri looked at him.

She did not speak.

Jake continued smiling.

> **JAKE:**  
> “What's up?”

Neri stared at him for several seconds.

Jake's smile weakened.

She sat beside him.

Neither spoke.

Eventually, Jake's smile disappeared.

He leaned against her.

He did not resume crying.

---

# 8. WHY PERSONNEL SHOULD NOT ASK

Personnel have repeatedly asked whether Jake is sad.

The question is understandable.

It is also frequently ineffective.

Jake's typical responses include:

> “I'm okay.”

> “Nothing.”

> “I'm just tired.”

> “It's fine.”

These answers should not automatically be treated as accurate assessments of his emotional state.

However, personnel are also instructed not to repeatedly interrogate him.

In particular, avoid asking:

> **“Why are you sad?”**

> **“What happened?”**

> **“Why are you crying?”**

> **“Tell me what's wrong.”**

> **“You can tell me.”**

The reason is simple.

Jake does not currently appear to want to explain it.

That preference should be respected.

---

# 9. PASSIVE SUPPORT

The recommended approach is presence without pressure.

A resident may:

- sit beside him;
    
- bring him food;
    
- read nearby;
    
- allow him to hold their hand;
    
- sit quietly;
    
- offer his teddy bear if he has put it aside;
    
- or simply remain nearby.
    

Conversation is optional.

Jake does not need to provide an explanation in exchange for companionship.

He does not need to justify being sad.

He does not need to perform happiness for other people.

---

# 10. THE PEOPLE HE TRUSTS

Jake's behavior changes around people he considers family.

Bao remains one of the strongest examples.

Neri remains another.

Nora and Aeterna also occupy particularly important positions in his social environment.

Jane, Anby, and other SAFEHOUSE residents have established varying degrees of trust with him.

The presence of trusted people can temporarily improve Jake's mood.

This should not be interpreted as evidence that the underlying distress has resolved.

Jake may laugh with someone for an hour.

He may then return to his room and cry afterward.

Both observations can be true.

---

# 11. SOCIAL MASKING

Jake appears to have developed an increasingly deliberate habit of hiding distress from others.

This may be partially motivated by his previous experiences of being overlooked.

His apparent reasoning is difficult to establish.

C.O.R.E. therefore avoids presenting a definitive psychological explanation.

However, the behavioral pattern is clear:

> **When alone, Jake permits himself to be visibly sad.**

> **When someone he cares about arrives, he attempts to look happy.**

This creates the possibility that residents may underestimate the severity of his ongoing distress.

---

# 12. THE GRASSLAND

The Grassland incident remains the temporal point associated with the beginning of this prolonged behavioral change.

Jake spent an extended period within the Grassland in an extremely distressed state.

He was found there sitting alone.

He later spoke about being forgotten.

He questioned why everyone had continued living without him.

He eventually returned to SAFEHOUSE.

His relationships were restored.

His memories returned.

His teddy bear was returned.

He was physically present again.

None of these developments erased what happened.

---

# 13. RESIDUAL EFFECT

C.O.R.E. does not currently consider Jake's emotional condition a temporary reaction that can simply be waited out.

Months have passed.

The crying continues.

The isolation continues.

The forced smiling continues.

The sensitivity remains.

The underlying sadness remains.

Jake has returned physically.

He has returned socially.

He has not yet returned emotionally to the person he was before the Grassland incident.

---

# 14. NO BIRTHDAY INTERVENTION

Personnel are specifically reminded that Jake's lack of a birthday should not be treated as a psychological problem requiring correction.

He does not want one.

That is enough.

Do not attempt to create a birthday for him as a therapeutic exercise.

Do not use a birthday celebration to demonstrate that he is loved.

Do not surprise him with a birthday party.

Jake does not need to be convinced that he is allowed to exist through a celebration he has explicitly rejected.

---

# 15. RESIDENT GUIDANCE

SAFEHOUSE residents are encouraged to treat Jake normally.

Do not constantly monitor him.

Do not hover.

Do not behave as though he is permanently fragile.

Do not pretend that nothing happened.

Instead:

> **Be there.**

If he wants to talk, listen.

If he wants to play, play.

If he wants to read, let him read.

If he wants to sit silently, sit silently.

If he cries, he does not need to be immediately fixed.

Sometimes the appropriate response is simply:

> **“I'm here.”**

---

# 16. MEDICAL ESCALATION

Emotional distress should not automatically be treated as a medical emergency.

However, personnel should escalate when Jake demonstrates significant functional deterioration, including:

- prolonged refusal of food or water;
    
- inability to perform basic activities;
    
- severe withdrawal beyond his established baseline;
    
- unusual changes in stellar output;
    
- loss of consciousness;
    
- abnormal teddy-bear cardiac activity;
    
- severe self-neglect;
    
- or statements indicating immediate danger.
    

The distinction between:

> **“Jake is sad.”**

and

> **“Jake is medically unsafe.”**

must be maintained.

---

# 17. LONG-TERM CONSIDERATION

There is no current timetable for Jake to "get over" the Grassland incident.

C.O.R.E. will not impose one.

The objective is not to restore Jake to an earlier version of himself through pressure.

It is to provide a stable environment in which he can gradually develop enough trust to stop hiding his distress.

This may take time.

Possibly considerable time.

---

# 18. FINAL OBSERVATION

Jake remains a child.

That fact becomes easy to forget.

His stellar nature makes him extraordinary.

His abilities make him dangerous under certain circumstances.

His history makes him anomalous.

His existence may span dimensions and astronomical distances.

None of that changes the fact that he is still six.

A six-year-old can understand loneliness.

A six-year-old can understand being forgotten.

A six-year-old can understand that someone did not come when they were needed.

And a six-year-old can smile because they do not want the people they love to know that they are still hurting.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Sometimes personnel find Jake sitting alone.

He is usually quiet.

His knees are against his chest.

His arms are wrapped around them.

His teddy bear is held close.

The room is dim.

The glow from his body is faint.

And he cries.

Then footsteps approach.

Jake hears them.

He wipes his eyes.

He sits up.

He smiles.

> **“Hi.”**

And for a little while, he pretends everything is okay.

Nobody is required to make him stop being sad.

Nobody is required to make him explain why.

They only need to remember that the smile is not necessarily proof that the sadness is gone.

Because Jake is a star.

But he is also a child.

And being made of starlight does not make a child immune to heartbreak.
