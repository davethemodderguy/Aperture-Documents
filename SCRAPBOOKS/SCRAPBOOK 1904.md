**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Residential Complex, Floor 7  
**PRIORITY:** HIGH

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Further observation has established that Sora's distress increases the longer he remains unable to communicate.

This is not simply frustration over losing a familiar possession.

Sora is mute due to severed vocal cords. He cannot speak, even when frightened, injured, or desperate to make himself understood. His original whiteboard, previously provided by Jake, became his primary means of written communication.

Since losing that board, Sora has refused every replacement offered to him.

This refusal has persisted even when the replacement was made to resemble the original and even when it carried Jake's signature on the rear.

**The signature made no difference.**

Sora did not accept the replacement. He did not begin writing on it. He continued searching for his original board.

The distinction is important. Sora is not merely requesting a writing surface. He is seeking the specific object he lost.

As time passes without it, his inability to communicate appears to produce progressively greater distress.

He initially attempts to manage without writing. He gestures, points, and tries to attract attention. When these methods fail to convey what he means, his behavior changes.

He becomes restless.

He repeats gestures.

He searches for his board.

He begins to panic.

Because Sora also experiences severe amnesia, the situation is especially difficult to resolve. He may forget that someone has already offered assistance, forget what he was trying to communicate, or become unable to explain why he is distressed.

The result is a child who can recognize that something is terribly wrong but cannot reliably tell anyone what it is.

---

## 2. THE IMPORTANCE OF THE ORIGINAL BOARD

Sora's original whiteboard was given to him by Jake after he moved into SAFEHOUSE Floor 7.

He carried it like a satchel.

It accompanied him throughout the residential complex and became an established part of his daily routine.

The rear of the board bears Jake's signature.

That signature is not the only reason Sora values the object. Its familiarity, history, and association with someone he trusts appear to be significant.

Following its loss, Sora was offered replacement boards.

He rejected them.

A subsequent replacement was prepared with Jake's signature on the rear, deliberately reproducing one of the original board's distinguishing features.

Sora still rejected it.

He did not appear reassured by the signature.

He did not treat the replacement as equivalent.

He continued looking for the original.

C.O.R.E. recognizes that the replacement may be functionally identical, but functional equivalence does not establish personal equivalence.

**Sora wants his board back. He does not want another board pretending to be it.**

Staff are prohibited from repeatedly presenting replacements as though Sora has simply failed to understand the offer.

He understands that they are replacements.

He does not want them.

## 3. INCIDENT A — THE FIRST ATTEMPT

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7, common room  
**Time:** 10:18

Sora was sitting beside Jake.

He had been attempting to communicate something through hand gestures.

Jake watched him carefully.

**JAKE:** Do you want something?

Sora pointed toward the corridor.

**JAKE:** Outside?

Sora shook his head.

He pointed toward the table, then made a small rectangular shape with his hands.

Jake looked at the table.

**JAKE:** Your board?

Sora nodded quickly.

Jake looked around.

**JAKE:** I haven't found it yet.

Sora repeated the gesture.

**JAKE:** I know. We're still looking.

Sora pointed toward the corridor again.

**JAKE:** Do you want me to help you search?

Sora nodded.

Jake stood up.

**JAKE:** Okay! Let's go!

Sora followed him.

They checked his den, the common room, and several nearby locations.

Nothing.

When they returned, a staff member approached with the replacement whiteboard.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Sora, we have something for you.

Sora looked at it.

The staff member turned it around, showing the signature on the rear.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Look. Jake signed it for you.

Jake leaned closer.

**JAKE:** See? I signed it!

Sora stared at the board.

For a moment, he did not move.

Then he shook his head.

**JAKE:** You don't want it?

Sora shook his head again.

The staff member tried to explain.

**STAFF MEMBER:** It works exactly like your old one. You can write on it, draw on it, carry it—

Sora turned away.

He returned to his den.

Jake followed, looking confused.

**JAKE:** But I signed it.

Sora sat inside his den and looked toward the entrance.

Jake lowered the replacement board.

**JAKE:** Oh.

He looked at the signature.

**JAKE:** You still want the old one.

Sora nodded.

**JAKE:** Okay. I'm sorry.

Jake placed the replacement nearby without putting it inside the den.

Sora did not touch it.

### Assessment

Sora's rejection was consistent and deliberate.

The presence of Jake's signature did not alter his response.

Personnel must not interpret this as rejection of Jake or the relationship represented by the signature.

Sora is rejecting the replacement object, not the person who signed it.

## 4. INCIDENT B — WHEN GESTURES AREN'T ENOUGH

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7, residential corridor  
**Time:** 14:52

Sora approached a staff member who was carrying supplies.

He pointed toward the maintenance storage area.

**STAFF MEMBER:** What is it, Sora?

Sora pointed again.

The staff member looked toward the storage area.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Do you need something from there?

Sora nodded.

**STAFF MEMBER:** What do you need?

Sora pointed toward the floor, then made a rectangular shape with his hands.

**STAFF MEMBER:** A box?

Sora shook his head.

He repeated the gesture, more urgently.

**STAFF MEMBER:** A book?

Another shake.

Sora began moving his hands more quickly.

The staff member tried several possibilities.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Your blanket?

No.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Your teddy bear?

No.

Sora pointed toward the storage area again.

His breathing became visibly faster.

He began searching his pockets and looking around the corridor.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Sora, slow down. It's okay.

Sora shook his head.

He pointed toward his den, then toward the storage area, then toward the floor.

His gestures became increasingly disorganized.

A second staff member arrived.

**SECOND STAFF MEMBER:** What's happening?

**STAFF MEMBER:** I can't figure out what he's asking for.

Sora looked between them.

He tried again.

Nothing.

His hands began trembling.

He covered his face.

The second staff member recognized the signs of escalating distress.

**SECOND STAFF MEMBER:** Stop asking him questions for a moment.

The first staff member stepped back.

**SECOND STAFF MEMBER:** Sora, you're safe. We aren't upset with you.

Sora lowered his hands.

He looked at them, then pointed toward the storage area one more time.

The staff members accompanied him.

They did not discover what he had originally intended to request.

After several minutes, Sora returned to his den.

He remained visibly distressed.

### Assessment

The incident demonstrates that Sora's gestures are not a universally reliable substitute for writing.

When others misunderstand him, he may repeatedly attempt to clarify his meaning until he becomes overwhelmed.

The resulting distress appears to be intensified by his inability to explain what he needs.

Staff should avoid turning communication attempts into guessing games.

If the intended message cannot be established, personnel should acknowledge the difficulty, reduce questioning, and offer patient assistance.

## 5. INCIDENT C — THE PANIC EPISODE

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7, Sora's den  
**Time:** 19:36

Mia found Sora searching through his belongings.

He had already checked the same small storage space several times.

His blanket had been moved, and several personal items were arranged on the floor.

**MIA:** Sora?

He continued searching.

**MIA:** What are you looking for?

Sora pointed toward the empty space where he normally kept his original board.

Mia understood immediately.

**MIA:** Your whiteboard?

Sora nodded.

**MIA:** It's still missing.

Sora froze.

Mia regretted the wording almost immediately.

**MIA:** I mean... we haven't found it yet.

Sora began searching again.

He checked beneath his blanket.

Then beside his bedding.

Then behind the entrance to his den.

**MIA:** Sora, wait.

He did not stop.

Mia crouched beside him.

**MIA:** We can keep looking tomorrow.

Sora shook his head.

He pointed toward the empty space again.

His breathing became rapid.

Mia reached out, then stopped before touching him.

**MIA:** Do you want me to help?

Sora nodded repeatedly.

**MIA:** Okay. I'll help.

She searched beside him.

After several minutes, they found nothing.

Sora began trembling.

He looked at Mia, opened his mouth as though attempting to make a sound, and produced none.

His hands moved desperately.

He pointed at the empty space, then at himself, then toward Mia.

The gestures did not form a clear message.

Mia's expression changed.

**MIA:** Sora...

He pressed both hands against his chest.

His eyes filled with tears.

Mia moved closer but did not force physical contact.

**MIA:** You don't have to explain it right now.

Sora shook his head.

He pointed toward the empty space again.

**MIA:** I know. I know you want it back.

Sora began crying harder.

Mia sat beside him.

**MIA:** I'm here. You don't have to keep trying to show me.

Sora eventually curled up inside his den.

Mia remained nearby until Bao arrived.

### Assessment

This incident represents a significant escalation from ordinary frustration.

Sora appeared increasingly distressed as his attempts to communicate failed. His inability to produce speech left him with no immediate way to clarify his needs.

The gesture of opening his mouth without producing sound was particularly concerning.

Staff must understand that Sora's silence is not an absence of communication needs.

He has things he wants to say.

He simply cannot say them aloud.

## 6. INCIDENT D — JAKE REALIZES THE PROBLEM

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7, common room  
**Time:** 20:11

Jake found Mia sitting outside Sora's den.

Sora was inside, resting beneath his blanket.

The replacement whiteboard remained untouched nearby.

**JAKE:** Is Sora sleeping?

**MIA:** I think so.

Jake looked at the replacement.

**JAKE:** He didn't use it?

Mia shook her head.

**MIA:** No.

**JAKE:** But it has my signature.

**MIA:** I know.

Jake picked it up and looked at the rear.

**JAKE:** I signed it carefully.

Mia remained quiet.

Jake looked toward Sora's den.

**JAKE:** Why doesn't he want it?

**MIA:** He wants his own.

**JAKE:** But this one is the same.

**MIA:** It's not his.

Jake looked down.

**JAKE:** Oh.

He placed the board back where it had been.

After a moment, he spoke again.

**JAKE:** When Sora can't write, he gets scared.

**MIA:** Yes.

**JAKE:** And when he gets scared, he can't tell us why.

Mia nodded.

Jake looked toward the den.

**JAKE:** That's really bad.

**MIA:** I know.

Jake sat beside her.

**JAKE:** I want to find it.

**MIA:** We'll keep looking.

**JAKE:** Not because he has to use it.

Mia looked at him.

**JAKE:** Because it's his.

Mia gave a small nod.

**MIA:** Exactly.

### Assessment

Jake demonstrated an increasingly accurate understanding of the problem.

His signature does not make the replacement acceptable to Sora. The original board has personal significance beyond its practical function.

Jake's desire to recover it is understandable, but staff must ensure he does not begin treating the search as his personal responsibility.

He is six years old.

Finding the board is an adult welfare and recovery responsibility, with Jake's participation permitted only if he wishes.

## 7. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. identifies the following observed pattern:

<list-item>**Misunderstanding.** The recipient cannot reliably determine what he means.</list-item>  
<list-item>**Repeated clarification.** Sora attempts to communicate again, often with increasing urgency.</list-item>  
<list-item>**Loss of control.** His gestures become less organized, and visible distress increases.</list-item>

This pattern is not necessarily present during every interaction. However, it has been observed often enough to require a formal response.

Sora's severe amnesia may further complicate these episodes. He may lose track of the original request while attempting to communicate it, or forget that someone is already helping him.

This can create a particularly distressing situation: Sora may be unable to explain what he needs and may also lose track of what he was trying to explain.

C.O.R.E. cannot currently determine whether his panic is primarily caused by the loss of the original board, the inability to communicate, the uncertainty of being misunderstood, or a combination of these factors.

All remain relevant welfare concerns.

## 8. MANDATORY SUPPORT DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

**A. Do not force replacement acceptance.**

No replacement whiteboard, including one bearing Jake's signature, is to be presented as an adequate substitute for the original.

Staff may keep an emergency communication aid accessible, but Sora must not be pressured to use it.

**B. Reduce communication pressure.**

When Sora becomes distressed, staff must stop repeatedly asking him to explain himself. Give him time, maintain a calm presence, and avoid turning the interaction into an interrogation.

**C. Offer simple alternatives without demanding a response.**

Staff may offer familiar objects, point to possible locations, or demonstrate available options. These are attempts to assist, not tests of Sora's ability to communicate.

**D. Do not mistake silence for consent.**

Sora's inability to answer does not indicate agreement, understanding, or lack of distress.

**E. Protect his dignity.**

No employee may imitate his gestures, mock his communication difficulties, or deliberately misunderstand him for entertainment.

Any such conduct will be treated as a serious welfare violation.

**F. Continue the search for the original board.**

The search must include relevant residential areas, maintenance spaces, furniture storage, and locations accessed during the period surrounding its disappearance.

The board's rear signature must be preserved.

**G. Provide support after panic episodes.**

Sora should not be forced to immediately resume activities or explain what happened. Allow him to settle in a familiar environment.

Jake and Mia may offer companionship, but neither is responsible for regulating Sora's distress.

## 9. CLOSING NOTE — HE HAS SOMETHING TO SAY

Sora is not quiet because he has nothing to say.

He is quiet because he cannot speak.

For a while, his whiteboard gave him a way around that silence. He could write a name, ask a question, explain that something hurt, or tell someone that he wanted them to stay.

It was not a perfect solution. His memory could still fail him. He could still struggle to find the right words.

But it gave him a way to try.

Now it is gone.

And every time someone asks him what he needs, he is reminded that he cannot simply answer.

He can point.

He can gesture.

He can try again.

But sometimes people misunderstand. Sometimes they guess incorrectly. Sometimes he cannot remember how to explain the thing he was trying to explain in the first place.

Then he panics.

The replacement board has Jake's signature. It has a clean writing surface. It can perform every practical function of the original.

Sora still does not want it.

We must respect that.

Jake understands something important: the board is not valuable merely because it has his signature. It is valuable because it is the board he gave Sora, the one Sora carried everywhere, the one that became familiar enough to feel like part of his everyday life.

A replacement cannot automatically inherit that history.

Until the original is recovered, staff must find ways to communicate with Sora that do not depend on forcing him to accept something he has already rejected.

And when he begins to panic, we must remember what is happening.

He is not being difficult.

He is not throwing a tantrum.

He is a mute child trying desperately to tell someone something, and he cannot make himself understood.

**Do not wait until Sora is crying to take his attempts at communication seriously.**

He has something to say.

We need to make sure he has a way to say it.
