## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora and Mia have had their first major argument since their reunion.

The disagreement escalated beyond an ordinary sibling quarrel.

Mia, who can become extremely volatile when angry, lost control of her temper during the confrontation. In the process, she brought up aspects of Sora's traumatic past and reminded him of experiences associated with some of his deepest psychological pain.

The result was devastating.

Sora was not merely upset by the argument. He was deeply hurt by the fact that the person speaking to him was Mia: his younger sister, the person he had believed lost forever, and someone whose return had given him a renewed sense of happiness and security.

Mia's words struck particularly hard because they came from someone Sora trusted.

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Mia's anger does not excuse the emotional harm she caused. Her own history and emotional difficulties deserve consideration, but they do not invalidate Sora's pain.

Likewise, Sora must not be pressured to forgive her simply because they are siblings.

Their relationship is important.

That is precisely why this incident requires careful intervention.

---

## 2. BACKGROUND — A RELATIONSHIP BUILT AFTER LOSS

Sora and Mia share an unusually painful history.

They were raised together by an elderly woman whom they regarded as their grandmother. They had a home, someone who cared for them, and a childhood in which they could rely on one another.

That life was destroyed.

They witnessed the apparent death of their grandmother. Her son subsequently subjected both children to severe abuse and deprived them of the safety and care they had previously known.

Mia was eventually presumed dead.

Sora survived alone for years, carrying the memory of the people and home he had lost. He eventually reached the Blacksite, but the emotional consequences of his past remained.

When Mia unexpectedly returned, Sora was overwhelmed with happiness.

He had believed his little sister was gone forever.

Their reunion was one of the most significant positive developments in his recovery. Mia's presence noticeably improved his mood, and the two became almost inseparable.

For Sora, Mia was not simply another friend.

She was family he had thought he would never see again.

This context is essential to understanding the severity of the current incident.

---

## 3. INCIDENT — THE ARGUMENT

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7  
**Time:** Undetermined

The disagreement began as a private argument between the two children.

The initial subject of the disagreement has not been established with sufficient certainty.

What is known is that Mia became increasingly angry.

Her usual restraint deteriorated as the confrontation continued. Rather than stopping when the argument became personal, she began bringing Sora's traumatic experiences into it.

She referred to painful parts of his past, including experiences that had left him frightened, vulnerable, and emotionally scarred.

The remarks were particularly damaging because they were not coming from an uninvolved observer.

They came from Mia.

Sora initially attempted to respond.

**SORA:** Mia, don't.

Mia continued arguing.

**SORA:** I don't want to talk about that.

The argument escalated further.

Sora's expression changed. His earlier frustration gave way to visible distress.

**SORA:** Please stop.

Mia did not immediately stop.

The confrontation eventually reached a point where Sora could no longer maintain his composure.

He became quiet.

He stopped trying to argue back.

The silence was not an indication that he had accepted what she said. It was a sign that the conversation had become too painful for him to continue.

The argument ended without resolution.

---

## 4. IMMEDIATE AFTERMATH — THE DAMAGE

Sora withdrew following the confrontation.

His response was noticeably different from his usual behavior during minor disagreements.

He did not simply become annoyed with Mia or seek a little time alone.

He appeared deeply wounded.

The person who had once been his closest remaining connection to his childhood had used that childhood against him during an argument.

This created an especially painful contradiction for Sora.

Mia was someone he loved.

Mia was someone he had searched for.

Mia was someone whose return had made him happy again.

And Mia had reminded him of things he desperately did not want to remember.

C.O.R.E. cannot determine whether Sora's response represents a temporary resurgence of traumatic distress or a more sustained deterioration in his psychological condition.

However, the incident warrants immediate attention.

**The fact that Sora is physically safe does not mean he is emotionally unharmed.**

---

## 5. MIA — ANGER DOES NOT ERASE RESPONSIBILITY

Mia's behavior during the argument is consistent with an established concern: when she becomes sufficiently angry, she can lose control of her reactions.

Her anger appears to overwhelm her ability to consider the consequences of what she says.

This may help explain how the argument escalated.

It does not excuse what happened.

Mia knows at least some of Sora's history. She knows that he lost their grandmother, endured severe abuse, believed Mia had died, and spent years surviving alone.

Those experiences are not ordinary subjects of sibling teasing or arguments.

They are deeply personal wounds.

C.O.R.E. will not characterize Mia as malicious based solely on this incident. There is insufficient evidence to conclude that she intended to cause the full extent of the harm Sora experienced.

Nevertheless, intent and impact are separate matters.

She may not have understood how deeply her words would hurt him.

She still hurt him.

Mia must be given an opportunity to understand the consequences of her actions without being humiliated or condemned as a person.

---

## 6. WELFARE DIRECTIVES

The following measures are now in effect.

### A. Do not force immediate reconciliation

Sora must be allowed to have space from Mia.

Staff must not insist that they sit together, hug, talk, or resolve the disagreement immediately.

Their relationship cannot be repaired through forced proximity.

### B. No further references to Sora's trauma during conflict

Mia must be made aware that Sora's history is not acceptable ammunition during arguments.

The same expectation applies to every resident.

### C. Support both children separately

Sora requires reassurance and a safe environment in which he can process what happened.

Mia also requires support in understanding and regulating her anger.

Neither child should be expected to manage the other's emotional state.

### D. Apologies must be meaningful

If Mia wishes to apologize, she must acknowledge what she said and why it was hurtful.

An apology should not demand immediate forgiveness.

### E. Monitor Sora's condition

Staff should observe changes in his willingness to socialize, communicate, eat, sleep, and participate in ordinary activities.

Given his history of severe withdrawal, significant changes must be reported to the welfare team.

---

## 7. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT — THE PERSON WHO KNOWS WHERE IT HURTS

There is a particular danger in conflicts between people who know each other very well.

They know what makes the other person laugh.

They know what frightens them.

They know which subjects are painful and which memories they would rather leave untouched.

That knowledge can make affection especially meaningful.

It can also make an argument especially destructive.

Mia knows Sora's history because she lived through much of it alongside him. She knows what they lost. She knows what happened after their grandmother was gone.

That shared history should have been something they could rely on.

Instead, during this confrontation, it became the source of Sora's pain.

C.O.R.E. does not consider one argument sufficient to define their entire relationship. Their reunion, affection, and shared experiences remain real.

But neither should those positive memories be used to minimize this incident.

Sora is allowed to be hurt by someone he loves.

Mia is allowed to feel angry, frustrated, and overwhelmed.

Neither of those things gives either child permission to emotionally injure the other.

---

## 8. CLOSING NOTE — WORDS FROM HOME

Sora spent years looking for Mia.

He believed she was dead.

When she finally returned, he cried, hugged her, and told her that he had missed her. He was happy that his little sister was alive.

For a while, the two children seemed almost inseparable.

They had Jake, their games, their meals, their quiet moments together. They had a chance to build something new after everything they had lost.

And then they argued.

That, by itself, would not be unusual. Siblings argue. Children get frustrated. Sometimes they say things they regret.

But there are things that should never be used as weapons.

Sora's childhood was not his fault.

His suffering was not something he chose.

His memories are not something Mia gets to throw at him when she is angry.

Perhaps Mia will eventually understand how deeply she hurt him. Perhaps she will regret what she said. Perhaps Sora will eventually feel comfortable speaking to her again.

C.O.R.E. will not predict when that happens.

For now, the two children need something different.

Mia needs to learn that being angry does not give her permission to hurt someone else's most vulnerable parts.

Sora needs to know that loving his sister does not mean he has to endure every hurt she causes.

And both need to understand that being family does not make apologies unnecessary.

**They survived losing their family once. They should not have to hurt each other to prove that they still belong together.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
