# THE BOY WHO BURNED, AND THE GHOST WHO DIDN'T FORGIVE

### Incident Report: Jake Akana's First Documented Transcendence-Associated Revival

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana sustained a gunshot wound to the neck during an incident at Heaven's Blacksite.

The injury was severe enough that Jake immediately collapsed. Personnel initially had reason to believe that the wound had killed him.

Before medical intervention could be completed, however, **white fire engulfed Jake's body.**

The flames did not leave him as a corpse.

Instead, Jake's body underwent an anomalous restoration event. After the flames subsided, he coughed out the bullet and a substantial amount of blood before returning to an apparently healthy state.

Jake subsequently demonstrated no obvious lasting impairment from the injury.

The event was initially suspected to be another manifestation of Jake's existing anomalous nature. Subsequent investigation identified a more specific possible cause: Phoenix's blood had accidentally entered a serving of potato soup prepared for Jake after Phoenix cut her finger.

Phoenix's Transcendence ability appears to have transferred to Jake through the contaminated food.

The resulting ability is consistent with a recurring resurrection phenomenon: whenever Jake dies, he rises from his ashes.

The precise limits of this transferred ability have not been established. C.O.R.E. has not authorized deliberate lethal testing.

Tom Akana, Jake's ghostly counterpart, reacted extremely poorly to the shooting.

While Jake's recovery was immediate enough to leave him bewildered rather than seriously distressed, Tom was not reassured by the outcome. The fact that Jake could return from death did not make the decision to shoot him acceptable.

Tom's anger toward the shooter remains a significant concern for personnel responsible for post-incident management.

**C.O.R.E. assessment:** Jake has apparently acquired a resurrection capability associated with Phoenix's Transcendence. The capability may make death reversible for Jake, but it does not make violence against him harmless, acceptable, or safe to repeat.

---

## 2. INCIDENT CHRONOLOGY

### 2.1 — The Shooting

The precise circumstances leading to the discharge of the firearm remain under investigation.

What is confirmed is that Jake was struck in the neck by a bullet.

The impact caused him to collapse.

For the personnel present, the situation was immediately alarming. Jake was a six-year-old child, and the wound was located in an area where serious injury could occur rapidly.

Tom was present during the aftermath.

He saw Jake fall.

For a moment, whatever reassurance Tom might ordinarily have offered was replaced by the sight of his brother lying motionless.

**Tom:** "Jake?"

There was no immediate answer.

**Tom:** "Jake. Get up."

Still nothing.

Tom moved toward him.

**Tom:** "No. No, no, no—Jake, look at me."

The available account does not establish whether Jake was conscious at this point. The silence was enough to frighten Tom.

Then the fire appeared.

### 2.2 — The White Fire

White flames erupted around Jake's fallen body.

Personnel initially prepared for a secondary anomalous event, as the fire's sudden appearance had no established explanation at the time.

The flames surrounded Jake, obscuring him from view.

Tom stopped.

**Tom:** "What is that?"

No one present had an immediate answer.

The fire continued for several moments. Despite its appearance, the event did not progress like an ordinary combustion incident. Jake's body was not left as a burned corpse.

Instead, the flames appeared to be associated with a restoration process.

Tom stared into the white fire.

**Tom:** "Jake?"

A cough came from inside the flames.

Then another.

**Jake:** "Khh—! Ugh!"

The fire began to subside.

Jake was alive.

### 2.3 — The Bullet

As the flames dissipated, Jake coughed violently.

A bullet came out of his mouth, followed by blood.

The projectile struck the floor.

Several personnel reportedly stared at it without speaking.

Jake, meanwhile, looked deeply unhappy about the experience.

**Jake:** "BLEGH! BLEGH—UGH!"

He wiped his mouth.

**Jake:** "WHY WAS THERE A BULLET IN MY NECK?!"

No one immediately answered.

Tom reached him first.

**Tom:** "Jake!"

**Jake:** "Tom?"

Tom grabbed him in a desperate embrace, checking him over as quickly as he could.

**Tom:** "You're here. You're actually here."

**Jake:** "Of course I'm here!"

Jake paused.

He looked at the bullet on the floor.

Then he looked at the blood.

Then he looked back at Tom.

**Jake:** "...Did I just die?"

Tom did not answer immediately.

**Jake:** "Tom?"

**Tom:** "I don't know."

His voice was shaking.

**Tom:** "I don't know, Jake."

Jake looked down at himself.

**Jake:** "I feel okay."

**Tom:** "You were SHOT."

**Jake:** "I noticed!"

**Tom:** "IN THE NECK!"

**Jake:** "I ALSO NOTICED THAT!"

A member of the medical team attempted to approach.

Tom immediately turned toward them.

**Tom:** "Don't touch him yet."

**Medical Officer:** "We need to examine him."

**Tom:** "Then examine him. But don't tell me this is normal."

**Medical Officer:** "Nobody is suggesting that it is."

**Tom:** "Good."

Tom turned back to Jake and held him close.

**Tom:** "Don't do that again."

**Jake:** "I didn't exactly schedule it!"

Under other circumstances, the exchange might have been funny.

Tom did not laugh.

Not yet.

## 3. MEDICAL AND ANOMALOUS FINDINGS

### 3.1 — Post-Event Physical Condition

Following the white-fire event, Jake appeared to have returned to an apparently healthy physical state.

The gunshot wound was no longer visibly present. The bullet had been expelled, and the bleeding associated with the injury had ceased.

No obvious lasting impairment was observed during the initial post-event assessment.

These observations are consistent with a restoration phenomenon, but C.O.R.E. has not established whether the process repairs all forms of injury, reverses every cause of death, or has any limitations related to the severity or nature of the damage.

The event should not be treated as proof of universal invulnerability.

Jake was shot.

He fell.

The fact that he recovered does not change what happened to him.

### 3.2 — White-Fire Phenomenon

The white fire appears to have accompanied Jake's restoration.

No ordinary combustion explanation has been established.

The available observations do not establish whether the flames are a necessary component of the resurrection process, a visible side effect of Transcendence, or an independent manifestation associated with the event.

C.O.R.E. has provisionally classified the phenomenon as **Transcendence-associated white-fire restoration**.

Further characterization must rely on naturally occurring events and non-harmful observations. Deliberately killing Jake to observe the process is prohibited.

### 3.3 — The Expelled Projectile

The bullet recovered from the incident scene is to be retained as evidence.

Its presence is consistent with Jake having sustained a projectile injury before his recovery.

The fact that Jake expelled the bullet by coughing is anomalous and should not be interpreted as a conventional medical process.

The recovered projectile may assist investigators in reconstructing the shooting, but it does not independently explain the restoration event.

## 4. THE POTATO SOUP CONNECTION

The initial investigation into the white-fire phenomenon produced an unexpected lead.

Phoenix had cut her finger while preparing food. A small quantity of her blood accidentally entered Jake's potato soup.

The soup was subsequently consumed by Jake.

Phoenix possesses a Transcendence ability associated with resurrection. Following the shooting, Jake exhibited a phenomenon consistent with that capability.

The connection is compelling.

It is not yet a complete explanation of the transfer mechanism.

### 4.1 — Reconstructed Sequence

**EVENT A:** Phoenix sustains a cut to her finger.

**EVENT B:** Blood from the injury accidentally enters Jake's potato soup.

**EVENT C:** Jake consumes the soup.

**EVENT D:** Jake sustains a gunshot wound to the neck and collapses.

**EVENT E:** White fire engulfs Jake.

**EVENT F:** Jake expels the bullet and blood, then returns to an apparently healthy condition.

**EVENT G:** Investigation identifies the soup as the likely route through which Phoenix's Transcendence transferred to Jake.

The temporal sequence and the similarity between Phoenix's ability and Jake's recovery support the transfer hypothesis.

C.O.R.E. has not established whether ingestion of Phoenix's blood is sufficient to transfer Transcendence in every case, whether the transfer is permanent under all circumstances, or whether other factors were necessary.

The current working conclusion is that the accidental blood contamination resulted in Jake acquiring the ability.

### 4.2 — Phoenix's Reaction

Phoenix was informed that the blood from her cut had likely transferred her Transcendence to Jake.

Her response was recorded as follows.

**Phoenix:** "Wait. My blood was in his soup?"

**Medical Officer:** "A small amount, yes."

**Phoenix:** "And he got my ability from it?"

**Medical Officer:** "That is the leading explanation."

Phoenix went quiet.

**Phoenix:** "I cut my finger making potato soup."

**Medical Officer:** "Correct."

**Phoenix:** "And now Jake comes back from the dead."

**Medical Officer:** "That is consistent with our observations."

**Phoenix:** "...I was making lunch."

The interview record indicates that Phoenix was surprised by the outcome. There is no evidence that she intended to transfer her ability or knowingly exposed Jake to it.

The incident is classified as an accidental ability transfer.

No disciplinary action is recommended against Phoenix for the blood contamination itself.

Food-preparation and wound-covering procedures should nevertheless be reviewed to prevent similar accidental exposure to anomalous biological material.

### 4.3 — Jake Learns the Explanation

Jake was briefed after the initial medical assessment.

**Jake:** "So I got Phoenix's power because her blood got into my soup?"

**Medical Officer:** "That is our current conclusion."

**Jake:** "The potato soup?"

**Medical Officer:** "Yes."

**Jake:** "The soup I ate?"

**Medical Officer:** "Yes, Jake."

Jake stared at the medical officer.

**Jake:** "I DIED BECAUSE SOMEBODY SHOT ME, AND NOW I CAN COME BACK BECAUSE OF POTATO SOUP?"

**Medical Officer:** "That is a simplified description of the event."

**Jake:** "IT'S A VERY GOOD DESCRIPTION!"

From the other side of the room, Tom spoke.

**Tom:** "I hate this place."

**Jake:** "Tom, I can come back from the dead!"

**Tom:** "YOU WERE SHOT!"

**Jake:** "I KNOW!"

**Tom:** "THAT IS NOT THE PART I'M CELEBRATING!"

Jake fell silent.

He looked at Tom.

Then, more quietly, he reached for his brother's hand.

**Jake:** "I'm okay now."

Tom squeezed his hand.

**Tom:** "I know."

A pause.

**Tom:** "I'm still angry."

**Jake:** "I know that, too."

## 5. TRANSFERRED TRANSCENDENCE: PROVISIONAL ASSESSMENT

### 5.1 — Apparent Resurrection Cycle

The currently observed sequence is as follows:

1. Jake sustains a fatal injury or otherwise dies.
    
2. His body becomes engulfed in white fire.
    
3. The restoration event proceeds.
    
4. Jake rises from the resulting ashes, restored to life.
    

The precise sequence has not been exhaustively documented. The shooting incident establishes the white-fire restoration and recovery from a severe wound, while the reported resurrection property associated with Phoenix's Transcendence indicates that subsequent deaths may produce the same outcome.

C.O.R.E. records the working ability description as:

**PHOENIX-DERIVED TRANSCENDENCE — RECURSIVE RESURRECTION**

_The subject appears capable of returning from death through a white-fire event, emerging from the resulting ashes._

The designation is provisional and does not imply that every aspect of Phoenix's original ability has transferred.

### 5.2 — Does Jake Still Feel Pain?

The incident provides no basis for concluding that Jake is immune to pain.

He coughed violently, expelled a bullet and blood, and reacted with considerable distress to the experience.

His restoration does not erase the fact that he experienced a traumatic injury.

Personnel are expressly prohibited from interpreting resurrection as permission to expose him to avoidable injury.

### 5.3 — Is Jake Immortal?

C.O.R.E. will not use the term _immortal_ as a confirmed technical classification.

The current evidence supports a resurrection capability, not absolute immunity to every possible form of harm.

Questions concerning the ability's limits, recovery interval, possible failure conditions, and interactions with other anomalous effects remain unanswered.

No experiments involving deliberate injury or death are authorized to answer them.

### 5.4 — Can the Ability Be Transferred Again?

The accidental transfer from Phoenix to Jake suggests that anomalous abilities may be capable of transmission through contact with biological material under certain conditions.

It does not establish that all abilities behave this way.

C.O.R.E. must avoid turning one extraordinary accident into a universal biological rule.

Phoenix's blood contamination is to be treated as a specific anomalous exposure event until further evidence supports a broader conclusion.

## 6. TOM AKANA: POST-INCIDENT BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATION

**Assessment:** Acute distress and anger directed toward the individual responsible for shooting Jake.

Tom's reaction was not unexpected.

He watched Jake fall after being shot in the neck. He did not know, at that moment, that Jake would return from the dead. He had no confirmed explanation for the white fire and no guarantee that his brother would recover.

The subsequent restoration resolved the immediate uncertainty about Jake's survival.

It did not resolve Tom's anger.

### Interview Excerpt

**C.O.R.E. Psychologist:** "Tom, can you tell me what you were thinking when Jake fell?"

**Tom:** "No."

**Psychologist:** "You don't remember?"

**Tom:** "I remember everything."

**Psychologist:** "Then can you tell me?"

Tom looked away.

**Tom:** "He was on the floor."

**Psychologist:** "Yes."

**Tom:** "There was blood."

A pause.

**Tom:** "He wasn't getting up."

**Psychologist:** "And then the fire appeared."

**Tom:** "I thought I was going to lose him."

The room fell quiet.

**Psychologist:** "But Jake recovered."

**Tom:** "I KNOW HE RECOVERED."

Tom's voice rose sharply enough that the recording registered a brief audio peak.

He took a moment to compose himself.

**Tom:** "Sorry."

**Psychologist:** "You don't need to apologize for being upset."

**Tom:** "I'm not upset because he recovered."

**Psychologist:** "What are you upset about?"

Tom looked directly at the psychologist.

**Tom:** "Someone shot my brother."

**Psychologist:** "And you're angry at the person responsible."

**Tom:** "Yes."

**Psychologist:** "Do you intend to hurt them?"

Tom did not answer immediately.

**Tom:** "I want them to understand what they did."

**Psychologist:** "And if they don't?"

**Tom:** "Then I don't know what I'll do."

The psychologist did not press the question.

**Psychologist:** "Tom, Jake is alive. You can stay with him while we investigate."

**Tom:** "I'm staying with him anyway."

**Psychologist:** "All right."

**Tom:** "And whoever shot him had better hope that the investigation finds them before I do."

_Interview paused._

### Behavioral Assessment

Tom's anger is understandable in the context of the incident. He witnessed an apparent threat to Jake's life and remains distressed by the fact that another person inflicted the injury.

His statement concerning the shooter indicates a risk of retaliatory behavior if he encounters the responsible individual while still emotionally overwhelmed.

It is not evidence that Tom has already acted on that anger.

C.O.R.E. recommends that Tom be given space to express his feelings, access to appropriate support, and reasonable reassurance that the shooting will be investigated.

Personnel must not dismiss his distress by telling him that Jake can simply resurrect.

**Tom did not watch a resurrection when the bullet struck. He watched his brother fall.**

That distinction must remain central to any welfare assessment.

## 7. THE BROTHERS — POST-INCIDENT OBSERVATION

Following the medical assessment, Jake and Tom remained together.

Jake appeared eager to reassure Tom that he was fine. Tom, in turn, remained physically close to Jake and continued checking on him even after medical personnel confirmed that the visible injury had disappeared.

The exchange below was recorded later that evening.

**Jake:** "Tom?"

**Tom:** "What?"

**Jake:** "You're still mad."

**Tom:** "Yes."

**Jake:** "At me?"

Tom turned toward him immediately.

**Tom:** "WHAT? No!"

**Jake:** "You sounded mad."

**Tom:** "I'm mad at the person who shot you!"

**Jake:** "Oh."

Jake considered that for a moment.

**Jake:** "Well, I don't like them either."

**Tom:** "You don't have to make jokes about it."

**Jake:** "I'm not."

Tom looked at him.

Jake's usual enthusiasm had quieted.

**Jake:** "I just don't want you to be scared anymore."

Tom's expression softened, but his voice remained strained.

**Tom:** "I can't promise that."

**Jake:** "That's okay."

**Tom:** "Jake, you were dead."

**Jake:** "And then I wasn't."

**Tom:** "That doesn't make it okay!"

**Jake:** "I know."

Jake leaned against him.

**Jake:** "But I'm here now."

Tom closed his eyes for a moment.

**Tom:** "Yeah."

He put an arm around Jake.

**Tom:** "You're here now."

A few seconds passed.

**Jake:** "Does this mean I can eat more potato soup?"

**Tom:** "J A K E."

**Jake:** "What?! I like potato soup!"

**Tom:** "YOU ARE NOT EATING ANYTHING PHOENIX MAKES UNTIL WE HAVE A CONVERSATION ABOUT KITCHEN SAFETY!"

From the doorway, Phoenix protested.

**Phoenix:** "IT WAS ONE CUT!"

**Tom:** "IT WAS ONE CUT THAT MADE JAKE COME BACK FROM THE DEAD!"

**Jake:** "Technically, the soup was really good."

**Tom:** "THAT IS NOT HELPING!"

The recording ends with Jake laughing and Tom telling him to stop laughing because he is still supposed to be resting.

Despite the exchange's humorous conclusion, C.O.R.E. notes that Tom's distress was not fully resolved. The brothers' ability to joke together should not be interpreted as proof that the incident had no lasting emotional effect.

## 8. SECURITY AND WELFARE DIRECTIVES

**8.1 — Investigate the Shooting**

The circumstances of the shooting must be established. The shooter's identity, motive, and responsibility are to be determined through the appropriate investigation.

Jake's resurrection must not be used to downgrade the severity of the incident.

**8.2 — No Deliberate Resurrection Testing**

No personnel may deliberately kill, injure, or expose Jake to lethal hazards to test the transferred Transcendence ability.

The ability is to be documented through naturally occurring events and non-harmful observation.

**8.3 — Protect the Subject's Dignity**

Jake must not be described as expendable, invulnerable, or suitable for lethal experimentation.

His ability to return from death does not remove his right to protection.

**8.4 — Support Tom**

Tom is to receive appropriate welfare support and reasonable opportunities to remain with Jake while the brothers recover from the incident.

Personnel should acknowledge his fear and anger without encouraging retaliation.

**8.5 — Prevent Retaliatory Escalation**

If Tom encounters the person responsible for the shooting while distressed, personnel should intervene to prevent further harm and allow the investigation to proceed.

This directive is preventive, not punitive.

**8.6 — Review Anomalous Food Safety**

Food-preparation procedures involving Phoenix and other residents with anomalous biological properties should be reviewed. Cuts and other open wounds must be appropriately covered during food preparation.

This is a precautionary measure, not an accusation of misconduct.

**8.7 — Preserve the Brothers' Relationship**

Neither Jake nor Tom should be forcibly separated as a response to this incident. Any necessary medical or investigative separation must be justified, explained, and handled with appropriate care.

Their mutual concern for one another is a source of support, not grounds for punishment.

---

## 9. FINAL DETERMINATION

The incident confirms that Jake Akana has undergone an anomalous restoration event following a gunshot wound to the neck.

White fire engulfed him, after which he expelled the bullet and blood and returned to an apparently healthy state.

The most likely explanation is that Phoenix's Transcendence transferred to Jake through accidental blood contamination of his potato soup.

The resulting ability appears to allow Jake to return from death, rising from his ashes through a white-fire resurrection process.

The precise limits of this ability remain unknown.

The shooting itself remains under investigation.

Tom's anger toward the person responsible is substantial and understandable. Appropriate measures should be taken to protect Jake, support Tom, and prevent further harm.

**C.O.R.E. determination:** Jake's resurrection capability is a significant anomalous development. It must be treated as a means of recovery, not as a justification for exposing him to danger.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There will undoubtedly be personnel who look at this incident and see the remarkable part first.

A child was shot in the neck. He fell. White fire swallowed him, and moments later he coughed out the bullet and stood up as though death had been an interruption rather than an ending.

The explanation, somehow, involves potato soup.

It is an extraordinary event. The research implications are considerable.

But Tom was not watching an experiment.

He was watching Jake.

When the bullet struck, Tom did not know that Phoenix's Transcendence had transferred. He did not know that white fire would appear. He did not know that Jake would cough, complain, and ask about soup afterward.

He only knew that his brother had fallen and was not getting up.

We must not let the miracle of Jake's recovery erase the reality of that moment.

And we must not let the fact that Jake can rise from his ashes convince anyone that shooting him is inconsequential.

Tom has every reason to be angry. The task before us is to ensure that his anger is heard, that the person responsible is held appropriately accountable, and that neither brother is left to carry the consequences alone.

As for the soup, the kitchen has been instructed to improve its handling procedures.

Phoenix has been asked to wear a bandage when preparing food.

Jake has been asked not to describe his resurrection as a reason to request extra servings.

Tom has been asked to take a breath before threatening anyone.

Only one of those instructions has been consistently followed.

**A miracle can bring someone back. It cannot undo the fear of watching them die.**

C.O.R.E. will remember both halves of that truth.
