# SAFEHOUSE

## Architectural Blueprint — Ten-Floor Residential Complex

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SAFEHOUSE is an apartment complex situated within a pocket dimension and designated as a permanent residential facility for its registered residents.

The complex consists of ten designated floors, each assigned a specific residential, communal, maintenance, or structural purpose. Its layout accommodates several distinct resident groups while maintaining shared access to common facilities and the building's vertical transportation system.

The building is not merely a collection of private residences. It is a shared living environment intended to accommodate individuals with different backgrounds, abilities, needs, and personal circumstances under one residential designation: SAFEHOUSE.

The following blueprint establishes the current floor assignments and resident-group classifications for administrative reference.

Important: This document records the stated layout and assignments. It does not establish unprovided room numbers, apartment counts, dimensions, emergency exits, or other architectural specifications.

# 5. GENERAL RESIDENTIAL DIRECTIVES

5.1 — Respect for Residential Groupings

The listed groups are recognized as the official residential assignments for this version of the SAFEHOUSE blueprint. Administrative records should preserve the distinction between each group while recognizing all listed individuals as SAFEHOUSE residents.

5.2 — Communal Access

The Entrance, Common Room, Main Kitchen, and Elevator Entrance on Floor 1 are designated shared facilities. Their use is not restricted to a single residential group by this blueprint.

5.3 — Unassigned Space

Floor 9 is to remain recorded as empty until an authorized decision establishes a new purpose. No department or resident may presume that an unassigned floor is automatically available for reassignment.

5.4 — Blueprint Integrity

Future changes to floor assignments must be reflected in an updated blueprint. Informal changes to household arrangements do not automatically amend the official floor directory.

5.5 — Resident Welfare

SAFEHOUSE is a residential environment, not merely a logistical arrangement. Residents must be treated as individuals rather than as interchangeable members of their assigned groups. Residential classifications exist to support organization and care, not to diminish personal identity or autonomy.

# 6. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

SAFEHOUSE has ten floors, several residential groups, shared facilities, and spaces whose purposes are defined by the work they support.

But a blueprint can only record where people are assigned to live. It cannot explain why a common room becomes important after a difficult day, why a shared kitchen may become the center of an ordinary family evening, or why a particular floor becomes associated with the people someone trusts most.

Those things do not appear on architectural drawings.

They appear in the lives of the residents.

C.O.R.E. recognizes SAFEHOUSE as more than a pocket-dimensional apartment complex. It is a shared home, maintained not only by its structure, but by the people who make it worth returning to.
