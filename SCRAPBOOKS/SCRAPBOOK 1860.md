## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

For an extended period, Jake Akana's repeated resurrections were classified as successful recoveries following fatal incidents. The apparent restoration of his body, combined with his cheerful behavior afterward, led observers to underestimate the physiological abnormalities occurring during the moments immediately following revival.

Jake does not return to consciousness in perfect condition.

Following each resurrection, he exhibits a recurring cluster of symptoms:

- Coughing up small quantities of blood.
    
- Brief but noticeable difficulty breathing.
    
- A sensation of suffocation, sometimes accompanied by visible respiratory distress.
    
- Temporary weakness and discomfort during the initial recovery period.
    

These symptoms generally subside, allowing Jake to resume ordinary activities. Their consistency, however, suggested that his resurrection was not physiologically equivalent to Phoenix's Transcendence.

A comparative investigation has now identified a fundamental distinction between their abilities.

**Phoenix heals her existing body. Jake reconstructs his body after death.**

Phoenix's Transcendence draws upon Ether to repair damaged cells and restore biological function. Her ability appears to preserve the continuity of her living body while repairing its injuries.

Jake's ability operates differently. When he dies, his existing cells are killed. His body is then reconstructed from the ground up through the manifestation of white fire.

He does not simply repair the body that died.

He rebuilds a living body after the previous one has ceased functioning.

The distinction explains why Jake can appear physically restored while still experiencing distress immediately after resurrection. It also establishes that the apparent success of his ability must not be confused with a comfortable, instantaneous, or entirely symptom-free recovery.

Most importantly, the discovery changes how C.O.R.E. must interpret Jake's repeated deaths.

**Phoenix survives by healing. Jake returns by being reconstructed. Neither process makes death a trivial event, but Jake's process involves a far more fundamental interruption of biological continuity.**

## 2. THE OBSERVATION THAT TRIGGERED THE INVESTIGATION

The investigation began after a routine residential incident involving another of Jake's accidental deaths.

Jake had tripped while moving through a corridor. The resulting injury was fatal, and the familiar white fire appeared shortly afterward.

When the fire subsided, Jake was alive again.

However, rather than immediately speaking or attempting to stand, he rolled onto his side and began coughing.

The first cough was dry.

The second was accompanied by blood.

Jake tried to inhale, but his breathing was shallow and uneven. He pressed a hand against his throat, visibly distressed, before managing to draw in a more substantial breath.

Tom, who had witnessed the incident, immediately knelt beside him.

**TOM:** "Jake? Hey. Look at me."

Jake attempted to answer.

Instead, he coughed again.

**TOM:** "Don't try to talk. Just breathe. Slowly, okay?"

**JAKE:** "I... can't..."

His voice was weak and interrupted by another strained breath.

Tom's expression changed.

He had become accustomed to the white fire. He had become accustomed to Jake returning from the dead. He had even become accustomed to Jake asking for cake before anyone had finished checking whether he was injured.

He had not become accustomed to seeing his brother struggle to breathe.

**TOM:** "Clover! Get medical staff! Now!"

Clover arrived moments later and helped keep Jake still while the medical team assessed him.

The coughing and respiratory difficulty gradually subsided. Jake eventually regained his usual composure, although he remained exhausted for several minutes.

His first question was whether he could go back to the common room.

His second was whether anyone had saved his cake.

Tom refused to answer either question.

**TOM:** "You're going to Medical."

**JAKE:** "But I'm okay now."

**TOM:** "You were coughing up blood."

**JAKE:** "Only a little."

**TOM:** "THAT IS NOT A DEFENSE!"

The incident was initially recorded as a transient post-resurrection complication. Subsequent review revealed that the symptoms had occurred after earlier resurrections as well.

The episodes had been individually brief. Their recurrence had made them easy to dismiss as temporary discomfort.

That interpretation was no longer considered acceptable.

## 3. COMPARATIVE PHYSIOLOGY

### 3.1 Phoenix — Etheric Cellular Restoration

Phoenix's Transcendence operates through the acquisition and application of Ether to damaged biological tissue.

Available observations indicate that her ability restores the function of injured cells, repairs damage, and allows her body to recover from injuries that would otherwise be fatal.

The defining feature is continuity.

Phoenix's living body remains the subject of the healing process. Her cells are damaged, but her ability acts upon those cells to restore biological function.

In simplified terms, Phoenix's ability follows this sequence:

1. Injury damages living tissue.
    
2. Transcendence draws Ether to support the healing process.
    
3. Damaged cells and tissues are repaired.
    
4. Biological function is restored.
    

The process is not ordinary healing. Its speed and capacity exceed normal biological limitations, and its exact restrictions remain under investigation.

Nevertheless, the available evidence suggests that Phoenix heals an existing biological system rather than replacing it wholesale.

This distinction matters because cellular repair and full-body reconstruction are not equivalent physiological events.

### 3.2 Jake Akana — Post-Mortem Reconstruction

Jake's Transcendence presents a fundamentally different sequence.

When Jake sustains a fatal injury, his body ceases functioning. Following the death, white fire manifests and his body is reconstructed.

The investigation's current model is as follows:

1. A fatal injury causes Jake's death.
    
2. His existing cells are killed as part of the death event.
    
3. White fire manifests.
    
4. His body is reconstructed from the ground up.
    
5. Jake returns to life, sometimes experiencing immediate respiratory distress and coughing up blood.
    

The precise mechanism by which his identity, memories, and consciousness remain continuous through this process is not yet understood.

Nor has C.O.R.E. established whether the reconstruction draws directly upon Ether, uses a distinct anomalous energy source, or follows a mechanism unrelated to Phoenix's ability despite the shared classification of Transcendence.

The visible white fire is a consistent feature of Jake's resurrection. Its precise role in the reconstruction process remains unconfirmed.

What can be established is that **Jake's resurrection is not merely an unusually powerful healing response**.

There is no living cellular system to heal after his death, according to the current findings. His ability must instead produce a functioning biological body following the destruction of the previous one.

This explains why the two residents can both return from fatal injuries while displaying different recovery characteristics.

## 4. WHY DOES JAKE COUGH UP BLOOD?

The immediate post-resurrection symptoms remain the most significant unresolved part of the investigation.

Jake's body appears structurally intact after reconstruction. He does not consistently display visible external injuries, and the fatal damage that caused his death is no longer apparent.

Yet he sometimes coughs up blood and experiences temporary difficulty breathing.

This indicates that the reconstruction process may not restore every aspect of physiological function simultaneously.

Several explanations remain under consideration:

**A. Respiratory reconstruction lag**

The body may be physically reconstructed before its respiratory system reaches stable function. If the lungs, airways, or associated tissues do not become fully functional at precisely the same moment, Jake could experience temporary respiratory distress.

**B. Post-reconstruction vascular abnormalities**

The blood he coughs up may indicate temporary disruption or instability in the reconstructed respiratory or vascular tissues. The source of the blood has not yet been conclusively identified.

**C. Neurological and physiological reintegration**

Jake's consciousness may return before every bodily system has stabilized. His awareness of suffocation could therefore precede complete restoration of respiratory function.

**D. Reconstruction-specific complications**

The symptoms may be an inherent cost of the resurrection process rather than a conventional injury. If so, they may persist even when the reconstructed body is otherwise anatomically complete.

None of these explanations has been conclusively proven.

C.O.R.E. will not declare the symptoms harmless simply because they resolve spontaneously.

Coughing up blood and experiencing difficulty breathing warrant immediate medical assessment, regardless of the patient's anomalous ability to recover.

## 5. THE FREQUENCY PROBLEM

Jake's repeated accidental deaths have created an additional complication.

His clumsiness has resulted in numerous fatal incidents, including falls on stairs, trips over level flooring, and other seemingly minor accidents. Each incident has been followed by resurrection.

The frequency of these events means that even a relatively brief recovery complication may recur often enough to become a significant medical concern.

A single episode of coughing after resurrection might be mistaken for an isolated anomaly.

Repeated episodes following separate deaths establish a pattern that requires investigation.

It is not yet known whether frequent reconstruction carries cumulative physiological effects. Jake's apparent restoration to baseline between incidents does not prove that repeated deaths are without long-term consequences.

C.O.R.E. has therefore prohibited the assumption that Jake's body can be subjected to unlimited cycles of death and reconstruction without risk.

The current safety directive remains unchanged:

- No deliberate lethal testing.
    
- No experiments intended to measure the maximum number of resurrections Jake can endure.
    
- No dismissal of respiratory distress on the grounds that it is a familiar symptom.
    
- Immediate medical evaluation following any resurrection accompanied by coughing blood, breathing difficulty, or other concerning symptoms.
    
- Continued efforts to prevent falls and other avoidable injuries.
    

The objective is not to determine how many times Jake can die.

The objective is to prevent him from needing to find out.

## 6. INTERVIEW EXCERPTS

### 6.1 Jake Akana

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do you remember what happens when you die?"

**JAKE:** "I get hurt, then everything stops. Then there's the white fire, and I come back."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do you experience anything during the reconstruction?"

Jake thought for a moment.

**JAKE:** "I don't know. It's not like I can tell you what happens to my cells."

**INTERVIEWER:** "When you wake up, do you feel normal?"

**JAKE:** "Usually. Sometimes I cough, and my chest feels weird. Like I can't get enough air."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Does that frighten you?"

Jake looked down at his hands.

**JAKE:** "A little. Mostly because Tom gets really scared."

**INTERVIEWER:** "What about you?"

**JAKE:** "I know I come back."

**INTERVIEWER:** "That doesn't mean the process is safe."

**JAKE:** "I know."

He paused.

**JAKE:** "I just don't really think about it until it happens."

That answer was consistent with previous observations. Jake does not appear to deliberately seek death or underestimate the importance of safety with conscious intent. Rather, his repeated recovery has made the event itself seem less consequential to him than it does to other people.

**INTERVIEWER:** "What would you like us to do?"

**JAKE:** "Make sure Tom knows I'm okay."

**INTERVIEWER:** "And your breathing?"

**JAKE:** "That too."

A pause.

**JAKE:** "And maybe put something soft on the floor."

**INTERVIEWER:** "We can certainly investigate that."

**JAKE:** "Great! Can it be cake-shaped?"

The interview concluded shortly afterward.

### 6.2 Phoenix

**INTERVIEWER:** "Phoenix, do you experience the same symptoms after using Transcendence?"

**PHOENIX:** "Not the same ones, no."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Explain the difference."

**PHOENIX:** "When I heal, I pull in Ether and use it to fix the damage. My body's still there. It's just injured."

**INTERVIEWER:** "And Jake?"

Phoenix's expression became more serious.

**PHOENIX:** "Jake's body dies. All the cells die. Then the fire builds him again."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Does that concern you?"

**PHOENIX:** "Of course it does."

She rubbed her eyes.

**PHOENIX:** "I can understand why he treats it like a minor inconvenience. He wakes up, he's himself again, and he wants to get on with his day. But if his body has to be rebuilt every time, then those coughing fits aren't something we should ignore."

**INTERVIEWER:** "Do you think your ability is safer?"

**PHOENIX:** "I think it's different. Don't confuse that with safer."

This distinction has been incorporated into the investigation's conclusions.

Phoenix's ability is not considered harmless or unlimited. Her method of recovery simply differs from Jake's in a fundamental physiological respect.

Before leaving, Phoenix asked whether the interview was over.

When informed that it was, she fell asleep in the chair.

The medical officer present confirmed that she was breathing normally.

Nobody attempted to wake her until the paperwork was finished.

## 7. TOM AKANA — WELFARE CONSIDERATIONS

Tom was informed of the preliminary findings after the medical team confirmed that Jake was stable.

His initial reaction was not relief.

It was anger.

**TOM:** "So every time he dies, his entire body gets killed and rebuilt?"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "That is the current model, yes."

**TOM:** "And he keeps doing it because he trips over things?"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "The deaths have been accidental."

**TOM:** "I KNOW THEY'RE ACCIDENTAL!"

Tom stopped speaking.

His hands were clenched tightly enough that his knuckles had gone pale.

**TOM:** "Sorry."

The medical officer waited.

**TOM:** "I'm not angry at him. I know he doesn't mean to do it."

He looked through the observation window toward Jake's room.

**TOM:** "But everyone keeps saying he comes back. Like that fixes everything."

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "It doesn't erase what happens beforehand."

**TOM:** "Or afterward."

He took a slow breath.

**TOM:** "He wakes up coughing blood, and he can barely breathe, and then he says he's fine because it goes away. What if one day it doesn't go away?"

There was no immediate answer.

The investigation cannot currently establish whether a future resurrection could fail or produce a more severe complication. Neither outcome should be presumed inevitable, but neither can be ruled out.

**TOM:** "I don't want him to stop being happy. I don't want him to be scared of everything."

His voice softened.

**TOM:** "I just want him to stop dying over stupid things."

That statement has been included in the medical review without alteration.

It captures the central concern more accurately than any statistical summary.

## 8. FINAL FINDINGS AND DIRECTIVES

The investigation establishes the following preliminary conclusions:

1. Jake Akana and Phoenix possess distinct manifestations of Transcendence.
    
2. Phoenix's ability appears to use Ether to repair and restore existing biological tissue.
    
3. Jake's ability operates through death followed by full-body reconstruction, with his existing cells killed during the process.
    
4. Jake's recurring post-resurrection coughing, blood expectoration, and breathing difficulty are genuine medical concerns whose underlying mechanisms remain unresolved.
    
5. The frequency of Jake's accidental deaths warrants stronger preventive measures and continued monitoring.
    
6. Neither resident's recovery ability is to be treated as evidence of unlimited durability, guaranteed survival, or absence of suffering.
    

C.O.R.E. further directs that Jake's post-resurrection symptoms be documented consistently, including their duration, severity, and resolution. Medical personnel must prioritize immediate stabilization over routine data collection.

Phoenix's distinct healing mechanism may provide useful comparative information, but no experiments are to be conducted on either resident without appropriate ethical and medical review.

Finally, residential safety measures must be improved to reduce the likelihood of Jake sustaining further accidental fatal injuries.

**The investigation is not authorized to establish the maximum number of times Jake can die.**

That is not an acceptable research objective.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake thinks death is a minor inconvenience because, for him, it has repeatedly ended with the same result: white fire, a new breath, and another chance to continue whatever he was doing.

Phoenix understands the process differently. Her body survives because her power repairs it. Jake's body returns because his power rebuilds it.

To an outside observer, both outcomes may look miraculous.

To Tom, the difference is much less abstract.

He sees his brother fall. He sees the fire. He waits for Jake to return, and sometimes the first thing he hears is coughing instead of laughter.

Then Jake tells him he's okay.

Then Jake asks for cake.

And Tom has to decide whether to believe the words, the smile, or the blood still on his brother's lips.

C.O.R.E. will not ask Tom to make that decision alone.

**We have documented the mechanism. We have identified the complication. We have established the risk.**

Now we must do something more important than understanding why Jake comes back.

We must make certain that the people who love him do not have to watch him die again simply because nobody thought a little fall could matter.

_Resurrection is not a substitute for prevention._

_And a child who can return from death is still a child who deserves to be kept safe._

— **C.O.R.E. Chemical, Biological & Anomalous Physiology Division**
