**SUBJECT:** Jake — Severe Distrust, Independent Wandering, and Failure to Return to Private Residence

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has begun walking around SAFEHOUSE while carrying his own IV pole.

He requires continued medical support, including fluids, and remains physically vulnerable following his recent episode of severe hypothermia.

He is still shaking.

He is still frightened of people.

And he no longer appears willing to rely on anyone to help him.

Not Bao.

Not Aeterna.

Not Clover.

Not even the people who have consistently demonstrated affection toward him.

Jake now attempts to meet his immediate needs without asking anyone for assistance.

When he needs water, he tries to obtain it himself.

When he needs to move, he takes the IV pole and walks.

When he becomes tired, he stops wherever he happens to be.

Most significantly, Jake recently fell asleep at a cafeteria table rather than returning to his private residence.

He had access to his own bed.

He knew where his room was.

He simply did not go there.

When questioned about the decision, Jake indicated that he did not want to return to his room.

He has not provided a comprehensive explanation.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet establish whether the cafeteria feels safer because it is a public space, because people are more likely to notice him there, because he fears being alone, or because his room has become associated with being forgotten.

Several explanations may be true simultaneously.

What is established is that Jake is now attempting to live around his fear rather than asking other people to help him manage it.

**He would apparently rather drag an IV pole across SAFEHOUSE, find his own water, and sleep on a cafeteria table than depend on someone he no longer trusts.**

This behavior is not evidence of recovered independence.

It is a significant welfare concern.

---

# 1. THE IV POLE

The first report arrived from a resident who encountered Jake walking through a common corridor.

Jake was moving slowly.

His white hair was disheveled.

His azure eyes appeared dull with exhaustion, although the shooting-star patterns remained visible.

His hands were trembling.

One hand held the IV pole.

The other held his teddy bear.

He was dragging neither item deliberately, but his limited stamina made the journey visibly difficult.

He stopped twice to rest.

On the second occasion, he leaned against the wall and remained there for nearly a minute.

A resident approached to offer assistance.

Jake immediately tightened his grip on the pole.

> **RESIDENT:** "Jake? Do you need help with that?"

Jake shook his head.

> **RESIDENT:** "I can carry the pole for you."

> **JAKE:** "No."

The resident stopped.

> **RESIDENT:** "All right."

Jake waited until the resident moved away before continuing.

He did not appear angry.

He appeared frightened.

The distinction was noted.

Jake was not attempting to demonstrate that he was stronger than the other residents.

He was attempting to retain control over something he believed he could no longer safely entrust to another person.

The IV pole represented a medical necessity.

In Jake's current state, however, even accepting help to move it had become difficult.

## Medical concern

Jake's ability to walk does not establish that he is medically fit to wander without assistance.

His recent hypothermia, persistent tremors, internal cold sensation, and need for IV support require continued clinical oversight.

An IV pole also introduces practical risks when a patient is exhausted, unsteady, or attempting to navigate common areas alone.

Personnel must not respond by forcibly confining Jake merely because he wants to move.

Instead, the medical team should establish a safe mobility plan, assess whether he can safely manage the equipment, and offer assistance in a way that preserves his choices wherever medically possible.

The objective is not to take control away from him.

It is to prevent his attempt to retain control from causing further harm.

# 2. WATER

Jake has repeatedly attempted to obtain water without asking for assistance.

This is particularly concerning because he is still receiving medical treatment.

His fluid requirements must be determined by the medical team, and any oral intake must be compatible with his current treatment plan.

During one observation, Jake entered the cafeteria carrying the IV pole.

He approached the water dispenser.

He placed the pole beside him and reached for a cup.

His hand was shaking badly enough that the cup rattled against the dispenser.

He tried again.

The cup slipped.

It struck the counter and fell to the floor.

Jake froze.

For several seconds, he simply stared at it.

A staff member standing nearby moved instinctively to help.

Jake recoiled.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "It's all right. I'll get another cup."

Jake shook his head.

He bent to retrieve the fallen cup himself, despite the IV tubing pulling slightly as he moved.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "Jake, please. Let me get it."

> **JAKE:** "No."

The staff member stopped immediately.

Jake retrieved the cup and placed it on the counter.

He tried again.

This time, he managed to fill it.

He drank slowly, holding the cup with both hands.

Some water spilled onto his sleeve.

He did not appear to notice.

When he finished, he stood quietly beside the counter, visibly trembling.

The staff member did not approach.

After a moment, Jake picked up the IV pole and left.

### C.O.R.E. observation

The incident demonstrates that Jake is willing to tolerate considerable physical difficulty rather than accept assistance.

His behavior should not be praised as self-sufficiency.

Nor should staff interpret his refusal as a reason to withdraw all offers of help.

Personnel should continue making assistance available without repeatedly pressing him.

Where medical needs require intervention, staff must explain the necessity, provide simple choices where possible, and use the least intrusive appropriate approach.

Jake must not be allowed to become medically dehydrated because he is afraid to ask for water.

Equally, he must not be made to feel that obtaining a drink requires surrendering control over his body or his movements.

# 3. CLOVER

Clover was among the first residents to recognize that Jake's behavior had changed.

She found him beside the cafeteria entrance, attempting to maneuver his IV pole around a chair.

She approached slowly.

> **CLOVER:** "Hey, Jake."

Jake looked up.

His hands tightened around the pole.

Clover stopped several feet away.

> **CLOVER:** "Do you want me to move that chair?"

Jake shook his head.

> **CLOVER:** "Okay."

She waited.

Jake moved the pole around the chair himself.

The maneuver took nearly a minute.

Clover watched without interfering.

When he reached the other side, she asked:

> **CLOVER:** "Would you like some water?"

Jake looked toward the dispenser.

Then at Clover.

> **JAKE:** "I can get it."

> **CLOVER:** "All right."

She did not insist.

Jake continued toward the dispenser.

Clover remained where she was, resisting the impulse to follow him.

She had learned that an apparently helpful gesture could become frightening if Jake felt cornered.

When he returned, she noticed that he had barely managed to drink without spilling.

> **CLOVER:** "I'm glad you got some."

Jake said nothing.

Clover smiled gently.

> **CLOVER:** "I'll be at my usual table if you need anything."

Jake looked at the table she indicated.

Then back at her.

> **JAKE:** "Don't wait for me."

Clover's expression changed.

> **CLOVER:** "I wasn't going to make you come over."

> **JAKE:** "I know."

A pause.

> **JAKE:** "Just don't wait."

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:** "Okay."

Jake left.

Clover remained beside the entrance for several seconds.

She understood that he had not simply declined an invitation.

He had asked her not to organize her time around the expectation that he might eventually approach her.

Perhaps he did not want to disappoint her.

Perhaps he did not want to owe her a conversation.

Perhaps he did not want to discover that someone waiting for him could eventually become someone who was no longer there.

Clover could not know which explanation was correct.

She did know that following him would not help.

So she returned to her table.

She left an unopened bottle of water on the nearby counter, where any resident could take it.

She did not put Jake's name on it.

She did not leave a note.

She did not tell him it was for him.

If he wanted it, he could take it without having to acknowledge a favor.

Later, the bottle was gone.

No one recorded who had taken it.

Clover did not ask.

# 4. BAO

Bao's experience has been more difficult.

She knows Jake's medical history.

She knows how vulnerable his human manifestation is.

She knows that he has a tendency to continue doing things despite exhaustion.

Under ordinary circumstances, she would intervene when she saw him struggling with an IV pole.

Now, every attempt to approach him risks increasing his fear.

During one encounter, Bao found Jake sitting against a cafeteria wall.

The IV pole stood beside him.

His teddy bear was pressed against his chest.

His hands shook continuously.

Bao approached only far enough to be heard.

> **BAO:** "Jake, sweetheart. Are you feeling dizzy?"

Jake shook his head.

Bao looked at the IV line.

> **BAO:** "May I check that the tubing is all right?"

Jake pulled the pole slightly closer.

> **JAKE:** "No."

Bao stopped.

> **BAO:** "All right. I won't touch it."

Jake watched her carefully.

> **BAO:** "I do need to make sure your treatment is running properly. Can we ask the nurse to check it while you keep the pole beside you?"

Jake said nothing.

> **BAO:** "You can choose whether you want me to stay nearby or wait outside."

He continued watching her.

Finally, he spoke.

> **JAKE:** "Why?"

Bao waited.

> **JAKE:** "Why do you want to help me?"

The question was quiet.

Bao looked at him.

> **BAO:** "Because I care about you."

Jake's expression did not change.

> **JAKE:** "You say that."

Bao absorbed the words without arguing.

> **BAO:** "Yes. I do."

> **JAKE:** "People say things."

His voice trembled.

His hands trembled more.

> **JAKE:** "Then they leave."

Bao felt the instinct to move closer.

She did not.

> **BAO:** "You've been left alone when you needed someone. And you've been hurt. I understand why hearing me say that isn't enough."

Jake lowered his head.

> **BAO:** "I can't make you trust me by asking you to. I can only be honest with you and keep showing you what I'm going to do."

He did not respond.

Bao told him that she would ask the nurse to check his IV and that she would remain in the corridor unless he wanted her nearby.

Then she followed that plan.

She did not turn the interaction into a test of whether Jake still loved her.

She did not tell him that his distrust was breaking her heart.

She did not ask him to reassure her.

She allowed him to remain frightened without making him responsible for her feelings.

For Bao, this was agonizing.

For Jake, it was necessary.

# 5. THE CAFETERIA TABLE

**INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION:** Unplanned Sleep / Failure to Return to Private Residence  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Cafeteria  
**TIME DISCOVERED:** 02:17

Jake was found asleep at a cafeteria table.

He had positioned himself near the wall, away from the main passageway.

The IV pole stood beside his chair.

His teddy bear was tucked against his chest.

His head rested on his folded arms.

His coat had slipped from one shoulder.

He was still trembling.

The cafeteria was quiet.

His private residence was available.

His bed was prepared.

Nothing had prevented him from returning.

He had simply remained in the cafeteria until he fell asleep.

A staff member approached, noticed the IV equipment, and immediately contacted the medical team.

The first concern was physical safety.

Jake was assessed for signs of deterioration, and the IV equipment was checked by qualified personnel.

No one attempted to lift him abruptly or wake him by shaking his shoulder.

When Jake began to stir, he opened his eyes and immediately looked around.

His gaze moved from the staff member to the doorway.

Then to the IV pole.

Then to his teddy bear.

His hands began shaking more severely.

> **JAKE:** "What?"

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "You fell asleep at the table. We're checking that you're all right."

Jake pushed himself upright.

> **JAKE:** "I'm going."

The staff member did not block his path.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "Where would you like to go?"

Jake looked at them as though the question itself was suspicious.

> **JAKE:** "Away."

The staff member stepped back.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "All right. Before you walk, we need to make sure you're steady enough to move safely."

Jake gripped the IV pole.

> **JAKE:** "I can walk."

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "I believe you. We still need to check whether walking is safe for you right now."

Jake stared at them.

His expression became increasingly frightened.

The medical team was called, and the immediate assessment was conducted with as little crowding as possible.

No one demanded that Jake return to his room.

No one treated the cafeteria as an unacceptable place for him to have fallen asleep.

The concern was that he was physically unwell, sleeping in a location that did not adequately support his medical needs, and unwilling to seek assistance.

Once the necessary assessment was complete, staff offered him a choice of resting in his room with a designated caregiver nearby or remaining in a medically appropriate observation area with greater visibility and less isolation.

Jake did not immediately choose either option.

He looked toward the cafeteria door.

Then toward the table.

> **JAKE:** "Can I stay here?"

The staff member answered carefully.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "We can arrange a safe place for you to rest here while the medical team decides what monitoring you need."

Jake stared at them.

> **JAKE:** "You won't make me go to my room?"

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "We won't force you to go there just because it's your room. We do need to keep you medically safe."

Jake lowered his head.

> **JAKE:** "Okay."

He did not look relieved.

But he stopped trying to leave.

# 6. WHY THE CAFETERIA?

C.O.R.E. has not concluded that Jake deliberately selected the cafeteria as a safe location.

Several possibilities remain under consideration.

### Hypothesis A — Visibility

The cafeteria is a shared space.

Someone entering it is likely to notice Jake.

If he becomes unwell, he may be found more quickly than if he is alone behind a closed bedroom door.

Given his recent experience of waiting outside without help, visibility may now be more important to him than privacy.

### Hypothesis B — Fear of Isolation

Jake may associate returning to his private residence with being alone and potentially unnoticed.

His room was previously a place where he could read, draw, sleep, and keep his belongings.

It may now represent a place where no one will know if something goes wrong.

### Hypothesis C — Control

In the cafeteria, Jake can see who approaches.

He can watch the entrances.

He can move to another table if someone makes him uncomfortable.

In his room, he may feel more dependent on whoever is responsible for checking on him.

### Hypothesis D — Exhaustion

Jake is still recovering physically.

He may have intended to sit down briefly and fallen asleep before he could continue.

This possibility must not be overlooked.

### Hypothesis E — Multiple Factors

Jake may be exhausted, frightened of being alone, and more comfortable in a location where other people can see him.

The explanations are not mutually exclusive.

No single interpretation should be imposed upon him.

**C.O.R.E. will not interrogate Jake about why he slept at the table.**

The immediate priority is to make sure he can rest somewhere medically appropriate without feeling that accepting care means surrendering control.

---

# 7. THE PEOPLE HE NO LONGER TRUSTS

The most troubling aspect of Jake's behavior is that his distrust now extends to people who previously served as his strongest sources of comfort.

Bao can offer food and medical assistance.

Jake may refuse.

Clover can offer quiet companionship.

Jake may leave.

Aeterna can remain nearby.

Jake may still watch her hands.

Neri can sit at a distance and say nothing.

Jake may still remember the door closing.

The problem cannot be solved by identifying one person Jake trusts and assigning that person responsibility for everything.

Even his strongest attachments are currently strained by fear.

It would also be incorrect to conclude that Jake no longer loves these people.

His behavior does not establish that.

He may still want Bao nearby while fearing her approach.

He may still want Clover's company while refusing to accept her help.

He may still love Aeterna while struggling to believe that she can keep him safe.

**Affection and trust are no longer moving together.**

Jake can care about someone without feeling safe around them.

He can miss someone while avoiding them.

He can want help while refusing the person offering it.

These contradictions are not evidence that he is manipulating anyone.

They are evidence that his understanding of safety has become deeply unsettled.

# 8. REVISED RESIDENT WELFARE PROTOCOL

The Welfare Division has issued the following immediate requirements.

## Medical care

- The IV must be managed by qualified personnel.
    
- Jake's persistent tremors and internal cold must be reassessed.
    
- His hydration and other fluid requirements must be determined clinically.
    
- Staff must assess his mobility before allowing prolonged independent wandering.
    
- Assistance must be offered without making him feel punished for needing it.
    
- He must not be left medically unattended merely because he refuses emotional interaction.
    

## Personal autonomy

- Jake should retain control over ordinary choices wherever medically possible.
    
- Staff must explain necessary interventions.
    
- He should be offered options rather than given unnecessary ultimatums.
    
- His refusal of nonessential assistance must be respected.
    
- Necessary medical care must be handled transparently and with the least restrictive appropriate approach.
    

## Rest and location

- Jake must have access to a safe, medically appropriate place to rest.
    
- His private residence must remain available.
    
- If he is afraid to return there, personnel must explore alternatives rather than forcing him into the room.
    
- Common-area sleeping must prompt a welfare assessment, not punishment.
    
- If he chooses a shared space, staff should determine whether a quiet, visible, medically supported resting arrangement can be provided.
    

## Interpersonal conduct

Personnel must not tell Jake:

> "You have to trust us."

They must not tell him:

> "Bao would never hurt you."

They must not tell him:

> "Clover is your friend, so stop avoiding her."

They must not tell him:

> "Your room is perfectly safe, so there's nothing to be afraid of."

Such statements ask Jake to accept conclusions he currently cannot believe.

Instead, personnel should demonstrate reliability through small, verifiable actions.

Tell him what is happening.

Do what was agreed.

Explain when circumstances change.

Admit mistakes.

Do not make promises on behalf of other people.

Do not use affection as leverage.

And never make Jake responsible for reassuring the people who are trying to help him.

# 9. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jake's current behavior represents a significant deterioration in both welfare and day-to-day functioning.

He is physically vulnerable.

He is persistently trembling.

He requires medical support.

He is attempting to move independently with an IV pole.

He is seeking water without assistance.

He is avoiding his private residence.

He has begun sleeping in a cafeteria rather than returning to his own bed.

And he appears unwilling to rely on people who would previously have helped him without hesitation.

These observations warrant urgent, coordinated medical and trauma-informed psychological care.

The medical team must not assume his shaking is exclusively emotional.

The psychological team must not assume that resolving his physical symptoms will automatically restore trust.

Both concerns require attention.

C.O.R.E. also recognizes that the residents around Jake may feel helpless, guilty, or frightened by his withdrawal.

Those feelings are understandable.

They must not become additional burdens for Jake.

He does not need to be told how much his behavior hurts everyone.

He does not need to comfort Bao when she is worried.

He does not need to reassure Clover that he still likes her.

He does not need to tell Aeterna that he knows she would protect him.

He does not need to forgive Neri to make the situation easier for anyone else.

**Jake's immediate responsibility is to receive care and recover.**

Everyone else's responsibility is to make that possible without demanding that he become emotionally comfortable before they help him.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING OBSERVATION

At 04:40, Jake was still in the cafeteria.

The medical team had arranged a safe resting space nearby while continuing to monitor his condition.

His IV pole stood within reach.

His teddy bear rested against his chest.

A bottle of water was placed on the table after the team confirmed that oral fluids were appropriate for him.

No one told him that the bottle had been placed there for his benefit.

No one asked him to say thank you.

No one asked him to come home.

Bao was elsewhere in SAFEHOUSE.

Clover had gone to rest.

Aeterna remained available if Jake requested her.

Neri had not approached him again.

For the first time that night, nobody was asking Jake to make a decision about a relationship.

He was allowed to sit quietly.

He picked up the water with both hands.

They were still shaking.

He drank.

Then he placed the bottle beside his teddy bear and rested his head on his folded arms.

A staff member remained at an appropriate distance, monitoring his condition without hovering over him.

Jake looked up once.

The staff member did not move closer.

Jake looked toward the doorway.

Then at the IV pole.

Then at the teddy bear.

Eventually, he closed his eyes.

He did not return to his room.

He did not ask anyone to stay with him.

He did not smile.

And no one interpreted his silence as proof that he was comfortable.

They simply made sure he was not alone in a way that placed his health at risk.

## FINAL NOTE

Jake used to go to his room because it was somewhere he wanted to be.

Now, he walks through SAFEHOUSE with an IV pole because he does not trust anyone enough to ask for help.

He looks for water because he would rather struggle than depend on another person.

He sleeps at a cafeteria table because, for reasons C.O.R.E. does not yet fully understand, returning to his own room is no longer something he feels comfortable doing.

The boy who once wandered around looking for flowers now wanders around looking for somewhere he can remain without being frightened.

The boy who once carried a teddy bear because it comforted him now clutches it because so little else feels dependable.

The boy who once loved being surrounded by friends now watches those same friends as though he must be prepared for something to go wrong.

He may still love them.

He may even want them near.

But wanting someone near and trusting them enough to depend on them have become two entirely different things.

C.O.R.E. cannot order that trust to return.

It cannot repair it with a speech.

It cannot make it happen by surrounding Jake with everyone who loves him.

And it cannot expect a frightened child to recover simply because the people around him have finally understood how badly he is hurting.

Trust will have to be rebuilt through experience.

One drink of water he does not have to struggle for.

One medical procedure explained before it begins.

One person who leaves when they say they will and returns when they promised.

One night when he can sleep without worrying that he will wake up alone somewhere unsafe.

One mistake acknowledged without excuses.

One offer of help that does not become an argument when he says no.

Perhaps, eventually, one person he can allow to sit beside him without watching their hands.

For now, Jake is still trembling.

He is still afraid.

He is still trying to do everything himself because depending on someone else feels more dangerous than struggling alone.

And the fact that he has chosen a cafeteria table over his own bed is not a small behavioral curiosity.

It is a warning that the places and people that once made him feel safe no longer reliably do so.

**He should not have to trust anyone before receiving water, medical care, warmth, or a safe place to sleep.**

Those things are his rights.

Not rewards for being brave.

Not favors he must repay.

Not proof that someone loves him.

Simply things he should have had all along.
