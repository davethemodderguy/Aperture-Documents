### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — DISCARDED**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD SAFETY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Incident:** Recovery of Original Sketchbook

---

# **DISCOVERY**

The original sketchbook was found in a garbage can.

A sanitation employee discovered it during routine disposal collection.

At first, they assumed it was ordinary damaged paper.

The cover was warped.

The pages were swollen from water damage.

Several had fused together.

Ink had bled through the paper.

Some drawings were almost completely illegible.

Then the employee recognized the handwriting.

> **Alice.**

The sketchbook was immediately removed from the garbage.

---

# **THE SKETCHBOOK**

It was the same sketchbook Alice had carried for months.

The same one containing drawings of the people she loved.

The same one containing her attempts to remember names.

The same one containing drawings made before she became completely nonverbal.

The same one that had been ruined when Alice was accidentally left outside during heavy rain.

It had been presumed to remain somewhere inside the Treehouse.

It wasn't.

Alice had thrown it away.

---

# **WHEN ASKED**

Corin found Alice sitting quietly.

She placed the sketchbook in front of her.

Alice looked at it.

She recognized it immediately.

Corin asked:

> "Alice... why did you throw this away?"

Alice looked at the ruined pages.

She didn't look upset.

She simply wrote:

> **"Broken."**

Corin swallowed.

> "But these were your drawings."

Alice nodded.

> **"Can't see."**

She was referring to the drawings.

They were too damaged to recognize.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin picked up the sketchbook.

> "We could have kept it."

Alice shook her head.

> **"Trash."**

Corin stared at her.

> "It's not trash."

Alice didn't answer.

She simply looked at the floor.

---

# **WHAT ALICE DIDN'T SAY**

Alice never said:

> _"I'm angry."_

She never said:

> _"Someone ruined my drawings."_

She never said:

> _"I want them back."_

She never blamed the rain.

She never blamed the employee who had forgotten her.

She never blamed anyone.

She simply reached a very simple conclusion:

**The drawings were broken.**

Therefore:

**The book was no longer useful.**

Therefore:

**It could be thrown away.**

---

# **THE REMAINING PAGES**

Personnel carefully inspected the book.

Some pages were beyond recovery.

Others still contained faint traces.

A carrot.

A pair of bunny ears.

A crooked drawing of a cat.

A star.

A flower.

A small figure with wings.

A black shape resembling a hole.

Most had been distorted by water.

But they were still recognizable to the people who knew what they were.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle was shown the damaged drawing of herself.

She immediately recognized it.

> "...That's me."

Alice nodded.

Belle smiled weakly.

Then looked at the ruined page.

> "You drew this."

Alice nodded again.

Belle hugged her.

Alice didn't resist.

---

# **STAR**

Star found the page containing the rhombus-shaped star.

Most of the drawing had dissolved.

The sticker remained.

Star stared at it.

> "...You kept my sticker."

Alice looked at the page.

Then nodded.

---

# **NEKO**

Neko found the drawing of himself holding a wrench.

Only the wrench remained clearly visible.

He laughed quietly.

> "You remembered the important part."

Alice didn't respond.

---

# **CORIN'S REALIZATION**

Corin eventually understood something.

Alice hadn't thrown the sketchbook away because she stopped caring about everyone inside it.

She threw it away because she believed the book had failed at the one thing she needed it to do.

Remember.

The drawings were no longer readable.

Therefore, in Alice's mind:

**The book couldn't help her anymore.**

---

# **RECOVERY**

The sketchbook was cleaned as carefully as possible.

It was not restored.

It could not be.

The surviving pages were separated and preserved.

The damaged pages were dried and stabilized.

Nothing was thrown away.

Not even the nearly blank pages.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

The recovered sketchbook was returned to her.

Alice held it for several seconds.

She opened it.

Looked at the pages.

Then closed it.

She did not begin drawing again.

But she did not throw it away this time.

She placed it beside her bed.

---

# **PERSONNEL DIRECTIVE**

No damaged personal item belonging to Alice is to be discarded without first confirming with SAFEHOUSE personnel.

This applies particularly to:

- Sketchbooks
    
- Journals
    
- Drawings
    
- Letters
    
- Clothing
    
- Personal keepsakes
    
- Objects Alice appears emotionally attached to
    

Alice may discard something simply because she believes it is no longer useful.

She may not understand its sentimental value.

She may not understand that something can be damaged and still be worth keeping.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

The garbage can was almost emptied before the sketchbook was found.

Nobody knows how long it had been there.

Alice had thrown away the thing that once contained her entire little world.

Not angrily.

Not dramatically.

She simply looked at the ruined pages and decided:

> **"Broken."**

And threw it away.

The recovered sketchbook now sits beside her bed.

Its pages are still ruined.

Its drawings are still faded.

Alice still doesn't draw.

But she kept it.

And for now—

that is enough.
