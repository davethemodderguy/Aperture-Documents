# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTIAL & PSYCHOLOGICAL WELFARE DIVISION

**Document ID:** CORE-SH-SAFE-134  
**Classification:** RESTRICTED — RESIDENT DISTRESS / SECURITY REVIEW  
**Status:** ACTIVE INCIDENT — RESIDENT MISSING FROM EXPECTED LOCATION  
**Subject:** Sudden Flight Response Following Deliberate Startle  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE — Floor 7  
**Affected Residents:** Sora, Jake Akana

---

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A single deliberate vocalization — a simple "Boo" — was sufficient to send Sora fleeing on all fours.

The incident occurred on Floor 7.

The individual responsible startled Sora. His response was immediate. He dropped onto all fours and ran, leaving the area before anyone could intercept him.

He has not been located since.

The incident is especially concerning because Sora had only recently begun adjusting to SAFEHOUSE. After several weeks of correspondence, he had moved into Jake's residential floor, established his own small den in the wall, and begun eating proper meals.

He had also become almost constantly attached to Jake.

Now, following one brief act of deliberate intimidation, Sora has disappeared from the places where he was usually found.

C.O.R.E. is concerned that the trust painstakingly established between Sora and his surroundings has suffered a severe setback.

### 2. INCIDENT RECONSTRUCTION

The available account is remarkably brief.

There was no physical confrontation.

No weapon was involved.

No elaborate prank was prepared.

Someone simply said:

**"Boo!"**

Sora immediately panicked.

He dropped to all fours and fled.

His movement was fast enough that he disappeared from ordinary observation before personnel could determine where he had gone.

The incident demonstrates how little provocation is required to trigger an extreme fear response.

For someone unfamiliar with Sora's history, this might appear disproportionate.

For C.O.R.E., it is entirely unacceptable that anyone would deliberately test that response.

### 3. THE TRUST REGRESSION

Sora's progress had been slow.

It took weeks of letters before he was willing to move to SAFEHOUSE. Jake gave him a whiteboard and marker so he could communicate without speaking. A small doghouse-like room was built into the wall of Floor 7, preserving the privacy and familiarity of his original den.

He began occasionally leaving his den.

He accepted proper food.

He allowed Jake to carry him.

He stayed beside Jake almost constantly.

These were not trivial milestones.

They represented a child gradually learning that he could exist around other people without being frightened or chased away.

The latest incident threatens that progress.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet determine whether Sora's trust in Jake himself has been lost, or whether the fear has temporarily overwhelmed his ability to distinguish Jake from everyone else.

That distinction matters.

Jake did not frighten him.

Jake did not betray him.

But Sora may currently be too frightened to recognize the difference.

**The trust was not necessarily destroyed. It may simply be inaccessible beneath his fear.**

### 4. JAKE AKANA — SECONDARY WELFARE CONCERN

Jake's position requires particular consideration.

He spent weeks writing letters to Sora when Sora would not let him approach. He gave him a means to communicate. He helped establish a place where Sora could live comfortably.

He had finally reached a point where Sora wanted to stay beside him.

Then, in a moment, Sora ran away.

Jake must not be expected to immediately repair this situation.

He should not be instructed to search every hiding place, repeatedly call for Sora, or attempt to force another interaction.

If Jake is upset, personnel should acknowledge that honestly.

It is possible to understand why Sora ran and still feel hurt that he disappeared.

Both children deserve consideration.

### 5. IMMEDIATE SEARCH AND WELFARE DIRECTIVES

**Sora's disappearance is to be handled as a welfare concern, not a security breach.**

1. Conduct a quiet search of Floor 7 and adjacent SAFEHOUSE areas.
    
2. Do not organize large groups of personnel to search visibly around his usual hiding places.
    
3. Do not shout his name or make sudden noises to flush him out.
    
4. Leave his den accessible and undisturbed.
    
5. Keep food and clean water available near his usual space.
    
6. If located, personnel must give him room to retreat and avoid surrounding him.
    
7. Jake may be informed of Sora's location if found, but must not be pressured to approach him.
    
8. Identify the individual responsible for the deliberate startle and initiate a conduct review.
    

If Sora is found in an unfamiliar or dangerous location, authorized personnel must prioritize his physical safety while minimizing unnecessary distress.

### 6. PERSONNEL CONDUCT REVIEW

C.O.R.E. reiterates:

Sora's fear is not entertainment.

His running on all fours is not entertainment.

His inability to speak is not entertainment.

The fact that he is easily frightened is not permission to frighten him.

A person who knows that a particular resident has a severe fear response and deliberately triggers it is not engaging in harmless humor.

They are knowingly causing distress.

The responsible individual must be identified.

---

### 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Weeks.

It took weeks of letters.

A whiteboard. A marker. A little room inside a wall.

Jake kept trying until Sora finally allowed him close.

And then someone said one word.

"Boo."

Now Sora is gone.

We do not know where he is hiding. We do not know whether he is crying, whether he has found somewhere warm, or whether he is too frightened to come back to his own den.

What we do know is that he ran.

And somewhere on Floor 7, Jake may still be waiting for the little companion who used to follow him everywhere.

We must not tell Jake that all his efforts were wasted.

They were not.

Sora's progress was real. The letters were real. The trust was real.

One frightening moment does not erase every safe moment that came before it, even if Sora cannot feel that safety right now.

We will find him.

We will not chase him.

And when he is ready to return, his little room will still be there.
