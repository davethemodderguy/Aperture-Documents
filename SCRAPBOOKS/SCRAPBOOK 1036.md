**SUBJECT:** Neri — Progressive Stamina Decline and Cardiovascular Strain

---

## Executive Summary

Neri's physical stamina is continuing to deteriorate.

The decline is gradual but persistent.

She remains capable of walking, eating, drinking, moving around the SAFEHOUSE, and performing ordinary activities when she has sufficient energy.

However, the amount of energy available to her is becoming progressively smaller.

One of the clearest behavioral indicators is her speech.

> **Neri is talking less and less.**

This is not because she has forgotten how to speak.

It is because speaking costs energy, and Neri is increasingly choosing not to spend what little energy she has on unnecessary conversation.

---

## Current Hematological Condition

Neri's red blood cells continue to be incorrectly targeted by her own white blood cells.

Her immune system is effectively treating her red blood cells as something that should be eliminated.

The result is a continuous problem:

**Red blood cells are being destroyed.**

**The body attempts to compensate.**

**The cardiovascular system works harder.**

**The supply still cannot remain stable.**

Bao's blood temporarily suppresses the abnormal white-cell activity and provides Neri with additional functional red blood cells.

It helps.

It does not permanently correct the underlying problem.

---

## Cardiovascular Strain

Neri's heart is increasingly compensating for the reduced ability of her blood to perform its normal function.

The heart is not simply experiencing occasional discomfort.

It is **working harder than it should, for longer than it should.**

This is particularly concerning because Neri already possesses a fragile cardiovascular system.

The situation can therefore be summarized internally as:

> **Her heart is trying to compensate for a problem that keeps coming back.**

The heart does not understand that Neri is tired.

It simply keeps working.

---

## Speech Decline

Neri's speech frequency has decreased noticeably.

Earlier:

> “I'm tired.”

Later:

> “Tired.”

More recently:

> _Neri shakes her head and sits down._

This progression should not be interpreted as Neri becoming less communicative by choice alone.

She still communicates when necessary.

She simply has increasingly little stamina available for speech.

Her remaining verbalizations are therefore becoming increasingly selective.

She may speak to report:

- pain;
    
- dizziness;
    
- motion sickness;
    
- exhaustion;
    
- difficulty breathing;
    
- something immediately important.
    

She is much less likely to speak simply for conversation.

## Observed Interaction

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE LOUNGE

Neri was sitting quietly.

Bao entered.

> **Bao:** “How are you feeling?”

Neri looked up.

There was a long pause.

> **Neri:** “Tired.”

Bao sat nearby.

> **Bao:** “Heart?”

Neri placed a hand over her chest.

A small nod.

> **Bao:** “Hurting?”

Another nod.

Bao immediately became more attentive.

Neri lowered her hand.

> **Neri:** “It's okay.”

Bao shook her head.

> **Bao:** “No, it isn't.”

Neri did not answer.

She simply closed her eyes.

She was too tired to continue the conversation.

## Jane Doe Observation

Jane has also noticed the change.

Neri used to occasionally speak during transportation.

Now, increasingly, she simply rests against Jane's back or shoulder.

During one recent walk:

> **Jane:** “You comfortable?”

Neri nodded.

> **Jane:** “Want me to slow down?”

Neri nodded again.

Jane slowed.

Several minutes passed.

> **Jane:** “Anything else?”

Neri shook her head.

Jane did not ask again.

## C.O.R.E. Assessment

Neri's silence must not be mistaken for improvement.

In her case, **less speech can mean less available stamina.**

This is especially important because Neri has historically been reluctant to interrupt other people's work or admit when she is struggling.

Personnel should therefore pay attention to behavioral changes without demanding verbal confirmation.

If Neri is unusually still, unusually quiet, or stops participating in an activity she normally tolerates, personnel should consider fatigue as one possible explanation.

However:

> **Do not force her to talk about it.**

If she needs assistance, make assistance available.

---

## Long-Term Concern

The current trajectory is concerning.

Neri's anemia is not simply making her tired.

Her reduced blood-cell availability is placing additional demand on an already fragile cardiovascular system.

Every day that the underlying process continues, Neri has less room to compensate.

Her shrinking stamina is becoming visible in ordinary things:

- shorter conversations;
    
- longer periods of rest;
    
- less walking;
    
- more frequent sitting;
    
- increased reliance on transportation;
    
- reduced willingness to expend energy unnecessarily.
    

And eventually:

> Even talking can become something she has to decide whether she can afford.

---

## Final Note

Neri once spent entire days in the background because she preferred quiet.

Now there are times when she is quiet because **she genuinely does not have much energy left.**

Those two things must not be confused.

She still likes being alone.

She still prefers silence.

She still does not want people constantly hovering around her.

But her body is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

Her heart keeps working.

Her red blood cells keep being targeted.

And Neri keeps spending less and less energy on anything that isn't necessary.
