# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Further information regarding Anby has been recovered from the seized Project: Silverline archives.

Several previous assumptions regarding Anby's background require revision.

Anby was not merely an experimental subject who happened to possess unusually capable physical characteristics.

She was **deployed.**

Project: Silverline produced Anby and at least eleven other artificial humans as part of a larger program.

Anby has:

> **11 sisters.**

Their current whereabouts are unknown.

All twelve were repeatedly deployed for combat operations by Project: Silverline.

Anby's apparent knowledge of combat is therefore not theoretical or artificially implanted in the conventional sense.

She has **experience.**

She remembers portions of it.

She remembers her sisters.

She remembers being deployed.

She remembers returning.

And she remembers being treated as an experiment afterward.

This has produced a persistent identity conflict:

> **"Am I a real person?"**

versus

> **"Am I just an experiment?"**

C.O.R.E. considers this question one of the most significant welfare concerns surrounding Anby's recovery.

---

# 1. APPEARANCE

Anby has white hair.

The hair is naturally occurring within her artificial physiology and requires no anomalous explanation.

She generally wears a small, simple dress.

The clothing is intentionally non-military.

This is significant.

Project: Silverline records repeatedly depict Anby and her sisters wearing standardized operational equipment during deployments.

Her current clothing represents a deliberate separation from that environment.

Phoenix selected the first civilian clothing provided to Anby.

Anby has continued wearing it.

---

# 2. PHYSICAL CAPABILITY

Anby's physical capabilities remain considerably above ordinary human baseline.

She possesses:

- exceptional balance;
    
- high reaction speed;
    
- significant endurance;
    
- precise motor control;
    
- rapid physical adaptation;
    
- and unusually efficient movement.
    

None of these characteristics are currently considered sufficient to classify Anby as an anomalous weapon.

Her body was designed.

Her personhood was not.

---

# 3. COMBAT KNOWLEDGE

The discovery of Silverline deployment records explains Anby's unusual combat knowledge.

During controlled assessment, Anby demonstrated familiarity with:

- close-quarters movement;
    
- defensive positioning;
    
- threat awareness;
    
- environmental awareness;
    
- coordinated movement with teammates;
    
- weapon handling principles;
    
- and rapid response to unexpected attacks.
    

She did not appear to be learning these concepts.

She already knew them.

When asked where she learned them:

> **ANBY:**  
> "Training."

The evaluator asked:

> **"How long?"**

Anby paused.

> **ANBY:**  
> "I don't know."

The evaluator asked:

> **"Who trained you?"**

Anby answered:

> **ANBY:**  
> "My sisters."

---

# 4. THE TWELVE

Project: Silverline records refer to a group of twelve artificial humans.

Anby was one of them.

The recovered documentation does not provide sufficient information to establish their current locations.

The other eleven are currently classified as:

> **MISSING**

They were apparently developed together.

Trained together.

Deployed together.

And repeatedly returned to the Silverline facility together.

The records frequently refer to them collectively rather than individually.

This suggests that their interpersonal relationship was significant even within the project's own documentation.

---

# 5. THE SISTERS

Anby refers to the other eleven as:

> **"My sisters."**

C.O.R.E. has not identified evidence that the relationship was purely genetic.

The term appears to describe their social and emotional relationship.

They were raised together.

They trained together.

They ate together.

They slept in the same facility.

They were deployed together.

They returned together.

Whatever Project: Silverline intended them to be, the twelve developed a familial relationship.

Anby remembers this.

---

# 6. DEPLOYMENT HISTORY

Recovered Silverline records indicate that the group was deployed multiple times.

The exact nature of the missions remains classified under the ongoing T.O.P.S. investigation.

However, records consistently indicate:

> **Twelve deployed.**

> **Twelve expected to return.**

The sisters were treated as a coordinated unit.

Anby therefore possesses practical combat experience that is considerably more extensive than previously assumed.

This experience should not be mistaken for enthusiasm.

Anby does not appear particularly proud of it.

When shown a recovered deployment photograph, she stared at it for several seconds.

Then:

> **ANBY:**  
> "We were good."

The researcher asked:

> **"At fighting?"**

Anby shook her head.

> **ANBY:**  
> "At staying together."

---

# 7. THE MISSING SISTERS

Anby's greatest current unresolved concern is the location of her sisters.

She has repeatedly asked whether T.O.P.S. has found them.

The answer remains:

> **No confirmed locations.**

Anby has accepted this answer.

She has not accepted the possibility that they may never be found.

She continues to believe they are somewhere.

This belief appears to provide significant emotional stability.

---

# 8. IDENTITY CONFLICT

Anby demonstrates persistent uncertainty regarding her own status.

The conflict can be summarized as:

> **PERSON**

versus

> **EXPERIMENT**

She understands intellectually that she is an artificial human.

She understands that Silverline created her.

She understands that her body was designed.

However, she also remembers experiences that were not described as experiments.

She remembers:

- laughing with her sisters;
    
- eating with them;
    
- becoming tired;
    
- being frightened;
    
- looking forward to returning home;
    
- worrying about her sisters;
    
- missing people;
    
- and wanting things.
    

These memories create a contradiction in her understanding.

If she was only an experiment:

> **Why did those things matter to her?**

If she is a person:

> **Why was she created as an experiment?**

C.O.R.E. does not consider this a question that can be solved by simply telling Anby:

> "You're a person."

The issue is deeper than terminology.

---

# 9. CONVERSATION WITH PHOENIX

Phoenix was the first person Anby directly discussed the issue with.

The conversation occurred late in the evening.

Anby was sitting on the floor.

Phoenix sat nearby.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Do you think I'm real?"

Phoenix looked at her.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Yes."

Anby looked down.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Even though I'm artificial?"

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Yes."

> **ANBY:**  
> "Even though I was made?"

Phoenix paused.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "People make things all the time."

Anby looked at her.

Phoenix continued:

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "That doesn't mean they get to decide whether those things matter."

Anby remained silent.

Then:

> **ANBY:**  
> "What if I'm only supposed to be useful?"

Phoenix's expression changed.

She understood the question immediately.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Then they were wrong."

Anby looked at her.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You don't have to be useful."

A long pause.

> **ANBY:**  
> "You believe that?"

Phoenix looked away.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "...I'm trying to."

---

# 10. PHOENIX'S ASSIGNMENT — UPDATED

The KCA has formally expanded Phoenix's responsibilities.

Phoenix is not required to:

- train Anby;
    
- command Anby;
    
- evaluate Anby's combat effectiveness;
    
- determine Anby's purpose;
    
- or decide what Anby should become.
    

Her role is instead to provide:

> **Safety.**

> **Choice.**

> **Stability.**

> **Access to care.**

> **Protection from exploitation.**

Phoenix has been explicitly instructed not to recreate the hierarchical relationship Anby experienced under Silverline.

---

# 11. COMBAT DEMONSTRATION

During a controlled assessment, Anby was asked to demonstrate her physical capabilities.

She initially refused.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Why?"

The evaluator explained that the purpose was simply to understand her capabilities.

Anby considered this.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Am I being tested?"

> **EVALUATOR:**  
> "No."

Anby looked toward Phoenix.

Phoenix nodded.

Anby agreed.

The demonstration proceeded.

Anby's movements were precise.

Fast.

Efficient.

Almost automatic.

At one point, the evaluator changed the exercise unexpectedly.

Anby's response was immediate.

She adjusted without hesitation.

When the exercise ended, she looked at Phoenix.

> **ANBY:**  
> "Was that good?"

Phoenix answered:

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You don't have to be good."

Anby blinked.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "You can just be Anby."

Anby lowered her hands.

> **ANBY:**  
> "...Okay."

---

# 12. CURRENT WELFARE CONCERN

The primary concern is not Anby's physical capability.

It is her understanding of herself.

She has spent a significant portion of her life being told, directly or indirectly:

> **You exist because someone needed something.**

She is now living among people who are attempting to communicate:

> **You exist because you exist.**

Anby has not fully accepted the second statement.

This is expected.

C.O.R.E. recommends allowing her to reach that conclusion herself rather than forcing it upon her.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Anby has white hair.

She wears a small dress.

She speaks quietly.

She lives in a SAFEHOUSE now.

She eats when she is hungry.

She sleeps when she is tired.

She can laugh.

She can become frightened.

She can miss someone.

She can remember eleven sisters who are currently missing.

And when someone asks her what she is, she sometimes doesn't know how to answer.

Because Silverline gave her a designation.

A body.

Training.

A purpose.

And eleven people she calls sisters.

But they never gave her the one thing she needed most:

**the right to decide what she is.**

Phoenix has been assigned to protect Anby.

Not to define her.

Not to command her.

Not to turn her into something useful.

Simply to make sure that, for once, nobody else gets to decide what Anby is allowed to be.

For now, that is enough.
