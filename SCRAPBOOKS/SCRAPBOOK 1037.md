## Executive Summary

Neri has returned to chemistry.

This represents a significant change from her previous relationship with the subject.

Chemistry was previously associated with:

- her childhood;
    
- her former family's evaluation of children according to usefulness;
    
- repeated dismissal of her abilities;
    
- the belief that being good at something should have made her worth keeping;
    
- and, ultimately, being discarded despite her abilities.
    

As a result, Neri had previously avoided chemistry laboratories, equipment, textbooks, demonstrations, and related discussion.

**That avoidance is no longer current.**

Neri has independently resumed participation in chemistry.

No personnel member initiated a rehabilitation program.

No member of C.O.R.E. instructed her to "reclaim" chemistry.

No one told her that she was wasting a talent.

She simply returned.

---

## Initial Observation

Neri was observed entering an R&D chemistry workspace carrying:

- her usual light-azure coat;
    
- prescription glasses;
    
- a notebook;
    
- one cup of coffee.
    

She did not announce her arrival.

She did not request assistance.

She did not explain why she was there.

She simply selected an available workstation and began working.

Approximately eleven minutes later, a researcher entering the room noticed her.

> **RESEARCHER:** "You're doing chemistry again?"

Neri looked up.

Pause.

> **NERI:** "Yes."

She returned to her work.

No further explanation was provided.

---

## Historical Significance

Personnel are reminded that Neri's return to chemistry **must not be interpreted as proof that her previous experiences no longer affect her.**

The following conclusions are specifically prohibited:

> "She's finally over it."

> "Chemistry doesn't bother her anymore."

> "We fixed her."

> "She's reclaiming her childhood."

> "She realized her family was wrong."

None of these statements are supported.

Neri's decision belongs to Neri.

She may engage with chemistry because she wants to.

She may stop again if she wants to.

She is not required to turn her former ability into a symbol of recovery.

---

## Current Academic Performance

Neri's prior competency has not disappeared.

Personnel have recorded rapid comprehension of concepts that would normally require significantly longer instruction.

She occasionally pauses for unusually long periods before answering questions.

This should not automatically be interpreted as uncertainty.

In several observed cases, Neri appeared to already know the answer but was deciding whether speaking was worth the stamina expenditure.

When written responses are acceptable, Neri generally prefers writing.

Her handwriting remains characteristically compact.

---

## Notable Interaction

During one session, a researcher asked:

> "Do you still like chemistry?"

Neri stopped writing.

She looked at the equipment.

Then at her notebook.

After approximately twenty seconds, she wrote:

> **"I don't know."**

The researcher did not press her.

Several minutes later, Neri continued working.

---

## Medical Considerations

Neri's return to chemistry does **not** override her existing medical limitations.

Her cardiovascular condition and progressive stamina reduction remain active concerns.

Chemistry sessions therefore require the same general accommodations already applicable to Neri:

- avoid unnecessary prolonged standing;
    
- allow her to stop without requiring explanation;
    
- do not require verbal participation when written communication is sufficient;
    
- do not assume silence indicates confusion;
    
- do not assume continued work indicates adequate stamina;
    
- allow transportation when she is too fatigued to walk safely;
    
- medical intervention remains mandatory if a genuine emergency occurs.
    

Most importantly:

**Neri must not feel obligated to finish an experiment simply because she started it.**

---

## Personnel Guidance

Neri's return to chemistry is not an invitation for everyone to suddenly become interested in her chemistry.

Do not crowd her workstation.

Do not ask her to demonstrate her abilities.

Do not praise her excessively.

Do not tell her that she is "wasting her potential."

Do not assign her additional work because she is capable of completing it.

Do not use phrases such as:

> "See? You were always meant to do this."

or

> "Your family was wrong about you."

If Neri wants to talk about her past, she may.

If she wants to discuss chemistry, she may.

If she wants to sit silently at a laboratory workstation for two hours and perform routine work without speaking to anyone, that is also acceptable.

---

## C.O.R.E. Assessment

There is an important distinction between **returning to something** and **being forced to confront it.**

Neri has returned.

She was not forced.

That distinction is sufficient.

Chemistry belonged to her before it belonged to her family.

It may belong to her again now.

Whether she keeps it this time is her decision.
