### RESIDENT WELFARE & PSYCHOLOGICAL DIVISION

**SUBJECT:** Jake — Severe Trust Erosion  
**TRIGGER EVENT:** Fourteen-Day Unintentional Exterior Exposure

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake's behavior has changed substantially following the fourteen-day exterior exposure incident.

The most significant change is not his fear of the weather.

It is his fear of **people.**

Jake has developed severe trust issues.

He now treats promises of safety as uncertain.

He has become reluctant to follow people through doors.

He repeatedly checks whether doors are unlocked before entering an area.

He asks where people are going.

He asks when they will return.

He watches people leave.

And, most concerningly:

> **Jake no longer assumes that someone who leaves will come back.**

---

# 1. THE FIRST CHANGE

The change was initially subtle.

Jake began standing near doors after someone opened them.

He would watch the person leave.

Then watch the door close.

He would wait.

If the person returned quickly, Jake appeared relieved.

If they took longer than expected, his heartbeat accelerated.

If the person did not return, Jake left the area.

He stopped waiting.

---

# 2. "ARE YOU COMING BACK?"

This question has become increasingly common.

Jake may ask:

> **"Are you coming back?"**

If the answer is:

> "Yes."

Jake may ask:

> **"When?"**

If given a vague answer such as:

> "Soon."

Jake becomes visibly uncomfortable.

He may ask again:

> **"How soon?"**

Personnel are advised not to make promises they cannot keep.

---

# 3. DOORS

Jake now checks doors.

Repeatedly.

Before entering a room, he may test the handle.

Before following someone outside, he may ask:

> **"Can we get back in?"**

If the answer is uncertain, he does not go.

This behavior has been observed even with residents he previously trusted.

Nora is included.

---

# 4. NORA

Nora has experienced the greatest emotional impact from the change.

Jake still cares about her.

He still approaches her.

He still allows her to sit beside him.

But he no longer follows her outside without confirmation that he can return.

On one occasion, Nora asked:

> **"Want to come outside?"**

Jake looked at the door.

Then at Nora.

> **"You'll stay?"**

Nora answered:

> **"Yes."**

Jake paused.

> **"Promise?"**

Nora nodded.

Jake still did not move.

After several seconds:

> **"And the door?"**

Nora understood.

She opened it.

Checked it.

Then demonstrated that it could be reopened from outside.

Only then did Jake follow.

---

# 5. THE SECOND CHECK

Several minutes later, Jake stopped walking.

He looked back.

The door was still visible.

He asked:

> **"It's still open?"**

Nora answered:

> **"Yes."**

Jake continued.

Approximately thirty seconds later, he looked back again.

The behavior repeated several times.

Nora eventually stopped commenting on it.

She simply allowed him to check.

---

# 6. AETERNA

Aeterna has experienced similar scrutiny.

Jake trusts Aeterna considerably.

However, even with her, he now asks:

> **"You're staying?"**

Aeterna answers honestly.

If she intends to leave:

> **"No."**

If she intends to stay:

> **"Yes."**

She does not use:

> "I'll be right back."

unless she actually intends to return shortly.

Aeterna has specifically instructed personnel not to make false reassurance statements around Jake.

Her reasoning:

> **"He does not need promises."**

> **"He needs evidence."**

---

# 7. UNFAMILIAR PERSONNEL

The change is more severe with unfamiliar adults.

Jake now frequently avoids them.

If someone approaches too quickly, he may retreat.

If someone offers to take him somewhere, he asks why.

If someone asks him to follow them, he may ask:

> **"Where?"**

Then:

> **"For how long?"**

Then:

> **"Who's coming?"**

This is a significant departure from his previous behavior.

---

# 8. PHYSICAL CONTACT

Jake's tolerance for unexpected physical contact has decreased.

Previously, he could occasionally allow residents to carry him or touch his shoulder.

He now reacts poorly to sudden contact from unfamiliar personnel.

He may immediately pull his teddy bear against himself.

The bear's heartbeat accelerates.

Jake may step backward.

He does not necessarily become aggressive.

He simply creates distance.

---

# 9. THE TEDDY BEAR

The teddy bear has become Jake's primary source of security.

He almost never leaves it unattended.

When walking:

It is with him.

When sitting:

It is against him.

When sleeping:

It is held tightly.

When frightened:

It is the first thing he reaches for.

Its heartbeat has become noticeably reactive to social situations.

When someone Jake does not trust approaches:

**The heartbeat accelerates.**

When someone he trusts sits beside him:

**It slows.**

When someone leaves unexpectedly:

**It becomes irregular.**

The bear has effectively become an external monitor of Jake's emotional security.

---

# 10. FOOD

Jake's relationship with food has also changed slightly.

He still eats.

He still enjoys cake.

He still experiences fullness.

However, he is now reluctant to accept food from unfamiliar personnel.

If someone offers him something, he may ask:

> **"Why?"**

He may wait for Nora, Bao, Neri, or another trusted resident to take it first.

This behavior is not caused by loss of appetite.

It is a trust issue.

---

# 11. THE MOST CONCERNING QUESTION

During a welfare check, Jake was asked:

> **"Who do you trust?"**

Jake remained silent for several seconds.

He looked at his teddy bear.

Then at the floor.

Eventually he named several residents.

Nora.

Neri.

Bao.

Aeterna.

He stopped.

The welfare officer asked:

> **"Anyone else?"**

Jake shook his head.

Then quietly said:

> **"I don't know yet."**

This answer is considered significant.

Jake previously developed relationships naturally.

He now appears to believe that trust must be **earned through repeated proof.**

---

# 12. "YOU PROMISED."

The phrase has begun appearing in Jake's interactions.

If someone says they will return and does not return within the expected timeframe, Jake may become distressed.

He does not necessarily confront them.

Instead, he may say:

> **"You promised."**

This statement is particularly concerning because Jake has historically been deeply affected by waiting for people who never returned.

Personnel should therefore avoid casual promises.

---

# 13. NORA'S RESPONSE

Nora has become increasingly careful.

She now tells Jake exactly what she is doing.

For example:

> **"I'm going to Neri's room."**

> **"I'll be back after that."**

Jake may ask:

> **"How long?"**

Nora answers honestly.

If she does not know:

> **"I don't know."**

Jake prefers this answer to false certainty.

---

# 14. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri has adopted an even simpler approach.

She does not promise.

She demonstrates.

If Jake asks:

> **"Are you staying?"**

Neri sits down.

If Jake asks:

> **"Can I come?"**

Neri opens the door.

If Jake asks:

> **"Will you come back?"**

Neri may simply point toward the clock and write a time.

Her communication is minimal.

But Jake appears to find it reassuring.

---

# 15. C.O.R.E. INTERPRETATION

Jake's trust issues are not irrational within the context of his experiences.

His history contains repeated examples of people disappearing.

His mother left him.

He waited.

He died.

He remained alone for decades.

His body was removed.

He was unable to follow.

He was subsequently left outside for fourteen days by accident.

From Jake's perspective, the distinction between:

> **"They forgot me."**

and

> **"They left me."**

may not be emotionally meaningful.

Both produce the same result.

He is alone.

---

# 16. CURRENT PSYCHOLOGICAL PATTERN

Jake now appears to follow a cycle:

**Someone approaches.**

↓

**Jake becomes interested.**

↓

**Jake begins assessing whether they can be trusted.**

↓

**He watches their behavior.**

↓

**They leave.**

↓

**Jake waits.**

↓

**If they return, trust increases slightly.**

↓

**If they do not, trust decreases sharply.**

This means trust recovery is currently much slower than trust loss.

---

# 17. PERSONNEL GUIDANCE

Personnel are instructed:

### Do not promise what you cannot guarantee.

Do not say:

> "I'll definitely be back."

unless you know you can return.

Do not say:

> "I won't leave."

if you intend to leave.

Do not disappear without explanation.

Do not force Jake to follow.

Do not punish him for checking doors.

Do not mock his questions.

Do not tell him:

> "You need to trust me."

Trust cannot be demanded.

It must be demonstrated.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's trust issues are severe.

However, C.O.R.E. does not interpret this as Jake becoming incapable of trust.

Quite the opposite.

He still wants to trust people.

He still wants companionship.

He still asks people to stay.

He still follows people he cares about.

He still hugs Nora.

He still sits beside Aeterna.

He still sleeps while holding his teddy bear.

The problem is that he now needs evidence before believing someone will remain.

And when someone says:

> **"I'll be back."**

Jake does not hear reassurance anymore.

He hears a question:

> **"Will you actually come back?"**

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake spent decades waiting for his mother.

Then he spent fourteen days waiting beneath a tree.

Both experiences taught him the same lesson:

**Waiting does not guarantee that someone returns.**

Now, when a door closes,

Jake watches it.

When footsteps disappear,

Jake listens.

When someone says goodbye,

Jake remembers.

And when someone tells him:

> **"I'll come back."**

he holds his teddy bear a little tighter.

Because the little boy who spent seventy-five years waiting has finally learned something he never wanted to learn:

**People can love you and still leave you behind.**

He does not know yet how to believe anything else.
