# 2. OBJECTIVE

The objective is extremely simple.

### **SURVIVE JANE DOE.**

Participants are required to remain conscious and avoid being successfully removed from the designated trial environment by Jane Doe.

The trial ends immediately when Jane successfully:

- incapacitates the participant;
    
- renders the participant unconscious;
    
- or removes the participant from the designated trial environment through kidnapping.
    

The exact method used by Jane is irrelevant.

The trial does **not** require participants to defeat Jane.

The trial does **not** require participants to interrogate Jane.

The trial does **not** require participants to restrain Jane.

The trial does **not** require participants to make Jane surrender.

The only requirement is:

> **Don't get caught.**

---

# 3. CRITICAL CLARIFICATION

C.O.R.E. has received several questions concerning the phrase:

> **“Survive Jane Doe.”**

It does **not** mean:

> “Interrogate Jane.”

It does **not** mean:

> “Extract information from Jane.”

It does **not** mean:

> “Defeat Jane in combat.”

And it certainly does not mean:

> “Try to interrogate the person who is actively trying to kidnap you.”

The participant's sole responsibility is to remain conscious and avoid capture.

---

# 4. LOSS CONDITION

The loss condition is intentionally straightforward.

If Jane knocks the participant unconscious:

> **PARTICIPANT — ELIMINATED**

If Jane successfully kidnaps the participant away from the designated trial area:

> **PARTICIPANT — ELIMINATED**

If the participant voluntarily surrenders:

> **PARTICIPANT — ELIMINATED**

If the participant is found unconscious somewhere outside the designated area:

> **PARTICIPANT — ELIMINATED**

There is no secondary scoring system.

There is no partial credit.

There is no:

> “But I almost got away.”

There is no:

> “I fought her for twenty minutes.”

There is no:

> “She only caught me because—”

No.

Jane caught you.

You lost.

---

# 5. JANE DOE — TRIAL ROLE

Jane has been designated the **Pursuer**.

She has been given complete freedom to use her established capabilities within the approved trial parameters.

Her intelligence, mobility, deception, intimidation, environmental awareness, and psychological manipulation are considered part of the challenge.

Jane is explicitly permitted to deceive participants.

She is permitted to:

- hide;
    
- stalk;
    
- approach indirectly;
    
- create distractions;
    
- exploit environmental features;
    
- impersonate normal behavior;
    
- manipulate participants into lowering their guard;
    
- wait for an opportunity;
    
- retreat and re-approach;
    
- use the environment to separate participants;
    
- and attempt surprise captures.
    

She is **not** required to announce herself.

---

# 6. PARTICIPANT EXPECTATIONS

Participants have been warned that Jane's greatest advantage is not physical strength.

It is that she is an intelligence operative.

She understands how people think.

She knows how people move when they believe they are safe.

She knows how people behave when they become paranoid.

She knows how to make someone look in the wrong direction.

And, perhaps most importantly:

> **Jane knows what people expect her to do.**

Participants have therefore been advised not to assume that obvious behavior is genuine.

# 11. SAFETY OVERSIGHT

Because the trial involves deliberate simulated pursuit and incapacitation, C.O.R.E. Medical and MSAC Safety personnel will monitor the event.

Jane's objective remains capture.

However, medical personnel retain authority to terminate a trial immediately if a participant experiences an actual medical emergency.

The trial does not override medical protocols.

A participant experiencing genuine medical distress will be removed from the trial regardless of current performance.

Neri was specifically asked whether this constituted an unfair advantage.

She answered:

# 14. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

The Hell Fire program has previously established a pattern.

The first-generation Tachyon Industries Limited Merchandise challenged participants through:

- endurance;
    
- observation;
    
- marksmanship;
    
- problem solving;
    
- patience;
    
- chemistry;
    
- and persistence.
    

The second generation appears to be moving toward something different.

**Adaptation.**

The first Hell Fire trial does not ask whether an operator can shoot accurately.

It asks whether they can recognize that they are being hunted.

And whether they can remain calm while the person hunting them is:

- faster;
    
- more experienced;
    
- highly intelligent;
    
- exceptionally difficult to predict;
    
- and already familiar with Blacksite personnel.
