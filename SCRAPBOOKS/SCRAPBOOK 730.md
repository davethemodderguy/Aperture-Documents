### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / MEDICAL OBSERVATION LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — MEMORY LOSS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **PROGRESSIVE MEMORY LOSS / UNDER OBSERVATION**

---

# **INITIAL OBSERVATION**

Alice began forgetting things.

At first, they were small things.

Where she had left her pencil.

What she had eaten earlier.

Which notebook she had been using.

Nobody was immediately alarmed.

Alice had always possessed fragmented memories.

But then the forgotten things became more important.

---

# **RECENT MEMORY LOSS**

Alice began forgetting events that had happened only days earlier.

She forgot that Star had found her beneath the tree during the heavy rain.

She remembered being cold.

She remembered being wet.

But she couldn't remember Star carrying her home.

She forgot portions of the week she spent recovering in the Treehouse.

She forgot conversations with Corin.

She forgot some of the grammar lessons with Lumi and Star.

She even occasionally forgot that she had lost her voice.

When asked why she wasn't speaking, Alice simply pointed at her throat.

Then wrote:

> **"Alice forgot."**

---

# **CORIN**

Corin noticed the pattern first.

She asked:

> **"Do you remember yesterday?"**

Alice shook her head.

Corin tried another question.

> **"Do you remember this morning?"**

Alice hesitated.

Then shook her head again.

Corin became visibly worried.

She pointed at herself.

> **"Who am I?"**

Alice looked at her for several seconds.

Then smiled.

She wrote:

> **"Big sis."**

Corin exhaled.

She hugged Alice.

Alice hugged back.

The relationship remained.

Somehow, the details were disappearing around it.

---

# **WHAT REMAINS**

Alice's memory does not appear to be disappearing uniformly.

Certain things remain remarkably persistent.

She knows:

**Corin is important.**

**Orphie is important.**

**Lumi is important.**

**Star is important.**

She knows the Treehouse is safe.

She knows she loves flowers.

She knows she likes being carried when she becomes tired.

She knows Corin makes soup.

She knows Alice is Alice.

At least for now.

---

# **THE NOTEBOOK**

Alice has begun using her notebooks differently.

She writes reminders to herself.

> **"Corin is Big Sis."**

> **"Orphie is Bigger Sis."**

> **"Lumi teaches words."**

> **"Star helps Alice."**

> **"Belle carries Alice."**

> **"Neko made scooter."**

Some pages simply say:

> **"Remember."**

Nobody knows whether Alice writes these because she is afraid of forgetting—

or because she already has.

---

# **ONE PARTICULARLY DISTRESSING INCIDENT**

Alice woke from a nap.

Corin was sitting beside her.

Alice looked at Corin for several seconds.

She became visibly confused.

She reached for her notebook.

Her hands trembled.

She wrote:

> **"Who?"**

Corin's heart sank.

She didn't correct Alice.

She simply answered:

> **"I'm Corin."**

Alice stared.

Corin continued:

> **"I'm your Big Sis."**

Alice looked at the words.

Then at Corin.

Her expression softened.

She slowly moved closer.

She rested her head against Corin.

Then wrote:

> **"Big Sis."**

Corin hugged her.

> **"Yeah."**

---

# **MEDICAL CONCERN**

The cause remains unknown.

Alice's prolonged malnutrition, previous injuries, chronic illness, anomalous physiology, and prolonged psychological stress are all being considered.

Her natural regeneration may also be involved.

One possibility being investigated is that Alice's regeneration prioritizes **physical survival over neurological restoration**, potentially leaving cognitive functions increasingly vulnerable when resources are insufficient.

This remains a hypothesis.

---

# **NEW PRECAUTIONS**

Alice now carries a small identification card.

It contains:

- Her name
    
- Her SAFEHOUSE designation
    
- Her location
    
- Trusted contacts
    
- A simple message:
    

> **"If Alice is confused, stay with her."**

Her notebooks are also being preserved.

The SAFEHOUSE has begun documenting important memories with Alice's permission.

Not to force her to remember.

But so that, if she forgets, she can someday read what happened.

---

# **CORIN'S RULE**

Corin established one simple rule:

> **"If Alice forgets something, we tell her again."**

No frustration.

No disappointment.

No:

> _"You already know this."_

Just again.

And again.

As many times as necessary.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice is beginning to lose pieces of her own life.

Some memories disappear completely.

Others remain only as feelings.

She may forget a conversation but remember that someone cared.

She may forget an event but remember that she was safe.

She may forget a name but still know that person is important.

For Alice, memory is becoming less like a book—

and more like scattered pieces of paper.

The SAFEHOUSE cannot guarantee that none will disappear.

But they can make sure she never has to face the blank spaces alone.

If Alice forgets who they are tomorrow,

they will introduce themselves again.

If she forgets tomorrow's events,

they will tell her again.

If she forgets yesterday,

they will sit beside her today.

And if she ever looks at Corin and asks:

> **"Who are you?"**

Corin will simply smile.

> **"I'm your Big Sis."**
