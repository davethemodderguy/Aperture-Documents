# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has acquired a room.

Technically, it is a storage locker.

More specifically, it is an **unused storage locker** that had been sitting at the end of the SAFEHOUSE storage wing with no assigned contents.

Jake has apparently decided that this qualifies as housing.

C.O.R.E. has reviewed the situation.

There is currently no compelling reason to disagree.

---

# 1. THE SIGN

The first indication that Jake had claimed the locker was a small wooden board attached to the outside.

It reads:

> **Jake's Room**  
> **please knock, I can't take suprises**

The spelling of **"suprises"** has not been corrected.

No resident has volunteered to correct it.

---

# 2. THE INTERIOR

The locker has been cleaned.

Jake has arranged his belongings inside with surprising care.

Current contents include:

- one small bedroll;
    
- one pillow;
    
- several blankets;
    
- his teddy bear;
    
- several books;
    
- a small box containing personal items;
    
- a cup;
    
- a few pieces of stationery;
    
- several drawings;
    
- a small lamp;
    
- and a plate which apparently exists primarily because Jake likes having a plate.
    

There is also a small amount of food.

Jake has specifically ensured that there is enough space for the door to close without touching anything.

This is likely intentional.

---

# 3. WHY A STORAGE LOCKER?

C.O.R.E. personnel asked Jake why he selected a storage locker instead of an available residential room.

Jake's response:

> **"It's quiet."**

Pause.

> **"Nobody goes in here."**

Pause.

> **"I can hear people outside."**

He then looked at the wooden sign.

> **"They'll knock."**

No further explanation was provided.

---

# 4. THE DOOR

The door is now considered the most important feature of Jake's room.

Not because it provides exceptional security.

It does not.

It is a normal storage locker door.

Jake simply likes knowing that it separates his room from the hallway.

The door gives him a clear boundary.

People outside can be heard.

People inside cannot simply appear.

This appears to be extremely important to him.

---

# 5. THE KNOCKING RULE

The sign is not decorative.

It is an explicit request.

Personnel entering Jake's room are expected to:

1. stand outside;
    
2. knock;
    
3. wait;
    
4. announce themselves if necessary;
    
5. wait for Jake to respond or otherwise indicate that entry is acceptable.
    

The instruction applies even when the door is open.

Jake has specifically stated:

> **"Open doesn't mean come in."**

C.O.R.E. has added this to the resident welfare guidelines.

---

# 6. SURPRISES

Jake's stated reason for the rule is simple:

> **"I can't take surprises."**

This is consistent with his recent behavioral profile.

Unexpected events frequently cause Jake to become visibly distressed.

Sudden doors opening.

Unexpected physical contact.

Someone appearing behind him.

Being approached without warning.

Someone entering a room without announcing themselves.

These events can cause him to immediately reach for his teddy bear.

The bear's heartbeat typically changes before Jake says anything.

---

# 7. FIRST UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY

The first incident occurred approximately three hours after Jake moved in.

An employee opened the door without knocking.

Jake was sitting on his bedroll.

He immediately startled.

The teddy bear tightened against his back.

Its heartbeat accelerated.

Jake stared at the employee.

The employee froze.

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> "Oh. Sorry."

Jake remained silent.

The employee slowly closed the door.

Approximately ten seconds later:

**Knock. Knock.**

A pause.

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> "Can I come in?"

Jake looked toward the door.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yes."

The door opened.

The employee apologized again.

Jake nodded.

No further incident occurred.

---

# 8. THE SECOND INCIDENT

A second resident later approached the room.

They read the sign.

They knocked.

Nothing happened.

They knocked again.

Still nothing.

They waited.

Approximately two minutes later:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Come in."

The resident opened the door.

Jake was sitting inside reading.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "You okay?"

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I like that you knocked."

The resident looked at the sign.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "That's what the sign says."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yes."

He returned to reading.

---

# 9. THE LOCKER IS NOW A ROOM

Following several observations, C.O.R.E. has officially stopped referring to the space as a storage locker in resident-facing documentation.

It is now:

> **Jake's Room.**

The storage wing has been updated accordingly.

A small label has been added to the facility map.

**JAKE — RESIDENT ROOM**

No permanent structural modification has been performed.

No modification is currently considered necessary.

---

# 11. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

The storage locker is not currently considered a suitable emergency shelter.

It is also not an approved secure containment structure.

However, Jake's continued use of the space is not inherently dangerous.

The following modifications have therefore been authorized:

- emergency release mechanism;
    
- interior lighting;
    
- ventilation inspection;
    
- removal from general storage assignment;
    
- clear identification on facility maps;
    
- no automatic locking mechanism;
    
- no storage of hazardous materials nearby;
    
- routine welfare checks conducted **from outside the door** unless an emergency occurs.
    

The final point is intentional.

---

# 12. THE IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE

Jake's previous experience with storage spaces was negative.

He was once trapped inside a storage locker for more than five hours.

He did not call for help.

He waited.

He assumed someone would eventually find him.

That incident resulted in significant physical and emotional distress.

The current situation is therefore unusual.

Jake has voluntarily chosen another storage locker.

The difference is:

**This time, he controls the door.**

He chose the location.

He placed the sign.

He established the rules.

He knows people are nearby.

And, most importantly:

> **He expects people to knock.**

---

# 13. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jake's choice of residence should not be interpreted as a desire for confinement.

It appears to represent something much simpler.

Privacy.

Predictability.

A door.

A small space that belongs to him.

A place where nobody enters without warning.

Jake cannot leave the SAFEHOUSE permanently.

He cannot return to the Starlands.

He cannot undo what happened to him.

He cannot remove the chain that binds his existence to this reality.

But inside this little storage locker, he can at least decide:

**Who comes in.**

And:

**When.**

That distinction matters.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The sign remains on the door.

The handwriting is uneven.

The wood is cheap.

The spelling is incorrect.

Nobody has fixed it.

Nobody intends to.

Because it says exactly what Jake wants people to know.

> **Jake's Room**  
> **please knock, I can't take suprises**

So people knock.

And Jake opens the door when he is ready.

Sometimes he lets people in.

Sometimes he doesn't.

Sometimes Nora comes inside and immediately starts talking.

Sometimes Neri leaves cake outside.

Sometimes Aeterna stands outside for a while without saying anything.

And sometimes Jake simply sits inside with his teddy bear and reads.

The door stays closed.

The sign stays up.

For once, the boundary is his.

**— C.O.R.E. Resident Welfare Division**
