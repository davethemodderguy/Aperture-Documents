## Executive Summary

For a continuous period of approximately two weeks following her return to chemistry, Neri's Dell laptop accumulated an extraordinary volume of research material.

At first, personnel assumed this represented a renewed interest in chemistry.

By the end of the second week, that assessment had become inadequate.

Neri had filled the machine with research notes, papers, simulations, diagrams, calculations, cross-references, experimental records, and several folders whose contents could not immediately be categorized.

The material covered conventional chemistry.

It also extended into areas considerably beyond what Neri had previously demonstrated interest in.

Most concerningly:

**Neko did not understand all of it.**

---

## I. DEVICE ACTIVITY

Neri's laptop showed unusually high activity during the observation period.

### Recorded material included:

- chemical reaction studies;
    
- molecular structure analysis;
    
- material behavior;
    
- computational models;
    
- biological-chemical interactions;
    
- anomalous material observations;
    
- mathematical modeling;
    
- energy-transfer calculations;
    
- several internally developed theoretical frameworks;
    
- extensive handwritten-equivalent digital notes;
    
- experimental hypotheses;
    
- references to Blacksite observations;
    
- and approximately **[REDACTED]** pages of material that Neko classified as "I have no idea what I'm looking at."
    

Neri did not appear to be copying existing research.

Large portions contained original annotations, modifications, corrections, and alternative explanations.

---

## II. NEKO'S REVIEW

Neko initially opened several of Neri's files expecting to find conventional chemistry research.

He instead encountered a 47-page document containing equations, diagrams, molecular structures, and several sections written entirely in Neri's preferred notation.

Neko reviewed the document for approximately 38 minutes.

He eventually sent the following internal message:

> **NEKO:**  
> "Neri."

> **NERI:**  
> "?"

> **NEKO:**  
> "What is this?"

Neri looked at the screen.

> **NERI:**  
> "Research."

> **NEKO:**  
> "I know it's research."

> **NEKO:**  
> "I mean what is it about?"

Neri paused.

> **NERI:**  
> "Chemistry."

Neko reportedly stared at the screen for several seconds.

> **NEKO:**  
> "That is technically an answer."

Neri returned to her work.

---

## III. SECOND REVIEW

Neko subsequently requested permission to inspect additional material.

Neri gave a brief nod.

The review did not improve matters.

One folder contained a document titled:

> **`reaction_model_17_final_FINAL2_actualfinal.nrd`**

The file contained a model Neko initially believed to be an attempt at predicting a chemical interaction.

It was.

It was also apparently doing something else.

Neko could identify approximately 71% of the underlying chemistry.

The remaining material involved mathematical relationships and theoretical assumptions he could not confidently reconstruct.

His assessment:

> **"The chemistry is understandable."**

> **"The problem is that she appears to be using it to investigate something I don't recognize."**

---

## IV. NERI'S WORKING HABITS

During the two-week period, Neri demonstrated several unusual behaviors.

She would sometimes work for hours without speaking.

She frequently kept her coffee beside the laptop.

She rarely asked anyone for assistance.

When encountering an error, she generally did not seek help.

She would instead stare at the relevant section, alter several variables, run the calculation again, and continue.

Her research was not performed continuously.

Neri would frequently stop working, close the laptop, and leave.

Several hours later, she would return and continue from exactly where she had stopped.

No evidence indicates that she was attempting to hide the research.

She simply appeared to consider it private work.

---

## V. THE MOST CONCERNING DISCOVERY

On Day 12, Neko discovered a folder containing research that appeared to combine several disciplines.

The folder was approximately **[REDACTED] GB**.

Its contents included:

**CHEMISTRY**

**BIOLOGY**

**MATERIAL SCIENCE**

**THERMODYNAMICS**

**ANOMALOUS PHYSICS**

**BLACKSITE OBSERVATIONS**

**UNRESOLVED**

The final directory contained 23 files.

Neko could not determine what several of them were attempting to model.

One file had no title.

It consisted almost entirely of diagrams.

At the bottom of the final page, Neri had written:

> **"This doesn't behave like it should."**

Below it:

> **"Try again."**

And beneath that:

> **"Don't assume the rules are the same."**

## VI. C.O.R.E. INTERVIEW

Neri was eventually asked whether she understood all of her own research.

She shook her head.

> **RESEARCHER:** "So some of this is experimental?"

Neri nodded.

> **RESEARCHER:** "And some of it is theoretical?"

Neri nodded again.

> **RESEARCHER:** "And some of it you don't understand?"

A pause.

Neri nodded.

> **RESEARCHER:** "Then why keep researching it?"

Neri looked at the laptop.

After several seconds:

> **NERI:**  
> "Because I want to know."

No further questioning was conducted.

Neri continues to research.

She has not indicated that she intends to stop.

C.O.R.E. has found no evidence that the research is malicious, externally directed, or connected to SPAWN.

**Research.**
