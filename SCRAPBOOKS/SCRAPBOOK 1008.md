## Executive Summary

Neri has recently begun drinking coffee.

The behavior is voluntary and appears recreational. There is currently no indication that Neri is using coffee as a substitute for food, medication, or Bao's blood-support treatment.

The more relevant consideration is that Neri **cannot currently speak** following the vocal-cord injury sustained during her previous medical crisis.

Unlike Alice during her own period of aphonia, Neri does **not** compensate for the loss of speech by writing frequently.

Neri writes when she considers communication necessary.

Often, she does not.

Her usual responses consist of:

- Nodding.
    
- Shaking her head.
    
- Pointing.
    
- Brief gestures.
    
- Looking toward an object or person.
    
- Handing someone an item.
    
- Using facial expressions.
    
- Remaining silent.
    

In many situations, Neri simply allows the interaction to end without providing a written response.

This is consistent with her established tendency to avoid bothering other people.

---

## I. Coffee Discovery

The first confirmed observation occurred in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria.

Neri entered alone and prepared a cup of coffee.

She added sugar, sat at her usual table, opened a book, and began drinking.

No announcement was made.

No note was written.

No attempt was made to explain the new behavior.

She simply drank the coffee.

Jane Doe eventually entered the cafeteria.

Jane looked at the cup.

Then at Neri.

Then back at the cup.

**JANE:**  
"...Is that coffee?"

Neri looked up.

She nodded.

**JANE:**  
"Since when?"

Neri gave a small shrug.

She returned to her book.

Jane waited.

**JANE:**  
"That's it?"

Neri looked at her.

A second shrug.

Jane eventually sat down.

---

## II. Communication Considerations

The above exchange is representative of Neri's current communication style.

Neri **could** have written an explanation.

She did not.

This should not automatically be interpreted as inability to communicate.

Neri remains capable of writing.

Rather, she often appears to make a conscious or habitual decision that writing is unnecessary.

This distinction is important.

### Alice's Previous Aphonia

During Alice's period of inability to speak, she compensated heavily through written communication.

Alice would frequently:

- Write messages.
    
- Pass notes.
    
- Use written explanations.
    
- Communicate thoughts in detail.
    
- Initiate conversations through writing.
    

### Neri's Current Aphonia

Neri behaves differently.

She generally communicates only enough to accomplish the immediate interaction.

For example:

**Question:** "Do you want coffee?"

**Neri:** _nods._

**Question:** "Do you want sugar?"

**Neri:** _points toward the sugar._

**Question:** "Are you okay?"

Neri may simply nod.

If the answer is more complicated, she may write.

But she may also choose not to.

This creates an important welfare concern:

**Silence cannot be interpreted as confirmation that Neri is fine.**

---

## III. Why Neri Writes Less

C.O.R.E. assessment suggests several overlapping factors.

Neri already possessed a strong tendency to avoid interrupting people before losing her voice.

Following the failed birthday and subsequent establishment of the **Non-Interference Rule**, this tendency intensified.

Writing requires:

1. Getting someone's attention.
    
2. Producing the message.
    
3. Handing or showing it to them.
    
4. Potentially creating a conversation.
    
5. Potentially taking up their time.
    

Neri appears capable of mentally categorizing this entire process as:

> **"Bothering someone."**

Consequently, if something can be communicated with a nod, Neri will generally nod.

If it can be communicated with a gesture, she will gesture.

If it does not absolutely need to be communicated, she may say nothing at all.

---

## IV. Coffee and Silent Routine

Neri's coffee habit has therefore developed into another quiet part of her routine.

A typical observation now consists of:

Neri enters cafeteria.

Neri prepares coffee.

Neri adds sugar.

Neri sits down.

Neri reads.

Neri drinks coffee.

Neri occasionally looks at someone approaching.

Neri may acknowledge them with a nod.

No conversation necessarily follows.

No written message necessarily follows.

She simply continues.

---

## V. Interaction With Jane Doe

Jane has gradually learned not to interpret Neri's silence as rejection.

One recorded exchange:

**JANE:**  
"You like it?"

Neri nods.

**JANE:**  
"Coffee?"

Nod.

**JANE:**  
"Want me to make you another one?"

Neri shakes her head.

**JANE:**  
"Too much?"

Neri shakes her head.

Jane pauses.

**JANE:**  
"You just don't want another one."

Neri nods.

**JANE:**  
"Got it."

Neri returns to her book.

No further communication occurs.

---

## VI. Interaction With Bao

Bao has taken a somewhat different approach.

Rather than requiring Neri to verbally or physically report everything, Bao has become accustomed to watching for changes in behavior.

This is especially important medically.

Neri may not write:

> "I'm feeling weak."

She may instead:

- Stop reading.
    
- Put down her cup.
    
- Sit unusually still.
    
- Lean against the table.
    
- Become pale.
    
- Avoid eye contact.
    
- Attempt to continue normally despite deterioration.
    

Therefore, personnel are reminded:

> **Neri's lack of a written complaint does not constitute a medical clearance.**

Her inability to speak must not become an additional reason for staff to wait for her to request help.

---

## VII. Comparison With Alice

C.O.R.E. specifically rejects the assumption that all non-speaking residents communicate similarly.

Alice's solution to aphonia was essentially:

> **"Fine. I'll write everything."**

Neri's solution is closer to:

> **"If I can communicate it without making anyone stop what they're doing, I'll do that."**

And sometimes:

> **"It isn't important enough to say."**

The latter is the greater concern.

Neri has already demonstrated a tendency to underestimate the importance of her own needs.

Her inability to speak therefore does not simply create a communication barrier.

It can **hide the fact that she has decided not to communicate at all.**

---

## VIII. Current Assessment

Neri's reduced writing frequency is not considered evidence of cognitive decline or inability to write.

She remains capable of written communication.

She simply uses it sparingly.

Personnel should therefore avoid repeatedly demanding:

> "Write it down."

unless the information is genuinely necessary.

Instead, simple questions and yes/no options are preferred when appropriate.

More importantly, staff should continue observing Neri's physical condition rather than relying exclusively on self-report.

Neri should not have to produce a written explanation before receiving assistance.

---

## IX. Final Observation

Later that evening, Jane found Neri sitting alone with her coffee.

Jane sat beside her.

Neri looked at Jane.

Jane looked at the coffee.

**JANE:**  
"Still good?"

Neri nodded.

Jane smiled.

No further conversation occurred.

Neri continued reading.

Jane stayed.

Neither of them needed to write anything.
