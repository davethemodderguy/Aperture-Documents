# SAFEHOUSE MEDICAL ALERT

## NERI — PROGRESSIVE SEVERITY OF ANEMIC EPISODES

**Classification:** MEDICAL — HIGH PRIORITY  
**Resident:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Condition:** Abnormal spontaneous blood degradation  
**Emergency Contact:** Bao  
**Status:** Active and progressively concerning

---

## 1. SUMMARY

Neri's anemic episodes have demonstrated a concerning pattern:

> **The more episodes she experiences, the more severe and dangerous subsequent episodes tend to become.**

This is not necessarily a simple linear progression.

Some episodes remain relatively mild.

Others deteriorate rapidly.

However, repeated episodes appear to place additional strain on Neri's already fragile cardiovascular system, leaving progressively less physiological margin before she reaches a critical state.

The medical team therefore considers **every episode significant**, even if Neri appears to recover quickly.

---

# 2. EARLY EPISODES

Neri's earlier episodes were comparatively subtle.

Typical presentation:

- tiredness,
    
- pallor,
    
- dizziness,
    
- blurred vision,
    
- difficulty breathing,
    
- collapse,
    
- relatively short recovery period.
    

Neri often described the experience simply as:

> **“Everything gets blurry then I can't breathe and fell.”**

She did not understand why it happened.

She still doesn't use the word _anemia_.

---

# 3. PROGRESSION

Later episodes began showing increasingly severe symptoms.

The interval between:

**“Something feels wrong”**

and

**loss of consciousness**

became less predictable.

Some episodes progressed rapidly enough that Neri had little opportunity to warn anyone.

Her ability to remain conscious also became increasingly unreliable.

This created a major concern for SAFEHOUSE personnel:

> **Neri may not always have enough time to ask for help.**

---

# 4. THE MOST IMPORTANT CHANGE

Neri's episodes are no longer treated as isolated incidents.

Each episode is now considered part of an ongoing medical condition.

Her body already possesses:

- a fragile cardiovascular system,
    
- abnormal blood physiology,
    
- extensive historical damage,
    
- reduced physiological reserve,
    
- and spontaneous blood degradation.
    

Repeated severe episodes place additional stress on that system.

Therefore:

> **A previous successful recovery does not guarantee the next episode will be equally survivable.**

---

# 5. NERI DOES NOT UNDERSTAND THE DANGER

This remains one of the most difficult aspects of treating her.

Neri may recover from an episode and conclude:

> **“I'm okay now.”**

From her perspective, that is reasonable.

She fell.

Bao helped her.

She woke up.

Therefore everything is okay again.

Medical personnel know that it is more complicated than that.

Neri's recovery means she survived the episode.

It does **not** mean the underlying condition has disappeared.

---

# 6. BAO'S ROLE

Bao remains Neri's most important emergency contact.

When Neri becomes critically anemic, Bao's blood can provide compatible functional blood and stabilize her failing cardiovascular system.

This has saved Neri repeatedly.

Neri knows this.

She may therefore become extremely attached to Bao after an episode.

A common sequence is:

> **Neri:** “Mom...”

> **Bao:** “Yes, Neri?”

> **Neri:** “I'm not feeling too well...”

> _Neri collapses._

> **Bao:** “NERI!”

Bao immediately intervenes.

Afterward, once conscious, Neri may simply reach for her.

> **Neri:** “Mom...”

and hug her.

There is no complicated explanation.

Neri knows:

**Mom saved me.**

---

# 7. THE PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECT

The increasing severity has also intensified Neri's fear.

She already has a profound fear of losing people.

Now she has another problem:

**Her own body can make her disappear.**

Neri has spent years believing that if someone disappears, she failed to protect them.

The possibility that _she_ could disappear creates a terrible contradiction.

She wants to protect everyone.

But sometimes she is the one who needs protection.

This can cause Neri to hide symptoms because she does not want to interrupt anyone's work.

That behavior is considered extremely dangerous.

---

# 8. “I'M FINE.”

Neri has repeatedly demonstrated a tendency to minimize her symptoms.

This is particularly concerning during busy periods.

She may sit quietly somewhere because she does not want to bother anyone.

Personnel may assume:

> “She's just sitting there.”

Meanwhile, Neri may already be becoming severely anemic.

One previous episode occurred while she was sitting against a wall during an exceptionally busy week.

Nobody initially recognized how unwell she was.

By the time Bao noticed, intervention was urgent.

---

# 9. CURRENT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE

If Neri reports:

> **“Everything's blurry.”**

Treat it seriously.

If she says:

> **“I can't breathe.”**

Treat it as an emergency.

If she collapses:

**CALL BAO.**

Do not wait for Neri to wake up and explain what happened.

Do not assume that because she survived previous episodes, this one will be harmless.

---

# 10. PROGNOSTIC CONCERN

Medical personnel cannot currently establish exactly how many severe episodes Neri can survive.

There is no known fixed threshold.

There is also no guarantee that progression will continue at a predictable rate.

The concern is simpler:

> **Neri's physiological margin for error is shrinking.**

An episode that previously left her conscious may eventually render her unconscious.

An episode that previously required immediate intervention may eventually progress faster.

An episode that she previously survived may eventually become fatal if help does not arrive in time.

This is why every episode matters.

---

# 11. THE CHILD BEHIND THE CONDITION

Neri's increasing medical danger must not cause SAFEHOUSE to treat her as a fragile object.

She is still Neri.

She still wants to:

- eat cake,
    
- play,
    
- work with chemistry,
    
- walk around SAFEHOUSE,
    
- spend time with her friends,
    
- tease people,
    
- learn new things,
    
- and occasionally make Neko regret giving her laboratory access.
    

She should continue living her life.

The objective is **management**, not confinement.

---

# 12. FINAL PSYCHOLOGICAL NOTE

Neri has already lost too much.

She lost her sister.

She lost friends.

She lost years of childhood.

She spent years believing that everyone she loved would eventually disappear.

She cannot afford to lose anyone else.

And now her greatest threat is sometimes her own body.

That is why the SAFEHOUSE response must be different from what Neri learned growing up.

When she falls:

**Someone comes running.**

When she says she's scared:

**Someone listens.**

When she cannot breathe:

**Someone helps.**

When she wakes up:

**Someone is still there.**

Neri does not have to save herself every time.

She is not expendable.

She is not an asset.

She is not a failed experiment.

She is a ten-year-old girl who has already survived far more than she should have had to.

And SAFEHOUSE intends to keep her alive.

**END ALERT**
