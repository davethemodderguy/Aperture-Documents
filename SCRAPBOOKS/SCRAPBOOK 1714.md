---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has made a **complete physical recovery** from the teddy bear impact incident.

No persistent cardiac symptoms have been observed.

No respiratory impairment remains.

No chronic pain has been detected.

Jake has returned to his normal physical activity.

He can walk, run, carry his teddy bear, climb, play, and perform ordinary activities without difficulty.

Medical monitoring has been reduced accordingly.

Clover's recovery has been considerably slower.

Although she understands intellectually that the incident was accidental, the event triggered her longstanding survivor's guilt surrounding her father's death.

She required considerably more time to emotionally recover.

---

# 1. JAKE — MEDICAL CLEARANCE

Jake has returned to his previous physical baseline.

### Current status:

**Chest pain:** None.

**Cardiac distress:** None.

**Respiratory difficulty:** None.

**Coughing:** None.

**Generalized pain:** None.

**Mobility:** Normal.

**Speech:** Normal baseline.

**Teddy bear sensory connection:** Active and stable.

**Permanent injury:** None identified.

Jake is considered medically recovered.

---

# 2. THE FIRST NORMAL DAY

The first indication that Jake had truly recovered was remarkably ordinary.

He picked up his teddy bear.

He threw it onto his mattress.

Then immediately climbed after it.

Clover froze.

Jake noticed.

He looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm okay."

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

Jake smiled.

Then jumped onto the mattress.

No pain.

No coughing.

No hesitation.

Just Jake.

---

# 3. RETURN TO NORMAL ACTIVITY

Over the following days, Jake gradually resumed his usual behavior.

He began:

- running through the SAFEHOUSE;
    
- carrying his teddy bear;
    
- following Bao around;
    
- bothering Jane;
    
- showing Neri his drawings;
    
- visiting the Obol Squad;
    
- sitting on Clover's lap while she worked;
    
- eating normally;
    
- climbing onto furniture;
    
- and generally behaving like the six-year-old child he is.
    

Nobody attempted to force him to slow down.

His body had already demonstrated that it was ready.

---

# 4. CLOVER

Clover was not ready nearly as quickly.

For several weeks, she remained unusually cautious around Jake.

If Jake moved too quickly:

She watched.

If Jake coughed:

She immediately looked up.

If he touched his chest:

She stopped whatever she was doing.

If the teddy bear fell:

She froze.

If Jake suggested playing catch:

Clover refused.

Not because she no longer trusted him.

Because she remembered.

---

# 5. THE TEDDY BEAR

The bear became the strongest trigger.

Before the incident, Clover could casually pick it up.

Afterward, she handled it extremely carefully.

She would place it down rather than toss it.

She would carry it with both hands.

She would check where it landed.

If Jake accidentally dropped it, Clover's attention immediately snapped toward him.

Jake eventually noticed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Clover."

She looked at him.

> **JAKE:**  
> "He's okay."

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

She did not sound convinced.

---

# 6. SURVIVOR'S GUILT

The incident reopened an old wound.

Clover's father died when she was young.

Clover survived.

She has spent much of her life carrying the emotional weight of that fact.

The teddy bear incident created a new association:

> **Someone she cared about was seriously injured.**

> **She was directly involved.**

> **She survived.**

> **He survived.**

Her mind repeatedly returned to the question:

> **"What if he hadn't?"**

C.O.R.E. considers this the central reason her recovery took so long.

---

# 7. CLOVER'S NIGHTTIME RESPONSE

Clover does not require sleep.

Consequently, there were many nights in which she simply remained awake thinking about the incident.

She would sit at her desk.

A candle would burn beside her.

Her accounting work would remain untouched.

Sometimes she would draw.

Often she would draw Jake.

Not injured.

Not dying.

Just Jake.

White hair.

Blue eyes.

Teddy bear.

Grass-covered floor.

She repeatedly drew the same thing until the image stopped feeling like a reminder of the incident and started feeling like an ordinary memory again.

---

# 8. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake did not fully understand why Clover remained frightened.

He eventually asked her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Are you still sad?"

Clover paused.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "A little."

Jake considered this.

Then he sat beside her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm not hurt."

Clover smiled faintly.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

Jake leaned against her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You didn't mean to."

Clover looked at him.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I know."

She became quiet.

Then:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Thank you."

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You're my friend."

Clover looked away.

She smiled.

---

# 9. THE FIRST TIME CLOVER HELD THE BEAR AGAIN

Several weeks later, Jake left his teddy bear beside Clover's desk.

Clover looked at it.

She hesitated.

Then picked it up.

Nothing happened.

Jake felt the contact but experienced no pain.

Clover held the bear normally.

She turned it over.

Checked its arms.

Its legs.

Its head.

Everything was fine.

Jake watched her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "See?"

Clover laughed quietly.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Yeah."

She hugged the bear.

Jake felt the hug.

He smiled.

---

# 10. RECOVERY MILESTONE

The most significant improvement occurred when Jake asked:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Catch?"

Clover stared at him.

Jake held the teddy bear.

She immediately shook her head.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Not yet."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Okay."

No pressure.

No argument.

Several days later, Jake asked again.

Clover thought about it.

Then:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Maybe."

Jake smiled.

That was enough.

---

# 11. CLOVER'S RECOVERY

Clover eventually stopped replaying the impact in her head.

She stopped checking Jake every time he moved.

She stopped freezing when the teddy bear fell.

She stopped treating ordinary contact with the bear as a potential emergency.

She still remembered.

She simply no longer lived inside the memory.

That distinction is considered the clearest indication of psychological recovery.

---

# 12. FINAL OBSERVATION

Jake was sitting in his room.

Clover was at her desk.

The teddy bear was between them.

Jake picked it up.

He tossed it gently onto his mattress.

Clover looked over.

She paused.

Then continued working.

Jake climbed onto the mattress.

No panic.

No hesitation.

No emergency response.

Just another ordinary moment.

Jake looked toward Clover.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Clover?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Hm?"

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm okay."

Clover smiled.

This time, she did not need to check.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

Jake returned to his book.

Clover returned to her work.

The room became quiet again.

But this time, the quiet was peaceful.

---

# C.O.R.E. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake has fully recovered.

Clover has also recovered, although her psychological recovery required substantially more time.

The incident remains classified as a near-fatal accident.

No blame has been assigned.

No disciplinary action has been taken.

The teddy bear sensory protocol remains permanent.

Clover is still Jake's caretaker.

Jake still trusts Clover.

Clover still trusts herself around Jake.

And, perhaps most importantly, neither of them treats the other as something fragile anymore.

Jake is alive.

Clover knows this.

Now she is finally beginning to believe it.
