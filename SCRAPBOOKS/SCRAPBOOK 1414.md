# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The consequences of Jake's transformation into a Nullwalker are considerably more extensive than initially understood.

Following Guiding Light's departure, C.O.R.E. initiated an emergency archival search for all records concerning Jake.

The search failed.

Not because the records were inaccessible.

Not because they were corrupted.

Not because they had been deleted.

Because:

> **They no longer existed.**

Every known physical or digital representation containing Jake has disappeared.

This includes:

- CCTV recordings;
    
- photographs;
    
- videos;
    
- medical records;
    
- resident profiles;
    
- security reports;
    
- incident reports;
    
- handwritten notes;
    
- drawings;
    
- archived documents;
    
- database entries;
    
- personal messages;
    
- room documentation;
    
- identification records;
    
- historical observations;
    
- and physical objects specifically containing representations of Jake.
    

The phenomenon appears to extend beyond conventional information storage.

If an object contained a meaningful representation of Jake, that representation has been removed.

The only surviving evidence of Jake is:

> **Memory.**

Every person who knew him now remembers him.

Perfectly.

And that is what makes the loss significantly worse.

Nothing.

---

# 2. C.C.T.V.

Security personnel checked historical CCTV.

Jake had appeared throughout the Blacksite and SAFEHOUSE for years.

He should have been visible in thousands of hours of footage.

The recordings remained intact.

The timestamps remained intact.

The locations remained intact.

But Jake was absent.

A hallway recording showed Bao walking through Sector 4-C.

The timestamp was correct.

The hallway was correct.

The lighting was correct.

Bao was visible.

A second resident was visible.

There was an empty space beside Bao.

Everyone watching the recording knew who should have been there.

Nobody was there.

The same phenomenon appeared throughout the archive.

---

# 3. PHOTOGRAPHS

Personal photographs were affected.

Residents began searching their phones.

Family photographs.

Group photographs.

Meals.

Birthdays.

Medical visits.

Casual photographs.

SAFEHOUSE gatherings.

Every image containing Jake had changed.

He was simply absent.

The photographs did not appear edited.

There were no visible artifacts.

The composition simply existed as though Jake had never occupied the space.

One photograph showed Nora sitting beside an empty chair.

Nora stared at it for several seconds.

> **NORA:**  
> “He was sitting there.”

No one disagreed.

---

# 4. THE TEDDY BEAR

The teddy bear remained.

This was initially considered an exception.

It was not.

The bear existed independently of Jake's visual representation.

Its physical body remained intact.

However, its relationship to Jake was no longer encoded anywhere externally.

Its core remained inert.

The bear was simply:

> **A teddy bear.**

Everyone remembered that it had once belonged to Jake.

But there was no surviving document proving it.

No label.

No ownership record.

No photograph.

No inventory entry.

Only memory.

---

# 5. JAKE'S ROOM

The residents returned to the elevator.

The anomalous grassy space remained.

The circle remained.

Jake remained.

But the SAFEHOUSE records no longer contained a designation for the room.

The directory no longer listed:

> **JAKE'S ROOM**

The room's existence had become strangely disconnected from its former occupant.

The sign outside the room remained physically present.

However, the writing had changed.

The name was gone.

Only the remainder could be read:

> **“please knock, I can't take suprises”**

The residents recognized the handwriting.

They knew who had written it.

There was no documentation confirming it.

---

No blank space.

No indication that anything had been removed.

# 10. THE MOST PAINFUL DISCOVERY

The memories also contained something else.

The conversations.

The requests.

The moments when Jake had asked for help.

The moments when someone had answered:

> **“I'm busy.”**

Or:

> **“Ask someone else.”**

Or:

> **“Not right now.”**

> **“No.”**

Tomorrow.

After work.

After the emergency.

After the meeting.

After the mission.

After they finished whatever they were doing.

Jake had always been there.

So everyone assumed he would continue being there.

# 17. THE HORRIBLE REALIZATION

The residents eventually stopped asking:

> **“Where is Jake?”**

They already knew.

He was still sitting in the grass.

They could still visit him.

They could still see the two closed eyes.

They could still sit beside him.

But there was no Jake left to return to them.

And everyone now remembered why.

Not because they hated him.

Not because they wanted him gone.

Not because they deliberately abandoned him.

Because they were busy.

Because they assumed someone else would help.

Because there was always tomorrow.

Because Jake was quiet.

Because Jake rarely complained.

Because Jake could survive things that other children could not.

Because everyone thought:

> **“Someone will notice.”**

Someone did not.

At the worst possible time.

# C.O.R.E. FINAL OBSERVATION

The most devastating aspect of the event is not that Jake's records disappeared.

It is that the records were never necessary.

Everyone already knew.

They knew his name.

They knew his face.

They knew his voice.

They knew his habits.

They knew where he slept.

They knew what he liked.

They knew who he trusted.

They knew when he needed help.

They knew he was six years old.

They knew he was a star.

They knew he was there.

And still—

When he asked for something,

they were busy.

When he needed someone,

someone else was supposed to help.

When he disappeared,

nobody noticed.

When he returned,

nobody recognized that he had been gone.

Now every photograph is empty.

Every recording is silent.

Every document is gone.

Every database has forgotten him.

But every person remembers.

And that is the cruelest possible outcome.

Because they finally remember every moment they could have been there.

Every time they said:

> **“Not right now.”**

Every time they thought:

> **“Later.”**

Every time they assumed:

> **“Someone else will handle it.”**

There is no later.

There is no recording to recover.

There is no document to correct.

There is no photograph to restore.

There is only a black figure sitting in an empty circle.

And the people sitting beside him.

Remembering.
