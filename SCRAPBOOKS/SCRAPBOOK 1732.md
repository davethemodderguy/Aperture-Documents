# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has regained consciousness.

His recovery has not proceeded as expected.

Although emergency treatment addressed the immediate consequences of his prolonged exposure to heavy snowfall, Jake's internal temperature regulation remains severely abnormal.

The most conspicuous symptom is his breathing.

**Jake is exhaling visible clouds of cold vapor that behave unlike ordinary breath condensation.**

Each exhalation produces a pale, almost blue-white plume that hangs in the air before dispersing. The surrounding air becomes noticeably colder near his face, and instruments have recorded a temperature decrease immediately following several exhalations.

The effect persists even when Jake is resting in a warmed medical room.

More concerningly, Jake describes the sensation as coming from _inside_ his body.

He is not merely cold on the surface.

He feels as though the cold has settled into his chest, his limbs, and the spaces beneath his skin.

His body appears to be struggling to return to its previous physiological baseline while simultaneously attempting to adapt to the condition imposed by the prolonged exposure.

C.O.R.E. has not established whether this represents a lingering complication of severe hypothermia, an anomalous response to the incident, or a combination of both.

The current working description is:

> **Persistent internal hypothermia with anomalous cold-vapor respiration and possible thermoregulatory adaptation.**

Jake remains physically fragile.

His recovery is ongoing.

And, despite the circumstances, he is trying very hard to understand what his body is doing.

# 1. FIRST OBSERVATION AFTER WAKING

**TIME:** 02:17  
**LOCATION:** MEDICAL WARD — RECOVERY ROOM

Jake regained consciousness shortly after a routine medical assessment.

His eyes opened slowly.

For several seconds, he did not move.

His white hair lay across the pillow. His wings were folded close to his back, and his teddy bear rested beside him beneath a carefully arranged blanket.

A medical monitor registered a change in his breathing.

Jake inhaled.

Then exhaled.

A thick plume of pale vapor escaped his mouth.

It drifted above the blanket.

The nearest temperature sensor registered a brief decrease.

The attending medic looked up.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "Jake?"

Jake blinked.

He exhaled again.

Another cloud formed.

This one was more pronounced, spreading outward before curling back toward his face.

Jake watched it.

He raised one hand and cautiously moved it through the vapor.

His fingers trembled.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Cold."

The medic approached the bedside.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "Where does it hurt?"

Jake considered the question.

He placed a small hand against his chest.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Inside."

He paused to breathe.

A fresh cloud emerged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm cold inside."

The medic immediately notified the attending physician.

Jake looked at the vapor hanging above his blanket.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Why am I doing that?"

No one had an answer yet.

# 2. THE BREATHING PHENOMENON

The visible vapor is not behaving like ordinary condensation alone.

Under normal circumstances, warm exhaled air meeting sufficiently cold surroundings may produce visible condensation.

Jake's current environment, however, is being maintained at a medically appropriate temperature.

The vapor continues to form.

Furthermore, nearby sensors have recorded brief, localized cooling associated with some of his exhalations.

The observed sequence is consistent enough to warrant further investigation:

1. Jake inhales.
    
2. His breathing briefly becomes shallow.
    
3. He exhales a visible plume of cold vapor.
    
4. The plume expands away from his face.
    
5. The surrounding air registers a temporary temperature decrease.
    
6. Jake frequently shivers or draws his blanket closer afterward.
    

The vapor is not consistently blue.

Under ordinary lighting, it appears white.

Under the ward's low-intensity azure monitoring lights, its edges occasionally acquire a faint blue tint.

No solid particles have been confirmed within the plume.

C.O.R.E. has therefore not classified the phenomenon as the same material as Jake's anomalous blue dust.

The distinction remains important.

**Jake is exhaling cold vapor, not demonstrably shedding his body into dust.**

### Clinical note

Jake's description suggests that the sensation is not limited to his skin or extremities.

However, subjective coldness alone cannot establish his core temperature or explain the underlying process.

Objective temperature measurements, cardiovascular monitoring, and continued medical assessment remain necessary.

Personnel must not interpret the visible vapor as a reliable indicator of his internal temperature.

# 4. THE TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear remains an essential part of his recovery.

When he first woke, he immediately looked for it.

The bear had been recovered beside him after the exterior incident and was subsequently assessed for physical damage.

No significant structural damage was identified.

Because the bear functions as Jake's sensory extension, medical personnel are treating it as part of the broader clinical situation.

Jake has been observed holding it against his chest, occasionally pressing his cheek into its fabric.

The behavior appears comforting.

However, an additional complication emerged during observation.

When Jake exhaled a particularly dense plume of vapor, the bear's outer fabric became noticeably colder.

A medic carefully checked the surrounding environment.

The cooling did not appear to be explained entirely by the room's ambient temperature.

Jake noticed the medic examining the bear.

He immediately pulled it closer.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Don't take him."

> **MEDIC:**  
> "I'm not taking him."

Jake looked at the medic for several seconds.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...He's cold too."

The medic paused.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "We'll make sure he's okay."

Jake nodded.

He continued holding the bear.

No one attempted to separate them.

### Additional concern

It is not yet clear whether the bear is simply being cooled by contact with Jake, whether the sensory connection is influencing the observed effect, or whether the bear itself is participating in the anomalous thermoregulatory response.

The bear's role remains under observation.

It will not be used as a test object.

# 6. ANOMALOUS THERMOREGULATION

C.O.R.E. has identified several possible explanations.

None has been confirmed.

### Hypothesis A — Persistent physiological hypothermia

Jake may still be experiencing consequences of severe hypothermia.

His body's temperature regulation may remain impaired despite initial treatment.

This possibility must be addressed medically before an anomalous explanation is accepted.

### Hypothesis B — Anomalous cooling response

The prolonged exposure may have triggered an abnormal response in Jake's manifestation.

The cold vapor and localized temperature decreases may indicate that his body is continuing to produce or release a cooling effect.

The mechanism is unknown.

### Hypothesis C — Failed thermal stabilization

Jake's human manifestation may be struggling to maintain a stable internal temperature.

Under this hypothesis, the visible vapor is a symptom of an unstable thermoregulatory process rather than a deliberate ability.

### Hypothesis D — Adaptive response

Jake's body may be attempting to adapt to the physiological stress caused by the incident.

Such adaptation could explain why the symptoms persist after he has regained consciousness.

However, adaptation should not automatically be interpreted as recovery.

A body can respond to an injury without having resolved it.

### Hypothesis E — Combined mechanism

The most cautious working possibility is that ordinary hypothermic injury and an anomalous physiological response are occurring simultaneously.

Jake's human body may be recovering from severe cold exposure while his anomalous nature produces additional effects that complicate that recovery.

C.O.R.E. cannot currently distinguish these mechanisms with confidence.

# 7. JAKE ATTEMPTS TO ADAPT

The most notable behavioral change occurred later that morning.

Jake had been resting for some time.

His condition remained under observation, and he was permitted to move only as medically appropriate.

He began paying attention to his breathing.

He inhaled slowly.

Then exhaled.

The vapor emerged again.

Jake watched it disperse.

He tried another breath.

This time, he held the exhalation briefly before releasing it.

The resulting plume was smaller.

He blinked.

Then tried again.

The plume became larger.

Jake frowned.

He tried to breathe more gently.

The vapor shortened.

He stared at it.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Oh."

The medic looked up.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "What is it?"

Jake took another slow breath.

A thin ribbon of vapor escaped his mouth.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I can make it smaller."

The medic approached, maintaining a safe distance and monitoring his condition.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "Does that make you feel warmer?"

Jake considered the question.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...I don't know."

He looked down at his hands.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It doesn't come out as much."

He attempted another controlled breath.

The vapor was less pronounced.

For a few moments, he seemed pleased.

Then he began shivering again.

The medic stopped the exercise.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "That's enough for now."

Jake looked disappointed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I was trying."

> **MEDIC:**  
> "I know."

The medic adjusted his blanket.

> **MEDIC:**  
> "You don't have to learn everything today."

Jake looked toward his teddy bear.

He placed one hand on its head.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Okay."

### Assessment

Jake appears to be attempting to regulate the visible manifestation of his breathing through deliberate control.

This is an important observation, but it does not demonstrate that he can regulate his core body temperature.

Reducing the visible vapor may affect the appearance of the phenomenon without correcting the underlying physiological condition.

Further attempts should not be encouraged until his medical team determines that they are safe.

Jake is a child attempting to understand an unfamiliar bodily response.

He should not be expected to solve it through experimentation.

# 9. MEDICAL DIRECTIVES

Until the underlying condition is better understood, the following measures remain in effect.

**Thermal management**

Jake's core temperature and cardiovascular status must continue to be assessed by qualified medical personnel. External warming must follow appropriate clinical procedures for severe hypothermia.

**Respiratory observation**

The cold-vapor phenomenon must be documented alongside breathing rate, responsiveness, measured temperature, and other clinically relevant observations.

The visible vapor alone must not be used to estimate the severity of his condition.

**No unsupervised experimentation**

Jake is not to be encouraged to manipulate his breathing, suppress the vapor, or activate other anomalous abilities to test whether he can warm himself.

**Rest**

Jake's tendency to exhaust himself remains a significant concern. His recent loss of consciousness makes unnecessary exertion particularly inappropriate.

**Teddy bear care**

The bear must remain accessible whenever practical. Personnel should avoid unnecessary separation and continue monitoring it for changes that may affect Jake through their sensory connection.

**Emotional reassurance**

Jake should be told what is known, what remains uncertain, and what medical personnel are doing to help him.

He should not be promised that the symptoms will disappear immediately.

He should also not be made to believe that his body is failing simply because it is behaving differently.

---

# 10. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake has survived the immediate incident.

He has regained consciousness.

He can communicate.

He can recognize his surroundings.

He is attempting to adapt to a new and frightening physical experience.

However, his recovery remains incomplete.

The persistent cold sensation, unusual vapor production, and localized cooling effects require continued medical investigation.

His current condition should not be interpreted as evidence that he has permanently lost control of his manifestation.

Nor should it be dismissed as a harmless side effect of cold weather.

Jake is attempting to compensate for something he does not yet understand.

That effort deserves patience.

It does not justify placing additional responsibility on him to correct the problem himself.

**The burden of treatment belongs to the medical team, not to the child experiencing the symptoms.**

> **MEDIC:**

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Okay."

For now, he continued breathing.

The cold continued coming from somewhere deep inside him.

And Jake, who had once looked at a rose in the snow and seen something beautiful, was now trying to learn how to live inside a body that had become unfamiliar to him.

He was not trying to become stronger.

He was not trying to become more dangerous.

He was simply trying to feel warm again.
