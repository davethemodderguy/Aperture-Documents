### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Camelia Candleheart no longer remembers the argument.

No recollection of raising her voice. No recollection of Jake's expression. No recollection of the sentence that changed their relationship.

No recollection of telling her little brother:

**“I don't want to be your older sister in the 1st place.”**

No member of staff has reminded her.

This was not an oversight. It was a deliberate decision made after consultation with the Social Welfare Division. Camelia's Etheric Retrograde Amnesia is severe, persistent, and involuntary. Reintroducing the argument would not restore the memory in any reliable or lasting fashion. It would, however, repeatedly expose her to distress while doing nothing to undo the injury Jake sustained.

The decision has a second consequence.

Camelia is permitted to continue her days without the memory of what she said.

Jake is not.

He remembers the sentence exactly. He remembers the argument that preceded it, the voice in which it was delivered, and the realization that the person he had trusted to be his older sister could apparently decide she no longer wanted him.

Jake has reached a conclusion that C.O.R.E. cannot dismiss as a mere misunderstanding:

**He believes Camelia betrayed him.**

He believes her memory loss has become an excuse—not necessarily one she consciously invented, but one that allows her to hurt him and then live as though the hurt never happened.

To Jake, the distinction between _forgetting that you hurt someone_ and _being spared the consequences of hurting someone_ has become painfully difficult to see.

The clinical reality is that Camelia did not deliberately erase the argument. Her memory loss is not a deception. There is no evidence that she intended to exploit her condition.

The emotional reality is that Jake cannot experience that clinical explanation as sufficient.

The sentence was spoken. He heard it. He believed her.

And when he was left alone with the memory, the person who had said it was no longer able to remember saying it at all.

### II. THE SENTENCE

The original argument lasted less than ten minutes.

The damage has lasted considerably longer.

The following statement was reconstructed from the accounts of those present and Jake's subsequent recollections.

> **CAMELIA:** “Maybe you should stop expecting me to be your sister.”
> 
> **JAKE:** “But you said you would.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Maybe I shouldn't have agreed to be your sister in the first place.”
> 
> **JAKE:** “...”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I don't want to be your older sister in the 1st place.”

The final sentence was the one Jake retained with the greatest clarity.

He did not describe it as a threat. He did not describe it as an insult. He did not even describe it as something said in anger.

He described it as an answer.

He had wanted to be remembered. He had wanted Camelia to recognize him, to know who he was without needing to consult a notebook, and to remember that they were family.

He had wanted his older sister to want him.

Her answer, as he understood it, was that she did not.

The argument ended. Several minutes later, Camelia experienced another memory lapse. The argument disappeared from her accessible recollection.

The words remained with Jake.

**C.O.R.E. NOTE:** It is tempting to classify this incident as a disagreement made worse by a memory disorder. That classification is incomplete. Camelia's impairment explains why she forgot. It does not explain away the meaning Jake assigned to her words, nor does it make his resulting pain less real.

### III. THE MORNING AFTER

At breakfast the following morning, Camelia approached Jake with her usual warmth.

She had no conscious recollection of the argument.

Jake was sitting with his teddy bear held close to him. Its heartbeat continued beneath the fabric. He had barely touched his food.

Camelia approached, then paused beside his chair.

> **CAMELIA:** “Morning, Jake.”
> 
> **JAKE:** “...”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Are you okay?”

Jake looked at her.

For a moment, he seemed to consider answering. His fingers tightened around the teddy bear instead.

> **JAKE:** “Go away.”

Camelia stopped.

> **CAMELIA:** “Did I do something?”

Jake looked down.

> **JAKE:** “You said it.”

She waited for him to explain.

He did not.

> **CAMELIA:** “Said what?”

Jake's expression changed. Not dramatically. He simply stopped looking at her.

The question had been innocent. She genuinely did not know.

That made it worse.

He had spent the night remembering something she could not even recognize as an event.

> **JAKE:** “Nothing.”

He left the table with his teddy bear.

Camelia remained where she was, visibly confused. She did not understand why he was upset, why he would not look at her, or why his usual willingness to sit beside her had vanished.

She asked a staff member whether Jake was angry with her.

The staff member did not recount the argument.

> **STAFF:** “He's having a difficult morning. Give him some space, please.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Did I forget something?”
> 
> **STAFF:** “You have been experiencing frequent memory lapses.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “That's not what I asked.”

The staff member recorded the exchange and redirected her toward breakfast.

No one reminded her.

Not because the sentence was unimportant.

Because Jake's pain was not an educational exercise, and his private wound was not information that others were entitled to use to make Camelia remember it.

### IV. JAKE'S INTERPRETATION

**Interview designation:** JA-048-A  
**Interviewee:** Jake Akana  
**Interviewer:** C.O.R.E. Child Welfare Officer  
**Conditions:** Voluntary. Teddy bear remained in Jake's possession throughout.

Jake was asked whether he wanted to discuss the change in his relationship with Camelia.

He initially shook his head.

After several minutes, he spoke.

> **JAKE:** “She forgot.”

The officer acknowledged the statement.

> **OFFICER:** “Yes. She doesn't remember the argument.”
> 
> **JAKE:** “I do.”

A pause followed.

> **JAKE:** “I remember all of it.”

The officer asked what he believed the memory loss meant.

Jake held the bear closer.

> **JAKE:** “She can say anything.”
> 
> **OFFICER:** “What do you mean?”
> 
> **JAKE:** “She can say she doesn't want me. Then she forgets.”
> 
> **OFFICER:** “Do you think she forgot on purpose?”
> 
> **JAKE:** “No.”

He answered immediately.

This distinction is important. Jake has not concluded that Camelia is deliberately fabricating her impairment. He knows she forgets things. He has seen it happen repeatedly.

His accusation is more painful than that.

> **JAKE:** “But she still said it.”
> 
> **OFFICER:** “Yes.”
> 
> **JAKE:** “And now she doesn't have to remember.”

He stopped speaking.

The officer did not fill the silence.

> **JAKE:** “If I hurt her, I would remember.”
> 
> **OFFICER:** “You might remember. But forgetting something wouldn't mean it was okay to hurt you.”
> 
> **JAKE:** “Then why does it feel like it doesn't matter?”

No immediate answer was offered.

There was no answer that could make the situation fair.

Jake does not believe Camelia's memory loss is fake. He believes it has left him alone with the consequences of something she did not have to keep carrying in her mind.

To him, the imbalance resembles betrayal: he trusted her with the vulnerable part of himself that wanted a family, and she rejected it. Now she can approach him with the same affection as before, while he must decide whether that affection is safe to believe.

He has no way to know whether the next loving promise will remain in her memory.

He has even less reason, at present, to trust that the next angry sentence will be remembered by the person who speaks it.

**Assessment:** Jake's interpretation is emotionally coherent given his experience, but it is not evidence of deliberate manipulation by Camelia. Staff must not argue him out of his feelings by reciting diagnostic explanations. Equally, staff must not reinforce the belief that Camelia intentionally weaponized her condition. Both children require support without being made responsible for resolving the other's pain.

### V. THE DECISION NOT TO REMIND CAMELIA

The Social Welfare Division formally reviewed whether Camelia should be informed of the argument.

The review distinguished three separate obligations:

1. **The obligation to acknowledge harm.** Jake's experience must be taken seriously, even when the person who hurt him cannot recall the incident.
    
2. **The obligation to support Camelia honestly.** Her memory impairment must not be treated as evidence of malice, and she must not be punished for a lapse she did not choose.
    
3. **The obligation to protect Jake's agency.** Jake must not be forced to recount the argument, confront Camelia, accept an apology, or restore their previous relationship for the sake of making the household comfortable.
    

For the present, staff will not independently remind Camelia of the sentence.

This is not a declaration that she should never be told. It is a decision not to expose her to the incident without a clear welfare purpose and without first considering Jake's privacy and wishes.

Should Camelia eventually ask directly whether she has hurt Jake, staff must not lie to her. They may explain that Jake is struggling and that he needs space, without disclosing his private statements or replaying the argument without cause.

Should Jake request that she be told, the request will be reviewed with him, not treated as automatic permission to stage a reconciliation.

No staff member is to tell Jake that Camelia's inability to remember means he should stop being upset.

No staff member is to tell Camelia that her condition makes her incapable of hurting people.

Neither statement is acceptable.

A memory can disappear without the consequences disappearing with it.

### VI. GUIDING LIGHT'S JOURNAL

**Source:** Private journal maintained by Guiding Light, documenting creations and foundational designations.

**Access status:** Supplied for restricted welfare review.  
**Authenticity:** Accepted as genuine by the reviewing authority.  
**Reproduction:** Authorized for this document.

The journal entry concerns Jake Akana, whom Guiding Light also identifies as _a blue dwarf_—one of the many treasures she and Curious Light created.

The entry reads:

> _"Jake, or a blue dwarf. One of the many treasures Curious and I built. A star that can grant 1 wish to anyone sincere enough. Someone wished him to be a kid, for them to take care of him. They left him, his purpose etched in his mind. Spread unconditional, unlimited love to the ones care about him. Don't break him. Not even Curious and I know what happens if he did. Let him love you, and love him back"_

The journal is not a clinical assessment. It is not a containment directive. It does not establish that Jake's current emotional state will produce any specific anomalous event.

It is, however, a statement written by one of his creators.

The entry describes a star who was wished into the shape of a child so that someone could take care of him. It describes a purpose etched into his mind, followed by abandonment. It describes his capacity to give love without limit—and ends with a warning.

**Don't break him.**

C.O.R.E. will not interpret this sentence as evidence that Jake is dangerous if hurt. The journal explicitly states that even his creators do not know what would happen if he were broken. Uncertainty is not proof of an impending catastrophe.

The warning is nevertheless impossible to disregard.

Jake has already experienced abandonment. He has already lost the certainty that those who love him will remain beside him. His heart is carried outside his body in the teddy bear he refuses to leave behind. He is a child whose continued existence depends upon an object that most people would mistake for a toy.

He has been restored to life.

He has been given a family.

And now, the person he considers his older sister has told him that she never wanted the role in the first place.

The journal's final instruction is not _make him forget the pain_.

It is not _remind him that he was created to love_.

It is not _tell him that love means forgiving those who hurt him_.

It is:

_Let him love you, and love him back._

That instruction cannot be fulfilled by demanding that Jake continue to love Camelia in the same way he did before the argument. It cannot be fulfilled by asking him to pretend her words did not matter. Nor can it be fulfilled by using his extraordinary capacity for love as a reason to expect endless patience from a six-year-old child.

If Jake's love is to remain unconditional, the responsibility to love him back cannot be conditional upon his ability to endure hurt quietly.

He is not obligated to prove that he is a good child by forgiving someone before he is ready.

He is not required to make Camelia feel better about an argument she cannot remember.

He is not required to return to her simply because she approaches him with the same affection she had before.

The journal calls him a treasure.

A treasure is not an instrument to be used until it breaks.

### VII. WELFARE DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

**1. No forced reconciliation.** Jake will not be instructed to hug Camelia, sit with her, reassure her, or accept an apology. Supported contact may occur only at a pace that respects his comfort.

**2. No emotional erasure.** Staff must not use Camelia's memory impairment to invalidate Jake's recollection or imply that the argument no longer matters because only one participant remembers it.

**3. No false accusation.** Camelia's lapse must not be represented as deliberate forgetting, calculated manipulation, or proof that she never loved Jake. No available evidence supports those conclusions.

**4. No compulsory disclosure.** Jake's private statements will not be relayed to Camelia merely to provoke recognition, guilt, or a desired emotional response.

**5. Independent support for Jake.** He will have access to a trusted adult who can listen without defending Camelia, pressuring him to forgive, or asking him to take responsibility for her distress.

**6. Continued support for Camelia.** Her memory aids, written instructions, safety accommodations, and access to assistance remain in place. She will not be punished for failing to remember an event she cannot recall.

**7. No assignment of caretaking duties.** Jake's ability to comfort Camelia, particularly in cat form, does not make him responsible for her emotional regulation. His own welfare takes precedence whenever the two needs conflict.

**8. Protection of the heart-bearing teddy bear.** Jake must retain access to the bear. Any care procedures must account for its anomalous significance and be undertaken only by authorized personnel. Emotional distress must not be allowed to compromise its protection.

**9. Observation without coercion.** Staff will document changes in Jake's behavior and the effects of any contact with Camelia. The objective is to identify distress and provide support—not to measure how quickly he returns to loving her.

**10. No predetermined outcome.** C.O.R.E. will not promise that the siblings will return to their former relationship. They may rebuild trust, establish new boundaries, or remain emotionally distant for a prolonged period. None of these possibilities permits staff to dictate Jake's feelings.

### VIII. FINAL OBSERVATION

At the time of this report, Camelia still believes that she and Jake are siblings.

She remembers the designation.

She remembers that Aries recognized her as Jake's older sister. She remembers that Jake is a child who needs care, that he sometimes takes the form of a white cat, and that he carries a teddy bear close to him.

She does not remember telling him she never wanted to be his older sister.

When she sees him now, she sometimes smiles and begins to approach.

Jake sometimes moves away.

He does not explain why.

He does not repeat the sentence to her. He does not ask her to take it back. He does not tell her that she has forgotten something important.

He simply remembers.

And perhaps the cruelest part of this incident is that neither child is lying.

Camelia genuinely cannot remember saying the words.

Jake genuinely cannot forget hearing them.

Her condition is real. His injury is real. Her affection may be genuine, and his mistrust may be justified by what he experienced. These truths do not cancel one another out.

C.O.R.E. cannot restore a memory Camelia has lost. It cannot remove a sentence from Jake's mind without violating the very person the journal warns us to protect. It cannot make the siblings' experiences equal by forcing them to revisit the argument together.

What it can do is refuse to make the child who remembers carry the burden of pretending it never happened.

Guiding Light wrote that Jake was made to be a child someone could take care of.

She wrote that his purpose was to spread love.

She wrote that he must not be broken.

We cannot know what she and Curious Light feared when they wrote that final warning. We will not invent a catastrophe to justify treating Jake gently.

We do not need to.

A child does not have to become dangerous for his pain to matter.

He does not have to stop loving his sister for his hurt to be legitimate.

And he does not have to forgive her to prove that the love inside him is still there.

**C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE**

_Jake remembers the sentence because, to him, it was the moment his sister stopped wanting him._

_Camelia has forgotten the sentence because forgetting is something her mind does to her. It is not a decision she made to escape responsibility, and it is not proof that her affection was false._

_But Jake cannot live inside that explanation. He lives inside the memory._

_We will not tell him that he misunderstood what he heard. We will not tell him that his sister never meant it, because we cannot know what she meant in that moment. And we will not tell him that love requires him to return to her before he is ready._

_The journal says: Let him love you, and love him back._

_Then let us begin with the part we can control._

_Let him be hurt without being blamed for it._

_Let him be a child without making him responsible for repairing his family._

_Let him decide when someone may approach him, when he is ready to speak, and whether he can trust again._

_And while he is learning whether the people he loves can hurt him and still be safe to love, let us make certain that no one asks him to prove his love by allowing himself to be hurt twice._

_He remembers._

_We will remember for him, too—not by keeping the argument alive forever, but by refusing to pretend that it never mattered._
