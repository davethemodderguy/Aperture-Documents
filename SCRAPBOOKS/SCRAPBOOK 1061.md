## Executive Summary

Following the incident in which Plushie Neri was unintentionally left outdoors for more than two days during rain and snowfall, C.O.R.E. personnel have observed a subtle but significant change in Neri's behavior.

Neri is physically recovering.

Her attitude toward the SAFEHOUSE residents, however, has changed.

She has developed a new belief:

> **Everyone is forced to have her around.**

This belief appears to have been reinforced by the incident itself.

Neri was left outside for an extended period without anyone noticing.

From her perspective, this produced a conclusion that is difficult to correct through simple reassurance:

> If everyone genuinely wanted her around, someone would have noticed.

The fact that people eventually came looking for her does not fully resolve the contradiction in her mind.

---

## Behavioral Change

Before the incident, Neri already possessed a strong tendency to consider herself a burden.

After the incident, that belief has become more externalized.

Previously:

> _I'm a problem._

Current interpretation:

> _They're keeping me because they have to._

This distinction is important.

Neri does not currently appear to believe that the residents dislike her.

She believes something more complicated:

**They care about her, but are obligated to tolerate her.**

She has begun interpreting ordinary acts of affection through this framework.

---

## Observed Examples

### 1. Being Carried

Alice picked up Plushie Neri after finding her sitting alone.

Neri allowed it.

Alice hugged her.

Neri remained quiet.

Alice asked:

> "Are you okay?"

Neri nodded.

A few seconds later, Neri quietly said:

> "You don't have to."

Alice asked:

> "Have to what?"

Neri did not answer.

---

### 2. Bao

Bao attempted to keep Neri nearby while she recovered.

Neri initially accepted this.

After several minutes, however, she moved herself a short distance away.

Bao did not follow.

Neri remained there.

Approximately ten minutes later, Neri moved back toward Bao on her own.

This is consistent with Neri's established preference for solitude.

However, the reason she gave through writing was new:

> **"You have to take care of me."**

Bao asked:

> "Who told you that?"

Neri wrote:

> **"Nobody."**

Then:

> **"That's the problem."**

---

### 3. Alice

Alice eventually asked Neri directly:

> "Do you think I don't want you?"

Neri stared at her.

She did not nod.

She did not shake her head.

Instead, she wrote:

> **"You forgot me."**

Alice became visibly upset.

Neri immediately added:

> **"It's okay."**

This was not interpreted by Welfare as genuine reassurance.

It appeared to be Neri attempting to remove responsibility from Alice.

---

## The Problem With "It's Okay"

Personnel are reminded that Neri frequently uses:

> **"It's okay."**

when something is not okay.

This is particularly common when she believes expressing discomfort will make her a burden.

Following the outdoor incident, this behavior has increased.

Neri is currently more likely to minimize:

- loneliness;
    
- discomfort;
    
- disappointment;
    
- fear;
    
- fatigue;
    
- desire for companionship;
    
- desire to be left alone.
    

The final item is especially important.

Neri genuinely likes being alone.

Personnel must therefore distinguish between:

> **"Leave me alone because I want solitude."**

and:

> **"Leave me alone because nobody actually wants me here."**

Both can produce identical outward behavior.

---

## Psychological Interpretation

The incident did not create Neri's belief that she is expendable.

It gave that belief additional evidence.

Neri already possessed the internal rule:

> **Attention → burden → disposal.**

The incident introduced another perceived rule:

> **If I disappear, people may not notice.**

Combined, these have produced:

> **If people are keeping me around, it must be because they have decided they are responsible for me.**

This is why Neri has begun behaving as though affection toward her is an obligation.

She may accept affection.

She may even seek it.

But she increasingly appears to believe that the person providing it has no choice.

---

## Notably, Neri Has Not Become Hostile

There has been no significant increase in aggression.

Neri is not angry at Alice.

She has not rejected Bao.

She has not stopped allowing herself to be carried.

She has not stopped accepting hugs.

Instead, she has become quieter.

When someone says:

> "I want you here."

Neri's expression often suggests she does not know what to do with the statement.

When someone says:

> "You don't have to stay."

Neri appears relieved.

This has led to an unfortunate behavioral loop.

---

## Current Interaction Pattern

**Resident:** "You can stay with me."

**Neri:** "You don't have to."

**Resident:** "I know."

**Neri:** "Then why?"

**Resident:** "Because I want to."

**Neri:** "You don't."

**Resident:** "I do."

**Neri:** "You have to."

**Resident:** "No."

**Neri:** "Everyone has to."

At this point, continued argument is discouraged.

Neri is not currently capable of resolving the contradiction through verbal reassurance.

---

## C.O.R.E. Directive

Personnel are **not** to repeatedly reassure Neri that she is wanted.

Do not turn this into a campaign.

Do not surround her with residents attempting to prove their affection.

Do not force her into group activities.

Do not repeatedly tell her:

> "We love you."

Do not ask:

> "Why don't you believe us?"

Do not make her explain herself.

And especially:

**Do not attempt to compensate for the incident by refusing to leave her alone.**

That would directly conflict with Neri's established need for solitude and would likely cause her to withdraw further.

---

## Correct Response

If Neri says:

> **"You don't have to."**

The recommended response is simply:

> **"I know."**

If she asks:

> **"Why are you here?"**

A sufficient answer is:

> **"Because I wanted to be."**

Then stop.

No lecture.

No argument.

No emotional interrogation.

Let the answer exist without demanding that Neri believe it.

---

## Alice-Specific Note

Alice has been particularly affected by Neri's new behavior.

Alice understands that she caused the original incident unintentionally.

She now appears determined to never forget Neri again.

C.O.R.E. recommends against allowing this guilt to become excessive.

Alice should not respond by constantly carrying Neri, constantly checking on her, or refusing to leave her alone.

That would turn:

> **"I forgot Neri."**

into:

> **"I must never let Neri be alone."**

Neither conclusion is healthy.

Neri likes being alone.

She simply needs to learn that being alone does not mean being unwanted.

---

## Final Assessment

The most concerning change is not that Neri believes she was forgotten.

She was.

The concerning change is what she believes that fact means.

Neri appears to have concluded:

> **"If they really wanted me, they would have remembered me."**

And from there:

> **"If they still keep me, they must be forced to."**

This belief should not be fought with constant attention.

It should be allowed to weaken naturally through consistent behavior.

Residents should continue living normally.

They should allow Neri to approach when she chooses.

They should respect her when she wants solitude.

They should help when she genuinely needs help.

They should hold her when she asks to be held.

And when she leaves again, they should let her leave.

Because the long-term lesson Neri needs is not:

> **"Everyone will always remember where you are."**

That would be impossible.

It is:

> **"Being forgotten once does not mean you were unwanted."**
