### **MEDICAL OBSERVATION NOTE**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – LEMON CONSUMPTION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 – Medical & Internal Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Medical Services have documented an unusual coping behavior exhibited by Corin Lockwood.

During periods of emotional distress or worsening intrusive memories, Corin has been observed consuming fresh lemon slices.

Contrary to initial assumptions, this behavior is not driven by preference alone.

---

# **OBSERVATION**

When asked why she eats lemons so frequently, Corin replied:

> "...It hurts."

Medical staff initially assumed she disliked the sour taste.

Corin clarified.

> "...That's why."

She explained that the intense sourness provides a strong physical sensation that briefly grounds her during periods of emotional overload.

In her own words:

> "...If I can focus on something simple..."

> "...my head gets a little quieter."

Psychological Services consider this a grounding behavior rather than a method of treating physical pain.

The sensation does not remove her emotional distress, but it can temporarily interrupt overwhelming thoughts and help her remain present.

---

# **SAFEHOUSE OBSERVATIONS**

Residents eventually noticed that Corin often reached for a lemon during difficult moments.

No one commented on it.

Fresh lemons simply became a permanent item in the SAFEHOUSE kitchen.

Occasionally someone would replace the bowl without mentioning it.

Corin noticed.

She never asked who did it.

# **MEDICAL NOTE**

Medical Services have advised that while Corin's use of lemons appears to function as a relatively safe grounding strategy, it should not replace appropriate medical or psychological treatment.

Personnel should continue to encourage Corin to use the broader coping strategies available to her, including medication, therapy, and support from trusted individuals.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Fresh lemons remain stocked in Project SAFEHOUSE as one of several small comforts available to Corin.

To most residents, they are just fruit.

To Corin, they are a familiar way to anchor herself when painful memories threaten to overwhelm her.
