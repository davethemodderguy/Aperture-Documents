## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

C.O.R.E. records confirm that Neri's former family is deceased.

Approximately five years after abandoning Neri, the family residence was destroyed in a severe house fire.

All remaining family members present at the residence were killed.

The incident occurred independently of Neri's abandonment and subsequent life within the Blacksite.

No evidence currently indicates that Neri caused, influenced, predicted, or otherwise contributed to the fire.

The deaths are therefore classified as an unrelated historical tragedy.

Nevertheless, the event carries considerable psychological significance when viewed alongside Neri's existing history.

---

## FAMILY HISTORY

Neri was raised within a family structure in which children were evaluated heavily according to perceived usefulness.

Neri's younger sister was considered expendable by the family and was ultimately killed after being deemed useless.

Neri subsequently experienced the deterioration of her own standing within the household.

Her interest and ability in chemistry failed to restore her perceived value.

Eventually, Neri herself was abandoned.

She was later captured and subjected to experimentation that resulted in her transformation into a Seal Thiren.

The family did not attempt to recover her.

They did not search for her.

They did not attempt to determine whether she survived.

From Neri's perspective, she had effectively ceased to exist to them.

---

## APPROXIMATELY FIVE YEARS LATER

The family residence was destroyed by fire.

Available records describe a rapidly developing residential fire that resulted in the deaths of the remaining occupants.

Emergency response was unsuccessful in recovering any survivors from the structure.

The family therefore ceased to exist approximately five years after Neri's abandonment.

No surviving immediate family members have been identified.

## NERI'S KNOWLEDGE OF THE FIRE

Neri has been informed that her former family is dead.

Her response was unexpectedly restrained.

No visible celebration was observed.

No anger was recorded.

No expression of relief was provided.

Neri simply wrote:

> **“Oh.”**

After several seconds, she added:

> **“So they're all gone.”**

She then stopped writing.

When asked whether she wanted to discuss it, Neri shook her head.

The subject was not raised again.

## PSYCHOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE

The deaths present an unusual complication.

Neri spent years believing that her family had deliberately decided she was not worth keeping.

That belief remains deeply embedded in her identity.

The subsequent death of the family does not undo that experience.

Their deaths cannot retroactively turn abandonment into acceptance.

They cannot give Neri the childhood she lost.

They cannot return her sister.

They cannot erase the years of neglect.

They cannot change what happened to her afterward.

Therefore, personnel should **not** frame the fire as some form of cosmic justice.

Neri does not need to be told:

> “They got what they deserved.”

Nor should personnel tell her:

> “At least they can't hurt you anymore.”

The first imposes an emotional response she may not share.

The second assumes that the primary thing she needed was protection from them.

Neither addresses the actual loss.

---

## THE MORE DIFFICULT QUESTION

Neri has occasionally appeared to struggle with an unresolved thought:

> **“If they are dead, why do I still feel like I was the one who was discarded?”**

Their deaths do not change the fact that they chose to abandon her.

Death does not retroactively validate their treatment of her.

Nor does it make Neri responsible for surviving when they did not.

She survived.

They did not.

That is all.

---

## C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

There is a notable irony surrounding Neri's former family.

They spent her childhood teaching her that a person's right to remain depended upon their usefulness.

Neri eventually survived without them.

She entered a completely different environment.

She became a resident rather than an asset.

She was cared for even when she had nothing to provide.

She was given food.

A room.

Clothing.

Medical treatment.

Friends.

Family.

A keycard.

A place to sit.

A place to return to.

None of these things were contingent upon her productivity.

Yet Neri still struggles to believe that this is real.

Her former family's deaths therefore do not represent the end of that chapter psychologically.

The physical family is gone.

The lesson they taught her remains.

## C.O.R.E. CONCLUSION

Neri's former family is deceased.

Their deaths were tragic.

Their treatment of Neri was also tragic.

One does not cancel the other.

The house fire does not provide closure.

It does not repair Neri's childhood.

It does not make her abandonment less real.

It does not require forgiveness.

It does not require hatred.

And it does not require Neri to feel anything at all.

The family that discarded Neri is gone.
