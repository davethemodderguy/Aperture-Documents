### **ANOMALOUS PHYSIOLOGY / SLEEPING POSITION REPORT**

# **CORIN — WING-RELATED SLEEP RESTRICTIONS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 — Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Condition:** **Permanent Sleeping Position Adaptation**  
**Status:** Stable

---

# **OVERVIEW**

The development of Corin's wings has permanently changed how she sleeps.

Corin can no longer comfortably sleep on her back.

The wings are directly integrated into her back, making a supine sleeping position physically impractical.

When Corin lies on her back, her entire body weight presses the wings against the surface beneath her.

To Corin, the sensation is comparable to:

> **"Crushing them underneath me."**

She therefore sleeps exclusively in positions that keep pressure away from the wings.

---

# **PREFERRED POSITIONS**

Corin currently sleeps in two primary positions:

### **Side Sleeping**

Her preferred position.

Corin folds her wings as tightly as possible and rests on one side.

The wings are positioned slightly behind her so that they are not trapped beneath her body.

She tends to alternate sides during longer periods of sleep.

---

### **Stomach Sleeping**

Corin can also sleep face-down.

In this position, her wings remain free behind her.

She generally spreads them slightly to prevent the feathers from being compressed.

This position is less comfortable than side sleeping but remains preferable to sleeping on her back.

---

# **BACK SLEEPING**

Sleeping on her back is effectively prohibited.

When Corin accidentally rolls onto her back during sleep, she usually wakes quickly.

The pressure causes discomfort across the wings and their attachment points.

She has described it as:

> **"Like sleeping on a pile of bones that are attached to my spine."**

The description was considered sufficiently accurate by medical personnel.

---

# **WING POSITIONING**

Corin has become surprisingly skilled at arranging her wings before sleeping.

She carefully folds them.

Then adjusts their position.

Then checks that neither wing is trapped beneath her.

This has become part of her nightly routine.

Orphie occasionally helps if Corin is particularly tired.

---

# **THE BED**

Corin's sleeping area has been modified accordingly.

The mattress has been adjusted to provide additional space behind her shoulders.

Soft bedding is placed around the wings rather than directly beneath them.

The goal is to allow Corin to change positions without compressing the feather structures.

---

# **ORPHIE'S OBSERVATION**

Orphie initially found Corin sleeping face-down unusual.

She asked:

> **"Is that actually comfortable?"**

Corin, half-asleep, replied:

> **"More than crushing my wings."**

Orphie nodded.

> **"...Fair."**

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL ADAPTATION**

Interestingly, Corin does not resent this particular limitation as much as she does the effects of rain.

She considers it an acceptable trade.

She lost one sleeping position.

In exchange, she gained the ability to fly.

Her reasoning:

> **"I'll take wings over sleeping normally."**

This represents a notably positive attitude toward her altered physiology.

---

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin's wings have changed nearly every aspect of her relationship with her body.

They changed how she travels.

They changed how she interacts with weather.

They changed how she moves through the world.

And now they have changed how she sleeps.

Corin can no longer lie on her back and forget about her wings.

She has to fold them carefully.

Make room for them.

Protect them.

Then settle onto her side or stomach.

It is another small inconvenience of having wings.

Corin's response remains characteristically simple:

> **"Worth it."**
