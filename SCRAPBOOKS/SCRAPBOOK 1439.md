# 1. AFTERMATH

Life in the SAFEHOUSE continued.

That was the first and most disturbing result of Jake's disappearance.

Nothing stopped.

The lights still turned on every morning.

The cafeteria still opened.

Residents still argued over food.

Neko continued working.

Neri continued her chemistry.

Alice continued wandering the halls.

Anby continued her training.

Bao continued cooking.

Phoenix continued treating patients.

Twiggy returned to her medical duties.

The elevator continued operating.

The Blacksite continued functioning.

There was no memorial.

There was no empty chair officially designated as belonging to someone.

There was no room left untouched because everyone missed its occupant.

There was simply...

**life.**

---

# 2. THE BIRTHDAY

Anby's birthday was eventually remembered as an ordinary birthday.

Nobody remembered why it had ended early.

Nobody remembered why the cake had been abandoned.

Nobody remembered why there had been apples on the floor.

Anby kept the card.

She did not know why.

Whenever someone asked where she got it, she could only answer:

> **ANBY:**  
> "I don't know."

She never threw it away.

She couldn't explain why.

---

# 3. ALICE

Alice eventually stopped thinking about the strange feeling that something was missing.

It happened less frequently.

At first, she would occasionally stop in the hallway.

She would look around.

She would feel like she had forgotten someone.

Then the feeling would disappear.

Eventually, it stopped bothering her.

One afternoon she said:

> **ALICE:**  
> "Anby, I feel like I forgot something."

Anby looked up.

> **ANBY:**  
> "What?"

Alice shrugged.

> **ALICE:**  
> "I don't know."

She smiled.

> **ALICE:**  
> "If I forgotten it then it isn't important anymore."

And they moved on.

---

# 4. BAO

Bao changed in ways nobody could explain.

She became slightly more attentive to residents who were quiet.

If someone sat alone for too long, she checked on them.

If someone said they were fine, she sometimes checked again later.

She began leaving food outside doors.

She began asking:

> **"Did you eat?"**

more frequently.

Nobody knew why.

Bao didn't know either.

Sometimes she would walk past an empty section of the SAFEHOUSE and feel a strange ache in her chest.

She never found its source.

She simply kept walking.

---

# 5. PHOENIX

Phoenix became more cautious with patients.

She stopped assuming that a quiet patient was comfortable.

She stopped treating silence as evidence that everything was fine.

She checked on people more often.

She would sometimes stand outside a patient's room for several seconds before leaving.

She could never explain why.

There was a particular feeling she could never shake.

The feeling that once—

She had failed to notice someone.

But there was no patient in her records that matched the feeling.

So she continued working.

---

# 6. TWIGGY

Twiggy remembered none of the conversation.

She remembered being at Anby's birthday.

She remembered drinking too much Nitro Fuel.

She remembered waking with a headache.

She remembered apologizing for something she couldn't remember doing.

She eventually stopped wondering about it.

Everyone else had.

There was nothing left to remember.

Twiggy returned to her work.

She treated patients.

She helped residents.

She laughed.

She ate.

She complained about paperwork.

She lived normally.

She did not know that somewhere in the missing portion of her memory was the moment she broke a child's heart.

---

# 7. NERI

Neri continued drawing.

Sometimes she would draw the same figure.

A small child.

White hair.

Round glasses.

A halo.

A teddy bear.

She never knew who it was.

Eventually she stopped trying to identify the figure.

She simply called the drawings:

> **"The Star."**

She never showed them to anyone.

She didn't know why she felt protective of the character.

---

# 8. ANBY

Anby occasionally ate apples.

She liked them.

She could not remember why.

Sometimes she would sit near a window and look toward the night sky.

She would see thousands of stars.

One particular region of the sky always felt strangely familiar.

She couldn't explain it.

She would simply stare for a while.

Then return inside.

She never knew that one of those stars had once looked back at her from inside a six-year-old boy.

---

# 9. THE TEDDY BEAR

The teddy bear remained in storage.

Nobody knew who owned it.

It was eventually placed in a general anomalous artifact archive.

The catalog entry was brief:

> **ITEM:** Children's teddy bear.  
> **ORIGIN:** Unknown.  
> **ANOMALOUS PROPERTIES:** None currently active.  
> **SIGNIFICANCE:** Unknown.

No one realized that the bear had once contained the heart of a star.

No one realized that it had been carried through every room of the SAFEHOUSE.

No one realized that a child had once held it against his chest while frightened.

No one remembered.

---

# 10. THE EMPTY FLOOR

The missing elevator destination eventually stopped being noticed.

The SAFEHOUSE's architecture adapted.

A blank space in the directory became normal.

A floor nobody could access became unremarkable.

Residents stopped asking why it existed.

New residents never noticed anything unusual.

There was simply an empty coordinate.

An architectural oddity.

Nothing more.

---

# 11. WHAT THEY DID NOT KNOW

Nobody knew what had happened.

Nobody knew that Jake had stood at the birthday party entrance carrying apples.

Nobody knew that he had written Anby's card.

Nobody knew that he had recovered after five weeks of illness.

Nobody knew that his halo had returned.

Nobody knew that his teddy bear's heart had begun beating again.

Nobody knew that he had walked into the party because he wanted to surprise them.

Nobody knew that he had heard every word.

Nobody knew that he had looked at them with shock and betrayal.

Nobody knew that he had left without saying anything.

Nobody knew that he had erased himself afterward.

And nobody knew why.

---

# 12. THE CHILD

That was the cruelest part.

They had not broken the heart of an enemy.

They had not destroyed a weapon.

They had not betrayed a soldier.

They had not abandoned an adult who could understand why they were busy.

They had broken the heart of a child.

A child who had spent weeks in a medical bed.

A child who had finally recovered.

A child who had remembered someone else's birthday.

A child who had brought apples.

A child who had written:

> **"I'm better now, can we go somewhere sometime?"**

And when he heard that nobody would care if he died—

he believed them.

---

# 13. LIFE CONTINUED

Months passed.

The SAFEHOUSE remained busy.

Residents laughed.

Residents argued.

Residents celebrated.

People fell in love.

People recovered from injuries.

New projects began.

Old projects ended.

The Blacksite changed.

The world moved forward.

Nobody mourned Jake.

They couldn't.

They didn't know they had lost him.

And that was exactly what Jake had wanted.

Or, perhaps more accurately—

exactly what Jake had believed they wanted.

---

# 14. ARCHIVAL CONCLUSION

There was no funeral.

There was no grave.

There was no photograph.

There was no name carved into anything.

There was no final goodbye.

There was only a birthday card.

An apple.

A teddy bear.

A blank elevator destination.

And a vague feeling in the hearts of people who could no longer remember why they sometimes felt sad.

They lived on.

They laughed.

They worked.

They slept.

They forgot.

And unbeknownst to every single one of them—

they had broken a child's heart so completely

that the child decided the kindest thing he could do

was make sure nobody ever had to remember him again.
