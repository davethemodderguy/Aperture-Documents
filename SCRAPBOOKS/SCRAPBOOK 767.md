# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — FINAL STRAW**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — CRITICAL CHILD SAFETY  
**Incident:** Repeated Unintentional Isolation  
**Status:** **PROTOCOL REVISION — IMMEDIATE**

---

# **THE INCIDENT**

It happened again.

Alice was left outside.

Nobody intended to leave her there.

Nobody deliberately abandoned her.

Someone went outside.

Alice followed.

The employee returned inside.

The door closed.

And Alice remained outside.

Again.

---

# **THE DIFFERENCE**

Previously, someone would eventually notice.

Someone would open a door.

Someone would look outside.

Someone would hear something.

This time—

Alice simply sat beneath the overhang.

Waiting.

Her white beanie rested loosely over her head.

Her rabbit ears hung downward under gravity.

Her hoodie and coat kept her warm enough for the moment.

Her boots rested against the ground.

Her small feather-like tail remained untouched.

She didn't knock.

She didn't wave.

She didn't call.

She couldn't.

---

# **NOBODY NOTICED**

Minutes passed.

Then longer.

People continued working.

People walked through the building.

People passed the same exterior doors.

Alice remained outside.

She wasn't making trouble.

She wasn't crying loudly.

She wasn't demanding attention.

She was simply there.

---

# **CORIN FOUND HER**

Corin eventually opened the door.

She stopped immediately.

Alice was sitting outside.

Alone.

Again.

Corin stared at her.

Alice looked up.

Their eyes met.

Alice's expression didn't change.

She simply stood.

Her knees nearly buckled.

Corin caught her.

---

# **CORIN DIDN'T SPEAK**

For several seconds, Corin said nothing.

She just held Alice.

Alice rested against her.

No complaint.

No anger.

No accusation.

Nothing.

That somehow made it worse.

---

# **THE FINAL STRAW**

Corin brought Alice inside.

Then she called everyone.

Not just the SAFEHOUSE residents.

Staff.

Security.

Medical personnel.

Anyone responsible for Alice's safety.

Everyone came.

Corin stood in the middle of the room with Alice in her arms.

Her voice was quiet.

> "This cannot happen again."

Nobody disagreed.

---

# **THE RECORD**

The incidents were reviewed.

The list was long.

Alice had been:

- Left outside.
    
- Forgotten in corridors.
    
- Closed behind doors.
    
- Left inside elevators.
    
- Forgotten in the cafeteria.
    
- Left in storage areas.
    
- Forgotten during walks.
    
- Nearly exposed to the Anti-Rodent System.
    
- Left outside during rain.
    
- Left outside during cold weather.
    
- Forgotten while trying to attend her own birthday.
    

And now—

again.

---

# **THE REALIZATION**

The problem was not that people didn't love Alice.

They did.

That was never in question.

The problem was that everyone had unconsciously relied on the same assumption:

> **"Someone else is probably with her."**

For an ordinary child, that assumption might occasionally be harmless.

For Alice—

it was dangerous.

Alice was too quiet.

Too small.

Too willing to wait.

Too unwilling to inconvenience anyone.

And too incapable of reliably asking for help.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Someone eventually asked Alice:

> "Why didn't you come inside?"

Alice looked toward the door.

Then toward the floor.

She slowly wrote on a piece of paper:

> **"Door closed."**

That was all.

Someone asked:

> "Why didn't you knock?"

Alice stared at the question.

She didn't write anything.

---

# **THE NEW RULE**

The previous instruction had been:

> **"Never leave Alice alone."**

It was no longer considered sufficient.

The new policy became:

# **ALICE MUST HAVE A SPECIFIC RESPONSIBLE PERSON.**

Not:

_"Someone should watch Alice."_

Not:

_"She's probably with Corin."_

Not:

_"I thought Belle had her."_

A specific person.

A specific responsibility.

A confirmed handoff.

If Alice leaves a room with someone, that person remains responsible until Alice is physically handed to another responsible person.

No assumptions.

No exceptions.

No "I thought someone else had her."

---

# **DOOR POLICY**

Before closing any exterior or restricted-access door:

**Personnel must visually confirm that Alice is not on the other side.**

This applies even when:

- Alice was not expected to be present.
    
- Alice was not part of the activity.
    
- Alice was seen elsewhere recently.
    
- Someone else was supposedly watching her.
    

A visual confirmation is mandatory.

---

# **ELEVATOR POLICY**

Alice is never permitted to enter an elevator without a responsible person accompanying her.

If Alice enters an elevator unexpectedly, the accompanying employee becomes responsible for her until she reaches a confirmed destination.

---

# **OUTDOOR POLICY**

Alice may not wander outdoors without supervision.

Not even around SAFEHOUSE.

Not even for a few minutes.

Not even when the weather is clear.

Not even when someone says:

> "I'll be right back."

---

# **THE IMPORTANT PART**

The policy was not created because Alice was troublesome.

It was created because Alice **wasn't.**

She wouldn't chase anyone down.

She wouldn't shout.

She wouldn't complain.

She wouldn't bang on doors.

She wouldn't demand to be found.

She would simply sit somewhere and wait.

And everyone had finally understood how dangerous that was.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle was furious.

Not at Alice.

At the situation.

She hugged Alice carefully.

> "You don't have to wait anymore."

Alice looked at her.

Belle continued:

> "If you're somewhere, we'll find you."

Alice didn't respond.

She simply leaned against Belle.

---

# **NEKO**

Neko immediately began designing a tracking system.

Miss Neko stopped him.

> "Not enough."

Neko looked at her.

> "What?"

> "We shouldn't need a machine to remember a child."

Neko went silent.

He put the tools down.

---

# **STAR**

Star stayed beside Alice.

She gently touched the top of Alice's beanie.

> "You're not a burden."

Alice looked down.

Star repeated it.

> "You're not a burden."

Alice didn't nod.

She didn't smile.

But she stayed beside Star.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin eventually carried Alice back to the SAFEHOUSE.

Alice was exhausted.

Her ears remained limp.

Her body was light in Corin's arms.

She fell asleep against her.

Corin looked down at her.

Quietly:

> "...I'm sorry."

Alice didn't hear.

She was already asleep.

---

# **FINAL POLICY STATEMENT**

The Blacksite has handled dangerous anomalies.

Containment breaches.

Dimensional events.

Hostile entities.

Reality distortions.

None of those justify allowing a five-year-old child to repeatedly disappear simply because she is too quiet to notice.

The responsibility is not Alice's.

It is ours.

From this point forward:

**Alice does not get forgotten.**

**Alice does not get left behind.**

**Alice does not wait alone.**

If she follows someone—

they know.

If she walks outside—

someone goes with her.

If she enters a room—

someone knows where she is.

If she becomes quiet—

someone checks.

And if someone says:

> _"I thought someone else had her."_

The answer is:

> **"Then you should have checked."**

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice never asked anyone to watch her.

She never demanded attention.

She never complained when people forgot.

She simply waited.

That was precisely the problem.

Because Alice was never going to be the one who saved herself.

So the adults around her finally made the decision for her.

She would not have to.

**Someone will remember.**

**Someone will notice.**

**Someone will stay.**

Every time.

**End of CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**
