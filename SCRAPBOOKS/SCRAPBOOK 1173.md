# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A previously undocumented property of the **12 Gauge Magnesium Magnum “Hellfire” shotshell** has been confirmed during post-camping analysis.

Hellfire projectiles do **not** remain stable indefinitely after leaving the weapon.

If the projectile remains airborne for approximately:

> **15 SECONDS**

without striking a physical object, its anomalous structure begins to destabilize.

After sufficient airborne exposure, the projectile:

> **disintegrates completely.**

No persistent projectile remains.

No delayed impact occurs.

No recovery is generally possible.

This substantially reduces the practical effective range of Hellfire ammunition.

---

# 2. IMPACT-DEPENDENT IGNITION

The fire-producing effect of Hellfire ammunition is also confirmed to be **impact-dependent.**

The magnesium projectile must physically contact an object before its extreme combustion behavior is initiated.

Therefore:

> **An airborne Hellfire projectile does not simply remain burning indefinitely.**

If fired into open air and allowed to remain airborne beyond its stability period, it will eventually disintegrate.

This was initially considered a major limitation.

It was subsequently recognized as a potentially useful safety characteristic.

---

# 3. EFFECTIVE RANGE

The discovery changes the previous understanding of Hell Fire's operational characteristics.

The theoretical range of the projectile and the **effective range of the Hellfire effect** are no longer equivalent.

The projectile may travel through open space.

However, prolonged flight reduces the likelihood that it will remain intact long enough to produce its intended effect.

C.O.R.E. therefore classifies Hellfire as having:

> **A deliberately limited practical effective range.**

The exact effective range is dependent on environmental and projectile-specific variables and is not included in this document.

---

# 4. OPEN-AIR FIRING

The discovery produced an immediate question:

> **What happens if someone fires Hellfire ammunition directly into the air?**

The answer is considerably less catastrophic than initially expected.

If the projectile does not encounter an object, it eventually reaches its airborne stability limit and disintegrates.

There is therefore no persistent burning projectile continuing indefinitely through the environment.

This does **not** make indiscriminate firing acceptable.

The projectile can still encounter:

- structures;
    
- vegetation;
    
- equipment;
    
- aircraft;
    
- wildlife;
    
- personnel;
    
- or other unintended objects
    

before the stability limit is reached.

Accordingly:

> **“It disappears after fifteen seconds” is not a safety authorization.**

---

# 5. CAMPING INCIDENT RECONSIDERED

The discovery prompted C.O.R.E. to revisit the recent camping incident involving Elsher.

Elsher had used HF-001 to ignite the campfire.

The projectiles struck the intended fuel source and produced the expected ignition.

This explains why the technique was so effective.

The Hellfire projectile did not remain airborne.

It encountered the wood.

The impact initiated the combustion behavior.

The resulting fire then continued independently.

---

# 6. HELL FIRE TESTING

A controlled materials test was conducted using appropriate containment procedures.

The projectile was observed during flight.

### Observation:

**0–15 seconds**

Projectile remains stable.

**Approaching 15 seconds**

Anomalous structural stability begins to decrease.

**Beyond stability threshold**

Projectile disintegrates.

No persistent projectile remains.

No secondary long-duration airborne combustion was observed.

# 9. UPDATED TECHNICAL CHARACTERIZATION

The Hellfire ammunition system is now characterized by three major behaviors:

### I. IMPACT ACTIVATION

The projectile must encounter a physical object for its extreme combustion effect to occur.

### II. AIRBORNE LIMITATION

The projectile cannot remain stable indefinitely while airborne.

### III. DISINTEGRATION

After approximately fifteen seconds of uninterrupted airborne travel, the projectile loses structural integrity and disintegrates.

Together, these properties substantially constrain the ammunition's practical effective range.

---

# 10. SAFETY CLASSIFICATION

C.O.R.E. has classified this limitation as:

> **BENEFICIAL BUT NOT RELIED-UPON SAFETY PROPERTY**

Personnel are reminded that the projectile remains hazardous during its stable flight period.

The disintegration mechanism should therefore never be treated as a substitute for a safe backstop, controlled firing environment, or appropriate range procedures.

The fact that an object **may disappear eventually** does not mean it is acceptable to fire toward that object.
