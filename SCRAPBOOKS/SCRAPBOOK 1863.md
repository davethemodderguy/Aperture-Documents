## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At an undetermined time on the first day of the incident, Jake Akana approached Phoenix and asked her to play with him.

Phoenix was severely sleep-deprived, physically exhausted, and in a condition that substantially impaired her ability to regulate her response. Rather than explaining that she could not play, she instinctively scolded him.

The precise wording of the reprimand has not been recovered. Both Phoenix and Tom remember the exchange differently, and neither account should be treated as a verbatim transcript.

What followed is established.

Jake withdrew.

He died.

His resurrection mechanism did not activate.

For more than four days, Jake remained dead.

Tom spent those days in escalating panic, repeatedly attempting to determine why the mechanism had failed and whether his friend would ever return. Phoenix, already exhausted before the incident, suffered further sleep loss under the weight of guilt and fear. Aries remained with her, attempting to keep her from collapsing under the belief that her words had caused Jake's death.

The revival finally occurred when Tom, exhausted and moving around Jake's body in a state of panic, accidentally kicked Jake in the leg.

The physical stimulus was followed by the familiar white fire.

Jake's body began reconstructing itself.

Tom did not immediately celebrate.

He screamed.

Phoenix did not immediately feel relieved.

She broke down.

And Aries, who had spent days trying to keep Phoenix together while fearing the same outcome as everyone else, found herself witnessing the moment they had all desperately wanted—and dreading what the moment meant.

The incident has established a further concern regarding Jake's resurrection: **the mechanism may remain inactive for prolonged periods, and an incidental physical stimulus may precede its activation.** This does not establish that kicking, touching, or any other particular action is a reliable trigger.

The incident also demonstrates that the emotional consequences of Jake's deaths extend beyond the person who dies. Tom's fear of losing Jake and Phoenix's guilt over their final interaction became crises in their own right.

No individual is to be treated as solely responsible for the incident.

No individual is to be permitted to dismiss it as harmless because Jake eventually returned.

## II. DAY ONE — A QUESTION, AN ANSWER, AND A CLOSED DOOR

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, Floor 7  
**Time:** Approximately 16:20  
**Source:** Phoenix and Tom Akana; reconstructed timeline

Phoenix had barely slept.

Her exhaustion was not subtle. She had been moving slowly, answering questions with minimal words, and struggling to remain attentive during ordinary conversations. She was not in a condition to offer anyone meaningful companionship.

Jake, however, did not understand the severity of her state.

He found her sitting in the common area and approached with the enthusiasm that had characterized his earlier attempts to find someone to play with him.

**JAKE:** "Phoenix!"

**PHOENIX:** "Hm?"

**JAKE:** "Do you wanna play with me?"

Phoenix shut her eyes.

For several seconds, she did not answer.

**JAKE:** "I found a new game! Tom said the rules don't make any sense, but that's because he doesn't understand the rules yet."

**PHOENIX:** "Jake. Not now."

**JAKE:** "It's really easy! You just—"

**PHOENIX:** "I SAID NOT NOW!"

The shout was louder than Phoenix intended.

Jake stopped talking.

Phoenix opened her eyes. She saw him standing there, his ears lowered, his hands drawn close to himself.

She was too tired to understand what had just happened until she saw his expression.

**PHOENIX:** "...Just leave me alone, okay? I can't deal with this right now."

There was a long silence.

**JAKE:** "Oh."

Phoenix looked away.

**JAKE:** "Okay."

He left.

Phoenix remained where she was, her head resting against the back of the seat.

She was asleep within minutes.

She did not see Jake disappear down the corridor.

She did not see him return to his room.

And she did not know that, for Jake, the exchange had become something much larger than one exhausted person refusing to play.

## III. THE FIRST NIGHT — THE ABSENCE OF WHITE FIRE

Tom returned to the room later that night.

He found Jake unresponsive.

At first, he assumed Jake had fallen asleep in an awkward position. He called his name, nudged him, and tried to get him to answer.

Jake did not respond.

Tom's initial confusion gave way to fear.

**TOM:** "Jake?"

No answer.

**TOM:** "Hey. Come on. You can't just sleep through everything. You were supposed to show me that stupid game."

He tried again.

Nothing.

Tom knew what Jake's death looked like.

He had seen it before.

He knew what was supposed to happen next.

White fire.

Reconstruction.

The awful, familiar interval in which Jake's body returned to life and his breathing struggled to catch up with it.

Tom waited.

The room remained quiet.

No white fire appeared.

**TOM:** "...Jake?"

He checked again, his hands trembling.

**TOM:** "Okay. Very funny. You can stop now."

There was no answer.

Tom's voice began to crack.

**TOM:** "Jake, please."

The first hour passed.

Then the second.

Tom stayed beside him.

At some point, he contacted the people responsible for medical and residential emergencies. The response was immediate, but no one could offer a reliable explanation for the inactive resurrection mechanism.

They had already learned that Jake could remain dead for hours before his revival began.

They had not learned how long that interval could last.

They had not learned what caused it.

And they had no evidence that it would eventually end.

Tom refused to leave.

When someone suggested that he rest while the medical team continued its assessment, he reacted with immediate panic.

**TOM:** "No."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "Tom, we're staying with him."

**TOM:** "You don't understand."

**STAFF MEMBER:** "Then tell me."

Tom stared at Jake.

**TOM:** "He doesn't wake up."

The staff member paused.

**TOM:** "He always comes back. He's supposed to come back. There's supposed to be fire, and then he coughs, and then he can't breathe properly, and then I have to help him, and he gets annoyed because everyone's making a big deal out of it."

His voice grew thinner.

**TOM:** "But there's nothing."

He swallowed.

**TOM:** "There's nothing happening."

No one told him that everything would be fine.

They could not promise that.

Tom remained beside Jake.

And the first night ended without a revival.

## IV. DAY TWO — PHOENIX LEARNS WHAT SHE SAID

Phoenix woke to find Aries sitting nearby.

She had initially assumed that the strange atmosphere in SAFEHOUSE was the result of an ordinary emergency. She had not slept enough to understand the situation immediately.

Then Aries told her.

Jake was dead.

He had not revived.

And Tom had been beside him for hours.

Phoenix sat motionless.

**PHOENIX:** "...What?"

**ARIES:** "Jake hasn't come back."

**PHOENIX:** "No. I mean—"

Phoenix stopped.

Her eyes moved toward the door.

**PHOENIX:** "When?"

Aries answered.

Phoenix remembered the previous afternoon.

The question.

The shouting.

Jake's lowered ears.

His quiet little _"Okay."_

**PHOENIX:** "...Oh."

Aries watched her expression change.

**PHOENIX:** "I yelled at him."

**ARIES:** "Phoenix—"

**PHOENIX:** "He just wanted to play."

She stood up too quickly and had to catch herself against the table.

**PHOENIX:** "He just wanted to play with me."

**ARIES:** "You were exhausted."

**PHOENIX:** "I told him to leave me alone."

**ARIES:** "You were exhausted, Phoenix."

**PHOENIX:** "That doesn't change what I said!"

Her voice broke.

For a moment, Phoenix looked as if she might try to run straight to Jake.

Instead, she sat down again.

She pressed both hands over her face.

**PHOENIX:** "I could've just told him I was tired."

Aries did not contradict her.

There was no point pretending the words had not hurt Jake. There was also no point pretending that Phoenix had deliberately wanted to hurt him.

Both facts could be true.

**ARIES:** "Yes. You could have."

Phoenix lowered her hands.

She looked at Aries as if she had expected a different answer.

**ARIES:** "...You're supposed to tell me it wasn't my fault."

**ARIES:** "I don't know what caused him to die, and neither do you. I won't lie to you about that."

Phoenix's eyes filled with tears.

**ARIES:** "But being tired doesn't make you cruel. And saying something cruel when you're exhausted doesn't mean you wanted this to happen."

**PHOENIX:** "He heard me."

**ARIES:** "I know."

**PHOENIX:** "He asked me to play with him, and I made him feel like he was bothering me."

Aries moved closer.

**ARIES:** "Then we need to remember that. We need to understand what happened and make sure he has support when someone can't play with him."

Phoenix shook her head.

**PHOENIX:** "What if he thought nobody wanted him anymore?"

Aries had no easy answer.

She remembered Tom's words from the previous review. She remembered what Jake had endured during his seventy-five years of starvation and isolation.

She remembered that this was not the first time his fear of being alone had become unbearable.

**ARIES:** "Then we have to make sure he knows that isn't true when he comes back."

Phoenix stared at her.

**PHOENIX:** "When?"

Aries's expression faltered.

She corrected herself quietly.

**ARIES:** "...If he comes back."

Phoenix began crying.

Aries held her.

Neither of them slept much that night.

## V. DAY TWO TO DAY FOUR — THE HOUSE THAT WOULD NOT SLEEP

The following days were not recorded as a continuous medical observation. They were reconstructed from statements by Tom, Phoenix, Aries, and the responding personnel.

The emotional pattern was consistent.

Tom became increasingly frantic.

Phoenix became increasingly exhausted.

Aries attempted to care for Phoenix without abandoning Tom or Jake.

The medical team continued monitoring Jake's condition and reviewing the available information regarding his resurrection mechanism.

No one knew how long they would have to wait.

### 5.1. Tom Akana — The Watcher

Tom spoke to Jake constantly.

He told him about things that had happened in SAFEHOUSE.

He complained about the game Jake had wanted to show him.

He told Jake that he was angry.

He told Jake that he was scared.

He told Jake that he needed him to wake up.

**TOM:** "You know what? I don't even like your stupid game."

Silence.

**TOM:** "It's probably another one of those games where you make up the rules as you go."

He rubbed his face.

**TOM:** "Which is cheating, by the way."

No response.

**TOM:** "You're supposed to argue with me."

He waited.

**TOM:** "Come on, Jake. Say something."

The room remained silent.

Tom's voice rose.

**TOM:** "I DON'T CARE IF THE GAME IS STUPID! I JUST WANT YOU TO WAKE UP!"

He immediately looked ashamed of the outburst, as though Jake might somehow hear the anger and decide that he had become another person who no longer wanted him around.

Tom leaned forward.

**TOM:** "...I'm not angry at you."

He swallowed.

**TOM:** "I'm scared."

He rested his forehead against the edge of the bed.

**TOM:** "I'm so scared."

At times, Tom insisted that Jake would come back.

At other times, he asked the medical team whether they had any evidence that the resurrection would happen at all.

When they explained that the mechanism's activation requirements remained unknown, he demanded to know why they had not figured them out already.

**TOM:** "You know how he works! You've seen him come back!"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "We've observed the mechanism. We don't fully understand it."

**TOM:** "THEN UNDERSTAND IT!"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "Tom—"

**TOM:** "HE'S BEEN DEAD FOR TWO DAYS!"

His voice echoed through the room.

Tom stared at the medical officer, breathing hard.

Then he looked at Jake.

**TOM:** "...Sorry."

He was not apologizing to the officer.

He was apologizing to Jake.

**TOM:** "I shouldn't have shouted."

He sat down again.

**TOM:** "You can shout at me when you wake up. You can tell me I'm being annoying. You can make fun of me for crying."

He took Jake's hand.

**TOM:** "Just wake up."

### 5.2. Phoenix — The Sleepless Witness

Phoenix's sleep deprivation became worse.

Whenever she closed her eyes, she remembered the exchange.

Whenever she woke, she remembered that Jake had not returned.

She began avoiding the place where she had spoken to him.

Aries eventually found her standing outside the room, unable to bring herself to enter.

**ARIES:** "You can go in."

**PHOENIX:** "I don't think he wants to see me."

**ARIES:** "He's not awake to tell us what he wants."

Phoenix flinched.

Aries regretted the wording immediately, but did not attempt to conceal the truth.

**ARIES:** "I'm sorry. That came out wrong."

**PHOENIX:** "No. It's true."

She stared through the doorway.

**PHOENIX:** "I don't even know if he can hear anything."

**ARIES:** "Neither do I."

**PHOENIX:** "Then what am I supposed to say?"

Aries stood beside her.

**ARIES:** "Whatever you wish you'd said before."

Phoenix was quiet for a long time.

Eventually, she entered.

She stood beside Jake's bed.

For a few seconds, she could not speak.

Then she sat down.

**PHOENIX:** "...Hey, Jake."

Nothing.

**PHOENIX:** "I know you're not answering."

She looked down at her hands.

**PHOENIX:** "I was really tired that day."

A pause.

**PHOENIX:** "That's not an excuse. I know that."

Her voice became softer.

**PHOENIX:** "You asked me to play with you, and I shouted at you. You didn't deserve that."

She wiped her eyes.

**PHOENIX:** "I'm sorry."

The apology did not produce white fire.

Jake did not stir.

Phoenix remained seated for several minutes, then began crying again.

Aries stayed with her.

When Phoenix finally tried to leave, she hesitated at the doorway.

**PHOENIX:** "What if he never gets to hear it?"

Aries did not offer a promise.

She only took Phoenix's hand.

**ARIES:** "Then I'll remember that you said it."

Phoenix looked at her.

**ARIES:** "And if he comes back, you can tell him again."

It was the only answer Aries could give without pretending to know the future.

### 5.3. Aries — The Person Holding Two People Together

Aries spent much of the incident moving between Tom and Phoenix.

She checked on Tom, encouraged him to drink water, and tried to persuade him to rest when medical personnel could safely maintain observation.

She sat with Phoenix when guilt became overwhelming.

She listened when Phoenix repeated the same questions.

Did Jake think she hated him?

Did he believe he was bothering her?

Had her words been the final thing he remembered?

Could she have prevented this by choosing different words?

Aries could not answer all of them.

She did, however, refuse to let Phoenix turn uncertainty into a verdict against herself.

**PHOENIX:** "I killed him."

**ARIES:** "You don't know that."

**PHOENIX:** "I made him feel unwanted."

**ARIES:** "You may have hurt him. That's something we need to face honestly. But you did not choose for him to die."

**PHOENIX:** "I should've been nicer."

**ARIES:** "Yes."

Phoenix stared at her, stunned.

Aries continued before she could mistake the answer for condemnation.

**ARIES:** "You should have told him you were exhausted instead of shouting. You know that now. You can apologize for that. You can change how you handle it next time."

She gently squeezed Phoenix's hand.

**ARIES:** "But you cannot take responsibility for every unknown part of what happened afterward. We don't know why his resurrection didn't activate. We don't know what he was thinking. We don't know what would have happened if you'd answered differently."

Phoenix's eyes filled again.

**ARIES:** "You can regret what you said without deciding you deserve to suffer forever."

**PHOENIX:** "...I don't know how to stop thinking about it."

**ARIES:** "Then don't try to solve the rest of your life tonight."

Phoenix gave a weak, miserable laugh.

**PHOENIX:** "I haven't slept properly in days."

**ARIES:** "I noticed."

**PHOENIX:** "You're not exactly sleeping either."

**ARIES:** "I'm aware."

**PHOENIX:** "You're supposed to be comforting me, not making fun of me."

**ARIES:** "I'm doing both."

For the first time in days, Phoenix almost smiled.

It vanished quickly.

But Aries noticed.

She did not treat it as a cure. She simply stayed.

## VI. DAY FOUR — THE ACCIDENT

By the fourth day, Tom was exhausted.

He had spent so long beside Jake that his sense of time had begun to deteriorate. Every sound made him look up. Every movement in the corridor made him expect someone to tell him that the medical team had discovered something.

Nothing changed.

Jake remained unresponsive.

Tom stood beside the bed, talking to him again.

**TOM:** "Okay. Listen."

He wiped his face with his sleeve.

**TOM:** "When you wake up, I'm going to be really angry with you."

He paused.

**TOM:** "...Not because you died."

He looked at Jake.

**TOM:** "I don't know. Maybe a little because you died. I don't know how that works."

He took a shaky breath.

**TOM:** "I'm going to be angry because you made me listen to myself talk for four days."

No answer.

**TOM:** "That's a terrible punishment, by the way."

Tom shifted his position.

His legs were stiff from sitting and standing in the same small area. He turned to reach for a chair, caught his foot against the edge of the bed, and stumbled.

His other foot swung forward instinctively to catch his balance.

It struck Jake in the leg.

**TOM:** "OW—!"

He grabbed the bedframe.

**TOM:** "Stupid—!"

Then he froze.

For a fraction of a second, nothing happened.

Tom stared at Jake's leg.

**TOM:** "...Jake?"

A thin line of white fire appeared.

Tom stopped breathing.

The fire spread across Jake's body, illuminating the room with the familiar, impossible light. It was the same phenomenon Tom had watched countless times before.

Except this time, he had waited four days to see it.

**TOM:** "NO WAY."

The reconstruction began.

Tom stumbled backward, knocking the chair over.

**TOM:** "OH MY GOD—"

White fire enveloped Jake.

His body began to rebuild.

Tom rushed forward, then stopped himself when the medical personnel moved in.

**TOM:** "JAKE!"

The fire intensified.

**TOM:** "JAKE, COME ON! COME ON!"

The reconstruction continued.

Tom was crying so hard that he could barely see.

**TOM:** "YOU'RE COMING BACK! YOU'RE ACTUALLY COMING BACK!"

Then Jake's chest moved.

He coughed.

Blood appeared at his lips.

His breathing became labored almost immediately.

The medical team moved to assist him, managing his airway and supporting his breathing while the reconstruction's immediate complications unfolded.

Tom could not stop shaking.

**TOM:** "He's breathing! He's—he's breathing, right?!"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "We're assisting him. Give us room, Tom."

**TOM:** "BUT HE'S BREATHING!"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "Tom, step back."

**TOM:** "I'M NOT LEAVING HIM!"

The officer paused, then spoke more gently.

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "You don't have to leave. Just give us space to help him."

Tom stumbled back half a step.

He watched every movement.

Every breath.

Every tremor.

Then Jake's eyes opened.

They were unfocused.

He tried to breathe again, coughing weakly.

Tom made a sound somewhere between a sob and a laugh.

**TOM:** "Jake?"

Jake blinked.

His voice was barely audible.

**JAKE:** "...Tom?"

Tom broke.

He rushed forward as soon as the medical team allowed it and wrapped his arms around Jake, careful not to interfere with treatment.

**TOM:** "YOU WERE DEAD FOR FOUR DAYS!"

Jake flinched at the volume.

**JAKE:** "Ow—don't yell..."

Tom pulled back immediately.

**TOM:** "SORRY! I'M SORRY!"

He was still crying.

**TOM:** "I'M SORRY, I JUST—YOU WOULDN'T WAKE UP!"

Jake looked confused.

He tried to speak, but his breathing caught. The medical officer instructed him to remain still and focus on breathing.

Tom clutched the edge of the bed.

**TOM:** "Don't talk. Don't—just breathe. Please just breathe."

Jake obeyed, too exhausted to argue.

After several moments, he looked at Tom again.

**JAKE:** "...Did you kick me?"

Tom froze.

He looked at Jake.

Then at his own foot.

Then back at Jake.

**TOM:** "...I DID WHAT?"

**JAKE:** "My leg hurts."

**TOM:** "I KICKED YOU BY ACCIDENT!"

**JAKE:** "...Oh."

Tom stared at him in disbelief.

**TOM:** "I SPENT FOUR DAYS THINKING YOU WERE NEVER COMING BACK, AND I ACCIDENTALLY KICKED YOU, AND THAT'S WHAT STARTED IT?!"

**JAKE:** "You kicked me?"

**TOM:** "YES!"

Jake considered this.

His eyes slowly closed as exhaustion overtook him.

**JAKE:** "...That was mean."

Tom made a strangled noise.

**TOM:** "I DIDN'T DO IT ON PURPOSE!"

Jake drifted back toward unconsciousness under medical supervision.

Tom immediately panicked again.

**TOM:** "WAIT! NO! HE'S CLOSING HIS EYES! IS HE DYING AGAIN?!"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "He's exhausted. We're monitoring him."

**TOM:** "YOU SAID THAT LAST TIME!"

**MEDICAL OFFICER:** "Tom, I understand you're frightened. He's being monitored. Stay with me and let us do our work."

Tom looked at Jake's face.

He forced himself to take a breath.

Then another.

He remained beside the bed, holding Jake's hand while the medical team continued treatment.

For the first time in four days, Tom could feel Jake's fingers holding his own.

He did not let go.

**PHOENIX:** "He's back?"

**PHOENIX:** "He's back?"

**ARIES:** "Phoenix—"

**PHOENIX:** "HE'S BACK?!"

Aries caught her by the shoulders.

**ARIES:** "They're helping him. We need to let them work."

Phoenix covered her mouth.

Her knees nearly gave way.

Aries held her upright.

For several seconds, neither of them spoke.

Not the quiet, exhausted tears of the previous days.

**PHOENIX:** "He's alive."

**ARIES:** "Yes."

**PHOENIX:** "I thought—"

**ARIES:** "I know."

**PHOENIX:** "What if he doesn't want to see me?"

**ARIES:** "Then we'll respect that. But you won't decide what he feels for him before he gets the chance to tell you."

Phoenix nodded, trembling.

When they reached the medical area, Tom was still beside Jake.

He looked up as they entered.

His eyes were swollen. His face was pale. His hands had not stopped shaking.

Phoenix took one step toward the bed.

Then stopped.

**PHOENIX:** "...Tom?"

Tom looked at her.

For a moment, she thought he might shout at her.

She would not have blamed him.

Instead, Tom's expression crumpled.

**TOM:** "He's back."

Phoenix covered her mouth.

**PHOENIX:** "I know."

**TOM:** "He was gone for four days."

**PHOENIX:** "I know."

**TOM:** "FOUR DAYS, PHOENIX!"

His voice cracked.

**TOM:** "I THOUGHT HE WAS NEVER COMING BACK!"

Phoenix started crying again.

**TOM:** "I THOUGHT—"

He stopped, unable to finish.

Phoenix lowered her head.

**PHOENIX:** "...I'm sorry."

Tom looked at her.

**PHOENIX:** "I know that doesn't fix anything. I know I can't undo what I said to him. I just—"

She wiped her face.

**PHOENIX:** "I'm sorry."

Tom looked back at Jake.

For a while, he said nothing.

Then he spoke in a small, exhausted voice.

**TOM:** "He asked you to play with him."

Phoenix flinched.

**PHOENIX:** "Yes."

**TOM:** "He was excited."

**PHOENIX:** "I know."

**TOM:** "He thought you'd play."

Phoenix began to sob.

Aries stepped closer, ready to intervene if the conversation became too much for either of them.

But Tom did not shout again.

He was too tired.

**TOM:** "...I don't know if that's why he did it."

Phoenix looked up.

**TOM:** "I don't know what he was thinking. I don't know why he didn't come back. I don't know why kicking him made the fire start."

He looked at his own foot with profound disgust.

**TOM:** "I don't know anything."

Phoenix sat down slowly.

**TOM:** "I'm just glad he's here."

His voice broke on the last word.

Phoenix covered her face.

Aries sat beside her and put an arm around her shoulders.

For a long time, the three of them remained in the room.

Jake slept under medical supervision.

Tom held his hand.

Phoenix sat nearby, unable to look away from the steady rise and fall of his chest.

Aries stayed between them, comforting Phoenix when she began to cry again and reminding Tom to breathe whenever he forgot.

No one declared the incident over.

Jake had returned.

That was not the same as everyone being all right.

## VIII. MEDICAL FINDINGS — THE KICK THAT WAS NOT A PROTOCOL

**Preliminary conclusion:** Jake's resurrection mechanism activated after Tom accidentally kicked him in the leg. The available evidence supports a temporal association between the stimulus and the appearance of white fire.

It does **not** establish that the kick itself was the necessary or sufficient cause.

The incident leaves several questions unresolved:

- Why did Jake remain dead for more than four days?
    
- Was the mechanism inactive throughout that period, or was it active but unable to initiate reconstruction?
    
- Did the accidental impact trigger activation, or did it coincide with an independent change in the mechanism?
    
- Would a different physical stimulus have produced the same result?
    
- Can the mechanism fail to activate indefinitely?
    

No current evidence answers these questions.

The medical team is expressly prohibited from reproducing the event through deliberate injury or lethal testing.

Tom's accidental kick must not be turned into a standardized revival procedure. He is not to be instructed to repeat it, and Jake must not be subjected to impact-based experimentation.

The incident was frightening enough without converting an accident into a protocol.

The medical team has also reaffirmed that Jake's reconstruction may be followed by coughing blood, temporary difficulty breathing, and respiratory distress. These complications require active medical care.

**The return of white fire is not the end of the emergency.**

## IX. RESIDENTIAL REVIEW — WHAT THE INCIDENT HAS CHANGED

### 9.1. For Phoenix

Phoenix's exhaustion explains the circumstances of her response. It does not erase the impact of her words.

Both facts must be allowed to coexist.

She needs rest, appropriate support, and an opportunity to process the incident without being consumed by guilt. Her sleep deprivation had already reached a dangerous level before Jake approached her; the crisis then made recovery even more difficult.

She must not be expected to remain awake indefinitely as a form of penance.

Nor should anyone tell her that her feelings are irrelevant because Jake has returned.

Phoenix is responsible for acknowledging how she treated him and for changing how she communicates when overwhelmed.

She is not responsible for predicting or controlling every aspect of an anomalous resurrection mechanism that no one fully understands.

When Jake is ready, she may apologize directly.

She must allow him to respond honestly, including if he is hurt or does not immediately wish to reconcile.

An apology is an offer of accountability, not a demand for forgiveness.

### 9.2. For Tom

Tom's panic was not an overreaction to a routine resurrection.

He spent more than four days beside a friend who remained dead despite a mechanism that had previously brought him back.

He had no guarantee that the mechanism would activate again.

His fear was justified by the uncertainty of the situation.

Tom must receive support and time to recover. He must not be praised for enduring four days without rest as though his suffering were proof of devotion.

He also must not be left with the belief that his accidental kick saved Jake in a way that makes him responsible for saving him next time.

**Tom did not fail Jake by being unable to revive him. He did not become Jake's sole rescuer because an accident happened to precede the revival.**

### 9.3. For Jake

Jake's wishes for companionship remain legitimate.

His need to feel wanted cannot be dismissed as childishness, nor can the crisis be reduced to Phoenix saying the wrong thing.

At the same time, the current evidence does not establish exactly what Jake believed or what directly precipitated his death. C.O.R.E. must speak with him when he is medically stable and emotionally ready, without leading questions or assumptions.

The immediate priority is to understand his experience, assess his safety, and provide consistent support.

He must not be confronted with the guilt of the people around him as soon as he wakes.

He must not be told that Phoenix has spent four days suffering because of him.

He must not be made to reassure Tom or comfort Phoenix before he has had the opportunity to recover himself.

Jake has just returned from more than four days of death.

He is not responsible for making everyone else feel better about it.

## X. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — THE THINGS WE CANNOT TAKE BACK

Phoenix had been tired.

Jake had wanted to play.

She had shouted.

He had left.

Four days later, Tom accidentally kicked Jake in the leg while trying to move around the bed, and white fire finally appeared.

It is tempting to turn these events into a simple story.

Phoenix said the wrong thing. Jake died. Tom kicked him. Jake came back.

Simple stories are comforting because they provide a clear cause and a clear solution.

But this incident offers neither.

We do not know whether Phoenix's words directly precipitated Jake's death. We do not know why his resurrection mechanism remained inactive for so long. We do not know whether the accidental kick was the trigger or merely the final event before a process that was already ready to begin.

We do know that Jake needed companionship.

We know Phoenix was too exhausted to offer it.

We know Tom was terrified.

We know Aries tried to comfort someone who could not stop blaming herself.

And we know that for more than four days, everyone who loved Jake had to face the possibility that he might never return.

Phoenix cannot take back her words.

Tom cannot take back the fear of those four days.

Aries cannot make either of them forget what they witnessed.

And Jake cannot be expected to wake up and make all of that pain disappear.

The next steps must be taken slowly.

Phoenix must be allowed to apologize without demanding forgiveness.

Tom must be allowed to recover without being told that he should feel grateful.

Aries must be allowed to stop holding everyone together long enough for someone to care for her, too.

And Jake must be given the time and support to tell us what happened from his side.

There is one thing this incident must not become.

It must not become a story in which Phoenix is the person who killed Jake.

It must not become a story in which Tom's accidental kick is the miracle that makes him responsible for every future revival.

It must not become a story in which Jake's ability to return from death makes the four days he spent dead an acceptable cost.

The people involved are not villains in a tragedy that could have been prevented by a single perfect sentence.

They are people who were tired, frightened, hurt, and unable to see the whole picture until it was too late.

That does not mean nothing needs to change.

It means the changes must be made without turning pain into a punishment.

Phoenix must learn to say, _"I can't play right now, but I still care about you."_

Tom must learn that leaving Jake with qualified support is not abandoning him.

SAFEHOUSE must learn that a child's repeated requests for companionship cannot be answered only by whichever exhausted person happens to be nearby.

And C.O.R.E. must learn that an anomalous ability to return from death is not a substitute for preventing the crisis in the first place.

Jake is alive.

For now, that is enough to be grateful for.

It is not enough to stop paying attention.

_— C.O.R.E. Residential Welfare & Medical Incident Review_

**FINAL OBSERVATION:** When Jake is ready, Phoenix will have the opportunity to tell him she is sorry.

She will not be able to erase the moment when she shouted.

But she can make sure that the next time she is too tired to play, he hears the truth instead of a rejection he must interpret alone.

And Tom, who spent four days waiting for a fire that never came, must never again be left believing that the life of the person he loves depends on him discovering the right way to touch a body that will not wake.

The kick was an accident.

The revival was real.

The fear was real, too.

None of those facts should ever be used to make the next crisis seem less serious than this one.
