### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana returned to his own grave in the Starlands.

This time, he did not go alone.

Camelia Candleheart accompanied him, having agreed to the visit without fully understanding what she would encounter. Her severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia has left her unable to recall her former name or life. She knows herself by a name she chose after seeing it in an anime, and the history that preceded it remains inaccessible.

Jake's circumstances are different, but no less painful.

He remembers enough to know that he was once alive. He knows that he had cat ears, a tail, and beautiful eyes. He knows that he died. His original body remains buried in the Starlands, while his current ghostly form carries visible signs of what happened to him.

During the visit, Camelia learned that Jake had not simply died.

**Jake had been murdered.**

Her surprise was immediate. She had known that her little brother was dead, and she had seen the condition of his ghostly body, but the fact that another person had deliberately killed him had not fully registered with her before.

The revelation connected two painful realities: Camelia had lost her former life and identity, while Jake had lost his life through violence.

They shared something neither child had chosen.

Neither could return to the person they had been before.

---

### II. ARRIVAL IN THE STARLANDS

The journey was quiet.

Jake travelled in his ghostly cat form for part of the visit, moving on four legs with his tail trailing behind him. He had no visible eyes or ears in this form, but he continued to navigate his surroundings without difficulty.

Camelia stayed close.

She carried her notebook. Before leaving, she had written down where they were going and who she was accompanying. She did not want to arrive somewhere unfamiliar and discover that she could no longer remember why she had come.

Jake had not asked her to memorize anything.

He had simply asked her to come with him.

**FIELD NOTES — ARRIVAL**

_The Starlands were calm when the pair reached the burial site._

_Jake approached the grave without speaking. Camelia followed, initially looking around at the surrounding area rather than at the marker itself._

**CAMELIA:** “Is this...?”

**JAKE:** “Mine.”

_Camelia stopped._

**CAMELIA:** “Your grave?”

**JAKE:** “Mm.”

_She looked down at the marker._

**CAMELIA:** “You brought me to your own grave.”

**JAKE:** “Yes.”

_His answer came in a short burst. He did not elaborate._

_Camelia approached the grave slowly. She appeared uncertain whether she should stand beside Jake or give him space._

**CAMELIA:** “Do you come here often?”

**JAKE:** “Sometimes.”

**CAMELIA:** “Why?”

_Jake remained quiet for a while._

**JAKE:** “Don't know.”

_Camelia accepted the answer._

She had learned that questions could be important even when they did not produce explanations. Sometimes, Jake could not say more. Sometimes, there was no answer he could offer.

She sat beside him.

Neither spoke for several minutes.

---

### III. THE REVELATION

Camelia eventually began asking about the grave itself.

She knew Jake was dead. She knew his ghostly appearance was not the one he had possessed in life. She knew he had once been a Cat Thiren, with ears and beautiful eyes that were no longer visible.

But she had not understood the full circumstances of his death.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake?”

**JAKE:** “Hmm?”

**CAMELIA:** “How did you die?”

_Jake's tail went still._

_The silence that followed was long enough that Camelia wondered whether she should withdraw the question._

**CAMELIA:** “You don't have to tell me. I just—”

**JAKE:** “Murdered.”

_The word was quiet._

_Camelia stared at him._

**CAMELIA:** “What?”

**JAKE:** “I... was murdered.”

_He struggled to continue._

**JAKE:** “Someone... killed me.”

_Camelia looked at the grave again, then back at Jake._

**CAMELIA:** “Someone did that to you?”

**JAKE:** “Mm.”

**CAMELIA:** “On purpose?”

_Jake did not answer immediately._

**JAKE:** “Yes.”

_Camelia's hands tightened around her notebook._

**CAMELIA:** “But you were just a little boy.”

**JAKE:** “Six.”

_He raised his head slightly._

**JAKE:** “I was... six.”

_Camelia's expression changed. She looked as though she had expected a terrible answer, but not one that could be stated so plainly._

**CAMELIA:** “You were six years old.”

**JAKE:** “Mm.”

**CAMELIA:** “Who would do that to a child?”

_Jake had no answer._

_He did not know who had killed him. The person responsible had not been identified._

_Camelia looked down at the notebook in her hands. She opened it, then closed it again without writing anything._

**CAMELIA:** “I'm sorry.”

**JAKE:** “Not... you.”

**CAMELIA:** “I know. I know it wasn't me.”

_Her voice faltered._

**CAMELIA:** “I just... I didn't know.”

**JAKE:** “Okay.”

_Jake moved closer until his side rested against her._

Camelia placed an arm around him.

For once, she did not try to find the right words.

---

### IV. TWO DIFFERENT KINDS OF LOSS

The pair remained beside the grave.

Camelia had learned something that she could not easily reconcile with the brother she knew.

Jake was not simply a child who had died too young. His death had been caused by another person's deliberate violence. The melted half of his head, the missing eyes and ears, and the scars on his ghostly body were not merely unusual features of a ghost. They existed alongside the fact that someone had killed him.

Camelia had no complete memory of her own former life.

She did not know her real name. She could not recall who she had been before becoming a ghost. She had selected _Camelia Candleheart_ as her name after seeing it in an anime, giving herself an identity she could use when her original one was beyond her reach.

Jake had an identity and a grave.

But the life connected to them had been violently taken from him.

Their experiences were not identical. C.O.R.E. cannot establish the circumstances of Camelia's death from the available information, and no assumption should be made that her past involved the same kind of violence as Jake's.

What connected them was the pain of an irreversible separation from their former lives.

Camelia could not remember the girl she had once been.

Jake could not return to the boy he had been.

And standing together beside his grave, they were forced to confront the fact that some losses could not be explained away.

**CAMELIA:** “I don't remember who I was.”

_Jake turns toward her._

**JAKE:** “Camelia... now.”

**CAMELIA:** “What?”

**JAKE:** “You're... Camelia.”

_She looks at him._

**JAKE:** “My... sister.”

_Camelia's eyes fill with tears._

**CAMELIA:** “Yes. I'm your sister.”

**JAKE:** “That's... enough.”

_The words come slowly, with effort._

_Camelia lowers her head._

**CAMELIA:** “I hope so.”

_Jake leans against her._

**JAKE:** “You... here.”

_She holds him closer._

**CAMELIA:** “I'm here, little brother.”

For Camelia, the exchange offered no recovered memories. For Jake, it changed nothing about the circumstances of his death.

But for a brief moment, neither of them had to face their loss alone.

---

### V. THE NOTEBOOK ENTRY

After some time, Camelia opened her notebook again.

She needed to record what she had learned. She could not guarantee that the information would remain accessible to her tomorrow, or even later that evening.

Jake watched her write.

**RECOVERED NOTEBOOK ENTRY — CAMELIA CANDLEHEART**

_Today I went to the Starlands with Jake._

_We visited his grave._

_Jake was murdered. Someone deliberately killed him when he was six years old._

_I did not know this before today._

_I don't know who did it. Jake doesn't know, either._

_I cannot remember my real name or my former life. I chose the name Camelia Candleheart myself._

_Jake and I have lost different things. I must not assume that I understand everything he feels just because I understand what it is like to lose something._

_He is my little brother._

_I am his older sister._

_When I forget, I can read this again. I can ask for help. I do not have to pretend I remember everything._

_And if Jake wants to visit his grave again, I can ask whether he wants company._

_I cannot give him back his life._

_I can be here with him now._

Camelia stopped writing.

She stared at the page, then turned toward Jake.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake?”

**JAKE:** “Mm?”

**CAMELIA:** “Can I come with you again? If you want to visit?”

_Jake looked at his grave._

**JAKE:** “Yes.”

**CAMELIA:** “Okay.”

**JAKE:** “Together.”

_Camelia smiled sadly._

**CAMELIA:** “Together.”

### VI. RETURN TO SAFEHOUSE

The return journey was uneventful.

Neither child offered a detailed account of what they had felt at the grave. C.O.R.E. did not request one.

On arrival, Camelia placed her notebook somewhere she could find it again. Jake settled nearby in his ghostly cat form.

There was no sudden breakthrough in his speech. No lost memories returned to Camelia. No new evidence identified Jake's murderer.

The visit resolved none of the underlying mysteries.

It did, however, establish that Camelia was now aware of the circumstances of Jake's death and that Jake had chosen to share this part of his history with her.

The welfare team recommends allowing the siblings to discuss the visit only when they wish to do so. Camelia should not be repeatedly tested on what she learned, and Jake should not be asked to retell the conversation simply because his speech makes the original account difficult to document.

Their notebook and this report can preserve the facts.

They cannot replace the experience.

### VIII. CLOSING OBSERVATION

Camelia had been surprised to learn that Jake was murdered.

She had known that he was dead. She had seen the melted half of his head, the absent eyes, the missing ears, and the little blue flame burning where his former life had ended.

But knowing that someone had killed him was different.

It meant that his death was not merely a tragedy that had happened to him.

Someone had made it happen.

And Jake, who was only six years old, had been left to carry the consequences.

Camelia could not remember her real name. She could not remember her old life. She could not tell Jake what kind of person she had been before she became the girl who called herself Camelia Candleheart.

Jake could not return to the life that had been stolen from him.

They shared no confirmed cause of death, no established history before SAFEHOUSE, and no explanation for why their paths had crossed.

They shared something else: the pain of being separated from the lives they should have been able to continue living.

On the journey home, Jake had managed only one short phrase when Camelia asked if he was ready to leave.

**JAKE:** “Go... home.”

And so they did.

Not to the homes they might once have known, and not to the lives they had lost.

To SAFEHOUSE.

To the people waiting for them.

To the small, imperfect life they could still share.

**C.O.R.E. FINAL NOTE:**

_We cannot promise either child that the past will become easier to understand. We cannot promise Camelia that her memories will return, or Jake that justice will be found quickly._

_We can ensure that his murder is not forgotten simply because he has returned as a ghost._

_And we can ensure that when these two children visit a grave, neither of them has to stand beside it alone._
