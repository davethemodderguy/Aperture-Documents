### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / MEMORY & PERSONNEL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — THE GRAVE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **MEMORY FRAGMENTATION / STABLE**

---

# **THE MEMORY**

Alice sometimes tries to remember her mother.

She closes her eyes.

She searches through what remains of her memories.

A face.

A voice.

A name.

Anything.

She cannot find them.

The memories are gone.

Not hidden.

Not simply difficult to access.

They appear to have been lost through prolonged deprivation, trauma, and the gradual deterioration of Alice's memory.

Alice knows there was a mother.

She knows her mother loved her.

She knows something happened to her.

And she knows she was taken away.

Almost everything else is gone.

---

# **THE GRAVE**

Alice occasionally visits the grave alone.

It is located near the place where she originally returned after disappearing.

There are no elaborate decorations.

No name.

No dates.

No photograph.

Only a small, poorly carved stone.

One word is written across it:

> **"Mom"**

Alice doesn't know her mother's name.

She doesn't remember what she looked like.

She doesn't remember her voice.

She doesn't remember what her mother used to call her.

But she remembers one thing with absolute certainty:

> **Mom loved Alice.**

---

# **FLOWERS**

Alice brings flowers whenever she visits.

Wild flowers.

Sometimes flowers from around the Treehouse.

Sometimes flowers she grows herself.

She places them carefully beside the stone.

Then sits quietly.

She doesn't always speak.

Sometimes she simply looks at the grave.

Sometimes she whispers:

> **"Hi, Mom."**

---

# **ONE VISIT**

On one particularly quiet afternoon, Alice sat in front of the stone.

A small bouquet rested beside her.

She stared at the word.

**Mom.**

Alice touched the stone.

> **"What's your name?"**

Silence.

She waited.

Nothing came back.

Alice frowned.

> **"...I forgot."**

She lowered her head.

For a moment, she tried harder.

She searched for a face.

Nothing.

A voice.

Nothing.

A name.

Nothing.

Her memories remained empty.

Alice eventually whispered:

> **"Sorry."**

---

# **WHAT SHE REMEMBERS**

Alice's surviving memories can be summarized into only a few fragments:

> **Mom was here.**

> **Mom loved Alice.**

> **Mom was taken away.**

> **Mom died.**

That's all.

There is no reliable visual memory.

No reliable chronological memory.

No reliable personal details.

Yet the emotional memory remains extraordinarily strong.

Alice doesn't remember **who** her mother was.

She remembers **what her mother meant.**

---

# **CORIN**

Corin knows about the visits.

She does not follow Alice unless Alice asks.

She understands that the grave is one of the few places Alice still considers private.

Corin once found Alice returning from it.

Alice was carrying fewer flowers than she had brought.

Corin asked:

> **"Did you leave some there?"**

Alice nodded.

> **"For Mom."**

Corin smiled gently.

> **"I'm sure she'd like them."**

Alice looked down.

> **"I don't remember her."**

Corin paused.

> **"You remember that she loved you."**

Alice nodded.

> **"Yeah."**

Corin placed a hand gently on her shoulder.

> **"That's still something."**

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie never attempts to fill in the missing memories.

She doesn't tell Alice what her mother looked like.

She doesn't invent stories.

She doesn't pretend to know things Alice doesn't remember.

If Alice asks:

> **"What did Mom look like?"**

Orphie simply answers:

> **"I don't know."**

Alice accepts it.

The truth is better than a fabricated memory.

---

# **THE GRAVE REMAINS**

The tombstone remains unnamed.

There is no evidence Alice will ever recover the missing memories.

The SAFEHOUSE has stopped treating memory recovery as an expectation.

Alice doesn't need to remember her mother's face for the love to have existed.

She doesn't need a name for the grave.

She doesn't need a photograph.

She only needs the one fragment she still carries.

> **"Mom loved Alice very much."**

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice has forgotten her mother.

She knows this.

Sometimes it hurts.

Sometimes she sits before the stone and tries desperately to remember.

She always fails.

But she still brings flowers.

She still says hello.

She still calls the stone **Mom**.

Perhaps someday even that memory will become harder to hold.

For now, however, Alice remembers one thing.

Not her mother's face.

Not her name.

Not her voice.

Just the feeling.

**She was loved.**

And Alice refuses to let that part disappear.
