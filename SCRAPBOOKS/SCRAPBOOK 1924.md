# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. — CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE WELFARE & ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOR DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** CORE-SH-INC-189  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — CHILD WELFARE / MEMORY DISORDER  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE OBSERVATION  
**SUBJECT:** Sora and Mia — Asymmetric Memory Retention Following Neurological Deterioration  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7  
**PRIORITY:** CRITICAL

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A significant difference has emerged between the memories of Sora and Mia.

**Mia remembers her big brother. Sora does not remember his little sister.**

Mia has gradually begun recalling their shared history. She remembers the farm, their grandmother, the doghouse, and the boy who used to protect her from wolves.

She remembers Sora.

She remembers what he looks like, how he behaves, and what their relationship means to her.

Sora, however, has lost access to almost all of those memories.

His concussion further deteriorated his already severe amnesia and left him with acquired face blindness. When he looks at another person, their face appears covered by black crayon-like scribbling.

Even if Mia stands directly in front of him, he cannot recognize her face.

More painfully, he cannot reliably remember her name, their relationship, or the experiences they shared.

Mia now knows exactly who she is looking for.

Sora does not know who is looking at him.

The two children are living with opposite experiences of the same lost relationship.

---

## 2. MIA — REMEMBERING EVERYTHING

Mia's recollection of Sora has become increasingly clear.

She remembers him as her big brother, despite their being the same age. Their arrangement was something they both agreed upon: Mia wanted to be his little sister, and Sora accepted that.

She remembers the farm.

She remembers the doghouse where she stayed while Sora chased wolves away from the sheep.

She remembers their grandmother.

She remembers the difficult years that followed.

And she remembers finding Sora again.

Most importantly, she remembers that he came back.

Mia has reportedly begun asking about him more frequently.

**MIA:** Is Sora awake?

**STAFF MEMBER:** We aren't certain.

**MIA:** Did he eat?

**STAFF MEMBER:** His intake is being monitored.

**MIA:** Does he remember me?

The staff member hesitated.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Not reliably.

Mia looked down.

**MIA:** Oh.

A pause.

**MIA:** But I remember him.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Yes.

**MIA:** I remember everything.

She remained quiet for a while.

**MIA:** That's not fair.

The staff member did not disagree.

### Assessment

Mia's memories are not a substitute for Sora's.

She cannot remember on his behalf, and she cannot restore his identity simply by telling him their history.

Nevertheless, her recollections may become useful if Sora is eventually able to engage with familiar stories and personal references.

She must not be made responsible for teaching him everything he has forgotten.

She is his little sister.

She is not his rehabilitation specialist.

---

## 3. SORA — THE FACE HE CANNOT SEE

Sora continues to spend most of his time inside his dark den.

His current accessible world remains extremely limited.

He recognizes his own name.

He recognizes the enclosure.

He knows that he talks to himself.

Almost everything else is uncertain.

The acquired face-recognition impairment makes interpersonal recognition particularly difficult.

When someone approaches, he can perceive their presence, clothing, body, and general appearance, but their face is obscured by the black scribbling phenomenon.

He cannot use facial features to distinguish familiar people.

Even when someone introduces themselves, the name may fail to connect with a memory.

### 3.1 Demonstration

During a supervised interaction, Mia approached the entrance of Sora's den.

She stopped outside.

**MIA:** Sora?

Sora looked toward her.

**SORA:** ...

**MIA:** It's me.

He tilted his head.

**SORA:** Who?

Mia hesitated.

**MIA:** Mia.

Sora looked at her face.

The black scribbling remained.

**SORA:** I can't see.

**MIA:** What can't you see?

**SORA:** Your face.

Mia's ears lowered.

**MIA:** Oh.

Sora looked at her again.

**SORA:** Black.

**MIA:** Can you see me?

**SORA:** Yes.

He paused.

**SORA:** But not you.

Mia did not immediately understand.

Then she realized what he meant.

He could see that someone was standing in front of him.

He simply could not see her as Mia.

**MIA:** I'm your sister.

Sora remained quiet.

**SORA:** Sister?

**MIA:** Your little sister.

A pause.

**SORA:** I have a sister?

Mia nodded.

**MIA:** Yes.

**SORA:** Where?

Mia's expression changed.

**MIA:** I'm here.

Sora looked toward her.

**SORA:** Oh.

He returned his attention to the floor.

**SORA:** Okay.

The interaction ended shortly afterward.

### 3.2 Assessment

Sora did not reject Mia.

He did not deliberately ignore her.

He did not understand who she was.

The distinction is essential.

Mia was standing in front of her big brother, but Sora was interacting with an unfamiliar person whose face he could not perceive.

His inability to recognize her must not be interpreted as a loss of affection or a conscious decision to distance himself.

At present, there is simply no reliable basis for him to understand the relationship.

---

## 4. INCIDENT — "YOU'RE MY BIG BROTHER"

During a later encounter, Mia attempted to explain their relationship using familiar childhood details.

**MIA:** Sora, do you remember the farm?

**SORA:** No.

**MIA:** The sheep?

**SORA:** No.

**MIA:** The doghouse?

Sora looked around his own enclosure.

**SORA:** This?

Mia nodded.

**MIA:** It looks like the one we had.

Sora touched the wall.

**SORA:** I live here.

**MIA:** You used to protect me.

He did not respond.

**MIA:** I stayed inside the doghouse, and you chased the wolves away.

Sora looked at her.

**SORA:** Why?

Mia smiled faintly.

**MIA:** Because you're my big brother.

Sora considered this.

**SORA:** Oh.

A pause.

**SORA:** I'm big brother?

**MIA:** Yes.

**SORA:** To you?

**MIA:** Yes.

He looked at the black scribbles covering her face.

**SORA:** I don't know you.

Mia's smile disappeared.

**MIA:** I know.

Sora lowered his head.

**SORA:** Sorry.

**MIA:** You don't have to be sorry.

**SORA:** I forgot.

Mia's eyes began to water.

**MIA:** I know.

She wanted to hug him.

She did not.

She remained outside the den, allowing him to keep his space.

---

## 5. THE CRUEL ASYMMETRY

C.O.R.E. considers the difference between the siblings' memories a major psychological concern.

Mia remembers a relationship that Sora cannot currently access.

She knows that the unfamiliar boy inside the den is her brother.

She remembers his kindness, his suffering, his childhood, and their reunion.

Sora sees an unfamiliar person.

He cannot recognize her face.

He cannot reliably remember her name.

He cannot reconstruct the history she is trying to describe.

The emotional burden is therefore uneven.

Mia experiences the loss of someone she remembers.

Sora experiences a world in which the person he lost may not even have a name.

Neither child should be expected to resolve this difference.

### 5.1 What Mia must understand

Mia may want to tell Sora everything.

She may want to show him photographs, describe their grandmother, recount their adventures, and explain why she calls herself his little sister.

These efforts may eventually help.

However, she must understand that he may not recognize the stories, and he may not respond as the brother she remembers.

He may ask the same question repeatedly.

He may forget her name shortly after hearing it.

He may look directly at her and still fail to recognize her.

None of these responses should be treated as deliberate rejection.

### 5.2 What Sora must not be expected to do

Sora must not be pressured to:

- Recognize Mia.
    
- Remember their childhood.
    
- Recall their relationship.
    
- Accept physical affection.
    
- Respond emotionally to stories about his past.
    
- Reassure Mia that he still loves her.
    

He is six years old and has sustained a serious neurological injury.

His recovery must not become a test of whether he can become the brother Mia remembers.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDED RECONNECTION PROTOCOL

C.O.R.E. recommends that Mia's future visits follow a consistent approach.

She should identify herself by name every time.

She should not rely on Sora recognizing her voice or appearance.

She may tell him simple, familiar facts without demanding that he remember them.

For example:

**MIA:** Hi, Sora. It's Mia. I'm your little sister.

**SORA:** ...

**MIA:** You don't have to remember me right now.

**SORA:** Okay.

**MIA:** I just wanted to say hello.

**SORA:** Hello.

**MIA:** Can I sit here?

**SORA:** If you want.

Mia may sit nearby.

She does not need to make him remember.

She does not need to hug him.

She does not need to make the visit meaningful.

For now, being two children sitting in the same room without fear or pressure is enough.

All visits must remain supervised and voluntary. Medical personnel must continue addressing Sora's neurological condition, sleep disruption, nutritional needs, and physical weakness.

---

## 7. CLOSING NOTE — ONE REMEMBERS, ONE DOES NOT

Mia remembers her big brother.

She remembers the little boy who stood outside the doghouse and kept the wolves away.

She remembers the boy who was supposed to be protected, too.

She remembers searching for him.

She remembers finding him.

She remembers hugging him.

She remembers telling him that she was still his little sister, even though they were the same age.

She remembers all of it.

Sora remembers a dark enclosure.

He remembers his name.

Sometimes, he remembers that he is supposed to talk.

And when Mia approaches, he sees a person with a black scribble where a face should be.

She says his name.

He asks who she is.

She tells him she is his sister.

He asks whether he has one.

There is no cruelty in his question.

There is no rejection in his expression.

He simply does not know.

Perhaps the most heartbreaking part is that Mia cannot even show him her face and expect him to recognize it.

She can stand in front of him, hold his gaze, and tell him that she is his little sister.

But the face he once knew is inaccessible to him.

And the memories that once connected them are buried somewhere he cannot reach.

Mia has a brother.

Sora has a name.

For now, those two facts exist separately.

C.O.R.E. cannot promise that Sora will remember Mia again. It cannot promise that his face blindness will resolve.

But Mia should not have to stop loving her brother simply because he cannot recognize her.

And Sora should not have to remember her to deserve her kindness.

**She remembers the boy who protected her. He cannot remember the girl he protected. We must protect them both while they find their way through that difference.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
