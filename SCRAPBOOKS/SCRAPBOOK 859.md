# 1. PURPOSE

This document records a routine food distribution event involving resident **Neri**.

> **ONE (1) SLICE OF CAKE**

No containment procedure was necessary.

The resident's general mood improved considerably.

---

# 2. CAKE DISTRIBUTION

At approximately 16:██, an Archivist entered the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria carrying a freshly prepared slice of cake.

Neri was already seated at the table.

She immediately noticed the cake.

The following exchange was recorded.

> **ARCHIVIST:** Neri.

> **NERI:** What?

> **ARCHIVIST:** Cake.

> **NERI:** ...

> **ARCHIVIST:** It's for you.

Neri stared at the cake for approximately three seconds.

> **NERI:** Really?

> **ARCHIVIST:** Really.

Neri accepted the plate.

> **NERI:** Thank you.

She proceeded to eat the cake.

Her mood improved almost immediately.

No further intervention was required.

Neri displayed the following behaviors:

- Immediate attention to cake.
    
- Noticeably reduced irritability.
    
- Increased willingness to remain in the cafeteria.
    
- Quiet eating.
    
- No complaints regarding sweetness.
    
- Complete consumption of the provided cake.

> **NERI:** Fine.

This response is considered exceptionally cooperative by SAFEHOUSE standards.

# 4. DIETARY NOTE

Neri has an unusually high tolerance for sweet foods.

Cake, candy, pastries, pudding, ice cream, chocolate, sweet drinks, and fruit desserts are all generally well received.

Her Seal Thiren physiology prevents the normal development of dental cavities, meaning her consumption of sweets presents substantially less dental risk than would normally be expected.

This does **not** mean Neri should live exclusively on cake.

Balanced meals remain required.

Neri continues to eat ordinary foods, particularly:

- Fish
    
- Vegetables
    
- Rice
    
- Soups
    
- Other meals prepared by trusted SAFEHOUSE residents
    

Cake is classified as a **comfort food**, not a replacement for nutrition or medical treatment.

---

# 5. PERSONAL PRECAUTIONS

Personnel are reminded that Neri's happiness should not be treated as a controllable resource.

Do not:

- Withhold food to manipulate her behavior.
    
- Use cake as leverage against her.
    
- Deliberately tease her with food.
    
- Attempt to determine how much sugar she can physically consume.
    
- Ask her to share her cake without permission.
    

Positive reinforcement is permitted.

Being nice to a child is generally considered sufficient justification.

# 6. IDENTITY / BIOLOGICAL RECORD

### ADMINISTRATIVE CLARIFICATION

Neri was originally **male prior to the experimental transformation that converted her into a Seal Thiren**.

The transformation resulted in substantial physiological changes, including a change to female physical development.

Neri currently identifies and presents as **female**.

Accordingly:

> **CURRENT REFERENCE:** Neri / she / her

Personnel are not to repeatedly draw attention to her pre-transformation sex.

The fact is retained in medical and historical records because it is relevant to understanding the transformation's physiological effects.

It is **not** a reason to treat Neri differently in everyday life.

# 10. MEDICAL REMINDER

Cake consumption should not be mistaken for treatment of Neri's underlying medical condition.

Neri's altered physiology can cause her blood to gradually become nonfunctional, resulting in spontaneous anemia.

This can occur:

- During physical activity.
    
- During periods of stress.
    
- During rest.
    
- While eating normally.
    
- While sitting in the cafeteria.
    
- While eating cake.
    

Sugar may provide energy when appropriate.

It does **not** replace functional blood.

It does **not** restart a stopped heart.

Personnel should therefore never assume:

> "She's eating cake, so she's fine."

If Neri becomes unusually pale, weak, dizzy, confused, or unconscious, medical procedures take priority over food.
