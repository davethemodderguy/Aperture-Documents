# SAFEHOUSE MEDICAL INCIDENT REPORT

## INCIDENT: THE TWO-HOUR SILENCE

**Classification:** Medical / SAFEHOUSE Internal  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Incident Type:** Anemic Shock / Delayed Discovery  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Cafeteria  
**Estimated Duration:** ~2 hours prior to discovery  
**Primary Concern:** Subject concealed physiological deterioration without conscious intent.

---

## 1. Circumstances

Jane Doe was unavailable.

Not simply busy.

**Too busy.**

Alice, one of Neri's closest friends, was also unavailable after developing a cold and remaining in her room to recover.

This removed two of the people Neri most frequently sought out.

Normally, Neri's day would involve moving between several familiar people and activities:

- **Neko** — chemistry discussions and projects.
    
- **Star** — archery practice.
    
- **Bao** — cupcakes, sweets, and occasional mother-daughter time.
    
- **Guiding Light** — baking, cupcakes, and general companionship.
    
- **Alice** — walking, riding around on her scooter, talking, or simply being together.
    
- **Jane** — work, conversation, teasing, and supervision that Neri increasingly tolerated despite insisting she wasn't being supervised.
    

On this particular day, almost none of that happened.

Neri instead went to the cafeteria.

She sat alone.

The cafeteria remained busy around her.

People talked.

Utensils clattered.

Workers moved between tables.

Someone laughed loudly several seats away.

Neri opened her chemistry book and began reading.

Nothing appeared unusual.

---

# 2. Initial Observation

Neri was behaving exactly as she normally would when reading.

She sat at the table with the book open in front of her.

Her posture was quiet.

Her glasses were in place.

Her hands were near the book.

Her eyes remained open.

There was no obvious distress.

No request for assistance was made.

No emergency was reported.

No one immediately noticed anything wrong.

This was the critical factor.

**Neri did not look like someone who had collapsed.**

She looked like a ten-year-old sitting quietly and reading.

---

# 3. Onset

At an undetermined point approximately two hours before discovery, Neri experienced an anemic episode.

Her blood degradation reached a critical level.

Her cardiovascular system began failing.

Neri lost consciousness.

However, she did not fall dramatically from the chair.

She did not hit the floor.

She did not make enough noise to attract immediate attention.

Her body remained positioned at the table.

Her head rested against her hands.

Her eyes remained open.

Her chemistry book remained open.

From several meters away, there was virtually no visual distinction between:

**Neri reading.**

and

**Neri unconscious.**

That distinction was the problem.

---

# 4. The Two-Hour Period

For approximately two hours, the cafeteria continued operating around her.

People passed by.

Conversations continued.

Meals were served.

Neri remained at the same table.

She did not move.

She did not turn a page.

She did not respond to the environment.

And yet nobody immediately recognized the difference.

The surrounding noise effectively concealed the absence of normal activity.

There was no dramatic collapse for anyone to witness.

There was simply a quiet child sitting unusually still.

---

# 5. Discovery

Approximately two hours after the estimated onset, a cafeteria worker approached Neri's table.

The worker initially assumed she was simply deeply focused on her book.

They nudged her.

Neri's body moved.

The book shifted.

Her posture collapsed.

The worker immediately realized something was wrong.

> **Worker:** “Neri?”

No response.

A second attempt to wake her produced no meaningful reaction.

Medical personnel were contacted immediately.

The situation was escalated.

---

# 6. Medical Assessment

Neri was found to be in severe anemic shock.

Her cardiovascular system was critically compromised.

The most concerning aspect of the incident was not merely the severity of the anemia.

It was the **delay between onset and discovery.**

Neri had been unconscious for an extended period while surrounded by people who had no reason to believe she was experiencing an emergency.

Medical personnel noted that had the worker not nudged her when they did, the outcome could have been fatal.

The incident therefore demonstrates a recurring danger associated with Neri's condition:

> **Her most dangerous episodes may be the ones nobody notices.**

---

# 7. Why Nobody Noticed

Neri has previously demonstrated an unusual tendency to suppress or conceal signs that she is unwell.

This incident was different in one important respect:

**She didn't have to consciously hide anything.**

Her physiology itself made the event difficult to detect.

There was:

- no loud collapse,
    
- no obvious injury,
    
- no call for help,
    
- no dramatic movement,
    
- no visible struggle,
    
- no immediate disruption to the surrounding environment.
    

Her eyes remained open.

Her book remained open.

Her posture remained sufficiently ordinary.

To an observer who did not already know what to look for, she appeared to simply be reading.

---

# 8. Social Context

The absence of Jane and Alice appears significant.

This is not being interpreted as either person's fault.

Jane was genuinely occupied with work.

Alice was sick.

Neither could reasonably have been expected to remain beside Neri indefinitely.

However, both normally provide Neri with frequent social contact.

Without them, Neri spent considerably more time alone.

Rather than seeking another person out, she chose to remain in the cafeteria and occupy herself with chemistry.

This is consistent with a concerning behavioral pattern previously observed in Neri:

**When she believes she should not bother people, she becomes quieter rather than asking for help.**

---

# 9. Post-Incident Concern

The most significant lesson from this incident is not:

> “Someone should have watched Neri more carefully.”

That would be neither practical nor fair.

The lesson is:

> **Neri cannot be expected to reliably recognize herself as an emergency.**

She may not interrupt someone who is working.

She may not seek someone out when she feels strange.

She may not consider her own symptoms important enough to report.

And in the worst cases, she may become unconscious while still appearing completely normal.

This creates a unique safety problem.

The SAFEHOUSE cannot rely exclusively on Neri saying:

**“I don't feel well.”**

Sometimes, she won't.

Sometimes, she can't.

---

# 10. Follow-Up

Medical personnel have recommended that SAFEHOUSE staff continue treating unexplained prolonged stillness around Neri as potentially significant, particularly when:

- she has been sitting alone for an unusually long period,
    
- she has not interacted with anyone for an extended time,
    
- she appears unusually pale,
    
- she has stopped turning pages or performing whatever activity she appeared to be doing,
    
- she does not respond normally to conversation,
    
- or her posture appears static for an abnormal duration.
    

This is **not** a recommendation to constantly monitor Neri.

It is a recommendation to recognize that her condition can produce an emergency without producing an obvious emergency scene.

---

## 11. Jane Doe — Later Statement

When Jane was informed, she reportedly remained silent for several seconds.

Her first question was:

> “How long?”

When told that Neri had been down for approximately two hours before someone discovered her, Jane's response was reportedly:

> “Two hours?”

She then asked whether Neri had been alone.

The answer was yes.

Jane reportedly looked at the cafeteria table for several seconds before responding:

> “She was waiting for someone.”

No further comment was recorded.

---

## 12. Final Assessment

Neri's friends being unavailable did not cause the medical episode.

Her anemia did.

But the circumstances demonstrated something the SAFEHOUSE had not fully accounted for:

**Neri can disappear in plain sight without physically going anywhere.**

She can sit in a crowded cafeteria, surrounded by dozens of people, and still effectively be alone.

And if her eyes are open, her book is in front of her, and nobody asks whether she is actually awake—

the difference may not be visible until it is too late.
