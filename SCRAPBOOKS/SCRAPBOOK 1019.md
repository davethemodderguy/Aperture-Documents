## BEHAVIORAL ADDENDUM

### THE “LIKE POLES” EFFECT

Further observation has identified a consistent response pattern in Neri's current behavior.

Attempts to become closer to Neri are presently producing the opposite result.

The relationship can be represented as:

> **Attempted closeness ↑ → Neri's distance ↑**

The effect is comparable to placing two identical poles of a magnet together.

The closer one is pushed toward the other, the stronger the repulsive force becomes.

This analogy is considered unusually accurate.

Neri is not necessarily rejecting the **person**.

She is rejecting the **approach.**

## WHY THIS OCCURS

Neri's current reasoning appears to be:

> **Someone approaches me → they want something → I am becoming a responsibility → I need to remove myself.**

The actual intention of the approaching resident is irrelevant.

Even genuine affection can trigger the response.

A hug can be interpreted as:

> _“They're checking on me because I'm a problem.”_

An invitation can become:

> _“They're making room for me when they shouldn't have to.”_

Following her can become:

> _“They won't let me handle myself.”_

Repeated reassurance can become:

> _“They are trying very hard to convince themselves that keeping me is worth it.”_

Thus, conventional attempts at emotional reconnection can unintentionally strengthen the exact belief they are intended to correct.

---

## IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Neri does **not** appear to want everyone to disappear.

She is capable of remaining in a room while other residents are present.

She may eat in the cafeteria while others are there.

She may read while Alice talks nearby.

She may remain in the same building as Jane.

She may accept Bao's medical assistance.

What she currently struggles with is **being the target of attention.**

There is a significant difference between:

> **“I want to be alone.”**

and

> **“Please don't come closer.”**

Neri currently exhibits both.

---

## RECOMMENDED RESPONSE

C.O.R.E. therefore recommends a counterintuitive approach.

### Do not chase Neri.

If Neri walks away:

**Let her walk away.**

If she moves to another seat:

**Do not follow her to the new seat.**

If she leaves a conversation:

**Do not demand that she finish it.**

If she refuses an invitation:

**Accept the refusal without making it emotionally significant.**

If she remains alone:

**Allow her to remain alone.**

The resident should remain available without actively closing the distance.

---

## THE “OPEN DOOR” APPROACH

The recommended behavioral model is:

> **Do not pull Neri toward you.**
> 
> **Leave the door open for Neri to come toward you.**

For example:

Instead of:

> “Neri, come sit with us.”

Use:

> “We're eating here if you want to join.”

Instead of:

> “Why are you leaving?”

Use:

> “Okay. I'll see you later.”

Instead of:

> “You need to talk to me.”

Use:

> “You don't have to talk. I'm here.”

Instead of following her down the hallway:

**Stop.**

Let Neri discover that walking away does not cause abandonment.

## C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Neri currently behaves like a **socially repulsive field**.

The more emotional pressure applied from outside, the stronger the withdrawal response.

This does **not** mean residents should stop caring.

It means they must change how that care is expressed.

Neri does not currently need:

> **More pursuit.**

She needs:

> **More room.**

But that room must not become abandonment.

The desired equilibrium is therefore:

> **Distance without disappearance.**

> **Availability without pursuit.**

> **Care without pressure.**

> **Companionship without requiring participation.**

Neri may sit alone in the cafeteria for an hour.

That is fine.

Neri may walk away from Jane, Bao, Frost, or Alice.

The goal is not to **catch Neri.**

The goal is to make sure that, when she eventually decides she wants someone beside her again, she knows exactly where to find them.

**They do not need to chase her.**

**They only need to remain there.**
