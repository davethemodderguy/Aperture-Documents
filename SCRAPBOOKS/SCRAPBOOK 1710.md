# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The previous assessment of the teddy bear impact incident has been revised following continued medical observation.

The incident was **near-fatal to Jake.**

Clover's accidental throw caused the teddy bear to strike the wall with approximately the force of a baseball impact.

Because Jake's sensory connection with the bear is effectively bidirectional with respect to physical sensation, the impact was transmitted directly to Jake's body.

The resulting event produced:

- severe systemic pain;
    
- extreme chest pain;
    
- acute cardiac distress;
    
- repeated coughing;
    
- significant respiratory difficulty;
    
- impaired speech;
    
- severe weakness;
    
- inability to perform ordinary physical tasks;
    
- and a period during which Jake's condition approached a life-threatening threshold.
    

Jake survived.

However, this was not a minor injury.

It was a **near-fatal anomalous medical event.**

---

# 1. SEVERITY

The initial assumption that the bear's relatively undamaged physical condition indicated limited injury to Jake was incorrect.

The sensory connection does not appear to operate according to conventional injury equivalence.

A relatively ordinary physical impact against the bear can produce disproportionately severe physiological consequences for Jake.

In this incident, the impact was sufficient to cause significant disruption to Jake's physical stability.

The medical team has therefore reclassified the event from:

> **Severe sensory feedback**

to:

> **Near-fatal sensory trauma.**

---

# 2. ACUTE CONDITION

Immediately following the impact, Jake experienced severe pain throughout his body.

The most pronounced symptoms were concentrated around his chest.

Jake repeatedly attempted to breathe.

His breathing became labored.

He coughed repeatedly.

He attempted to speak but could barely form words.

Observed communication included:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...hurts..."

Followed by coughing.

Later:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Chest..."

He attempted to continue.

He could not.

---

# 3. CARDIAC CONCERN

Jake's teddy bear functions as his cardiac vessel.

Consequently, the relationship between the bear and Jake's physical heart is already anomalous.

The transmitted impact produced significant cardiac distress.

Medical personnel were particularly concerned because the pain was not merely superficial.

Jake repeatedly held his chest and appeared unable to maintain normal breathing.

For a period, personnel could not safely assume that Jake would remain stable without intervention.

---

# 4. RESPIRATORY DISTRESS

Jake's ability to breathe was substantially impaired.

He remained conscious but struggled to draw comfortable breaths.

Repeated coughing further exhausted him.

Attempting to speak made the problem worse.

Medical personnel therefore instructed everyone nearby:

> **Do not make Jake talk.**

Questions were minimized.

Jake was allowed to communicate through nods, gestures, and brief words whenever necessary.

---

# 5. CLOVER'S REALIZATION

Clover initially believed Jake had simply been hurt.

She did not immediately understand how serious the situation was.

That changed when she saw the medical response.

The equipment.

The urgency.

Jake's inability to speak.

The way he struggled simply to breathe.

Clover became visibly pale.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...He's dying?"

No one gave her a reassuring answer.

The medical team was still working.

Clover stared at Jake.

She looked at the teddy bear.

Then at the wall.

She understood.

Her throw had caused this.

---

Clover's psychological response escalated rapidly.

Her thoughts became increasingly repetitive.

> **CLOVER:**

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I threw him."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I knew the bear was connected."

> **CLOVER:**

Personnel repeatedly reminded her that the throw was accidental and that she had not understood the severity of the sensory connection.

Clover acknowledged this intellectually.

She did not appear capable of accepting it emotionally.

# 8. JAKE

Jake remained conscious through the worst portion of the incident.

He was frightened.

She stopped several steps away.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Jake..."

Jake looked toward her.

Clover began crying.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'm sorry."

Jake attempted to respond.

His voice was extremely weak.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...not..."

He coughed.

Clover moved closer.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...your..."

> **CLOVER:**

---

# 9. MEDICAL STABILIZATION

Jake eventually stabilized.

The immediate life-threatening symptoms subsided.

His breathing became less erratic.

His cardiac condition improved.

However, significant pain remained.

The recovery period is expected to be considerably longer than originally anticipated.

Jake currently requires assistance with many ordinary activities.

Standing.

Walking.

Picking up objects.

Carrying his teddy bear.

Eating comfortably.

Changing position.

Even sitting upright for extended periods can become painful.

---

# 10. LONG-TERM CONCERN

The incident has demonstrated that Jake's teddy bear is not merely a comfort object.

It is functionally connected to his body.

Any sufficiently severe physical event affecting the bear may potentially become a severe medical event for Jake.

This creates an unusual vulnerability.

Jake's physical durability cannot be evaluated independently from the bear.

The bear must therefore be considered part of Jake's medical condition.

---

# 11. UPDATED PROTOCOL

The following rules are now mandatory:

### Rule 01

The teddy bear is not to be thrown.

### Rule 02

The teddy bear is not to be used during physical games involving significant force.

### Rule 03

The teddy bear is not to be deliberately exposed to dangerous environments.

### Rule 04

Personnel must not perform impact testing.

### Rule 05

If the bear is damaged, dropped from significant height, struck, or otherwise subjected to unusual force, Jake must be checked immediately.

### Rule 06

If Jake experiences sudden chest pain, coughing, respiratory difficulty, or collapse following an incident involving the bear, treat it as a medical emergency.

---

# 12. CLOVER'S ROLE

C.O.R.E. does not recommend removing Clover from Jake's life.

She remains someone Jake trusts.

She remains his primary day-to-day caretaker.

She remains welcome in his room.

However, the incident has demonstrated that Clover herself needs support.

Her guilt is not merely ordinary regret.

It is interacting with a much older psychological wound.

She survived her father's death.

She was blamed for surviving.

Now she nearly watched Jake die after accidentally hurting him.

Those experiences are dangerously easy for her mind to connect.

C.O.R.E. Medical recommends psychological support.

Not punishment.

Not isolation.

Support.
