# Executive Summary

Neri no longer smiles.

This is not currently assessed as a temporary facial-expression change.

Following her recent sequence of medical, psychological, and social events, Neri has largely stopped displaying spontaneous smiles.

Personnel are reminded that this does **not** create an obligation to make her smile.

In fact, repeated attempts to do so have produced the opposite result.

Neri becomes visibly annoyed when people:

- Tell jokes specifically to make her laugh.
    
- Make exaggerated faces at her.
    
- Tease her about smiling.
    
- Tell her to "cheer up."
    
- Ask why she isn't smiling.
    
- Attempt surprise gestures solely to get a reaction.
    
- Say things such as "There, that's better!" after successfully provoking even a tiny expression.
    
- Treat a neutral expression as a problem requiring correction.
    

**C.O.R.E. recommendation: Stop.**

Neri does not need to be made cheerful.

---

# I. Current Presentation

Neri's current baseline expression is generally neutral.

She may appear:

- Tired.
    
- Unimpressed.
    
- Quiet.
    
- Irritated.
    
- Emotionally distant.
    
- Focused on whatever she is doing.
    

These expressions should not automatically be interpreted as hostility.

Likewise, the absence of a smile should not automatically be interpreted as an emergency.

Neri is still capable of enjoying things.

She may enjoy:

- Cake.
    
- Coffee.
    
- Reading.
    
- Drawing.
    
- Spending time near people she trusts.
    
- Her room.
    
- Quiet company.
    

She simply does not necessarily express enjoyment through smiling.

---

# II. The "Make Neri Smile" Problem

Several residents initially interpreted Neri's lack of smiling as something that needed to be fixed.

This resulted in attempts to deliberately produce a positive reaction.

These attempts were unsuccessful.

One particularly notable interaction involved a resident attempting increasingly elaborate jokes.

Neri stared at them.

The resident continued.

Neri stared harder.

The resident eventually asked:

> "Can you at least smile?"

Neri shook her head.

The resident attempted another joke.

Neri picked up her book and left.

No smile was produced.

---

# III. Jane Doe's Attempt

Jane initially approached the situation differently.

She attempted to make Neri laugh by deliberately making a ridiculous expression.

Neri looked at her.

No reaction.

Jane tried again.

Neri narrowed her eyes.

Jane stopped.

**JANE:**  
"...You're annoyed."

Neri nodded.

**JANE:**  
"Okay."

Neri continued reading.

Jane sat down beside her anyway.

No attempt was made to make her smile again.

This was considered a significantly more successful interaction.

---

# IV. Alice's Attempt

Alice also noticed the change.

She attempted to cheer Neri up.

Neri became increasingly irritated.

Alice eventually asked:

> "Do you want me to stop?"

Neri nodded.

Alice stopped.

No argument followed.

No further attempt was made.

C.O.R.E. considers this the correct response.

---

# V. Important Distinction

Neri's lack of smiling should not be confused with inability to experience positive emotion.

The distinction is:

> **Neri can enjoy something without demonstrating that enjoyment through a smile.**

She can drink coffee and enjoy it.

She can eat cake and enjoy it.

She can sit beside someone she trusts and appreciate their presence.

She can receive something meaningful and simply nod.

She can be happy that someone stayed without visibly showing happiness.

Personnel should not demand an outward emotional performance as proof that an interaction was successful.

---

# VI. Communication Complication

Neri's inability to speak makes this distinction particularly important.

Unlike a speaking resident, Neri cannot easily say:

> "I'm enjoying this, I just don't feel like smiling."

She may simply stare.

She may nod.

She may continue whatever she was doing.

She may gesture for someone to stop.

She may write something if the situation becomes sufficiently important.

Often, she will not.

Therefore:

**Neutral expression + silence ≠ unhappiness.**

Likewise:

**Neutral expression + silence ≠ permission to keep trying.**

---

# VII. Why Forced Cheerfulness Is Counterproductive

Attempts to make Neri smile can unintentionally communicate several things she is already sensitive to:

> "You are behaving incorrectly."

> "Your current emotional state is unacceptable."

> "We need you to look happier."

> "Your sadness makes us uncomfortable."

> "You should perform happiness for us."

This is particularly counterproductive given Neri's existing tendency to suppress herself in order to avoid becoming a burden.

She does not need another behavior to perform correctly.

---

# VIII. Recommended Interaction

If Neri is sitting quietly:

**Leave her alone if she appears to want to be alone.**

If she is sitting near people:

**Allow her to remain there without demanding participation.**

If she looks irritated:

**Stop whatever caused the irritation.**

If she accepts companionship:

**Stay.**

If she drinks coffee:

**Let her drink her coffee.**

If she eats cake:

**Let her eat her cake.**

If she does not smile:

**Do nothing.**

The last recommendation is intentional.

---

# IX. SAFEHOUSE Notice

The following statement has been circulated to permanent SAFEHOUSE residents:

> **Neri does not need to smile for you.**
> 
> Do not make her smile.
> 
> Do not ask her to smile.
> 
> Do not tell her she looks happier when she briefly does.
> 
> Do not treat her neutral expression as something that needs fixing.
>

> 
> If she does not, leave it alone.

A handwritten addition was later found beneath the notice:

C.O.R.E. agrees with the assessment.

# XI. Final Assessment

Neri's smile is not a welfare metric.

Her lack of one should not become another problem that SAFEHOUSE personnel attempt to solve.

She does not need jokes.

She does not need forced positivity.

She does not need someone standing in front of her making increasingly ridiculous faces until she reacts.

She needs people who can sit beside her without demanding an emotional response.

The appropriate goal is not:

> **"Make Neri smile."**

It is:

> **"Let Neri exist without having to perform."**
