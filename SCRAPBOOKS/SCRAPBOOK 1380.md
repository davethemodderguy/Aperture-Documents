# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### RESIDENT WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-WEL-4C-PHOENIX-241  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — RESIDENT WELFARE / PSYCHOLOGICAL  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Phoenix Raffedella  
**SECONDARY SUBJECT:** Jake  
**EVENT:** Private nighttime reaction following cardiac-core incident

---

# 1. OBSERVATION

At approximately **03:17**, Phoenix was observed alone in the SAFEHOUSE common room.

No other residents were present.

The lights were dim.

Phoenix had been unable to sleep.

She was sitting on the sofa with a blanket around her shoulders, staring at nothing.

Her medical kit was beside her.

Her phone was face-down.

She was not working.

She was not reading.

She was simply sitting there.

---

# 2. THE MEMORY

Phoenix remembered Jake.

Not Jake as he is now.

Jake as he had been that day.

Weak.

Barely responsive.

His glow fading.

His breathing becoming shallow.

His teddy bear missing.

Phoenix remembered holding him.

She remembered checking his pulse repeatedly.

She remembered how light he had felt in her arms.

She remembered thinking:

> **“He's dying.”**

Then:

> **“I can't stop it.”**

Then:

> **“Please.”**

The memory remained extraordinarily vivid.

Phoenix began crying.

Quietly.

She covered her mouth so nobody would hear.

---

# 3. PHOENIX'S REASONING

Phoenix knew Jake would notice if he saw her.

And Phoenix knew Jake.

If he saw her crying because of something that happened to him, he would almost certainly assume he had done something wrong.

He would ask:

> “Did I hurt you?”

Or:

> “Are you scared of me?”

Or:

> “Is it because of my powers?”

Or, worse:

> “Did I do something bad?”

Phoenix did not want that.

The incident was not Jake's fault.

He had not intentionally frightened her.

He had not chosen to lose his teddy bear.

He had not chosen to become critically ill.

He had simply been dying.

And Phoenix had been there.

---

# 4. DECISION

Phoenix decided not to tell him.

Not tonight.

Possibly not ever.

She understood that keeping something to herself was not always healthy.

But this particular secret was not about hiding danger.

It was about protecting a child from blame he did not deserve.

Phoenix whispered:

> **“You didn't do anything wrong.”**

She wiped her eyes.

> **“You don't need to know.”**

---

# 5. THE CONTRADICTION

Phoenix was deeply traumatized by what happened.

But she was not afraid **of Jake.**

This distinction is important.

She was afraid of remembering him dying.

She was afraid of being unable to help.

She was afraid of seeing his heartbeat disappear.

She was afraid of holding someone she cared about while there was nothing she could do.

Jake himself was not the threat.

The memory was.

---

# 6. PRIVATE BREAKDOWN

At approximately **03:31**, Phoenix's composure deteriorated further.

She pulled the blanket tighter around herself.

Her breathing became uneven.

She whispered:

> **“Five hours.”**

Pause.

> **“Five hours without his heart.”**

Her hands began shaking.

> **“I thought I was holding a dying child.”**

She stopped.

Silence.

Then:

> **“I thought he was going to die in my arms.”**

Phoenix lowered her head.

She cried for several minutes.

Nobody was called.

No emergency response was required.

---

# 7. WHY SHE HID IT

Phoenix has a longstanding tendency to minimize her own needs when she believes addressing them will inconvenience someone else.

This is particularly pronounced at night.

She already believes:

> **“I don't want anyone's time wasted on me.”**

In Jake's case, another concern was added:

> **“I don't want him thinking this is his fault.”**

Phoenix knows Jake has a tendency to overthink.

She has seen how quickly he can turn an accident into a question about whether he is dangerous, unwanted, or responsible for hurting someone.

She therefore made a deliberate decision:

**Jake does not need to carry this.**

---

# 8. FINAL OBSERVATION

At approximately **04:02**, Phoenix wiped her face.

She checked her reflection.

Her eyes were red.

She waited until her breathing stabilized.

Then she stood.

She picked up her medical kit.

Before leaving the room, she looked toward the hallway leading to Jake's room.

She quietly said:

> **“Goodnight, kid.”**

There was no response.

Jake was asleep.

Phoenix remained there for several seconds.

Then she walked away.

---

# C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Phoenix is experiencing a significant unresolved emotional response to Jake's cardiac-core emergency.

The response is genuine and severe.

However, Phoenix's decision to conceal it from Jake appears motivated by **protectiveness**, rather than avoidance of Jake himself.

She does not want Jake to believe:

- he frightened her intentionally;
    
- his existence is dangerous;
    
- his powers make him unacceptable;
    
- she regrets caring for him;
    
- or he is responsible for her distress.
    

Those conclusions would be inaccurate.

The trauma belongs to Phoenix.

The blame does not belong to Jake.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Phoenix knows what death feels like.

She has experienced it herself.

Again.

And again.

And again.

But Jake was different.

She wasn't the one dying.

She was the one holding him.

And for five hours, she thought she was going to lose him.

Now she sits alone at night because the memory has returned.

She cries where nobody can see.

Not because she hates Jake.

Not because she fears him.

Because she loves the kid enough that watching him die terrified her.

And she knows that if Jake found out she was crying because of him, he would probably spend the next week wondering what he had done wrong.

So Phoenix keeps the memory to herself.

She carries it quietly.

Because, in her mind, one person being hurt by that memory is enough.

Jake doesn't need to be hurt by it too.
