# SAFEHOUSE INCIDENT REPORT

**Incident Classification:** Resident Welfare / Medical Emergency  
**Resident:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Species:** Seal Thiren  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Exterior Perimeter  
**Duration Unaccounted For:** Approximately 5+ hours  
**Primary Responder:** Bao  
**Medical Cause of Collapse:** Acute anemia resulting from spontaneous blood degradation  
**Current Status:** Stable  
**Psychological Impact:** Confirmed

---

## 1. Summary

Neri was inadvertently locked outside the SAFEHOUSE during a period of sustained rainfall.

For approximately five hours, Neri remained outside without being noticed as missing.

No hostile activity, deliberate abandonment, or evidence of an attempted abduction was identified.

The incident instead resulted from a catastrophic failure of ordinary resident accountability.

Neri eventually collapsed from anemia.

Importantly, the anemia was **not caused by the rain, cold, hunger, physical exertion, or any external injury.** Neri's blood degradation process occurred spontaneously, as documented in previous medical examinations.

Bao eventually realized that Neri had not been seen for an abnormal length of time, located her outside, and immediately intervened.

Neri survived.

---

# 2. Timeline

### 00:00 — Neri exits SAFEHOUSE

Neri leaves the main building.

Reason for leaving is currently considered mundane and non-suspicious.

No concern is raised at the time.

### ~00:15 — Rain begins

Rain begins across the SAFEHOUSE exterior.

Neri remains outside.

There is no indication that she attempted to force entry.

### ~01:00–04:30 — Resident remains unaccounted for

Neri receives no welfare check.

No resident immediately identifies her absence.

Security systems record no emergency event.

The incident therefore remains unnoticed.

### ~05:00+ — Bao notices Neri is missing

Bao realizes that she has not seen Neri for several hours.

She begins searching the SAFEHOUSE.

Upon discovering that Neri is not inside, the search expands to the exterior.

### Shortly afterward — Neri located

Neri is discovered outside in the rain.

She is already in a severely weakened condition.

Her clothing is soaked through.

She is conscious but minimally responsive.

### Medical emergency

Neri's blood volume/function is found to have fallen below the threshold necessary to sustain normal cardiovascular operation.

She loses consciousness.

Bao immediately provides compatible blood.

Her cardiovascular system is successfully restarted/stabilized.

Neri survives.

---

# 3. Medical Findings

The medical team confirmed that the event was consistent with Neri's established condition.

There was:

- No major external injury.
    
- No evidence of physical attack.
    
- No evidence that Neri deliberately harmed herself.
    
- No evidence that the rain directly caused the anemia.
    
- No indication that Neri had undertaken strenuous activity beforehand.
    

Her blood had simply continued its abnormal degradation process.

The incident therefore demonstrates once again that:

> **Neri does not need to do anything wrong for her condition to become life-threatening.**

She can be sitting quietly, eating cake, walking around the SAFEHOUSE, or standing outside in the rain.

The process can occur regardless.

Bao's intervention prevented a fatal outcome.

---

# 4. Psychological Impact

The physical consequences were successfully treated.

The psychological consequences were considerably less straightforward.

Neri's established psychological profile contains a severe fixation surrounding **being remembered, being protected, and whether anyone will notice when she disappears.**

Her childhood repeatedly taught her the opposite.

People disappeared.

People stopped caring.

People who were supposed to help did not come.

Her younger sister died in front of her while Neri was unable to intervene.

Friends subsequently disappeared from her life as well.

One of Neri's most important SAFEHOUSE experiences had been learning that:

> **Someone will come.**

The rain incident did not completely erase that belief.

Bao came.

Bao found her.

Bao saved her.

But Neri's interpretation of the event appears to have become:

> **“It took five hours.”**

That distinction matters.

---

# 5. Post-Incident Behavior

After stabilization, Neri became noticeably quieter.

She did not immediately accuse anyone.

She did not become violent.

She did not demand an explanation.

Instead, she repeatedly asked variations of the same question:

> “Did anyone notice?”

When informed that Bao eventually realized she was missing, Neri remained silent.

After several seconds, she asked:

> “How long?”

She was told.

Approximately five hours.

Neri reportedly stared at the floor.

Her response:

> “Oh.”

No further statement was initially made.

Later, while sitting beside Bao, she quietly said:

> “I thought somebody would remember.”

Bao reportedly responded:

> “I did remember.”

Neri:

> “After five hours.”

Bao:

> “Yes.”

Neri did not argue.

She simply leaned against Bao.

---

# 6. Assessment

Neri's concept of being remembered has been **damaged, but not destroyed.**

This distinction is considered extremely important.

The incident provides contradictory evidence to her existing beliefs.

### Evidence supporting her fear:

**Nobody noticed for five hours.**

### Evidence contradicting her fear:

**Bao eventually noticed.**

**Bao searched for her.**

**Bao found her.**

**Bao saved her.**

Neri is therefore not returning completely to her previous belief that nobody will ever come.

Instead, a more dangerous intermediate belief may be forming:

> **“Someone might come. Eventually.”**

Psychological staff consider this significantly less healthy than the belief SAFEHOUSE had been attempting to establish.

The objective of resident care should therefore not be to convince Neri that she was “never forgotten.”

That would be factually dishonest.

The appropriate message is:

> **“You were missed later than you should have been. That was our failure. It was not because you were unimportant.”**

---

# 7. Corrective Measures

Effective immediately:

1. Neri is included in SAFEHOUSE resident accountability checks.
    
2. Exterior departures by juvenile residents must be logged during severe weather.
    
3. Rain/weather alerts automatically trigger exterior resident verification.
    
4. Staff are prohibited from assuming that a quiet resident is necessarily safe.
    
5. Medical staff are reminded that Neri's anemia may occur without warning.
    
6. Bao is **not** to be made solely responsible for monitoring Neri.
    
7. Neri is not to be told that the incident occurred because she was “forgotten.”
    
8. Staff must not make false promises such as _“We'll never let that happen again.”_
    
9. Staff should instead demonstrate, consistently, that Neri's presence matters.
    
10. Neri must not be treated as fragile property. She remains a resident.
    

---

# 8. Final Note

The most significant failure of this incident was not that Neri became anemic.

That was already known to be possible.

The failure was that a ten-year-old resident could remain outside for more than five hours without anyone realizing she was missing.

Bao saved her.

That fact must not be diminished.

But neither should the failure preceding it.

Neri does not need to be told that she was wrong for feeling hurt.

She needs to see, repeatedly and consistently, that being remembered is not something she has to earn.

**She is not an expendable asset.**

**She is not a forgotten experiment.**

**She is Neri.**

And someone should notice when she is gone.
