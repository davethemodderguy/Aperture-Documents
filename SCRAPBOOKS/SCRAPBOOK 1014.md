# Executive Summary

Previous observations incorrectly categorized several foods Neri has consumed as established personal preferences.

This assessment has been corrected.

**Neri does not have a particular favorite food.**

Her current eating pattern is considerably simpler:

> **She eats whatever the cafeteria has listed as "Today's Special."**

This includes sushi, cooked meals, fish, vegetables, meat, rice dishes, pasta, soups, desserts, and other meals depending on the day's menu.

Her consumption of raw-fish sushi therefore does **not** indicate a particular preference for sushi.

She simply ate it because it was that day's special.

---

# I. Normal Meal Pattern

Neri generally enters the cafeteria, checks what is available, and selects the day's designated special.

There is little evidence of prolonged decision-making.

A typical interaction is approximately:

**Neri:** _looks at Today's Special._

**Neri:** _takes Today's Special._

**Neri:** _sits down._

**Neri:** _eats._

No ranking system has been observed.

No consistent food is selected over another.

No evidence suggests that Neri is secretly waiting for a particular meal to appear.

---

# II. The Sushi Misinterpretation

The previous report identified Neri's consumption of raw-fish sushi as a newly established preference.

This was incorrect.

The sushi had been the cafeteria's **Today's Special**.

Neri ate it because that was what was being served.

When asked whether she liked it, she nodded.

This should be interpreted as:

> **"Yes, this is fine."**

Not:

> **"Sushi is my favorite food."**

Neri has demonstrated the same response to numerous other cafeteria specials.

---

# III. Examples of Today's Special

Observed meals consumed by Neri include:

- Raw-fish sushi
    
- Cooked fish
    
- Rice dishes
    
- Meat dishes
    
- Vegetable dishes
    
- Pasta
    
- Soup
    
- Sandwiches
    
- Other rotating cafeteria meals
    

No statistically meaningful favorite has been identified.

Neri appears to have a remarkably uncomplicated standard:

> **If the cafeteria prepared it for today, she will probably eat it.**

---

# IV. Sweets Are Not an Exception

Neri's long-standing enjoyment of cake and other sweets remains documented.

However, even this should not be interpreted as a strict requirement that every meal contain dessert.

Cake has particular emotional significance because of her younger sister and later associations with Bao and Guiding Light.

Neri genuinely enjoys sweets.

That does not mean she refuses ordinary food.

If today's special is vegetables and rice, she eats vegetables and rice.

If today's special is sushi, she eats sushi.

If today's special is something she has never encountered before, she will generally try it.

---

# V. Coffee Is Separate

Neri's coffee consumption remains an independent beverage habit.

It should not be interpreted as evidence that she has developed a complicated set of dietary preferences.

Her current cafeteria routine can therefore be summarized as:

**Today's Special + Coffee.**

Anything else is incidental.

---

# VI. Interaction With Jane Doe

Jane eventually asked Neri directly:

> **JANE:**  
> "Okay, so what's your favorite food?"

Neri stared at her.

Jane waited.

Neri shook her head.

Jane tried again.

> **JANE:**  
> "You don't have one?"

Neri nodded.

Jane looked genuinely confused.

> **JANE:**  
> "So what do you eat?"

Neri pointed toward the cafeteria's menu board.

The board read:

> **TODAY'S SPECIAL**

Jane looked at it.

Then at Neri.

> **JANE:**  
> "...You just eat whatever they put there?"

Neri nodded.

Jane paused.

> **JANE:**  
> "That's actually incredibly easy."

Neri returned to eating.

---

# VII. Behavioral Interpretation

This eating pattern is consistent with Neri's broader tendency toward simplicity.

She does not appear particularly interested in making food decisions.

There is no observed:

- "I want this instead."
    
- "Can we make something else?"
    
- "I don't like today's meal."
    
- "I was hoping for something different."
    

She simply checks the menu.

If food is available, she eats.

This also means personnel should not overinterpret individual meals.

Seeing Neri eat sushi does not establish a sushi preference.

Seeing Neri eat cake does not establish cake as her exclusive favorite.

Seeing Neri eat vegetables does not establish a vegetable preference.

The most reliable conclusion is:

> **Neri eats what is available.**

---

# VIII. Welfare Consideration

This behavior is generally convenient for meal planning.

However, personnel should avoid allowing convenience to become neglect.

Neri's willingness to eat whatever is provided should not be interpreted as:

> "She doesn't care what she gets."

She still requires appropriate nutrition and medical consideration because of her existing physiology.

The cafeteria remains responsible for providing suitable meals.

Neri simply does not appear interested in demanding a custom menu.
