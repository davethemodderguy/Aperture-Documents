# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake is considerably easier to care for when he is in his cat form.

This finding has been independently confirmed by several SAFEHOUSE residents.

In human form, Jake requires considerable reassurance, especially following his recent assault and repeated experiences of being left alone. He may cling to a trusted person's sleeve, ask when they are returning, become distressed when someone approaches a doorway, or remain visibly watchful even when a familiar caregiver is present.

In cat form, many of these behaviors become easier to manage.

Jake can curl up on someone's lap.

He can sleep in a warm blanket.

He can follow a trusted resident from room to room.

He can settle against someone's side without needing to explain what he wants.

If he becomes tired, he can simply curl up and rest.

If he wants company, he can sit beside someone.

If he wants attention, he can nudge a hand, climb onto a lap, or quietly place himself in the middle of whatever that person is doing.

He does not need to formulate a sentence to ask for comfort.

He does not need to explain why he is frightened.

And, perhaps most importantly, he can accept companionship without feeling that he has to participate in a conversation.

However, C.O.R.E. emphasizes the following distinction:

**Jake is easier to tend to in cat form. This does not mean his underlying distress has disappeared.**

The cat form makes his needs easier to accommodate.

It does not make those needs less important.

---

# 1. OBSERVED DIFFERENCES

## Human Form

Jake's current human-form behavior is characterized by heightened awareness of separation.

He monitors the movements of familiar people.

He notices when someone checks the time.

He notices when a phone receives a work notification.

He notices when someone stands up.

He may ask where they are going, whether they will return, and who will stay with him.

Even when reassured, he may continue watching the door.

Physical contact can help, but it does not necessarily eliminate his fear.

He may hold Aries's sleeve with both hands, clutch his teddy bear, or lean against Bao while keeping his eyes on the room's entrance.

He is capable of explaining some of his fears.

Unfortunately, being able to express them does not make them easier for him to bear.

## Cat Form

In cat form, Jake's behavior is more physically direct.

He approaches a trusted person.

He settles down.

He stays close.

He sleeps when he is tired.

He seeks warmth when he is cold.

He can communicate discomfort through ordinary feline behavior, although caregivers must remain familiar with his individual signals rather than assuming every behavior is self-explanatory.

His attachment needs are still present.

They are simply expressed through proximity instead of repeated questions.

A cat curled against someone's chest does not have to ask whether that person intends to leave.

A cat sleeping on someone's lap does not have to explain why the empty chair beside it feels frightening.

And a cat tucked into the crook of someone's arm can receive comfort without being expected to discuss the circumstances that made comfort necessary.

For Jake, this appears to reduce the immediate effort required to accept care.

# 2. THE ARIES EXCEPTION

Aries has discovered that caring for Jake in cat form is considerably easier in several practical respects.

Human-form Jake may grip his sleeve and ask:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Where are you going?"

> **ARIES:**  
> "Engineering."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Why?"

> **ARIES:**  
> "Work."

> **JAKE:**  
> "How long?"

> **ARIES:**  
> "I'm not sure."

At this point, Jake may become distressed because the answer does not provide the certainty he needs.

Cat-form Jake, by contrast, may simply climb into Aries's lap and refuse to move.

This is not necessarily less emotionally significant.

It is merely less complicated to accommodate.

Aries can sit down, let Jake settle against him, and stroke his fur while reviewing his work notifications.

Jake can remain close without either of them needing to speak.

There is, however, one recurring difficulty.

Jake has learned that Aries sometimes has to leave.

And he has not yet learned to trust that every departure will be followed by a return.

Consequently, cat-form Jake may react to an impending departure by:

- climbing onto Aries's shoulder;
    
- hooking his paws into Aries's clothing;
    
- curling tightly around his arm;
    
- hiding inside his jacket;
    
- or attempting to follow him through the door.
    

Aries has occasionally looked down at the small animal attached to him and realized that he is not, in fact, holding his work documents.

He is holding his little brother.

Again.

### Recorded Exchange

Aries was preparing to leave for Engineering.

Jake, in cat form, was asleep across his lap.

**ARIES:**  
"Jake."

One ear moved.

**ARIES:**  
"I need to get up."

No response.

Aries carefully shifted one leg.

Jake stretched, turned around, and went back to sleep.

Aries waited.

Five minutes passed.

**ARIES:**  
"Little star."

Jake opened one eye.

**ARIES:**  
"You're going to have to move."

Jake closed his eye again.

Aries stared at him.

**ARIES:**  
"You understand me perfectly well."

Jake's tail moved once.

**ARIES:**  
"That isn't an answer."

Jake tucked his nose beneath his tail.

Aries looked toward the ceiling.

**ARIES:**  
"I am being held hostage by a cat."

From the doorway, Clover replied:

**CLOVER:**  
"That's your brother."

**ARIES:**  
"I'm aware."

Clover considered the situation.

**CLOVER:**  
"He looks comfortable."

**ARIES:**  
"He's on my legs."

**CLOVER:**  
"Very comfortable, then."

Aries eventually contacted the incoming caregiver and arranged a handover before carefully moving Jake.

Jake woke immediately.

He looked at Aries.

Then at the person who had arrived to stay with him.

After a brief pause, he climbed onto the new caregiver's lap.

Aries was permitted to stand.

**ARIES:**  
"Traitor."

Jake began washing one paw.

C.O.R.E. notes that Jake's behavior was not interpreted as a deliberate attempt to obstruct work. His attachment-seeking behavior appears more consistent with a desire to remain physically close to someone he trusts.

The fact that he can express that desire by becoming an exceptionally inconvenient cat does not alter the welfare assessment.

# 3. BAO'S OBSERVATION — "HE JUST SETTLES"

Bao has reported that Jake is often easier to comfort in cat form because he does not feel obligated to explain himself.

During one observation, Jake approached Bao while she was reviewing medical records.

He rubbed against her leg.

Bao lowered a hand.

Jake immediately leaned into it.

She lifted him onto her lap.

He turned around twice, tucked his paws beneath himself, and settled against her.

Bao continued reading.

Jake fell asleep.

No questions were asked.

No reassurance was demanded.

No conversation was necessary.

Approximately forty minutes later, Bao needed to stand.

Jake woke as soon as she moved.

He looked up at her.

Bao stopped.

**BAO:**  
"I need to get up, sweetheart."

Jake's ears angled backward.

Bao placed a hand beside him.

**BAO:**  
"Frost is here. She'll stay with you while I finish this."

Frost, who had been waiting nearby, approached and held out her arms.

Jake looked at her.

Then at Bao.

After several seconds, he climbed into Frost's arms.

Bao waited until Frost had securely taken him before standing.

Jake watched her leave the room.

He did not immediately relax.

Frost sat down and allowed him to settle against her chest.

Eventually, his eyes closed.

### Bao's Assessment

> "He doesn't need me to talk to him all the time."
> 
> "Sometimes he just needs somewhere safe to sleep."
> 
> "The difficult part is making sure he doesn't wake up and discover that everyone has gone."

Bao considers cat form helpful because it allows Jake to accept care without requiring prolonged interaction.

She does not consider it a replacement for reliable supervision.

# 4. THE TEDDY BEAR REMAINS MANDATORY

Jake's teddy bear must remain with him in either form.

This requirement is unchanged.

The bear contains Jake's actual heartbeat and functions as his cardiac vessel. It is not an ordinary comfort object, despite its importance as one.

When Jake transforms into a cat, the teddy bear must be accommodated safely.

Personnel must not:

- leave it unattended while transporting Jake;
    
- separate Jake from it to make handling easier;
    
- place it somewhere he cannot reach;
    
- assume his smaller form makes the bear medically irrelevant;
    
- or treat the bear as an interchangeable toy.
    

If Jake curls up against someone, the bear should be placed within his immediate reach or positioned safely beside him.

If he is carried, the caregiver must ensure both Jake and the bear remain secure.

Jake's transformation does not suspend his existing medical requirements.

**The cat is Jake. The teddy bear is still his heart.**

---

# 5. WHY CAT FORM MUST NOT BECOME THE DEFAULT SOLUTION

C.O.R.E. recognizes the practical advantages of Jake's cat form.

It is easier to provide warmth.

It is easier to accommodate his desire for physical proximity.

He can rest without needing to explain that he is tired.

He can remain beside someone while they perform ordinary tasks.

He can seek comfort without feeling that he is asking for a lengthy conversation.

However, personnel must not deliberately encourage transformation simply because the resulting care is more convenient.

Jake is not required to become smaller, quieter, or easier to manage to deserve companionship.

He is still a six-year-old child when he has two hands and two feet.

He is still Jake when he has four paws.

His human form should not become associated with being difficult, inconvenient, or undeserving of care.

Nor should his cat form be treated as proof that he has recovered from his fear of being alone.

In some circumstances, the transformation may itself be a response to distress.

If Jake transforms immediately after someone announces a departure, hides beneath furniture, or becomes unusually difficult to locate, personnel should consider whether he is attempting to escape a frightening situation.

Do not assume that a quiet cat is necessarily a comfortable cat.

---

# 6. CARE GUIDANCE — CAT FORM

When Jake is in cat form, personnel should:

1. Maintain the same named-caregiver arrangement used in human form.
    
2. Allow him to approach and settle beside a trusted person voluntarily.
    
3. Provide a warm, quiet resting place.
    
4. Keep his teddy bear close and secure.
    
5. Observe his individual behavior for signs of discomfort, fear, or medical distress.
    
6. Tell him before moving him or transferring him to another caregiver, using a calm voice even if he cannot or does not choose to respond verbally.
    
7. Confirm that another caregiver has accepted responsibility before leaving.
    
8. Avoid chasing or crowding him if he hides.
    
9. Avoid carrying him into unsuitable work areas simply because he is small.
    
10. Never use his willingness to curl up on someone's lap as evidence that he no longer needs supervision.
    

Cat form may make companionship easier.

It does not eliminate the need to organize that companionship.

# 7. A QUIET AFTERNOON

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTIAL LOUNGE  
**PARTICIPANTS:** Jake Akana, Clover, Aries Akana

Jake had transformed sometime after lunch.

He was now curled up on Clover's lap, his white fur rising and falling with each quiet breath.

His teddy bear rested against his side.

Clover was reading.

Aries sat nearby, working on a report.

For almost an hour, nobody said anything.

Jake slept.

Clover turned pages.

Aries typed.

The room was quiet enough that the ventilation system was clearly audible.

Eventually, Aries received a message.

He read it, sighed, and began gathering his things.

Jake's eyes opened.

Clover noticed the change immediately.

Jake raised his head.

Aries stopped packing.

**ARIES:**  
"I need to go downstairs."

Jake stared at him.

His ears lowered slightly.

Aries set his bag down again.

**ARIES:**  
"Clover is staying here with you."

Jake looked at Clover.

She nodded.

**CLOVER:**  
"I'm not going anywhere right now."

Jake looked back at Aries.

Aries crouched beside the couch.

**ARIES:**  
"I'll tell you if anything changes."

Jake remained still.

Aries held out one finger.

Jake sniffed it.

Then he pressed his forehead against it.

Aries gently stroked between his ears.

**ARIES:**  
"Love you, little star."

Jake's tail curled around Clover's wrist.

Aries waited until Clover confirmed the handover.

Then he left.

Jake watched the door close.

For a few seconds, he remained alert.

Clover did not try to distract him.

She simply rested one hand against his back.

Eventually, Jake lowered his head and returned to sleep.

He had not forgotten that Aries left.

He had not suddenly decided that being alone was safe.

He had simply remained with someone he trusted while Aries went to work.

For now, that was enough.

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake is easier to tend to in cat form because the form allows him to accept physical comfort with fewer demands placed on him.

He can be held.

He can sleep.

He can stay close.

He can communicate through movement and proximity rather than having to explain what he needs.

For caregivers who are already stretched thin, this can make ordinary companionship more manageable.

But the most important finding is not that Jake becomes an easier cat.

It is that **Jake can be frightened without having to explain his fear before someone helps him.**

That should remain true in either form.

Aries cannot be expected to stay beside him every hour.

Bao cannot suspend her medical duties.

Clover and Phoenix cannot be permanently available.

The solution remains a reliable care arrangement in which one trusted person can leave because another trusted person has agreed to stay.

Jake should not have to choose between remaining in human form and receiving comfort.

He should not have to transform into a cat to make himself easier to look after.

And he should never have to become quieter, smaller, or more convenient before anyone takes his fear seriously.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There is a certain irony in the situation.

Jake is a six-year-old boy who has been through more than most adults should ever have to experience.

When he is frightened, he sometimes becomes a small white cat.

Then he climbs into someone's lap.

He curls up.

He tucks his paws beneath himself.

And, if the room remains quiet and the person stays where they are, he falls asleep.

No questions.

No explanations.

No need to find the right words.

Just a little cat trusting someone enough to close his eyes.

The difficulty begins when that person has to stand up.

Because Jake may wake before they do.

He may look around.

He may find the room empty.

And he may remember that the last time he waited for someone to come back, nobody came when he expected them to.

That is why the handover matters.

That is why the teddy bear must remain close.

That is why the next person must actually arrive.

Jake may be easier to tend to as a cat.

But he should never have to rely on being a cat to make someone stay.
