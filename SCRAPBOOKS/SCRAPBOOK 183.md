# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **TRAUMA RESPONSE ASSESSMENT**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – STARTLE RESPONSE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 – Executive & Medical Personnel Only

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Following multiple observations during search operations, Medical and Psychological Services have identified a persistent trauma-related startle response in Corin Lockwood.

Although Corin's autobiographical memory has largely returned, many reactions no longer require conscious thought.

Certain movements, sounds, and situations trigger immediate defensive behavior before Corin has time to recognize where she is or who is approaching.

Clinicians describe these responses as deeply conditioned reflexes shaped by years of repeated trauma.

---

# **OBSERVED TRIGGERS**

Corin has demonstrated immediate flight responses to several forms of unexpected stimulation, including:

- Sudden movements toward her.
    
- Someone reaching unexpectedly for her.
    
- Loud metallic impacts.
    
- Rapid footsteps approaching from behind.
    
- Raised voices, even when not directed at her.
    
- The sound of medical restraints or surgical instruments being prepared.
    

In nearly every observed case, Corin reacts first.

Recognition comes later.

---

# **FIELD OBSERVATION**

During one search operation, a Security officer spotted Corin sitting beneath a snow-covered pine.

The officer remembered Medical Services' instructions.

He remained where he was.

He spoke calmly.

> "Corin?"

She looked up.

For several seconds, neither moved.

Then another officer, unaware of the situation, hurried through the trees toward the first.

The sudden motion caused Corin to visibly tense.

Before either officer could say another word...

She had already disappeared into the snowfall.

Review of helmet-camera footage showed that Corin began moving **before** she turned to identify the source of the motion.

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Psychological Services concluded that Corin's reactions are not deliberate decisions.

Her body responds to perceived danger before conscious recognition can occur.

One clinician summarized the condition during case review.

> "Her memory became a reflex."

Another clarified,

> "She doesn't relive every event consciously."

> "Sometimes her body remembers before her mind does."

---

# **LUMI**

After learning what had happened during the previous search, Lumi changed the way she looked for Corin.

She no longer walked directly toward places where Corin might be hiding.

Instead...

She stopped several meters away.

She sat down where Corin could see her.

Then she waited.

Sometimes she quietly read a book.

Sometimes she simply watched the snow fall.

She wanted Corin's first impression to be stillness.

Not pursuit.

Not surprise.

Just someone who wasn't going to chase her.

---

# **SEARCH PROTOCOL UPDATE**

Effective immediately, all personnel are instructed to observe the following procedures if Corin is sighted:

- Stop moving once visual contact is established.
    
- Speak before changing position.
    
- Avoid approaching rapidly or from behind.
    
- Do not attempt to grab or restrain Corin unless there is an immediate threat to life.
    
- Allow Corin time to recognize who is present before any further interaction.
    

Psychological Services believe these measures reduce the likelihood of triggering an involuntary flight response.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin's memories have returned.

Some of them, however, no longer exist only as memories.

They have become automatic reactions.

To Corin, a sudden movement is no longer merely a movement.

For a fraction of a second...

It is every operating table.

Every restraint.

Every frightened child who learned that people rushing toward her rarely meant anything good.

Only after that moment passes...

Does she remember where she really is.
