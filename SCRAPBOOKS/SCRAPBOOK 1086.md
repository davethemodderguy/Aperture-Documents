# 1. PURPOSE

New employees assigned to Sector 4-C are frequently unfamiliar with Neri.

This is understandable.

Personnel orientation covers:

- anomalous entities;
    
- Blacksite security;
    
- emergency procedures;
    
- SAFEHOUSE access;
    
- Architect protocols;
    
- restricted areas;
    
- containment procedures.
    

It does not adequately prepare personnel for encountering a tiny, white-haired plush girl in an oversized hoodie quietly walking through a corridor while wearing prescription glasses.

Consequently, first-contact reactions are remarkably consistent.

---

# 2. FIRST SIGHTING

### EMPLOYEE #1 — MAINTENANCE

Employee was carrying equipment down a corridor.

A tiny figure walked past.

Employee stopped.

The figure stopped.

They looked at one another.

Employee looked down.

Neri looked up.

Employee:

> "...Is that a plushie?"

Neri nodded.

Employee:

> "Oh."

Neri continued walking.

Employee resumed walking.

Approximately twelve seconds later:

> "WAIT."

---

# 3. FIRST ATTEMPT AT IDENTIFICATION

New employees frequently assume Neri is:

- an ordinary stuffed animal;
    
- a SAFEHOUSE resident's personal plush;
    
- an anomalous toy;
    
- a children's item;
    
- a mascot;
    
- or something that should not be walking around by itself.
    

The final assumption is generally corrected very quickly.

A security employee once asked:

> "Who does this belong to?"

Neri pointed at herself.

The employee looked confused.

> "You?"

Neri nodded.

> "...You're Neri?"

Neri nodded again.

The employee checked the personnel database.

**NERI Z-4C-NERI**

**AGE:** 10  
**SPECIALTY:** Chemistry  
**STATUS:** SAFEHOUSE RESIDENT

The employee looked back down.

Neri was sitting on the floor.

---

# 4. THE INTELLIGENCE PROBLEM

New employees who learn Neri's identity are often surprised by her research credentials.

The usual sequence is:

> **Employee:** "She's a plushie?"

> **Senior Employee:** "Yes."

> **Employee:** "She lives here?"

> **Senior Employee:** "Yes."

> **Employee:** "She's ten?"

> **Senior Employee:** "Yes."

> **Employee:** "And she's a chemist?"

> **Senior Employee:** "An extremely good one."

> **Employee:** "How good?"

> **Senior Employee:** "She filled a 12 TB SSD with chemistry research in approximately one month."

Pause.

> **Employee:** "...That plushie?"

> **Senior Employee:** "That plushie."

---

# 5. THE NEKO COMPARISON

The situation becomes more confusing when new employees learn that Neri is also one of the few people in the Blacksite who can outperform Neko in a technical field.

Usually:

> "Neko?"

Yes.

**Neko.**

Head of Engineering & Archives.

The person employees routinely assume understands everything happening inside the Blacksite's computer infrastructure.

Neri can surpass him specifically in chemistry.

New employees frequently stare at Plush Neri afterward.

Neri is generally doing something completely unrelated.

Such as:

- drinking coffee;
    
- sitting on a chair;
    
- being carried by Alice;
    
- receiving headpats;
    
- sleeping;
    
- staring at a wall;
    
- biting someone's sleeve.
    

---

# 6. THE ALICE EXPLANATION

The easiest way for new employees to understand Neri's current social status is to observe Alice.

Alice is six.

She treats Neri approximately like her favorite stuffed animal.

A new employee once watched Alice pick Neri up from the floor, hug her, put her over one shoulder, and walk away.

The employee asked:

> "Is that allowed?"

Nearby personnel answered:

> "Neri doesn't mind."

The employee watched Neri calmly settle against Alice.

> "...Okay."

---

# 7. HEADPATS

New employees are sometimes surprised when Neri voluntarily approaches them.

This usually happens without warning.

Neri may walk up.

Look at them.

Then stand still.

Some experienced personnel recognize this immediately.

New personnel often do not.

One employee eventually asked:

> "Do you want something?"

Neri shook her head.

The employee hesitated.

Then gently patted her head.

Neri's eyes closed.

The employee froze.

Neri remained there.

The employee gave her another headpat.

Neri leaned into it.

The employee later described the experience as:

> "The weirdest employee orientation I've ever had."

---

# 8. THE BITE

New personnel are also eventually introduced to another characteristic.

Neri bites.

Usually sleeves.

Sometimes hands.

Occasionally anything she decides is worth grabbing.

The bite is not necessarily painful.

The problem is that her plush jaw has enough mechanical strength to **attach**.

One new employee discovered this after Neri bit their sleeve.

They attempted to continue walking.

Neri remained attached.

The employee looked down.

> "...What do I do?"

A senior employee replied:

> "Nothing."

> "Nothing?"

> "She'll let go."

Neri did not let go.

> "How long?"

> "Depends."

Neri eventually released the sleeve.

The employee has since stopped questioning the phenomenon.

---

# 9. THE "IS SHE SAFE?" QUESTION

New employees frequently ask whether Neri is safe to handle.

The answer is:

**Yes, with normal care.**

She is unusually durable in plush form.

She can survive ordinary bumps and falls that would otherwise be concerning.

She can be carried.

She can be hugged.

She can be placed on furniture.

She can ride on someone's back.

However:

**She is not indestructible.**

She can still be seriously injured by sufficiently severe physical force.

Personnel must not:

- throw her;
    
- violently swing her;
    
- crush her;
    
- squeeze her excessively;
    
- deliberately drop her;
    
- test her durability.
    

If she squeaks, stop.

---

# 10. THE MOST COMMON MISTAKE

New employees occasionally assume:

> **"She's a plushie, so she doesn't understand me."**

This is incorrect.

Neri understands considerably more than most people expect.

She can follow complicated conversations.

She can communicate through gestures.

She can speak when she wants or needs to.

And, if the conversation involves chemistry, her cognitive performance can become extremely difficult to follow.

She is simply not particularly interested in proving that she understands you.

---

# 11. REPRESENTATIVE FIRST-CONTACT INCIDENT

A newly hired laboratory technician encountered Neri sitting on a laboratory counter.

The technician assumed she was an ordinary plush.

They moved her aside to make room for equipment.

Neri calmly sat where she had been placed.

The technician continued working.

Approximately ten minutes later, the technician noticed a complex chemical model displayed on a nearby workstation.

They commented that one of the reaction pathways seemed incorrect.

Neri looked at the screen.

She climbed down.

She approached the workstation.

She pointed at a specific section.

Then another.

The technician corrected the model.

It worked.

The technician stared at her.

> "...Did you just fix my chemistry?"

Neri nodded.

> "You're a chemist?"

Neri nodded.

> "You're the plushie."

Neri nodded.

> "...Right."

Neri walked away.

### Neri likes headpats.

Gentle ones.

Let her.

# 13. FINAL OBSERVATION

The average new employee expects their first encounter with an anomalous Blacksite resident to be intimidating.

They expect:

> glowing eyes;

> impossible abilities;

> ancient entities;

> reality distortion;

> something that requires three pages of safety instructions.

Instead, they encounter a tiny plush girl wearing glasses.

She may silently walk past them.

She may stare at them.

She may ask for coffee.

She may climb onto their back.

She may request a headpat without saying a word.

She may bite their sleeve.

And then, several hours later, that same tiny plushie may casually produce chemistry research sophisticated enough to make Neko reconsider his life choices.
