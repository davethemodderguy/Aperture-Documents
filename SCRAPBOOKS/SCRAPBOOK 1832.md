# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. — CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE WELFARE & ANOMALOUS RESIDENTS DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** CORE-SW-CC-026  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — RESIDENT WELFARE / MEDICAL  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE INVESTIGATION  
**SUBJECT:** _Five Weeks of Snow_  
**INVOLVED RESIDENTS:** Camelia Candleheart, Jake Akana, Bao  
**INCIDENT TYPE:** Prolonged Environmental Exposure / Missing Resident / Memory-Related Safety Failure

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Camelia Candleheart had never seen snow.

For a girl whose memories of her former life had been almost entirely swallowed by severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia, the discovery of something as simple as falling snow was enough to inspire genuine curiosity. Jake Akana, who understood what it meant to experience the world differently from everyone else, offered to take her outside.

He intended to show her something beautiful.

He did not intend to spend the next five weeks outside.

Camelia returned indoors before Jake did. At some point, she sealed the entrance behind her. Whether she consciously intended to leave him outside or simply forgot that he was still there has not been established. Given her documented condition, investigators consider the latter a serious possibility.

Then she forgot about Jake.

The residents of SAFEHOUSE became occupied with their respective routines. No one raised the alarm. No one realized that the child who had gone outside to show his new older sister the snow had never come back.

For five weeks, Jake remained outside in the cold.

Bao discovered him while conducting a routine weather survey for meteorological data. By then, his blue flame was flickering weakly. His speech had suffered permanent disfigurement, leaving him capable of producing only short bursts of words.

Camelia eventually learned what had happened.

She had forgotten her little brother.

And Jake had been left outside long enough for that forgotten absence to change him permanently.

---

### I. THE FIRST SNOWFALL

The initial outing was not considered unusual.

Camelia had been living at SAFEHOUSE long enough to understand that the outside world contained things she had never experienced, even if she could not always retain the names, explanations, or circumstances associated with them.

Snow was one such thing.

Witness accounts indicate that Jake noticed her watching the snowfall from indoors. He approached her and offered to show her what it looked like up close.

There was no scheduled expedition. No formal supervision request. No reason for anyone to believe that a brief walk outside would become a welfare incident.

Jake simply wanted to share something with her.

**Recovered recollection — SAFEHOUSE resident interview:**

> **CAMELIA:** “I had never seen snow before.”
> 
> **INTERVIEWER:** “What did Jake say?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I don't remember his exact words.”
> 
> **INTERVIEWER:** “Do you remember going outside with him?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I remember the snow. I remember that it was cold.”
> 
> **INTERVIEWER:** “Do you remember coming back inside?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Yes.”
> 
> **INTERVIEWER:** “Do you remember Jake coming inside with you?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “...”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “No.”

Her inability to recall the return is consistent with her established amnesia. It does not, by itself, establish what she was thinking when she entered the building.

The available evidence cannot determine whether Camelia believed Jake was following her, thought he had already returned, or simply lost the memory of the outing altogether.

What is established is that Jake did not return with her.

And no one noticed.

---

### II. FIVE WEEKS

**Duration:** Approximately five weeks  
**Location:** Exterior grounds, SAFEHOUSE  
**Environmental conditions:** Prolonged cold exposure and snowfall

There is no complete account of Jake's movements during this period.

No one maintained a reliable count of how long he had been absent. No one recorded a last-seen time. No one initiated a search.

The absence of these records is itself part of the incident.

Jake was six years old.

He was also a ghost, a fact that appears to have contributed to a dangerous assumption: that ordinary environmental conditions could not seriously endanger him.

That assumption was not supported by adequate evidence.

Jake could still experience pain. He could still be hurt. His anomalous nature did not make him invulnerable, and his inability to return indoors did not become less dangerous because he was dead.

The snow kept falling.

Eventually, his flame began to weaken.

The blue light that normally marked his presence diminished to a faint, unstable flicker. His condition deteriorated to the point that, when he was finally discovered, even his ability to speak had been profoundly affected.

C.O.R.E. cannot reconstruct every hour of those five weeks. Jake's current communication limitations make a detailed retrospective account particularly difficult, and he should not be subjected to exhausting interviews simply to fill gaps in the record.

What he has managed to communicate is being preserved exactly as received.

No demand for a complete explanation will be placed upon him.

**C.O.R.E. MEDICAL NOTE:**

Jake's ghosthood must not be used to dismiss observable deterioration. The weakening of his flame and the permanent impairment of his speech establish that the incident had lasting consequences.

The precise mechanism of injury remains under investigation.

---

### III. THE DISCOVERY

Bao was surveying the weather for data collection when she found him.

She had been conducting an ordinary task. She was not searching for Jake, and she had not been dispatched in response to a missing-resident report.

She saw a small figure outside.

At first, the most conspicuous detail was the flame.

It was barely flickering.

Bao recognized Jake.

The following reconstruction is based on Bao's account and the limited interaction that followed.

---

**INCIDENT RECONSTRUCTION — EXTERIOR SURVEY AREA**

_Snowfall continues. Bao notices an anomalous blue light near the ground and approaches._

**BAO:** “...Jake?”

_The flame flickers._

**BAO:** “Jake, sweetheart?”

_No complete sentence follows. Jake attempts to answer, but his speech emerges in short, broken bursts._

**JAKE:** “B... Bao...”

_Bao abandons the survey and approaches him._

**BAO:** “Oh, no. No, no—what happened to you?”

_Jake attempts to speak again. The words are too fragmented to form a reliable account._

**JAKE:** “C... cold...”

_Bao immediately prioritizes getting him to safety and obtaining medical assistance._

---

Bao's survey was terminated.

The weather data she had intended to collect became irrelevant.

She had found a child who should have been indoors five weeks ago.

She had found a child who, until that moment, had apparently been missing without anyone knowing he was missing.

---

### IV. MEDICAL AND COMMUNICATIONS ASSESSMENT

Jake's condition following rescue required immediate medical attention.

The most alarming finding was not merely the weakness of his flame. His speech had been permanently disfigured.

Before this incident, Jake could communicate in ordinary short sentences, despite his unusual voice and physical condition. Following the five-week exposure, he could produce only brief bursts of words.

His current communication must be treated as a restricted resource. Questions should be short, responses should not be rushed, and silence must not be interpreted as refusal, indifference, or lack of understanding.

The permanent nature of the speech impairment has been established in the incident report. The exact physiological or anomalous process responsible remains unresolved.

No one is authorized to promise Jake that his speech will return to normal.

No one is authorized to treat his difficulty speaking as an inconvenience.

**Medical directive:**

- Jake is to receive continued assessment and supportive care.
    
- Communication attempts must be paced according to his condition.
    
- Staff must allow nonverbal responses where possible, without assuming that every gesture has an obvious meaning.
    
- Investigators must not pressure him to recount the five weeks in detail.
    
- His current limitations must not be used as evidence that he cannot understand what is happening around him.
    

The incident has caused a permanent loss.

The investigation must acknowledge that without reducing Jake to that loss.

### V. THE PERSON WHO FORGOT

Camelia's involvement requires a different kind of investigation.

She has severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia. Her condition affects far more than autobiographical memories. She can forget names, people, roles, routes, instructions, and ordinary daily events. Her notebook exists because information she learns may not remain accessible to her.

Jake was the notable exception.

She almost never forgot him.

She recognized him. She knew him. She called him her little brother.

That persistent recognition was never proof that her memory was infallible. It was an observed pattern, not a guarantee.

During this incident, the pattern failed.

Camelia forgot Jake.

The current evidence does not establish whether she forgot the outing, forgot that Jake was outside, forgot that he existed, or experienced some combination of memory failures. C.O.R.E. will not manufacture certainty where there is none.

But the distinction between _forgetting deliberately_ and _forgetting because of a severe neurological anomaly_ matters for understanding her actions. It does not erase the consequences.

Jake was left outside.

No one noticed.

And the person who would ordinarily have been most likely to remember him did not.

---

**FOLLOW-UP INTERVIEW — CAMELIA CANDLEHEART**

_Interview conducted after Camelia was informed of Jake's condition. Staff were instructed not to demand that she reconstruct memories she could not access._

**INTERVIEWER:** “Camelia, do you remember taking Jake outside?”

**CAMELIA:** “I remember the snow.”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Do you remember Jake being with you?”

_Camelia looks down at her notebook._

**CAMELIA:** “I... don't know.”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Do you remember leaving him outside?”

**CAMELIA:** “No.”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Do you remember looking for him afterward?”

_Long pause._

**CAMELIA:** “Did I look for him?”

**INTERVIEWER:** “No search was initiated.”

_Camelia turns a page in her notebook, then another. She appears to be searching for an entry that might explain what she has been told._

**CAMELIA:** “Where was he?”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Outside.”

**CAMELIA:** “For how long?”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Approximately five weeks.”

_Camelia stops turning pages._

**CAMELIA:** “Five... weeks?”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Yes.”

**CAMELIA:** “No.”

_She looks at the interviewer, visibly distressed._

**CAMELIA:** “No, I wouldn't—”

_She stops. Her next words do not come._

**INTERVIEWER:** “We are not asking you to remember something you cannot remember. We need to understand what happened, but we will not ask you to invent an answer.”

**CAMELIA:** “He's my little brother.”

**INTERVIEWER:** “Yes.”

**CAMELIA:** “I know him.”

_She opens the notebook again, as if the written record might provide an answer that her own memory cannot._

**CAMELIA:** “I know him.”

_The interview was paused at Camelia's request._

### VI. THE NOTEBOOK

Camelia's notebook remains an essential accessibility aid.

It cannot, however, be treated as a complete defense against every possible memory failure. It is useful only when information is recorded, accessible, and consulted at the appropriate time. It cannot automatically alert its owner that a person has been forgotten.

Nor can it guarantee that a familiar name will summon the memories attached to it.

C.O.R.E. will review Camelia's existing support arrangements without confiscating or replacing her notebook. Additional safeguards must support her independence rather than punish her for a disability she cannot simply choose to overcome.

These safeguards will include:

1. **Supported outings.** Camelia and any resident accompanying her must have a reliable return check for exterior excursions.
    
2. **Resident accountability.** A resident who leaves with another resident must not be presumed to have returned until their return is confirmed.
    
3. **Missing-person escalation.** An unexplained absence involving a child must trigger an immediate welfare check, regardless of the child's anomalous classification.
    
4. **Accessible reminders.** Camelia's memory aids and routines must be reviewed with her participation, with emphasis on practical safeguards rather than shame.
    
5. **No transfer of responsibility to Jake.** Jake is a child. He must never be made responsible for monitoring Camelia's memory, preventing future incidents, or reassuring others about her condition.
    

These measures are not a declaration that Camelia is dangerous.

They are an acknowledgment that good intentions and familiar routines were not enough to protect either of them.

---

### VII. ACCOUNTABILITY

The incident cannot be attributed solely to Camelia.

Her actions were the immediate circumstances under which Jake was left outside, but the five-week duration also reflects a broader failure of resident welfare oversight. No one noticed Jake's absence. No one confirmed his return. No system raised an alarm.

C.O.R.E. must investigate each failure independently.

Camelia requires support for her amnesia. Jake requires care for his injuries. SAFEHOUSE requires procedures that do not depend upon every resident remembering everything correctly, every day.

Those requirements are compatible.

The organization will not demand that Camelia suffer in order to demonstrate that Jake's suffering matters. Nor will it use her condition to minimize the harm done to him.

Both residents are entitled to care.

Both deserve to be treated as people rather than as the worst moment of their lives.

---

### VIII. CLOSING OBSERVATION

The original reason for the outing was simple.

Camelia had never seen snow.

Jake wanted her to see it.

He took her outside because he wanted to share something with her—not because anyone had ordered him to, not because he had been assigned to help her, and not because he understood how fragile her memory could be.

He was six years old. He wanted to show his older sister something beautiful.

Five weeks later, Bao found him in the snow.

His flame was weak.

His words were broken.

And Camelia, who had once been the person who almost never forgot him, could not remember where he had gone.

There will be no reassuring conclusion appended to this report. Jake's speech impairment is permanent. The five weeks cannot be returned to him. A notebook cannot undo the incident, and an apology cannot restore what he lost.

Camelia may never recover the missing memories.

She may have to learn what happened through the testimony of other people, reading the same facts more than once because she cannot rely on them remaining in her mind.

Jake may never be able to tell her, in his own words, everything he experienced.

C.O.R.E. cannot promise that the two of them will find a way to make sense of this.

It can ensure that Jake is no longer left outside without anyone noticing.

It can ensure that Camelia receives help without being told that her disability makes her unworthy of a family.

And it can ensure that neither child is expected to repair this alone.

**FINAL DIRECTIVE:**

_The next time snow falls, no resident will be presumed safe simply because no one remembers leaving them behind._
