## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **PSYCHOLOGICAL / ANOMALOUS PERSONNEL RECORD**

# **THE THING BETWEEN LIFE AND DEATH**

**Classification:** LEVEL 9 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Current Status:** Recovered / Stable  
**Primary Concern:** **Persistent Identity Distortion**  
**Psychological Theme:** **"I am not a person."**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin has returned to the treehouse.

Her voice has been restored.

She can eat.

She can experience taste.

She can interact with others.

She can laugh.

She can care for Jay.

She can sit with Orphie.

From an external perspective, she appears considerably more stable than she was before her disappearance.

Internally, however, one thought remains deeply fixed within her mind.

> **"I'm not alive."**

And immediately after:

> **"I'm not dead either."**

Corin has increasingly described herself as something that exists between the two.

Not a living person.

Not a dead person.

Something else.

Something created in a laboratory.

Something she believes should never have continued existing.

---

# **THE WORD "THING"**

Corin continues to refer to herself as a **thing**.

The wording has not disappeared despite repeated reassurance from those around her.

Examples include:

> **"I'm not a person."**

> **"I'm a thing that happened to survive."**

> **"People don't need to be stitched together."**

> **"I'm something the lab left behind."**

> **"You keep treating me like a person."**

When corrected, Corin rarely argues.

She simply looks away.

---

# **THE FIXATED THOUGHT**

The most persistent thought currently identified is:

> **"I am an abomination from a laboratory."**

Corin does not merely remember what happened to her.

She has incorporated it into her identity.

She does not think:

> _"Something horrible happened to me in a laboratory."_

She thinks:

> _"The laboratory made me into something horrible."_

That distinction is central to her current psychological state.

---

# **LIFE OR DEATH**

Corin understands that she died.

She remembers enough of her past to know that her biological life ended.

She also understands that she continues to function.

Her body does not behave like a living human body.

Her organs do not operate conventionally.

Her Ether sustains her.

Her limbs require stitching.

Her wounds repair themselves.

Food is dissolved into Ether.

She does not possess a conventional heartbeat.

Yet she continues to think.

To remember.

To feel.

To care.

To speak.

This contradiction has become one of the foundations of her identity crisis.

She once wrote:

> **"Dead people don't do this."**

Then:

> **"Alive people aren't supposed to do this either."**

And finally:

> **"So what am I?"**

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT'S RESPONSE**

Guiding Light has repeatedly told Corin:

> **"You're Corin."**

Corin understands the statement.

She simply does not believe it answers the question.

During one conversation:

> **Corin:**  
> "Am I alive?"

Guiding Light paused.

> **"Not in the way most people are."**

Corin looked down.

> **"Am I dead?"**

Another pause.

> **"Not completely."**

Corin gave a small, bitter smile.

> **"So I'm a thing."**

Guiding Light immediately responded:

> **"No."**

Corin did not argue.

She simply looked at her hands.

---

# **CURIOUS LIGHT**

Curious Light has struggled with this question from an entirely different perspective.

He can describe Corin's physiology.

He can describe her Ether.

He can document the mechanisms keeping her body functional.

He can explain why her limbs detach.

He can explain why food dissolves.

He can document her reconstructed vocal cords.

But none of those explanations answer:

> **What is Corin?**

He eventually stopped trying to define her scientifically.

His latest note reads:

> **"I can explain how she functions."**

> **"I cannot explain what she is."**

> **"And perhaps that isn't something I am supposed to decide."**

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie refuses to use Corin's terminology.

When Corin says:

> **"This thing needs its arm stitched back on."**

Orphie responds:

> **"You need your arm stitched back on."**

When Corin says:

> **"This thing is hungry."**

Orphie responds:

> **"You want a sandwich."**

When Corin says:

> **"This thing shouldn't exist."**

Orphie responds:

> **"My sister exists."**

Corin rarely has an answer to that.

---

# **THE LABORATORY**

The laboratory remains central to Corin's self-image.

She remembers being experimented upon.

She remembers being killed.

She remembers her body being cut apart and reassembled.

She remembers being discarded.

The scars remain.

The stitches remain.

Her altered physiology remains.

To Corin, her body is evidence.

Evidence that the laboratory did not merely hurt her.

It **changed what she was.**

She therefore views her current existence as the laboratory's final product.

Not a survivor.

Not a resurrection.

An artifact.

An abomination.

---

# **THE CONTRADICTION**

Everyone around Corin sees evidence of a person.

She sees evidence of a procedure.

They see:

- A girl who likes sandwiches.
    
- Someone who loves pickles.
    
- Someone who cares for Jay.
    
- Someone who laughs at Mischevious Light's jokes.
    
- Someone who sits beside Orphie.
    
- Someone who remembers her family.
    
- Someone who can be hurt.
    

Corin sees:

- Ether.
    
- Stitches.
    
- Scars.
    
- Missing organs.
    
- A dead body.
    
- A laboratory history.
    
- A continued existence that should not have happened.
    

Both descriptions refer to the same individual.

That contradiction remains unresolved.

---

# **CURRENT PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE**

Corin is currently capable of functioning socially.

She can enjoy ordinary experiences.

She can communicate normally.

She can express affection.

She can experience humor.

She can participate in daily activities.

However, the underlying belief remains:

> **"I am not a person."**

The belief has not been eliminated by her recovery.

It has simply become quieter.

It resurfaces whenever Corin examines her body or thinks about the laboratory.

---

# **FINAL ASSESSMENT**

Corin exists in a state that defies the categories she understands.

She is not conventionally alive.

She is not conventionally dead.

She is sustained by something that should not exist.

Her body is damaged beyond what ordinary medicine could repair.

Her existence began anew through circumstances she never chose.

And somewhere in her mind, one conclusion has become deeply entrenched:

> **"I'm an abomination from a lab."**

The Architects disagree.

Orphie disagrees.

The people who care about her disagree.

But simply telling Corin that she is a person has never been enough.

Perhaps the answer is not to convince her that her body is normal.

It isn't.

Perhaps the answer is to show her that **being abnormal does not make her less of a person.**

Because Corin does not need to become alive again to deserve a life.

She does not need to become biologically normal to deserve love.

And she does not need to erase what happened in that laboratory to be more than what was done to her.

She is not the laboratory.

She is the one who survived it.

**End of Psychological / Anomalous Personnel Record**
