# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### RESIDENTIAL SAFETY & WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-SAFE-4C-NERI-095  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — RESIDENTIAL WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Neri — Reduced Social Signaling Following Critical Medical Incident  
**RELATED SUBJECTS:** Bao / Jane Doe

---

# 1. Behavioral Change

Neri's physical recovery has been accompanied by a noticeable change in her behavior.

She has stopped smiling.

Not entirely.

There are still occasional moments in which her expression softens when she sees someone she trusts.

However, spontaneous smiles have become extremely uncommon.

More concerning is that Neri appears to have stopped attempting to attract attention altogether.

She does not wave people over.

She does not interrupt conversations.

She rarely approaches someone first.

She does not call for anyone.

She cannot speak at present, but even before the loss of her voice, Neri was already reluctant to do so.

Now she appears to have adapted to silence as a permanent expectation.

---

# 2. Observed Social Pattern

Neri will frequently enter a room, identify someone she wants to speak with, and then stop.

She waits.

If the person notices her, she communicates through writing or gestures.

If they do not notice her, she leaves.

She does not attempt again.

This behavior has been observed repeatedly.

There is no evidence that Neri is intentionally punishing others through withdrawal.

The opposite appears more likely.

She appears to have reached the conclusion that:

> **If people want me around, they'll notice me.**

And if they do not notice her, she assumes she should not bother them.

---

# 3. Facial Expression

Personnel have specifically noted the absence of Neri's previous small expressions of happiness.

She still enjoys food.

She still reads.

She still spends time with SAFEHOUSE residents.

She still accepts affection from Bao.

She still responds positively to familiar people.

But the visible excitement that once accompanied these interactions has diminished.

A particularly notable example occurred when Bao brought Neri a dessert.

Neri accepted it.

She nodded.

She ate it.

She did not smile.

Previously, the same situation would almost certainly have produced at least a small smile.

---

# 4. Bao's Response

Bao has become increasingly attentive to Neri's practical difficulties.

After several incidents involving locked doors, Bao made a simple modification.

Neri's SAFEHOUSE keycard was previously carried separately from her clothing.

This created a recurring problem:

Neri would leave a room.

A door would lock.

Neri would discover that she did not have immediate access.

She could not call for assistance.

She would then wait.

Bao decided that this was unacceptable.

---

## ACCESS MODIFICATION

Bao clipped Neri's keycard directly onto the **waist area of her oversized coat**.

The card remains attached to Neri's clothing at all times unless deliberately removed.

The placement was selected because:

- Neri's coat is almost always worn;
    
- the card is physically accessible;
    
- Neri does not need to search through pockets;
    
- the card is less likely to be forgotten;
    
- the arrangement remains compatible with her limited movement.
    

Bao reportedly checked the attachment several times to ensure it would not interfere with Neri's movement.

Neri looked down at the card.

Then at Bao.

Bao simply said:

**BAO:**  
"Now you don't have to worry about getting locked out."

Neri nodded.

---

# 5. Neri's Reaction

Neri did not smile.

She did not make a joke.

She did not protest.

She simply touched the keycard with one hand.

Then she wrote on a nearby notepad:

> **"Okay."**

Bao looked at her.

**BAO:**  
"That's it?"

Neri shrugged.

Bao sighed.

She gently adjusted the card on Neri's coat.

**BAO:**  
"You're allowed to be happy about it."

Neri looked down.

After several seconds, she wrote:

> **"I am."**

Bao did not press the issue.

She simply hugged her.

---

# 6. Jane Doe's Observation

Jane has noticed the behavioral change as well.

She has specifically observed that Neri no longer attempts to get her attention when Jane is busy.

Previously, Neri might have stood nearby until Jane noticed her.

Now she often turns around and leaves.

Jane has begun deliberately checking the room whenever she senses that Neri may be nearby.

On one occasion:

**JANE:**  
"Neri?"

Neri looked up from her book.

Jane walked over.

**JANE:**  
"You were trying to get my attention?"

Neri shook her head.

Jane looked at her for several seconds.

**JANE:**  
"You don't have to lie."

Neri looked away.

Jane sat beside her.

**JANE:**  
"You can bother me."

Neri remained silent.

Jane continued reading her own book.

She did not force the conversation.

After several minutes, Neri moved slightly closer.

---

# 7. Welfare Assessment

The reduction in attention-seeking behavior should **not** be interpreted as improvement.

Neri is quieter because she is recovering.

She is physically exhausted.

She cannot speak.

She has experienced repeated accidents resulting from being overlooked.

She has recently survived a near-fatal medical event.

And she has repeatedly received evidence that asking for attention does not always produce a response.

Consequently, her withdrawal may represent an adaptation:

> **Don't ask. Don't interrupt. Don't bother anyone. Just wait.**

This is precisely the behavior C.O.R.E. previously attempted to discourage.

---

# 8. Current Safety Philosophy

The SAFEHOUSE is therefore adopting a different approach.

Personnel are not expected to force Neri to socialize.

They are expected to **notice her without requiring her to announce herself.**

If Neri is sitting quietly:

Say hello.

If she is reading:

Ask what she's reading.

If she is eating alone:

Ask whether she wants company.

If she walks away:

Do not assume she wanted to be alone.

Give her the opportunity to say yes or no through her preferred communication method.

Most importantly:

**Do not wait for Neri to become noticeable.**

---

# 9. Final Assessment

The keycard modification is a small intervention.

It prevents a very specific problem.

Neri is now substantially less likely to become accidentally locked out of a secured area simply because she forgot or was unable to retrieve her access card.

But the larger issue cannot be solved with a keycard.

Neri is disappearing socially while remaining physically present.

She walks through the SAFEHOUSE.

She sits at tables.

She reads books.

She eats.

She listens.

She watches everyone else.

And increasingly, she does nothing to make sure anyone watches her.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Bao solved the locked-door problem in about thirty seconds.

She clipped Neri's keycard to her coat and made sure it would always be there.

Simple.

Practical.

Effective.

Unfortunately, nobody has found a similarly simple solution for the other problem.

Neri has stopped trying to make people notice her.

She has stopped smiling when people do.

And she has begun treating being overlooked as normal.

The SAFEHOUSE should not ask Neri to become louder.

**The SAFEHOUSE needs to become better at listening to quiet.**
