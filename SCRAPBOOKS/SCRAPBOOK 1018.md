**SUBJECT:** Neri — Renewed Social Withdrawal  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE / Sector 4-C

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Neri has resumed a pattern of deliberate social isolation.

Following the recent conversation with Jane Doe regarding Neri's question:

> **“Am I useful?”**

Neri's presence throughout SAFEHOUSE has noticeably decreased.

This is not simply a continuation of her naturally solitary behavior.

Neri is now actively avoiding several residents with whom she previously maintained the strongest relationships:

- **Bao**
    
- **Frost**
    
- **Alice**
    
- **Jane Doe**
    

Neri continues to eat, read, drink coffee, maintain her room, and attend to necessary medical needs.

However, she has significantly reduced voluntary interaction.

She does not appear to be attempting to leave SAFEHOUSE permanently.

She appears to be attempting to make herself **socially unavailable.**

---

## OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

Neri's current movement pattern has returned to:

> **ROOM → CAFETERIA → ROOM**

with occasional necessary medical trips.

She is rarely observed:

- walking beside another resident;
    
- sitting with another resident voluntarily;
    
- approaching someone for conversation;
    
- showing another resident her drawings;
    
- remaining in a shared room when someone else enters;
    
- waiting for someone in a hallway;
    
- initiating written communication;
    
- responding to casual attempts at conversation.
    

More concerningly, Neri has begun altering her behavior when specific residents approach.

---

## 1. BAO

Bao remains Neri's primary medical support and maternal figure.

Neri still permits Bao to provide necessary medical care.

Outside of medical necessity, however, Neri increasingly avoids her.

When Bao enters the cafeteria, Neri may finish eating sooner than usual and leave.

When Bao approaches Neri's room, Neri has occasionally remained silent until Bao leaves.

When Bao asks whether Neri needs anything, Neri generally responds with a shake of the head.

The distinction is important.

Neri is **not refusing Bao's medical assistance.**

She is refusing opportunities for Bao to provide additional care.

### Observed exchange

**BAO:**  
“Neri, you don't have to leave.”

_Neri continues walking._

**BAO:**  
“I wasn't asking you to do anything.”

_Neri stops._

_She looks back._

**BAO:**  
“You can just stay.”

_Neri looks away._

_After several seconds, she continues walking._

---

## 2. FROST

Neri has similarly reduced contact with Frost.

Previously, Frost could approach Neri without much resistance.

Current behavior is different.

If Frost sits nearby, Neri may move to another seat.

If Frost attempts to walk alongside her, Neri slows briefly, then creates distance.

If Frost asks whether something is wrong, Neri generally shakes her head.

No written explanation has been provided.

Frost appears to understand that Neri is avoiding her but does not understand why.

---

## 3. ALICE

Alice has experienced the most visible change.

Neri previously tolerated — and occasionally participated in — Alice's presence despite Alice's energetic personality.

Current behavior indicates active avoidance.

When Alice enters the cafeteria, Neri may leave.

When Alice approaches Neri's table, Neri may gather her books and move elsewhere.

When Alice calls for her, Neri occasionally continues walking without turning around.

Alice has attempted to interpret this as anger.

There is currently no evidence that Neri is angry with Alice.

The more likely interpretation is that Neri does not want to become the subject of another conversation about herself.

Alice's attempts to reconnect have therefore occasionally produced the opposite effect.

---

## 4. JANE DOE

Jane has received the strongest avoidance response.

This is particularly notable given Jane's previously established relationship with Neri.

Neri previously approached Jane voluntarily.

She ate with Jane.

She showed Jane drawings.

She trusted Jane with information that she had never disclosed to other Blacksite personnel.

She even disclosed Jane's private name to Jane herself and subsequently protected that information.

Current behavior is substantially different.

When Jane enters a room, Neri may leave.

When Jane calls her:

_Neri keeps walking._

When Jane attempts to sit beside her:

_Neri relocates._

When Jane leaves a note:

_Neri may read it but does not respond._

Jane has not attempted to physically stop Neri.

She has instead begun allowing Neri to leave.

This has been difficult for Jane.

## INTERPRETATION

The timing strongly suggests a connection between the renewed isolation and the previous conversation concerning usefulness.

Neri's established internal framework remains:

> **If I am useful, I have a reason to stay.**

The recent conversation attempted to dismantle that framework.

Instead, Neri appears to have encountered a new problem:

> **If I don't have to be useful, why do they want me?**

This question may be particularly uncomfortable when directed toward people who provide substantial care.

Bao provides blood support and medication.

Frost repeatedly attempts companionship.

Alice actively seeks Neri's company.

Jane protects Neri and has repeatedly demonstrated that she wants her around.

From Neri's perspective, these relationships may now be interpreted as **debts.**

If someone gives her something, she may feel obligated to justify receiving it.

If someone spends time with her, she may feel obligated to make that time worthwhile.

If someone worries about her, she may conclude that she has caused an inconvenience.

The resulting solution is consistent with Neri's established behavior:

> **Remove herself from the situation.**

No interaction means no interruption.

No interruption means no burden.

No burden means nobody has to spend resources on her.

---

## SIGNIFICANT CHANGE

Neri's isolation should not be interpreted as evidence that she has stopped caring about the SAFEHOUSE residents.

In fact, her choice of people to avoid suggests the opposite.

She is specifically avoiding:

**Bao** — because Bao cares for her.

**Frost** — because Frost treats her like family.

**Alice** — because Alice wants her around.

**Jane** — because Jane has repeatedly told Neri she belongs.

These are precisely the relationships Neri may currently find most difficult to accept.

---

## PERSONNEL RESPONSE

Personnel are advised **not** to respond by repeatedly pursuing Neri.

Do not:

- corner her in hallways;
    
- demand explanations;
    
- ask why she is avoiding people;
    
- guilt her for leaving;
    
- repeatedly tell her she is hurting everyone;
    
- force her into group activities;
    
- demand that she smile;
    
- require her to write an explanation;
    
- interpret silence as agreement;
    
- interpret withdrawal as rejection.
    

Neri's current inability to speak makes repeated verbal questioning particularly ineffective.

Instead, residents should continue making their presence available without requiring Neri to reciprocate.

Examples include:

> “You can sit here if you want.”

> “I'm going to the cafeteria. You can come with me, or not.”

> “I'm here if you need me.”

> “You don't have to talk.”

> “You don't owe me anything.”

Most importantly:

> **“You don't have to earn being here.”**

Then leave the decision to Neri.

## C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Neri's renewed isolation represents a **significant welfare concern**, particularly because it is occurring alongside her severe anemia and established tendency to conceal medical deterioration.

However, her preference for solitude remains legitimate.

The objective is therefore **not to eliminate Neri's solitude.**

The objective is to prevent solitude from becoming:

> **“Nobody is allowed to notice me.”**

Neri is still eating.

Neri is still reading.

Neri is still drawing.

Neri is still drinking coffee.

Neri is still receiving necessary medical care.

Neri is still here.

But she is once again making a deliberate effort to ensure that almost nobody has to interact with her.

The most concerning aspect is not that Neri wants to be alone.

It is that she appears to believe that **being alone is the safest way to avoid becoming a burden.**

---

### C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Neri asked Jane:

> **“Am I useful?”**

Jane tried to tell her that usefulness was irrelevant.

Neri may have heard:

> **“You're not useful, but we keep you anyway.”**

Now she is avoiding the people who might tell her otherwise.

Not because she hates them.

Not because she wants them gone.

But because being wanted without having to earn it remains something Neri does not yet understand.

And, for now, she would rather disappear into the background than risk asking anyone to prove it again.
