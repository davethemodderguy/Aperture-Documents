**SUBJECT:** Nora Tachyon — Acute Sleep Deprivation and Temporary Bedridden Period

---

# Executive Summary

Nora Tachyon has experienced a severe deterioration in sleep over the past week.

She has been **unable to obtain meaningful sleep for approximately seven consecutive days.**

This period coincided with a significant physical illness.

Nora became sufficiently unwell that she was temporarily **bedridden** and required continuous rest, hydration, food monitoring, and resident assistance.

The combination of:

- prolonged sleep deprivation;
    
- physical illness;
    
- worsening nightmares;
    
- reduced appetite;
    
- reduced fluid intake;
    
- and emotional exhaustion
    

has resulted in a substantial decline from Nora's normal activity level.

For the duration of the illness, Nora was not permitted to participate in normal training, recreational activities, or extended Blacksite movement.

This restriction was medical rather than disciplinary.

---

# 1. INITIAL PRESENTATION

The first indication that something was wrong was not a nightmare report.

It was breakfast.

Nora arrived late.

She normally arrived with considerably more energy.

On this occasion, she remained standing beside the table without immediately sitting down.

Aeterna noticed first.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Nora."

Nora looked up.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "How long have you slept?"

Nora paused.

> **NORA:**  
> "...I don't know."

This was unusual.

Aeterna approached.

Nora attempted to take another step.

She stopped.

Then quietly said:

> **NORA:**  
> "I'm tired."

She was assisted back to her room.

Within several hours, it became apparent that this was not ordinary fatigue.

---

# 2. PHYSICAL ILLNESS

Nora subsequently developed a significant acute illness.

Symptoms observed during the initial period included:

- pronounced fatigue;
    
- weakness;
    
- elevated sleep pressure without successful sleep;
    
- reduced appetite;
    
- reduced fluid intake;
    
- headaches;
    
- intermittent chills;
    
- periods of dizziness;
    
- prolonged periods of lying down;
    
- reduced interest in play;
    
- and difficulty maintaining her usual level of activity.
    

Nora remained conscious and responsive.

However, she increasingly preferred to remain in bed.

She did not possess sufficient energy for her normal routine.

Her usual response to being told to rest would ordinarily involve attempting to negotiate.

During this episode, she simply accepted the instruction.

This was considered significant.

---

# 3. THE SLEEP PROBLEM

The illness alone did not explain the continued lack of sleep.

Nora repeatedly attempted to rest.

She closed her eyes.

She remained still.

She remained in bed for hours.

Sleep did not follow.

On several occasions she appeared to enter a state of near-sleep before abruptly waking.

The cause appears to be multifactorial.

Her nightmares had already been increasing before the illness.

Her brain appeared to associate sleep with the possibility of returning to the memories surrounding her death.

Consequently:

> **Nora wanted to sleep.**

But she was afraid of what happened when she did.

This created a particularly damaging cycle.

**Exhaustion → attempted sleep → nightmare onset or fear of sleep → awakening → further exhaustion.**

The cycle repeated.

For approximately one week.

---

# 4. TEMPORARY BEDRIDDEN STATUS

Nora was placed under temporary bed-rest precautions.

This did not mean she was physically incapable of standing.

Rather, she lacked the stamina required for normal activity and was at risk of worsening her condition by repeatedly attempting to remain active.

For the duration of the episode:

- unnecessary movement was discouraged;
    
- training was suspended;
    
- recreational combat activity was suspended;
    
- extended walks were suspended;
    
- range activity was suspended;
    
- Tachyon Merch Store duties were suspended;
    
- prolonged social activity was discouraged;
    
- hydration was monitored;
    
- food intake was monitored;
    
- and sleep opportunities were continuously provided.
    

Nora's Night Stalker remained secured.

She did not object.

---

# 5. AETERNA'S ROLE

Aeterna remained with Nora for most of the episode.

She did not attempt to force sleep.

This was deliberate.

Aeterna understood that commanding Nora to sleep would not make her feel safer.

Instead, she remained nearby.

Sometimes she sat beside the bed.

Sometimes she carried Nora when she needed to be moved.

Sometimes she simply remained standing in the room.

When Nora woke from another failed attempt at sleep, Aeterna was usually still there.

One exchange was recorded during the fourth night.

Nora had been lying awake for several hours.

Her eyes were open.

Aeterna was sitting beside the bed.

> **NORA:**  
> "Are you tired?"

> **AETERNA:**  
> "No."

Nora stared at the ceiling.

> **NORA:**  
> "You've been here all night."

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Yes."

Pause.

> **NORA:**  
> "You should sleep."

> **AETERNA:**  
> "You should as well."

Nora looked toward her.

> **NORA:**  
> "...I can't."

Aeterna did not argue.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Then we will remain awake."

Nora slowly turned toward the wall.

A few seconds later:

> **NORA:**  
> "...Okay."

---

# 6. NERI

Neri remained involved despite her increasingly demanding chemistry work.

Her availability was limited.

However, she made repeated visits to Nora's room.

Neri did not attempt to force conversation.

She generally sat nearby.

Sometimes she brought something.

Sometimes she simply remained there.

During one visit, Nora was awake but visibly exhausted.

Neri sat beside the bed.

Nora looked at her.

> **NORA:**  
> "Big Sis?"

Neri nodded.

> **NORA:**  
> "I'm sick."

Neri nodded again.

> **NORA:**  
> "...I know."

Neri remained beside her.

After several minutes, Nora quietly asked:

> **NORA:**  
> "Are you going back to chemistry?"

Neri nodded.

Nora looked away.

> **NORA:**  
> "Okay."

Neri remained for another ten minutes before returning to her work.

No further conversation occurred.

---

# 7. THE NIGHTMARES

The nightmares did not stop simply because Nora was already awake.

She experienced intrusive fragments while resting.

Darkness.

Falling.

Cold.

The sensation of being unable to move.

The memory of being discarded.

And, most consistently:

**Neri's face.**

Nora has repeatedly identified this as the most emotionally significant component.

She does not appear primarily afraid of remembering that she died.

She appears afraid of remembering **Neri watching it happen.**

This distinction remains important.

Nora survived.

The event is over.

But her memory does not consistently behave as though it understands that distinction.

---

# 8. DAYTIME BEHAVIOR

By the fifth day, Nora's normal behavior had almost completely disappeared.

She did not ask to play.

She did not ask for her sword.

She did not request Sky.

She did not ask Aeterna to manifest cat ears.

She did not visit Alice.

She did not ask about the Tachyon store.

She did not loudly announce Neri's intelligence.

She did not demand cake.

She simply remained in bed.

At one point, Aeterna brought her a small piece of cake.

Nora looked at it.

Then closed her eyes.

> **NORA:**  
> "Later."

Aeterna removed the plate.

No pressure was applied.

---

# 9. HYDRATION

Fluid intake became another concern.

Nora occasionally drank when directly offered water.

At other times, she simply stared at the cup.

Aeterna adapted accordingly.

She would place the cup within reach.

Wait.

And occasionally remind Nora:

> **AETERNA:**  
> "A little."

Nora would take a few sips.

Sometimes that was all she could manage.

The objective was not to force normal consumption immediately.

It was to prevent further deterioration.

---

# 10. SEVENTH DAY

On the seventh day, Nora remained bedridden.

However, her physical illness had begun to improve.

She was still exhausted.

Still pale.

Still quiet.

Still unable to sleep normally.

But she was no longer deteriorating at the same rate.

Aeterna remained beside her.

Nora stared at the ceiling for several minutes.

Then:

> **NORA:**  
> "Aeterna?"

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Yes, my child."

> **NORA:**  
> "When I sleep..."

Aeterna waited.

> **NORA:**  
> "...will I see it again?"

Aeterna did not give her a false promise.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "You may."

Nora's expression tightened.

Aeterna continued.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "But you will wake here."

Nora looked at her.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Not there."

Pause.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Here."

Nora slowly nodded.

She closed her eyes.

She remained still.

For approximately eleven minutes.

Then opened them again.

She had not slept.

But she had tried.

---

# 11. CURRENT STATUS

Nora is no longer considered medically unstable.

However, she remains physically depleted.

Her temporary bedridden status will continue until sufficient recovery has occurred.

Return to normal activity will be gradual.

Personnel are specifically instructed not to interpret her eventual return to activity as evidence that the episode has been psychologically resolved.

The physical illness may end before the sleep disturbance does.

Likewise, improved sleep may occur before the nightmares disappear.

These processes should be monitored separately.

---

# C.O.R.E. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Nora's condition represents the convergence of several existing concerns.

She was already experiencing increasingly frequent nightmares.

Her daytime positivity had already begun declining.

Her appetite and hydration had become inconsistent.

She was increasingly tired.

Then she became physically ill.

The resulting week without meaningful sleep has significantly amplified all of these factors.

Nora is not currently being treated as though she is "misbehaving."

She is sick.

She is exhausted.

And she is frightened of sleeping.

Those are separate problems.

They require separate patience.

---

## Personnel Guidance

Do not tell Nora:

> "Just go to sleep."

Do not tell her:

> "You need to stop thinking about it."

Do not tell her:

> "The nightmare isn't real."

Instead:

> "You're here."

> "You're safe."

> "You can rest."

> "I'll stay nearby."

And, when appropriate:

> **"You don't have to fall asleep immediately."**

---

# FINAL OBSERVATION

For an entire week, Nora's usual world became very small.

Her bed.

Her room.

Aeterna.

Occasionally Neri.

A glass of water.

A little food.

And the ceiling.

The child who normally ran through the SAFEHOUSE, climbed onto Sky, played with Alice, shouted about how brilliant her sister was, and treated every quiet moment as an opportunity for another question was reduced to barely moving beneath a blanket.

Not because she had stopped being Nora.

Because she was exhausted.

Because she was sick.

Because every attempt to sleep carried the possibility of remembering the worst moment of her life.

And because, for seven days, her body desperately wanted something her mind was afraid to allow.

Sleep.

Aeterna remained beside her.

Neri continued visiting when she could.

The rest of SAFEHOUSE was instructed to give her space.

No demands.

No forced positivity.

No expectation that she should immediately become herself again.

For now, recovery means something considerably smaller.

Eat a little.

Drink a little.

Rest a little.

Try again tomorrow.

And if sleep comes—

let it come.
