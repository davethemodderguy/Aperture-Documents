## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

C.O.R.E. has reached a new and deeply inconvenient conclusion regarding Jake Akana.

The child is capable of dying under circumstances that would ordinarily be considered mundane, harmless, or insufficiently dangerous to warrant specific safety precautions.

The current working description, coined by Blacksite personnel, is:

**“Dumb Ways to Die: Jake Akana Edition.”**

The phrase began as a joke.

It is no longer entirely a joke.

Jake has demonstrated an extraordinary tendency to encounter lethal outcomes through ordinary daily activities. The exact mechanism varies, and the available evidence does not establish that every incident shares a single cause. What is consistent is the alarming frequency with which situations that should be uneventful become medical emergencies.

The latest example involved a cup of Phoenix's hot tea.

A cup of tea.

Not an anomalous chemical solution. Not an experimental compound. Not an unstable Etheric preparation. Not a weapon disguised as a household object.

Tea.

Phoenix's tea.

The incident has prompted renewed concern that Jake's resurrection ability is being mistaken for a universal safety net. It is not. Jake continues to experience death, and his reconstruction may activate independently or remain delayed until an external stimulus precedes the appearance of white fire.

Tom Akana has attempted to protect his younger brother through increased supervision since his return. These efforts have not prevented the recurring incidents.

C.O.R.E.'s official position is therefore as follows:

1. Jake's repeated deaths are serious emergencies, regardless of the apparent triviality of the triggering circumstance.
    
2. His ability to resurrect does not make everyday activities inherently safe.
    
3. No individual, including Tom, is expected to prevent every possible incident through constant vigilance.
    
4. The Blacksite must investigate the pattern without turning Jake's ordinary life into an endless sequence of restrictions, tests, and surveillance.
    

The tea incident is documented below.

Regrettably, it was not the first incident to justify this report.

It is simply the one that finally made someone ask whether Jake could be trusted around a cup.

## II. INCIDENT REPORT — THE CUP OF TEA

**INCIDENT DESIGNATION:** SH-096-A  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7  
**INVOLVED PERSONNEL:** Jake Akana, Tom Akana, Phoenix  
**OBJECT INVOLVED:** One cup of hot tea  
**INITIAL RISK ASSESSMENT:** Ordinary household beverage  
**FINAL RISK ASSESSMENT:** Under review, largely because of the person drinking it

### 2.1 — Before the Incident

Phoenix had prepared herself a cup of hot tea.

The preparation was routine. No anomalous additives were reported, no experimental ingredients were identified, and no evidence suggested that the tea had been deliberately altered.

Phoenix was tired.

This, by itself, was hardly unusual.

Tom was nearby, keeping Jake company. He had been attempting to give his little brother a relatively normal day, a task that had become increasingly difficult because Jake's definition of a normal day apparently included several events that required emergency medical attention.

Jake noticed the cup.

**JAKE:** “What's that?”

**PHOENIX:** “Tea.”

**JAKE:** “Can I have some?”

Phoenix looked at him.

**PHOENIX:** “It's hot.”

**JAKE:** “I know!”

**TOM:** “Do you?”

Jake turned toward his brother with the indignation of a child whose intelligence had just been questioned.

**JAKE:** “YES!”

**TOM:** “You asked me last week whether the sun was hot enough to cook a cake.”

**JAKE:** “IT WAS A SCIENCE QUESTION!”

**TOM:** “You put the cake outside.”

**JAKE:** “SCIENCE!”

Phoenix, apparently deciding that this conversation was not worth her limited energy, moved the cup farther from the edge of the table.

**PHOENIX:** “Let it cool first.”

**JAKE:** “Okay!”

Tom watched Jake carefully.

For a brief moment, everything appeared to be proceeding normally.

This was the moment at which the Blacksite should have been able to relax.

It was not.

### 2.2 — The Incident

The available witness accounts agree on the essential sequence: Jake interacted with Phoenix's tea, an unexpected lethal event followed, and Jake died.

The exact physical mechanism remains under investigation. The current evidence does not justify attributing the event solely to the tea's temperature, the cup, or any ordinary property of the beverage.

What can be confirmed is that a mundane household interaction resulted in another death.

Tom was the first to react.

**TOM:** “Jake?”

No answer.

Tom moved immediately to his brother's side.

**TOM:** “Jake. Hey!”

Phoenix put the cup down.

Her exhaustion disappeared beneath the immediate urgency of the situation.

**PHOENIX:** “What happened?”

**TOM:** “I don't know!”

Tom called for medical assistance.

The response was rapid. Personnel initiated emergency procedures while Phoenix moved out of the way, visibly struggling to reconcile what she had just witnessed with the fact that the object involved had been her tea.

Tom remained near Jake until medical personnel directed him to give them room.

The white fire did not manifest immediately.

Tom's expression changed.

He had seen this before.

He knew what a delayed activation could mean, and he knew that the people present could not afford to assume the resurrection would happen on its own.

**TOM:** “Come on, Jake. Please.”

No response.

**TOM:** “Buddy, you can't die from tea.”

Phoenix looked at him.

**PHOENIX:** “Apparently, he can.”

**TOM:** “THAT IS NOT HELPING!”

**PHOENIX:** “I'm aware!”

The exchange ended when the medical team requested silence.

The emergency continued.

Eventually, white fire manifested and Jake's body began to reconstruct. His return was accompanied by the familiar complications that required continued medical attention.

Only after the reconstruction and immediate response had progressed sufficiently did the room begin to settle.

Jake was alive again.

Tom was shaking.

Phoenix was staring at the cup.

The tea had not moved.

### 2.3 — Post-Resurrection Conversation

Jake eventually became responsive.

He looked at Tom first.

**JAKE:** “...Tom?”

Tom leaned forward immediately.

**TOM:** “I'm here.”

**JAKE:** “What happened?”

Tom opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Looked at Phoenix.

Phoenix looked back.

Neither appeared to possess an explanation that would make the situation any less absurd.

**TOM:** “You died.”

Jake considered this.

**JAKE:** “Oh.”

**TOM:** “You died.”

**JAKE:** “I heard you the first time.”

**TOM:** “FROM TEA!”

Jake blinked.

**JAKE:** “...Tea?”

Phoenix lifted the cup slightly, then seemed to realize that this was not a useful contribution.

She set it down again.

**PHOENIX:** “Yes.”

Jake stared at it.

Then at Tom.

Then back at the cup.

**JAKE:** “But tea is for drinking.”

Tom looked at the ceiling.

**TOM:** “I KNOW WHAT TEA IS, JAKE.”

**JAKE:** “Then why did it kill me?”

Nobody answered.

Not because they did not want to.

Because they did not know.

Jake looked genuinely puzzled.

After a few seconds, he turned toward Phoenix.

**JAKE:** “Sorry about your tea.”

Phoenix stared at him.

**PHOENIX:** “You died, and you're apologizing about my tea?”

**JAKE:** “I think I ruined the moment.”

Phoenix closed her eyes.

Tom covered his face with both hands.

**PHOENIX:** “I need to sleep.”

**TOM:** “GET IN LINE.”

Jake, apparently interpreting this as a discussion about household scheduling, raised his hand slightly.

**JAKE:** “Can I have cake?”

Tom and Phoenix answered simultaneously.

**TOM:** “NO.”

**PHOENIX:** “NOT RIGHT NOW.”

Jake frowned.

**JAKE:** “Why?”

Tom slowly lowered his hands.

**TOM:** “Because I am going to need at least five minutes to understand how you turned a cup of tea into a medical incident.”

**JAKE:** “I didn't mean to!”

Tom's expression softened immediately.

**TOM:** “I know, little star.”

He reached over and took Jake's hand.

**TOM:** “I know.”

The absurdity of the incident did not make the fear any less real.

## III. THE GROWING LIST OF IMPOSSIBLE HAZARDS

The tea incident is not being treated as proof that hot beverages are universally lethal to Jake, nor that every ordinary object presents the same risk.

It is evidence that C.O.R.E.'s current understanding of the pattern remains incomplete.

Staff have begun informally referring to the phenomenon as the **Akana Mundane Lethality Problem**.

The name is not a diagnosis.

It is an attempt to describe the experience of watching Jake interact with ordinary life and discovering, repeatedly, that the outcome is anything but ordinary.

The following categories have been identified for continued review:

- **Ordinary household activities:** Situations normally considered routine have preceded fatal incidents.
    
- **Food and beverages:** The tea incident demonstrates that an ordinary eating or drinking context cannot automatically be assumed safe for Jake.
    
- **Unremarkable environmental interactions:** Personnel have reported that seemingly insignificant circumstances can precede unexpected emergencies.
    
- **Post-incident unpredictability:** Even after Jake has died, the timing of his resurrection remains uncertain. Some activations occur independently; others are delayed.
    
- **False reassurance following revival:** Jake's return to consciousness can make an incident appear resolved before his physical and psychological condition has been fully assessed.
    

C.O.R.E. will not produce a speculative list of every object that might kill Jake. Doing so would create the illusion that the hazard can be solved by identifying enough individual objects.

The issue is broader.

Jake's apparent vulnerability cannot currently be mapped onto a reliable list of causes.

A cup of tea is not inherently a lethal object.

For Jake, on this occasion, the interaction was lethal.

That distinction is essential.

## IV. INTERNAL MEMORANDUM — “DUMB WAYS TO DIE”

**AUTHOR:** Blacksite Residential Safety Review  
**DISTRIBUTION:** C.O.R.E. Executives, Medical Division, SAFEHOUSE Resident Liaison  
**PURPOSE:** Clarify informal terminology and prevent inappropriate handling of Jake's condition

The phrase _Dumb Ways to Die_ originated as dark humor among personnel who had witnessed the repeated pattern of unexpected deaths.

The phrase is understandable.

It is also dangerous if taken too literally.

Jake is not dying because he is stupid. He is a six-year-old child with a history of profound trauma, an anomalous reconstruction mechanism, and an unexplained tendency to experience fatal incidents under circumstances that should not ordinarily produce such outcomes.

His curiosity and impulsiveness may contribute to some incidents, but the available evidence does not establish that his behavior explains the entire pattern.

Calling him the Blacksite's “definition of Dumb Ways to Die” may be acceptable as private gallows humor among personnel who understand the gravity of the situation.

It is not acceptable as a justification for dismissing his deaths.

Nor should Jake be made to feel that he is a nuisance because his condition repeatedly frightens the people around him.

**C.O.R.E. Directive:** Humor may be used to relieve tension among staff, but it must never replace appropriate emergency response, respectful communication, or serious investigation.

Jake is not a punchline.

The circumstances surrounding him may be absurd.

The fear experienced by his family is not.

## V. INTERVIEW — TOM AKANA

**INTERVIEWER:** C.O.R.E. Residential Oversight Representative  
**SUBJECT:** Tom Akana  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7  
**NOTE:** Tom requested that the interview remain short so he could return to Jake.

**INTERVIEWER:** You were present during the tea incident.

**TOM:** Unfortunately.

**INTERVIEWER:** What did you observe?

**TOM:** Jake asked Phoenix for tea. Phoenix told him it was hot. I was watching him. Then he died.

**INTERVIEWER:** Did you identify a cause?

**TOM:** No.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you believe you could have prevented it?

Tom went quiet.

**TOM:** I was right there.

**INTERVIEWER:** That wasn't my question.

**TOM:** I know what you asked.

He rubbed his eyes.

**TOM:** I keep thinking that if I stay close enough, if I pay attention to everything, I'll catch something before it happens.

**INTERVIEWER:** Has that worked?

**TOM:** No.

The answer came without hesitation.

**TOM:** Sometimes I'm right beside him. Sometimes I'm watching him. Sometimes I'm telling him not to do something, and he listens. And then something else happens.

**INTERVIEWER:** What happens when Jake dies?

Tom's expression tightened.

**TOM:** I try to get him back.

**INTERVIEWER:** And when the white fire does not appear?

**TOM:** I call for help. I stay with him. If the medical team needs me to move, I move. Sometimes it starts on its own. Sometimes it starts after I touch him.

He looked down at his hands.

**TOM:** I hate waiting.

**INTERVIEWER:** Because you are afraid he will not return?

**TOM:** Because I already know what it feels like to lose him.

Tom stopped.

Then he corrected himself.

**TOM:** No. That's not right. I know what it feels like to think I've lost him. And every time the fire doesn't start, I have to sit there and wonder whether this is the time it won't.

The room remained quiet.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you think Jake understands how frightened you are?

**TOM:** He knows I get scared. He doesn't always understand why.

**INTERVIEWER:** Why do you think that is?

Tom glanced toward the door.

**TOM:** Because he comes back.

He paused.

**TOM:** He wakes up, and the worst part for him is over. He's breathing again. He can talk. He can ask for cake. He wants to know whether we can finish the game.

Tom's voice grew quieter.

**TOM:** But I remember the part where he wasn't there.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you feel that you have to prevent every incident?

**TOM:** I want to.

**INTERVIEWER:** That is different from having to.

Tom gave a tired, humorless smile.

**TOM:** Aries says that, too.

**INTERVIEWER:** Is he right?

**TOM:** Yes.

A long pause followed.

**TOM:** I just wish being right would make it easier.

The interviewer ended the session shortly afterward.

Tom returned to Jake.

He did not return to the tea.

Phoenix had already taken it away.

## VI. INTERVIEW — PHOENIX

**INTERVIEWER:** C.O.R.E. Residential Oversight Representative  
**SUBJECT:** Phoenix  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7  
**CONDITION:** Fatigued; cooperative

Phoenix sat with a replacement cup of tea in front of her.

She had not touched it.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you believe the tea itself caused Jake's death?

**PHOENIX:** I don't know.

**INTERVIEWER:** Was the beverage altered?

**PHOENIX:** Not intentionally.

**INTERVIEWER:** Did you observe anything unusual before the incident?

**PHOENIX:** Jake asked for tea. I told him it was hot. Tom was there. Then Jake died.

**INTERVIEWER:** How did you react?

Phoenix looked at the cup.

**PHOENIX:** I put the tea down.

**INTERVIEWER:** And afterward?

**PHOENIX:** I watched Tom try to get his little brother back.

She was quiet for several seconds.

**PHOENIX:** I have seen Jake die before. I know what his resurrection looks like. I know what it looks like when it doesn't happen immediately.

**INTERVIEWER:** Did the familiarity make the incident easier?

**PHOENIX:** No.

She answered so quickly that the interviewer did not immediately continue.

**PHOENIX:** Knowing what might happen is not the same as being comfortable when it happens.

She shifted in her chair.

**PHOENIX:** I was tired. I wanted a cup of tea. It was supposed to be an ordinary thing.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you feel responsible?

Phoenix looked directly at the interviewer.

**PHOENIX:** I didn't know the tea would be involved in another death. Neither did Tom. Neither did Jake.

A pause.

**PHOENIX:** If you want me to say that I should never have made tea, I'm not going to say that. If you want me to say we should investigate what happened, we should.

She glanced toward the door.

**PHOENIX:** And if you want me to say that Jake being alive again means it wasn't frightening, absolutely not.

**INTERVIEWER:** Understood.

**PHOENIX:** Good.

She picked up the cup.

Then put it down again.

**PHOENIX:** I'm going to drink this somewhere else.

## VII. MEDICAL DIRECTIVES — EVERY INCIDENT COUNTS

Effective immediately, all personnel interacting with Jake Akana must observe the following principles.

**1. Do not assume an ordinary situation is automatically safe simply because it would be safe for most people.** The tea incident establishes that ordinary context alone cannot rule out a lethal outcome for Jake.

**2. Do not assume every ordinary object is inherently dangerous.** No evidence currently supports a universal prohibition on household objects, foods, beverages, or routine activities. Restrictions must be evidence-based and proportionate.

**3. Treat every death as a medical emergency.** Jake's resurrection is not guaranteed to begin immediately. Staff must not wait for white fire before initiating appropriate emergency procedures.

**4. Do not intentionally test the resurrection mechanism.** No controlled deaths, deliberate injuries, or experimental stimuli are authorized to determine whether a particular trigger works.

**5. Do not make Tom solely responsible for Jake's safety.** Tom is Jake's older brother, not a one-person emergency department. Medical personnel and the wider SAFEHOUSE community must share responsibility.

**6. Assess Jake after every reconstruction.** His return to consciousness does not establish that the process was painless, harmless, or complete. Respiratory distress and other complications must be taken seriously.

**7. Preserve ordinary life wherever reasonably possible.** Jake should still be able to play, eat, spend time with his brothers, and participate in family life. A safety plan that leaves him afraid of every object and every interaction would create additional harm.

**8. Investigate patterns without blaming the child.** Any recurring association must be documented carefully. Correlation must not be presented as established causation.

## IX. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake Akana has become a recurring source of emergency medical incidents involving circumstances that ordinary safety procedures would not predict.

The tea incident is an especially clear illustration of the problem: an everyday object, an ordinary social interaction, and a familiar residential environment culminated in another death.

The exact cause remains unknown.

Tom continues to attempt to protect Jake, and those attempts continue to fail to prevent the next incident. The failures are not evidence that Tom is inattentive or that he has neglected his brother. They demonstrate that the current pattern cannot be controlled through one person's vigilance.

Jake's resurrection allows him to return.

It does not make dying safe.

The Blacksite may joke about “Dumb Ways to Die,” but C.O.R.E. must maintain a more serious understanding of what that phrase represents: an unpredictable pattern of recurrent deaths, a child whose ordinary activities can precede fatal outcomes, and a family that must repeatedly confront the possibility of losing him.

Tom is allowed to be Jake's big brother without being expected to prevent every catastrophe.

Phoenix is allowed to drink tea without being treated as though she has created a weapon.

Jake is allowed to remain a curious child without being blamed for an anomalous pattern that nobody fully understands.

And C.O.R.E. is required to keep investigating until it has a better answer than _“Apparently, he can.”_

## X. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — PLEASE LET THE BOY HAVE A NORMAL DAY

There was a time when we believed the greatest danger to Jake was the obvious one.

The extraordinary anomaly. The hostile entity. The dangerous experiment. The place marked with a warning sign.

Those things remain dangerous.

But Jake has developed a talent for turning the unremarkable into an emergency.

A few hours of peace can be interrupted without warning. A family game can become a medical response. A brother who has only just returned can find himself kneeling beside Jake again, asking him to wake up.

And now there is Phoenix's tea.

Phoenix wanted a drink.

Jake wanted to know what it was.

Tom was there.

For a little while, it was just another ordinary moment in SAFEHOUSE.

Then it wasn't.

We cannot tell Tom to watch harder and pretend that solves the problem. We cannot tell Jake to stop being curious and pretend that explains everything. We cannot ban every object that has ever been present when something went wrong.

We must do the difficult work of understanding the pattern without letting the pattern become Jake's entire identity.

He is not merely the boy who dies.

He is Aries's youngest brother.

He is Tom's little brother.

He is a child who argues about cake, invents absurd rules for board games, asks questions he cannot always answer, and expects his family to be there when he opens his eyes.

We owe him more than an ever-growing list of things he might die from.

We owe him a chance to live.

And, for the love of every functioning safety protocol in this facility, we owe Tom at least one afternoon in which he can sit with his little brother without having to wonder whether the next harmless object in the room is about to become an incident report.

**The tea has been removed from the table.**

**Jake remains under observation.**

**Tom has been ordered to rest.**

Tom has appealed the order.

Aries has supported the order.

Jake has asked whether the tea can be replaced with cake.

C.O.R.E. has declined to answer that question until Medical finishes its assessment.
