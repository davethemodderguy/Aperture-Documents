# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. — CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE WELFARE & PSYCHOLOGICAL SUPPORT DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** CORE-SH-INC-173  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — RESIDENTIAL CONFLICT / MEMORY DISORDER  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE — SUBJECT WITHDRAWN  
**SUBJECT:** Sora's Withdrawal Following Conflict with Mia  
**RELATED PERSONNEL:** Sora, Mia, Jake Akana, Tom Akana, Bao  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — FLOOR 7  
**PRIORITY:** HIGH — PSYCHOLOGICAL WELFARE

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the severe argument documented in CORE-SH-INC-172, Sora ran away from Mia.

He eventually returned to his personal den on SAFEHOUSE Floor 7, where he has remained.

Mia, meanwhile, appears to have forgotten the argument.

Due to her own memory difficulties, she no longer remembers what happened between them. She does not recall the words she said, the escalation of the confrontation, or the emotional consequences that followed.

The only thing she currently understands is that Sora is no longer around her.

He is in his den.

This has created an especially difficult situation.

Sora remembers being hurt.

Mia does not remember hurting him.

From Mia's perspective, her older brother has suddenly become distant. From Sora's perspective, the person he trusted has hurt him in a way he cannot simply disregard.

Neither child currently has the same understanding of what happened.

C.O.R.E. considers this a significant welfare concern for both.

---

## 2. INCIDENT AFTERMATH — SORA RUNS

Following the argument, Sora left the immediate area.

He did not attempt to continue the disagreement or confront Mia again.

Instead, he ran.

He eventually reached his personal den, the small doghouse-like room built into the wall of Floor 7.

He entered and remained there.

Staff observed no indication that he intended to leave immediately.

His withdrawal is particularly concerning given his history.

Sora has previously used isolation as a response to overwhelming fear and emotional distress. His den has served as a place of security, especially during periods when the outside environment felt unpredictable.

However, staff must distinguish between healthy solitude and distress-driven isolation.

At present, Sora's behavior appears more consistent with the latter.

The den is where he feels safe.

It is also where he can avoid seeing Mia.

---

## 3. MIA — THE MEMORY GAP

Mia's memory difficulties have complicated the situation considerably.

At some point following the confrontation, she forgot what had happened.

She no longer appeared to remember the argument or her own involvement in it.

When asked about Sora, she expressed confusion.

**MIA:** Where's Sora?

**STAFF MEMBER:** He's in his den.

**MIA:** Why?

**STAFF MEMBER:** He's taking some time alone.

Mia looked toward the hallway.

**MIA:** Did I do something?

The staff member paused.

**STAFF MEMBER:** You and Sora had an argument.

Mia frowned.

**MIA:** We did?

**STAFF MEMBER:** Yes.

**MIA:** About what?

The staff member did not immediately answer.

**MIA:** Is he mad at me?

No definitive answer was given.

Mia looked increasingly confused.

**MIA:** He doesn't usually stay in there this long.

She turned toward Sora's den.

**MIA:** I want to see him.

The staff member advised her to give him some time.

Mia did not appear to understand why.

### Assessment

Mia's lack of recollection does not establish that she no longer cares about Sora.

She is aware of his absence and wants to see him.

However, she currently lacks the memory needed to understand why he is avoiding her.

Her confusion is genuine, but it does not undo the harm caused by the earlier confrontation.

---

## 4. INCIDENT — THE CLOSED DEN

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7  
**Time:** 18:34

Mia eventually approached Sora's den.

She stopped outside.

**MIA:** Sora?

There was no response.

She crouched slightly to look inside.

Sora was present.

He was sitting inside the den, facing away from the entrance.

**MIA:** It's me.

Sora did not turn around.

**MIA:** Can I come in?

No response.

Mia waited.

**MIA:** Sora?

He shifted slightly, but did not look at her.

Mia's ears lowered beneath her hair.

**MIA:** Are you upset?

Sora remained silent.

**MIA:** Did I make you upset?

There was a long pause.

Sora finally looked toward the entrance.

His expression was difficult to read, but he did not appear happy to see her.

Mia hesitated.

**MIA:** I'm sorry?

Sora looked away again.

Mia remained outside for a little longer before eventually leaving.

She did not understand what had happened.

Sora did.

---

## 5. OBSERVATION — TWO DIFFERENT REALITIES

The incident has produced an unusual emotional imbalance.

|Sora|Mia|
|---|---|
|Remembers the argument.|Does not remember the argument.|
|Remembers what Mia said.|Does not remember what she said.|
|Understands why he is hurt.|Does not understand why Sora is avoiding her.|
|Has withdrawn into his den.|Wants to see Sora.|
|Is not ready to interact with her.|Does not understand why interaction is being refused.|

This disparity creates a risk of further distress.

If Mia repeatedly approaches Sora without understanding his boundaries, he may feel pressured or cornered.

If Sora's withdrawal is treated as an ordinary memory lapse, staff may mistakenly assume that reminding him of Mia's identity will resolve the problem.

It will not.

**Sora has not forgotten who Mia is. He remembers exactly who she is, and that is part of why he is hurting.**

---

## 6. REQUIRED WELFARE MEASURES

### A. Respect Sora's den as a safe space

No one is to enter without permission unless an immediate safety emergency requires intervention.

Mia must not be encouraged to repeatedly approach him in an attempt to force reconciliation.

### B. Do not erase the incident because Mia forgot it

Staff must retain an accurate account of the argument.

Mia's memory loss does not mean the event did not happen.

Sora's experience must not be dismissed simply because the other participant cannot remember it.

### C. Support Mia without concealing the truth indefinitely

Mia should receive an age-appropriate explanation when appropriate, with care taken not to overwhelm her.

Staff must not deliberately shame her or present her as irredeemable.

However, neither should she be told that nothing happened merely to avoid an uncomfortable conversation.

### D. Allow Sora to decide when he is ready

Sora must not be pressured into accepting an apology, speaking with Mia, or returning to their previous routine.

### E. Monitor both children

Sora's isolation and Mia's confusion require separate welfare observations.

Jake and Tom may offer companionship, but they must not be made responsible for mediating the conflict.

---

## 7. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT — THE ONE WHO REMEMBERS

There is something particularly painful about this situation.

Mia has forgotten the argument.

She has forgotten the anger, the words, and the moment when she hurt her brother.

For her, the most visible consequence is that Sora is no longer beside her.

She may genuinely wonder why.

Sora, however, has not been granted the same relief.

He remembers.

He remembers what she said. He remembers how it made him feel. And he remembers that the person who said those things was the little sister he had spent so long believing he had lost.

Her memory loss does not automatically restore his sense of safety.

It also does not make him responsible for explaining everything to her.

The adults must carry that responsibility.

---

## 8. CLOSING NOTE — THE DEN IS STILL THERE

Before the argument, Mia and Sora were rarely seen apart.

They ate together.

They played together.

They sat beside one another, sometimes with Jake joining them.

Mia called herself his little sister, and Sora accepted it without hesitation.

Now Mia stands outside a little den, asking her brother to come out.

And Sora stays inside.

She cannot remember why.

He cannot forget.

Perhaps Mia will eventually understand what happened. Perhaps Sora will eventually feel safe enough to leave his den and speak to her again.

But neither outcome can be demanded.

Mia deserves help understanding the consequences of her actions, even when her memory cannot preserve them.

Sora deserves to have his pain acknowledged, even when the person who caused it cannot remember.

And the little den must remain a place where Sora can feel safe, not a place where everyone waits for him to forgive someone.

**Sometimes, the person who hurt you forgets the argument. That does not mean you forget the hurt.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
