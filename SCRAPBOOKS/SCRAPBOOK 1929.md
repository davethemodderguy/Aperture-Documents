## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sora's memory deterioration has progressed to a point where he can no longer reliably recognize the people around him.

His existing face blindness remains severe. Faces appear to him as black scribbles, preventing meaningful visual identification.

However, a further complication has been documented.

**Sora can no longer reliably recognize familiar voices.**

He cannot consistently identify Mia, Jake, Tom, or other people he previously trusted by listening to them speak. Additionally, his memories of what those people looked like have deteriorated.

The result is a child who has lost almost every ordinary means of identifying someone familiar.

He cannot recognize a face.

He cannot reliably recognize a voice.

He cannot confidently recall the appearance of someone he loves.

This vulnerability has been exploited by several SAFEHOUSE employees.

Employees approached Sora and impersonated people he trusted, using phrases such as:

**"Hey, it's me, Mia. I have something for you."**

Sora believed them.

He came out of his hiding place expecting to meet someone familiar. Instead, employees pranked him, laughed at his confusion, and made fun of him.

The incidents were not harmless jokes.

They deliberately exploited a neurological disability in a six-year-old child who had already experienced severe memory deterioration, social isolation, and repeated abandonment.

A second consequence has also emerged.

Sora has forgotten how to return to his own den.

Unable to identify the correct hallway or remember the route to his room, he has begun sleeping on the floors of SAFEHOUSE corridors.

He is not choosing communal sleeping arrangements.

He is becoming lost inside his own residence.

C.O.R.E. classifies these incidents as serious welfare violations and deliberate exploitation of a vulnerable child.

## 2. NEUROLOGICAL STATUS — LOSS OF RECOGNITION

Sora's ability to recognize familiar individuals has deteriorated beyond the previously documented face blindness.

The following limitations are now observed:

|Function|Current condition|
|---|---|
|Facial recognition|Severely impaired; faces appear as black scribbles.|
|Voice recognition|Cannot reliably identify familiar voices.|
|Visual memory|Increasingly unable to recall the appearance of trusted individuals.|
|Relationship memory|Cannot consistently remember who people are to him.|
|Personal history|Previously documented severe deterioration.|
|Spatial memory|Unable to reliably remember the route to his own den.|

These impairments compound one another.

Previously, an individual could approach Sora and identify themselves verbally. Even if he could not recognize their face, he might have recognized their voice.

That safeguard is no longer dependable.

Similarly, showing Sora a photograph or describing someone's appearance may not help. He may not remember what the person looked like in the first place.

Even Mia, the only person who previously retained Sora's history during his disappearance, cannot assume that he will recognize her.

**Familiarity is no longer something Sora can independently verify.**

Staff must understand that this is not stubbornness, inattentiveness, or deliberate rejection.

He genuinely cannot reliably identify the people speaking to him.

## 3. INCIDENT A — "HEY, IT'S ME, MIA"

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE — Floor 7 residential corridor  
**Time:** 14:32  
**Classification:** Deliberate exploitation of neurological impairment

Several employees encountered Sora near the residential hallway.

He was standing alone, apparently unsure where he was supposed to go.

One employee approached him.

**EMPLOYEE:** Hey!

Sora turned toward the voice.

**EMPLOYEE:** It's me, Mia. I have something for you.

Sora hesitated.

**SORA:** ...Mia?

**EMPLOYEE:** Yeah! Come here.

Sora slowly approached.

He appeared uncertain but did not flee.

**SORA:** Have... something?

**EMPLOYEE:** Yep. Come closer.

Sora followed.

The employee then revealed that the statement was a prank. Other employees began laughing.

**EMPLOYEE 2:** He actually believed you!

**EMPLOYEE 3:** He doesn't even know what Mia sounds like anymore!

Sora stopped moving.

**SORA:** ...Not Mia?

The employees continued laughing.

**EMPLOYEE:** No, silly. It's me!

Sora looked at the speaker.

His expression reportedly became confused.

**SORA:** Who?

This prompted further laughter.

**EMPLOYEE 2:** He doesn't know!

**EMPLOYEE 3:** Ask him who we are!

Sora lowered his head.

**SORA:** I...

He could not finish.

The employees eventually left him in the corridor.

No one immediately escorted him back to his den.

### Assessment

The employee deliberately impersonated Mia to attract Sora's attention.

This was not an accidental misunderstanding.

The prank depended entirely on Sora's inability to recognize familiar voices and his continued willingness to trust someone who claimed to be familiar.

The resulting humiliation was foreseeable.

Sora approached because he believed someone he trusted wanted to see him.

Instead, he became the subject of entertainment.

**C.O.R.E. FINDING:** The employees exploited a known neurological disability for amusement.

No mitigating interpretation is accepted.

## 4. INCIDENT B — THE PEOPLE HE CAN NO LONGER PICTURE

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE — Floor 7 common area  
**Time:** 16:08

A separate observation recorded Sora attempting to describe people he once knew.

He was sitting near the wall, speaking quietly.

**SORA:** Mia...

A pause.

**SORA:** Mia looks...

He stopped.

**SORA:** White?

Another pause.

**SORA:** I think.

He looked down at his hands.

**SORA:** What does Mia look like?

No one answered.

He continued speaking.

**SORA:** Jake...

**SORA:** Jake has...

He appeared to struggle.

**SORA:** Wings?

He touched the side of his head.

**SORA:** White?

He paused again.

**SORA:** I don't know.

He then attempted to recall Tom.

**SORA:** Tom...

**SORA:** Voice...

He stopped.

**SORA:** Can't hear it.

The statement was not a reference to literal hearing loss. Sora was attempting to recall the sound of Tom's voice and could not reliably reconstruct it.

He eventually returned to repeating names.

**SORA:** Mia.

**SORA:** Jake.

**SORA:** Tom.

**SORA:** Bao.

A long silence followed.

**SORA:** What do they look like?

No response was recorded.

### Assessment

Sora is losing more than the ability to recognize people in front of him.

He is also losing the mental representations that would allow him to remember them when they are absent.

This creates a particularly distressing situation.

Even when he is alone, he may be unable to picture the people he wants to remember.

The names may remain accessible while the associated faces, voices, and relationships do not.

Staff must not interpret his inability to describe someone as evidence that the person was unimportant to him.

## 5. INCIDENT C — THE LOST WAY HOME

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE — Floor 7 residential hallways  
**Time:** 22:41

Sora was discovered sleeping on the hallway floor.

He was lying near a wall, curled up with his tail close to his body.

A passing resident initially believed he had fallen asleep while waiting for someone.

Upon closer observation, it became apparent that Sora had arranged himself as though the hallway were a place to sleep.

He was not waiting.

He had been unable to find his den.

### Recorded interaction

**STAFF MEMBER:** Sora?

Sora stirred.

**SORA:** Hm...

**STAFF MEMBER:** What are you doing out here?

**SORA:** Sleeping.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Why aren't you in your den?

Sora looked toward the corridor.

**SORA:** Where?

**STAFF MEMBER:** Your room. Your little den.

Sora remained silent.

**SORA:** Don't know.

**STAFF MEMBER:** You don't remember where it is?

Sora shook his head.

**SORA:** Lost.

**STAFF MEMBER:** How long have you been here?

**SORA:** Don't know.

The staff member crouched beside him.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Do you want me to help you find it?

Sora looked toward the speaker.

**SORA:** Who?

The staff member identified themselves.

Sora did not appear to recognize the name.

**SORA:** Okay...

He remained on the floor.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Come on. Let's go.

Sora hesitated.

**SORA:** Where?

**STAFF MEMBER:** Home.

Sora looked down the hallway.

**SORA:** This... home?

The staff member did not immediately answer.

### Assessment

Sora's inability to return to his den represents a significant deterioration in his spatial memory.

His den is a familiar structure built specifically to resemble the doghouse from his childhood.

It was intended to provide security, familiarity, and a place he could identify as his own.

Now he cannot reliably remember where it is.

The hallway floor has become an improvised sleeping location because it is the place where he happens to be when he becomes tired.

This is not an acceptable long-term arrangement.

Sora must not be expected to navigate the SAFEHOUSE independently until his ability to recognize routes and locations has been properly assessed.

He does not consistently return to the same place.

He may fall asleep near a wall, beside a doorway, or in an otherwise unused section of the corridor.

When asked where he lives, he may not be able to provide a meaningful answer.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Where do you live, Sora?

**STAFF MEMBER:** Which room?

**SORA:** ...

**STAFF MEMBER:** Do you remember your den?

**SORA:** Dog...

Sora listened.

**SORA:** Don't know.

Sora's den remains his assigned residence.

However, its existence does not mean he can independently locate or access it.

A room cannot function as a safe place if its occupant cannot remember how to reach it.

## 7. PERSONNEL MISCONDUCT — FORMAL INVESTIGATION

The employees responsible for impersonating Mia and mocking Sora are subject to formal investigation.

The following conduct is under review:

- Deliberately impersonating trusted individuals.
    
- Exploiting Sora's inability to recognize familiar voices.
    
- Encouraging Sora to approach under false pretenses.
    
- Laughing at and humiliating a neurologically impaired child.
    
- Leaving Sora distressed and confused after the incident.
    
- Contributing to an environment in which Sora cannot safely distinguish genuine assistance from deliberate deception.
    

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Sora's willingness to approach someone is not permission to deceive him.

His inability to recognize the speaker does not make the interaction a joke.

His confusion is not entertainment.

His trust is not something employees are permitted to exploit.

**Immediate directive:** Any employee found to have participated in these incidents is to be removed from unsupervised contact with Sora pending investigation and disciplinary review.

Additional measures:

1. No employee may impersonate Mia, Jake, Tom, Bao, Guiding Light, or any other person to attract Sora's attention.
    
2. No employee may use false promises, fabricated gifts, or deceptive familiarity to make him approach.
    
3. Staff must identify themselves consistently and provide simple explanations of their intentions.
    
4. Sora must never be mocked, quizzed, or deliberately confused regarding his recognition difficulties.
    
5. All incidents must be reported to SAFEHOUSE Welfare Oversight.

## 8. REQUIRED INTERVENTION

C.O.R.E. is issuing the following immediate welfare measures.

### A. Guided navigation

Sora must receive assistance when moving between his den and shared facilities.

A consistent, simple route should be maintained.

Staff must not assume that he remembers directions given previously.

### B. Den identification

His den should be made easier to identify through consistent visual and tactile landmarks that do not rely on facial recognition or memory of spoken instructions.

The arrangement must be reviewed with Sora rather than imposed without explanation.

### C. Safe identification protocol

Staff must not rely on voices alone to establish familiarity.

Every interaction should include a clear introduction and an explanation of why the individual is approaching him.

Sora must never be pressured to identify the speaker.

### D. Hallway sleeping

Sora is not to be left sleeping on corridor floors without welfare assessment.

If he falls asleep in a hallway, staff must check his condition and arrange an appropriate, supervised resting location.

He must not be reprimanded for failing to find his den.

### E. Emotional protection

Mia, Jake, and Tom may spend time with Sora, but none of them is responsible for correcting his memory or ensuring that he recognizes them.

If Sora does not recognize them, they must not take it personally or pressure him to remember.

### F. Medical reassessment

His worsening recognition and spatial-memory difficulties require renewed neurological assessment.

C.O.R.E. makes no claim that these symptoms are reversible.

His previous concussion and existing physical vulnerabilities must remain part of the medical record.

## 9. CLOSING NOTE — HE DOESN'T KNOW WHO IS CALLING

There used to be a time when Sora could recognize Mia.

He knew her as his little sister.

He knew Jake as the boy who gave him a whiteboard.

He knew Tom.

He knew Bao.

He knew the people who made him feel safe.

Then he forgot their faces.

After that, he forgot their voices.

Now, even the memories of what they looked like are slipping away.

And some employees decided to make a game out of it.

They called him Mia.

They told him they had something for him.

And Sora came.

Of course he came.

He was a little boy who wanted to believe someone familiar was waiting for him.

He couldn't see their faces.

He couldn't recognize their voices.

He had no reliable way to know they were lying.

And when he discovered that they were not Mia, they laughed.

There is something deeply wrong with an institution where a child can be tricked into approaching someone he trusts, only to become the punchline.

But the hallway incident is perhaps even more difficult to read.

Sora has forgotten how to get home.

His little doghouse is still there. The entrance is still built into the wall. His belongings are still associated with that space.

But he cannot remember where it is.

So he sleeps in hallways.

A little white dog boy, curled up against a wall, sleeping wherever exhaustion catches him.

Perhaps he wakes up and doesn't know where he is.

Perhaps he looks down the corridor and cannot remember which direction to go.

Perhaps he wants to return to his den but cannot remember what it looks like.

And perhaps he doesn't even remember that there is somewhere he is supposed to return to.

C.O.R.E. has documented his deterioration repeatedly.

Documentation is not care.

A report cannot walk him home.

A disciplinary notice cannot make him recognize Mia.

A familiar voice cannot guarantee that he knows who is speaking.

We must stop expecting Sora to navigate a world that his memory can no longer reliably explain.

And we must stop allowing people to make that world more frightening for him.

**He has forgotten the faces of the people who love him.**

**That does not give anyone permission to make him afraid of the voices that remain.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
