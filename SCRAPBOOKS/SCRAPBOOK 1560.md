**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — JAKE'S FLOOR

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake spent **seven consecutive days alone** on his residential floor.

No resident visited.

No member of the Obol Squad visited.

No one sat with him.

No one brought him food.

No one knocked on his door.

No one asked him to come outside.

This was not intentional abandonment.

It was the result of an unusually demanding period across SAFEHOUSE and its associated organizations.

For the duration of the observation period, residents were occupied with:

- Blacksite operations;
    
- medical responsibilities;
    
- C.O.R.E. research;
    
- KCA duties;
    
- CED deployments;
    
- L.S.I.N. assignments;
    
- external relations;
    
- engineering projects;
    
- and other ongoing obligations.
    

The current expectation is that these commitments will continue **until further notice.**

Consequently, Jake's social contact decreased to effectively zero.

His floor remained quiet.

---

# 1. DAY ONE

Jake's first day alone did not appear unusual.

He woke on his old mattress.

His teddy bear was beside him.

The grass covering the floor remained undisturbed.

Several sketched flowers moved gently despite the absence of detectable airflow.

Jake ate breakfast.

He read.

He drew.

He spent several hours sitting near the window.

No one visited.

Jake did not appear concerned.

At approximately 19:00, he looked toward the hallway.

Then returned to his book.

---

# 2. DAY TWO

No visitors.

Jake continued his normal routine.

He spent considerable time drawing.

Several new flowers appeared throughout the room.

One drawing depicted a small plush animal.

The drawing later manifested into a physical plushie.

Jake placed it beside the others.

He ate alone.

He slept alone.

No unusual behavior was observed.

---

# 3. DAY THREE

No visitors.

Jake spent most of the day inside his room.

He occasionally walked through the empty floor.

There was no reason for him to leave.

There was nowhere he needed to go.

He eventually sat in the middle of the grass.

His teddy bear rested against his side.

He looked toward the doorway.

Nothing happened.

After approximately thirty seconds, he quietly said:

> **JAKE:**  
> "They're busy."

He then returned to drawing.

---

# 4. DAY FOUR

No visitors.

The lack of social contact was now becoming noticeable.

Not because Jake complained.

He did not.

There were no messages.

No requests for company.

No attempts to contact anyone.

Jake continued eating.

He continued reading.

He continued drawing.

His room became increasingly cluttered.

More plushies appeared.

More flowers appeared.

One section of the floor became almost completely covered by grass.

Jake did not clean it.

There was no need.

---

# 5. DAY FIVE

No visitors.

Jake spent approximately four hours lying on his mattress.

He was awake.

His teddy bear was held against his chest.

The glow from his eyes was faint.

No medical emergency was detected.

He eventually sat up.

He looked at the flowers.

One of them moved slightly.

Jake reached down and touched it.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Hi."

There was, of course, no response.

He smiled anyway.

It lasted approximately two seconds.

Then disappeared.

---

# 6. DAY SIX

No visitors.

Jake began drawing more frequently.

His drawings increasingly depicted groups of people.

Six figures appeared repeatedly.

They were not identified by name.

Some were holding food.

Some were sitting.

One was carrying a plushie.

Another appeared to be sleeping.

The drawings were not considered distressing.

They appeared to represent memories.

Or perhaps expectations.

Jake continued producing them.

None of the figures manifested.

---

# 7. DAY SEVEN

No visitors.

The seventh day was the quietest.

Jake woke.

Ate.

Read.

Drew.

Sat in the grass.

Slept.

There were no conversations.

No laughter.

No footsteps outside his room.

No voices from the hallway.

No knock on the door.

No one calling his name.

The floor remained completely still.

At approximately 21:30, Jake sat on his mattress with his teddy bear.

He looked toward the doorway.

He waited.

Nothing happened.

Jake eventually lowered his head.

---

# 8. WELFARE OBSERVATION

There is currently no evidence that Jake has been physically neglected.

He has:

- continued eating;
    
- continued drinking;
    
- continued sleeping;
    
- continued maintaining his basic routine;
    
- remained physically responsive;
    
- retained his anomalous abilities;
    
- and demonstrated no immediate medical crisis.
    

However, the absence of social contact remains significant.

Jake's previous psychological history makes prolonged isolation particularly relevant.

He has already experienced a period in which everyone he trusted effectively disappeared from his life.

That event was not voluntary.

This situation is different.

Everyone still remembers him.

Everyone still cares about him.

Everyone is simply elsewhere.

Jake does not necessarily know that distinction emotionally.

---

# 9. NO ONE IS AT FAULT

C.O.R.E. has determined that no individual resident should be assigned responsibility for the current situation.

Everyone is genuinely occupied.

The current workload is unusually high.

Residents cannot abandon critical assignments simply because Jake is alone.

Likewise, Jake cannot reasonably be expected to understand every external obligation occurring around him.

The result is therefore not intentional neglect.

It is simply:

> **Everyone is busy.**

And Jake is six years old.

---

# 10. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake has not complained.

He has not demanded attention.

He has not asked anyone to come.

He has not sent messages.

He has not attempted to leave his floor.

He has simply continued existing.

This is consistent with his established tendency to avoid becoming a burden.

The absence of a request should therefore not be interpreted as evidence that Jake does not want company.

He may simply believe that everyone has more important things to do.

---

# 11. THE EMPTY FLOOR

At the end of the seventh day, C.O.R.E. reviewed the residential floor.

The result was almost unsettlingly ordinary.

Jake's door was open.

Grass covered much of the floor.

Sketched flowers grew between the blades.

Plushies were scattered across the old mattress.

Several books were lying open.

A drawing remained unfinished on the floor.

Jake's teddy bear was beside him.

Jake was sitting quietly in the grass.

He was not crying.

He was not smiling.

He was simply there.

Alone.

No residents were present.

No one was coming upstairs.

Everyone was still busy.

And, for the foreseeable future, that remained the situation.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There are no dramatic incidents to report.

No containment breach.

No anomalous attack.

No medical emergency.

No argument.

No disappearance.

Just seven days.

Seven quiet days.

Seven days in which Jake's entire floor remained almost completely empty.

The flowers continued growing.

The grass continued moving.

The plushies remained where he left them.

His drawings continued accumulating.

And Jake continued waiting without ever admitting that he was waiting.

The workload across SAFEHOUSE and C.O.R.E. remains unresolved.

Everyone is busy **until further notice.**

Jake understands that.

At least, he tells himself he does.

He sits down on the old mattress.

His teddy bear rests against his side.

He looks around the empty room.

Then toward the hallway.

Nothing.

He quietly picks up his pencil.

The floor remains silent.

The flowers sway.

Jake begins drawing again.

And tonight, just like yesterday,

**he is alone.**

**— C.O.R.E. Medical & Resident Welfare Division**
