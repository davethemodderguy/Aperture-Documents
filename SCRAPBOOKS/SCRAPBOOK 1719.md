# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The fifteen specialist contractors responsible for the unauthorized Blacksite raid have begun cooperating with C.O.R.E. investigators.

Their testimony revealed the party responsible for financing and facilitating the operation:

**WOODLAND CORPORATION.**

The contractors had believed they were participating in a sanctioned recovery operation backed by legitimate authority. Instead, Woodland Corporation had manipulated the arrangement, concealed critical information, and abandoned the contractors once the raid failed.

During the interrogation, Bao questioned the detained personnel while Jake remained nearby.

Jake had brought his teddy bear.

He had also brought the modified flashbang device used during the original incident.

C.O.R.E. personnel permitted Jake to demonstrate his anomalous sensory-disruption effect under controlled conditions. Bao retained responsibility for questioning the contractors.

The result was an interrogation that produced five incapacitated contractors before the remaining personnel began providing substantial information.

**No fatalities were recorded.**

C.O.R.E. has classified Woodland Corporation's involvement as a major breach of the existing relationship between the organizations.

---

# 1. THE INTERROGATION ROOM

Bao entered the secure room first.

The fifteen contractors had been medically cleared for questioning, although several remained visibly uncomfortable with the prospect of encountering Jake again.

Jake entered behind Bao.

He was carrying his teddy bear in one arm.

In the other hand was the anomalous device.

The contractors immediately recognized it.

One of them visibly recoiled.

> **CONTRACTOR 01:**  
> "You've got to be kidding me."

Jake looked at him.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Hi."

Nobody answered.

Bao placed the interrogation file on the table.

> **BAO:**  
> "We're going to have a conversation."

She looked at the contractors.

Then at Jake.

> **BAO:**  
> "And we're going to do this properly."

Jake nodded.

He sat down beside her.

---

# 2. THE FIRST QUESTIONS

Bao began with the operation's authorization.

Who had hired them?

Who had provided the intelligence?

Who had approved the target?

The contractors initially maintained that they had been acting under instructions from an intermediary representing Woodland Corporation.

They claimed they had been promised legal protection and substantial compensation.

They also claimed the operation was intended to secure Jake alive.

Bao listened without interrupting.

Then she asked the most important question.

> **BAO:**  
> "What were you told would happen if the operation failed?"

The team leader hesitated.

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "We were told extraction would be arranged."

> **BAO:**  
> "By whom?"

Silence.

Jake raised the device slightly.

Several contractors immediately began speaking at once.

Bao held up one hand.

> **BAO:**  
> "One at a time."

Jake lowered it.

---

# 3. FIVE DOWN

The contractors' cooperation was inconsistent.

Some answered questions.

Others attempted to coordinate their accounts.

Several insisted that their employer would intervene.

One repeatedly claimed that the Blacksite had no authority to detain them.

Bao remained patient.

Jake, less interested in the legal arguments, asked whether he could use his device.

Bao looked at him.

> **BAO:**  
> "Only if the medical and security teams have cleared the demonstration."

They had.

The effect was restricted to the established, non-lethal sensory-disruption phenomenon. Jake was not permitted to use the device on anyone who had not been medically cleared or to continue once a participant was incapacitated.

The first contractor volunteered nothing.

The second followed suit.

The third demanded legal counsel.

The fourth insisted that Woodland Corporation would never abandon its own people.

The fifth laughed nervously when Jake approached.

The demonstration began.

Within the ensuing sequence, **five contractors were incapacitated** by the anomalous effect. Their familiar kaleidoscopic vision, severe disorientation, and rotating star-shaped halos returned.

Medical personnel intervened immediately.

The demonstration stopped.

Jake put the device down.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Five."

Bao closed her eyes for a moment.

> **BAO:**  
> "Thank you, sweetheart."

She turned back to the remaining contractors.

> **BAO:**  
> "Now. Who wants to explain why Woodland Corporation left you here?"

The room became considerably quieter.

---

# 4. THE FIRST TESTIMONY

Contractor 08 spoke first.

> **CONTRACTOR 08:**  
> "They weren't going to extract us."

Bao looked up.

> **BAO:**  
> "Explain."

> **CONTRACTOR 08:**  
> "We received a contingency briefing. If we were compromised, Woodland would deny authorizing the operation."

> **BAO:**  
> "And the promised extraction?"

> **CONTRACTOR 08:**  
> "There wasn't one."

A second contractor immediately confirmed the statement.

The team leader attempted to interrupt.

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "You don't know that."

> **CONTRACTOR 08:**  
> "I saw the message."

He looked toward Bao.

> **CONTRACTOR 08:**  
> "They were already preparing to disavow us before we entered the building."

That statement changed the direction of the interrogation.

The contractors were no longer merely discussing a failed mission.

They were describing a deliberate betrayal.

---

# 5. WOODLAND CORPORATION'S INVOLVEMENT

The testimony established the following preliminary sequence:

1. Woodland Corporation's intermediaries approached the contractors with an offer.
    
2. The contractors were provided with intelligence concerning Jake and the Blacksite.
    
3. They were promised compensation for securing Jake alive.
    
4. They were assured that extraction and institutional protection would be available.
    
5. Woodland retained plausible deniability throughout the operation.
    
6. Following the contractors' capture, the promised support failed to materialize.
    

The contractors had believed they were expendable only in the event of an unforeseeable operational failure.

The evidence suggested something considerably worse.

Their expendability had been planned.

**Woodland Corporation had not merely abandoned the operation. It had arranged matters so that the people conducting it could be disowned.**

---

# 6. BAO'S RESPONSE

Bao did not raise her voice.

That was what made the next exchange particularly uncomfortable.

> **BAO:**  
> "You were paid to take a child."

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "We were paid to secure an anomalous individual."

Bao stared at him.

> **BAO:**  
> "His name is Jake."

The contractor said nothing.

> **BAO:**  
> "He's six."

Another pause.

> **BAO:**  
> "And the organization that hired you knew exactly what it was doing when it sent you here."

Jake sat beside Bao, quietly holding his teddy bear.

He appeared more interested in the rotating halo still visible above one of the recovering contractors than in the argument itself.

Bao noticed.

> **BAO:**  
> "Jake."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Hm?"

> **BAO:**  
> "Don't touch the device again unless I say so."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Okay."

He put it farther away.

The interrogation resumed.

---

# 7. THE REMAINING TEN

After the first five incapacitations and the initial testimony, the remaining contractors became substantially more cooperative.

Their accounts were compared against recovered communications, payment records, and the mission briefing.

C.O.R.E. investigators began separating confirmed facts from statements made under pressure.

The principal findings were:

- Woodland Corporation's intermediaries had facilitated the recruitment.
    
- The contractors had been given a specific target: Jake.
    
- The objective required the target to be captured alive.
    
- The operation had been designed to remain deniable.
    
- The promised extraction arrangements were not supported by the communications recovered so far.
    
- At least some contractors had been selected partly because they could be disavowed without exposing Woodland's leadership.
    

The contractors had expected a difficult operation.

They had not expected to be left behind by the organization that hired them.

---

# 8. THE CONTRACTOR LEADER BREAKS

The team leader remained silent longer than the others.

Eventually, Bao placed a recovered communication transcript in front of him.

It contained instructions concerning the treatment of captured personnel if the operation failed.

The leader read it.

His expression changed.

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "They knew."

> **BAO:**  
> "Yes."

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "They knew we might get caught."

> **BAO:**  
> "Yes."

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "And they were going to leave us."

Bao did not answer immediately.

> **BAO:**  
> "That's what the evidence indicates."

The leader lowered his head.

> **CONTRACTOR LEADER:**  
> "We were working for them."

> **BAO:**  
> "And they decided that you were worth less to them alive than the operation was worth keeping secret."

The room fell silent.

Jake looked at the leader.

For once, he did not say anything.

---

# 9. JAKE'S QUESTION

After the formal questioning paused, Jake approached Bao.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They weren't going to help them?"

Bao looked down at him.

> **BAO:**  
> "It doesn't look that way, sweetheart."

Jake looked at the contractors.

The five recovering personnel were receiving medical attention. The remaining ten sat under guard.

> **JAKE:**  
> "But they worked for them."

> **BAO:**  
> "I know."

Jake thought about this.

> **JAKE:**  
> "That's mean."

Bao gently rested a hand on his head.

> **BAO:**  
> "Yes, it is."

Jake picked up his teddy bear.

He did not ask to use the device again.

---

# 10. C.O.R.E. COUNTERINTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT

Woodland Corporation's involvement is considered a major security concern.

The attempted seizure of a SAFEHOUSE resident constitutes an unacceptable escalation.

The deliberate abandonment of the contractors suggests that Woodland's operation was designed to preserve the organization's interests regardless of the consequences to its own personnel.

C.O.R.E. is pursuing three parallel investigations:

**A. Operational responsibility** — identifying the individuals who authorized the raid.

**B. Financial responsibility** — tracing the bribes, intermediaries, and payment channels.

**C. Strategic responsibility** — determining whether the attempted seizure of Jake was an isolated operation or part of a broader Woodland initiative.

The existing relationship with Woodland Corporation is under formal review.

No conclusion regarding the organization's entire leadership will be made until the evidence has been examined.

However, the incident has established one fact beyond reasonable dispute:

**Someone within Woodland Corporation was willing to send fifteen specialists into the Blacksite to seize Jake—and leave them to face the consequences alone.**

---

# 11. MEDICAL AND WELFARE ADDENDUM

The five incapacitated contractors received medical evaluation and monitoring.

The anomalous sensory effects were treated as temporary, with continued observation required until each individual returned to baseline.

No further demonstrations were authorized.

Jake's participation was terminated for the remainder of the interrogation.

The following restrictions remain in effect:

- No repeated exposure to the sensory-disruption effect.
    
- No use of the device as a coercive instrument.
    
- No deployment against recovering or medically unstable individuals.
    
- No independent use by Jake during formal questioning.
    
- No further demonstrations without explicit medical and security approval.
    

Bao retains responsibility for resident welfare and may participate in the remaining interviews.

The contractors' testimony will be assessed against independent evidence rather than accepted solely because it was provided during interrogation.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Fifteen specialists entered the Blacksite.

They intended to take Jake.

They failed.

Then they discovered that the organization paying them had already prepared to deny their existence.

Five of them learned, rather unpleasantly, that Jake's blue dust could make an interrogation room feel like the inside of a broken kaleidoscope.

The remaining ten learned something considerably more important.

Woodland Corporation had promised them protection.

Woodland Corporation had promised them extraction.

Woodland Corporation had promised them that the operation was worth the risk.

But when the operation failed, the promised rescue never came.

Jake eventually left the interrogation wing with Bao.

He was carrying his teddy bear.

The device remained secured in C.O.R.E. custody.

As they reached the corridor, Jake looked up at Bao.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Are we going home?"

> **BAO:**  
> "Yes, sweetheart."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Can we get cake?"

Bao paused.

After a morning spent uncovering a corporate betrayal, fifteen failed mercenaries, and a potential hostile operation against the SAFEHOUSE, she decided that cake was a perfectly reasonable request.

> **BAO:**  
> "Of course."

Jake nodded.

The investigation continued without him.
