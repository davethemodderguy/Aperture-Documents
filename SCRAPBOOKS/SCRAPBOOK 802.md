# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### BEHAVIORAL THREAT ASSESSMENT

## ALICE — POST-SPAWN DETERMINATION

**Classification:** C.O.R.E. / RESTRICTED  
**Status:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**Subject:** Alice  
**Age:** 6 Years  
**Operational Authorization:** Perimeter Patrol / SPAWN Response  
**Threat Classification:** Anomalous Juvenile — HIGH HOSTILE RESPONSE POTENTIAL

---

# I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Alice has undergone another significant behavioral change following confirmation that SPAWN intends to attack the Heaven's Blacksite.

The change is not characterized by emotional maturity.

It is not characterized by detachment.

It is not characterized by conventional military discipline.

Alice is still a child.

In fact, personnel have described her current behavior as **"scarily childlike."**

She still speaks softly.

She still draws.

She still asks simple questions.

She still seeks affection from people she trusts.

She still allows Corin and Orphie to look after her.

She still carries a small pencil clipped to her coat.

The difference is what she now believes that pencil is for.

Alice has developed an extremely straightforward interpretation of the approaching conflict:

> **SPAWN wants to hurt her friends.**
> 
> **Therefore, SPAWN must be stopped.**

There is very little abstraction beyond this.

---

# II. THE CHANGE

Before confirmation of an impending SPAWN attack, Alice's hostility toward the organization was primarily defensive.

She would respond if SPAWN appeared.

She would patrol the perimeter.

She would identify threats.

She would defend herself and others if necessary.

Following the new intelligence, this has changed.

Alice now appears to be **anticipating** the attack.

She watches the perimeter more closely.

She asks when certain personnel will be present.

She pays attention to security patrol schedules.

She asks whether the forcefield is working.

She asks whether SPAWN has been seen.

She asks whether everyone is safe.

When told that the Blacksite is preparing for a possible attack, Alice reportedly responded:

> "...Okay."

The attending officer initially interpreted this as ordinary acknowledgment.

Alice then asked:

> "...When are they coming?"

---

# III. CHILDLIKE DETERMINATION

The most concerning characteristic is that Alice does not appear to conceptualize her intentions in the same way an adult combatant would.

She does not discuss tactics.

She does not talk about revenge.

She does not express hatred in conventional terms.

She does not boast about her weapon.

She does not speak about killing.

Instead, her reasoning remains remarkably simple.

When asked what she would do if SPAWN entered the Blacksite:

> "...Stop them."

When asked what she meant:

> "...They'll hurt people."

When asked what she would do if they continued:

Alice looked down at the pencil clipped to her coat.

> "...Then I'll make them stop."

The response was delivered without anger.

Without excitement.

Without hesitation.

---

# IV. THE AXE

Alice's battle axe has become an increasingly visible extension of this mindset.

The weapon remains disguised as a pencil.

It remains clipped to her small coat.

Alice does not carry it like a soldier carrying a rifle.

She carries it like a child carrying her favorite pencil.

This contrast is deeply unsettling.

The same small hand that uses the pencil to draw flowers and simple pictures can transform it into a weapon capable of cutting through one-meter Nekonium.

Alice does not appear to perceive a contradiction.

To her, both are simply things she carries.

---

# V. "ANYTHING THAT HURTS THEM"

The scope of Alice's protective instinct has also expanded.

Alice has begun referring to threats in broader terms.

During one observation, she was asked whether she was specifically angry at SPAWN.

Alice shook her head.

> "...No."

The interviewer asked:

> "Then what are you angry about?"

Alice considered the question.

> "...Things that hurt people."

A pause.

> "...My people."

The interviewer asked:

> "Who are your people?"

Alice began listing names.

Corin.

Orphie.

Belle.

Neko.

Miss Neko.

Niko.

Bao.

Frost.

Taiga.

Mart.

Astral.

Others followed.

She eventually stopped.

> "...Everyone here."

The statement appears genuine.

---

# VI. THE DISTURBING PART

Alice does not appear to understand the concept of proportionality in the same manner as an adult security professional.

Her current internal rule appears closer to:

> **Threat → Protect.**

There is little evidence of a developed intermediate process.

She does not appear interested in punishing SPAWN.

She does not appear interested in making them suffer.

She simply wants them unable to hurt anyone.

This distinction is important.

Alice's objective is not cruelty.

It is **removal of the threat.**

Unfortunately, her means of accomplishing this objective are extraordinarily effective.

---

# VII. CORIN — BIG SIS

Corin has become increasingly concerned.

She recognizes the behavior immediately.

Alice is not becoming emotionless.

Alice is not becoming evil.

Alice is becoming **protective.**

Extremely protective.

When Corin told Alice that she did not need to fight SPAWN alone, Alice responded:

> "...I know."

Corin then asked:

> "Then you won't?"

Alice was quiet.

> "...I'll stay with everyone."

Corin understood the answer.

Alice did not promise not to fight.

She promised not to leave.

Corin subsequently made it clear that Alice was not expected to put herself between every threat and every person she cared about.

Alice listened.

Whether she agreed was unclear.

---

# VIII. ORPHIE — BIGGER SIS

Orphie appears to have recognized the same problem.

Alice's determination is not rooted in hatred.

It is rooted in attachment.

That makes it considerably harder to address.

Alice does not want to be a soldier.

She wants her friends to be safe.

The weapon simply happens to give her the ability to enforce that desire.

Orphie reportedly asked:

> "Alice, what happens if someone hurts you?"

Alice answered:

> "...They shouldn't."

> "And if they do?"

Alice looked at her.

> "...Then you help me."

Orphie reportedly smiled slightly.

> "Exactly."

Alice nodded.

This interaction appears to have helped reinforce an important concept:

**Alice does not have to protect everyone by herself.**

---

# IX. SAFEHOUSE OBSERVATIONS

The SAFEHOUSE residents have noticed the change.

Belle has described Alice as "more serious."

Bao has become more attentive to her emotional state.

Neko has reportedly expressed concern that Alice is beginning to associate her weapon too closely with her responsibility to protect others.

Frost and Taiga have both noticed that Alice spends more time observing the perimeter.

Mart has noted that Alice can switch from completely ordinary childlike behavior to an unnervingly serious expression almost instantly when SPAWN is mentioned.

Astral has similarly observed that Alice remains emotionally attached to the people around her.

The consensus is broadly the same:

**Alice is still Alice.**

That is exactly what makes the change so noticeable.

---

# X. NORMAL ALICE

Outside of SPAWN-related situations, Alice continues to behave like a six-year-old.

She can still become distracted.

She can still get sleepy.

She can still quietly ask for food.

She can still sit beside someone without speaking.

She can still become embarrassed when praised.

She can still ask to be carried.

She still enjoys drawing.

She still likes having her head petted.

She still keeps the pencil clipped to her coat.

She is not constantly angry.

She is not constantly aggressive.

She is not constantly thinking about combat.

She is simply a child who has become convinced that the people she loves are in danger.

And when she believes someone is threatening them, the childish softness disappears.

---

# XI. C.O.R.E. CONCERN

C.O.R.E. does not currently recommend removing Alice from perimeter operations.

Doing so may be counterproductive.

Alice has demonstrated an unusual ability to identify SPAWN-related threats and possesses direct knowledge of their behavior.

However, personnel are advised to monitor her closely.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Increasing willingness to initiate violence.
    
- Loss of distinction between confirmed and suspected threats.
    
- Attempts to patrol alone.
    
- Attempts to leave the perimeter in pursuit of SPAWN.
    
- Refusal to retreat.
    
- Excessive concern for the safety of others.
    
- Self-sacrificial behavior.
    
- Emotional deterioration following engagements.
    

The primary concern is not that Alice will become uncontrollable.

The primary concern is that **Alice will decide that protecting everyone is her responsibility.**

---

# XII. CURRENT BEHAVIORAL PROFILE

### Alice remains:

**Childlike.**  
**Gentle.**  
**Quiet.**  
**Affectionate.**  
**Easily embarrassed.**  
**Emotionally attached.**

But now also:

**Extremely protective.**  
**Highly determined.**  
**Fearless toward confirmed SPAWN threats.**  
**Willing to use lethal force.**  
**Increasingly attentive to perimeter security.**

The contrast can occur within seconds.

A security officer may find Alice sitting on a bench drawing.

Then someone mentions SPAWN.

Alice stops drawing.

She looks up.

Her expression becomes completely serious.

> "...They're coming."

And the pencil remains clipped to her coat.

---

# XIII. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Alice has not grown up.

She has not become a soldier.

She has not become emotionally hardened.

She is still a six-year-old rabbit girl.

That is precisely why her current behavior requires attention.

Her understanding of the approaching conflict is frighteningly simple.

She has people she loves.

SPAWN intends to hurt them.

Alice intends to prevent that.

There is no grand speech.

No ideology.

No desire for glory.

No desire for revenge.

Just a child's absolute certainty that:

> **"They hurt my friends."**

And therefore:

> **"I have to stop them."**

The pencil remains clipped neatly to her little coat.

Everyone at the Blacksite knows what it becomes.

And increasingly, everyone is beginning to understand something else:

**Alice is not carrying the axe because she wants to fight.**

**She's carrying it because she has decided that nobody is going to hurt her people again.**
