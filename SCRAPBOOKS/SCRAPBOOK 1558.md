# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake lives alone.

Unlike Frost, Neri, and Nora, Jake does **not** live with Bao.

He has his own residential space within SAFEHOUSE.

Technically, however, calling it a **room** is generous.

Jake's residence was the only space in SAFEHOUSE that remained unfurnished.

It contains no conventional bedroom furniture.

There is no proper bed.

No wardrobe.

No desk.

No shelves.

No decorative furniture.

No carefully arranged personal belongings.

Instead, the space contains:

- grass;
    
- randomly manifested sketched flowers;
    
- assorted plushies;
    
- an old mattress;
    
- Jake's books;
    
- his teddy bear;
    
- various drawings;
    
- and whatever else Jake has accumulated without bothering to organize.
    

The result is less a conventional bedroom and more a **messy patch of Grassland that happens to exist inside an apartment.**

Jake appears perfectly comfortable with it.

---

# 1. THE UNFURNISHED ROOM

When SAFEHOUSE was originally furnished, Jake's space was left untouched.

There was no particular reason at the time.

It was simply the last room remaining.

Then Jake moved in.

C.O.R.E. eventually discovered that he had no interest in furnishing it.

When asked what furniture he wanted, Jake reportedly answered:

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm okay."

The question was asked again.

Jake looked around the empty room.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I have a mattress."

The matter was considered settled.

---

# 2. CURRENT CONDITION

The room is messy.

Not dangerously messy.

Just unmistakably inhabited by a six-year-old.

The old mattress sits directly on the floor.

Plushies are scattered around it.

Some are underneath the mattress.

Nobody knows how several got there.

Books are stacked in uneven piles.

Drawings are scattered across the floor.

Some have become real.

Others remain ordinary pieces of paper.

Grass grows through the entire space.

Sketched flowers emerge between the blades.

A few plushies have become partially buried in the grass.

Jake does not appear concerned.

---

# 3. THE GRASS

The grass is not conventional vegetation.

It is part of Jake's anomalous illustrative manifestation phenomenon.

It resembles the grass of LSIN-001 — THE GRASSLAND.

Unlike ordinary grass, it requires:

- no soil;
    
- no irrigation;
    
- no fertilizer;
    
- no artificial lighting;
    
- and no conventional maintenance.
    

It simply grows.

Occasionally, Jake draws more.

Occasionally, new patches appear without an identifiable drawing event.

No explanation has been established.

---

# 4. THE FLOWERS

The flowers are the most recognizable feature.

They are visually two-dimensional.

Their outlines resemble simple sketches.

Their colors are slightly inconsistent.

Some petals appear unfinished.

Some have visible pencil-like borders.

Despite their appearance, they are physically present.

They bend when touched.

They move with the grass.

They can be picked up.

They can be moved.

And they sometimes return to where they originally grew.

The flowers are directly comparable to those documented within the Grassland.

Jake is capable of drawing them himself.

Once sufficiently complete, the drawing may produce a physical flower.

---

# 5. THE PLUSHIES

Jake has accumulated a considerable number of plushies.

Their origins vary.

Some were gifts.

Some were purchased for him.

Some were simply left with him.

Several appear to have been created by Jake himself.

They are not organized.

There is no designated plush shelf.

There is no storage container.

They simply exist wherever Jake last left them.

One is on the mattress.

Three are underneath it.

Two are currently sitting in the grass.

Another has been observed inside an empty cardboard box.

Jake claims this arrangement is intentional.

No evidence supports this claim.

---

# 6. THE MATTRESS

The mattress is old.

It is also the closest thing Jake has to a conventional bed.

It lies directly on the floor.

No bedframe has been installed.

No replacement has been requested.

Jake appears to prefer it this way.

The mattress is frequently surrounded by plushies.

His teddy bear is usually nearby.

On several occasions, Jake has been observed sleeping with his face partially buried in the grass and one arm wrapped around the teddy bear.

Medical personnel have confirmed that this posture is not medically dangerous.

They remain confused by it.

---

# 7. NO CONVENTIONAL BEDROOM

C.O.R.E. previously considered furnishing the space.

This proposal was discontinued.

The room is Jake's.

He has not requested conventional furniture.

He has not complained about the mattress.

He has not requested removal of the grass.

He has not requested that the flowers be contained.

He has not requested the plushies be organized.

The only significant request recorded was:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Don't move my stuff."

Personnel have complied.

---

# 8. DRAWING MANIFESTATION

Jake's ability to turn drawings into physical objects appears responsible for much of the room's current condition.

A drawing of a flower can become a flower.

A drawing of grass can become grass.

A drawing of a small plush animal may become a small plush animal.

The mechanism remains unknown.

The transformation is not accompanied by conventional dimensional activity.

There is no detectable rift.

The object simply becomes physically present.

This phenomenon is currently classified as:

> **Illustrative Manifestation.**

---

# 9. RELATIONSHIP TO THE GRASSLAND

The similarities between Jake's residence and LSIN-001 remain significant.

Both contain:

- soft grass;
    
- two-dimensional flowers;
    
- simplified visual forms;
    
- unusually quiet environmental conditions;
    
- and a general absence of conventional architecture.
    

However, Jake's residence is **not** the Grassland.

There is no infinite horizon.

There is no anomalous sky.

There is no U-01 access point.

There is no known mnemonic effect.

There is no spatial extension beyond the physical boundaries of the SAFEHOUSE.

Jake appears to have recreated only the parts of the Grassland he wanted.

---

# 10. JAKE'S RELATIONSHIP WITH THE SPACE

Jake does not appear embarrassed by the mess.

He does not attempt to make it presentable for visitors.

He does not apologize when someone steps over a plushie.

He simply moves things when he needs somewhere to sit.

During one observation, a C.O.R.E. welfare officer asked:

> **"Do you want us to get you proper furniture?"**

Jake looked around.

He considered the question.

Then pointed toward the mattress.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I've got one."

The officer looked at the grass.

Then at the plushies.

Then at the mattress.

> **"A proper bed?"**

Jake shook his head.

> **JAKE:**  
> "This one's fine."

The subject was not raised again.

---

# 11. RESIDENT ACCESS

Jake lives alone, but this does not mean he is isolated from SAFEHOUSE.

Residents may visit when invited.

Bao, Neri, Nora, Jane, Anby, and others have all been observed visiting his space.

Visitors are expected to respect the room as Jake's private residence.

The grass is not to be removed.

The flowers are not to be harvested for research.

Plushies are not to be rearranged.

Drawings are not to be taken without permission.

And personnel are strongly advised not to "clean everything up."

The last instruction exists because someone already attempted this.

Jake noticed immediately.

He was not pleased.

---

# 12. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Jake living alone is not, by itself, considered a welfare concern.

His residence appears to provide him with:

- privacy;
    
- familiarity;
    
- control over his environment;
    
- a quiet place to rest;
    
- and a physical connection to something meaningful from the Grassland.
    

The distinction between **living alone** and **being forgotten** remains extremely important.

Jake has his own space.

He does not have to share it.

But people know where he is.

People remember him.

People can visit.

And, most importantly:

**Jake knows they remember him.**

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING OBSERVATION

Jake's residence is technically a room.

C.O.R.E. continues to use the term for administrative convenience.

In practical terms, it is:

An old mattress.

A ridiculous number of plushies.

Books scattered across grass.

Drawings everywhere.

Two-dimensional flowers growing from the floor.

And one small six-year-old star living quietly in the middle of it.

There is no wardrobe.

No proper bed.

No desk.

No carefully decorated walls.

No matching furniture.

The room was the only unfurnished space in SAFEHOUSE.

Jake simply made it his.

And somehow, without anyone planning it, the empty room became the most unmistakably **Jake-shaped** place in the entire apartment.

He lives alone.

Not because nobody wants him around.

Not because there is nowhere else for him to go.

But because, for once, he has a place that belongs entirely to him.
