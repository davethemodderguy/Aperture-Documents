# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At approximately 23:00, Jake Akana was left alone in the SAFEHOUSE cafeteria after accompanying his older brother, Aries Akana, for a late-night snack.

Aries departed.

Jake remained.

Aries subsequently forgot that Jake was still in the cafeteria.

Jake was not discovered until approximately 07:35 the following morning.

For eight hours and thirty-five minutes, a six-year-old child who had recently survived a near-fatal assault remained alone in a largely deserted cafeteria.

When discovered, Jake was shivering, visibly frightened, and exhibiting signs of severe psychological distress.

He did not appear to have spent the night resting comfortably.

He appeared to have spent it waiting.

Waiting for Aries to return.

Waiting for someone to notice he was missing.

Waiting for the door to open.

Waiting for the possibility that he had been forgotten permanently.

This incident represents a serious failure of resident supervision.

It is particularly concerning because Jake's current psychological condition is already characterized by acute separation distress. His previously established tolerance for solitude is approximately five minutes at most before panic may overwhelm him, potentially triggering a transformation into a cat and an attempt to flee or hide.

He was left alone for more than one hundred times that interval.

**C.O.R.E. considers this incident a significant psychological welfare emergency.**

No evidence currently establishes that Aries intentionally abandoned Jake. Available information indicates that he forgot Jake was still in the cafeteria.

That distinction matters when determining responsibility.

It does not erase the consequences.

# 1. INCIDENT TIMELINE

### 22:47 — Departure From Residential Quarters

Aries and Jake were reportedly preparing to obtain a late-night snack.

Jake accompanied his older brother.

His teddy bear was with him.

No formal caregiver handover was recorded because Aries was expected to remain responsible for Jake throughout the outing.

### 23:00 — Last Confirmed Observation

Aries left the cafeteria area.

Jake remained behind.

The exact reason Jake did not follow him has not yet been established. Possibilities include fatigue, hesitation, distraction, or an assumption that Aries would return momentarily.

No evidence suggests Jake understood that Aries had forgotten him.

### 23:05 — Initial Waiting Period

Jake remained in the cafeteria.

At this point, his distress may have been limited to confusion or concern that Aries had gone elsewhere.

No direct observation is available to establish his exact emotional state during these first minutes.

### 23:10 — Expected Return Window Exceeded

Jake's established tolerance for being alone had already been exceeded.

No caregiver was present.

No one had accepted responsibility for monitoring him.

No alert was raised.

### 00:00 — Midnight

Jake remained unaccounted for in the cafeteria.

The absence of a formal missing-resident alert meant that the incident had not yet been recognized as a supervision failure.

### 02:00 — Overnight

No verified record establishes that Jake slept.

His condition upon discovery suggests substantial distress, but C.O.R.E. cannot conclusively reconstruct every hour of the night.

Investigators must not invent observations that were never made.

### 05:00 — Pre-Dawn

Jake remained alone.

No handover occurred.

No one came to check whether he was safe.

### 07:35 — Discovery

Jake was discovered in the cafeteria.

He was shivering.

He appeared frightened and profoundly distressed.

He did not respond to the situation with the ordinary relief expected from a child who had merely spent an inconvenient night waiting for someone.

His behavior was more consistent with someone who had spent hours anticipating that nobody would return.

**Total time alone: 8 hours and 35 minutes.**

# 2. DISCOVERY REPORT

The first personnel member to encounter Jake that morning was a SAFEHOUSE resident entering the cafeteria for breakfast.

Jake was sitting near the table where he and Aries had been earlier that night.

His teddy bear was held tightly against his chest.

His shoulders were raised.

He was visibly shivering.

When the resident approached, Jake looked up immediately.

He did not stand.

He did not approach.

He simply stared.

The resident initially assumed he was cold.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "Jake?"

Jake's fingers tightened around the teddy bear.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "What are you doing here so early?"

Jake did not answer.

The resident approached slowly.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "Where's Aries?"

Jake looked toward the cafeteria entrance.

Then back at the resident.

His voice was barely audible.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...He went for a snack."

The resident paused.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "When?"

Jake swallowed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Last night."

A longer pause followed.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "Jake, did he come back?"

Jake shook his head.

The resident immediately contacted C.O.R.E. Welfare.

When asked whether he wanted to leave the cafeteria, Jake did not move.

He looked at the entrance again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm waiting."

The resident crouched nearby.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "You don't have to wait here anymore. We'll take you somewhere warm."

Jake's expression changed.

He pulled the teddy bear closer.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...What if he comes back?"

The resident did not immediately answer.

Jake looked down.

> **JAKE:**  
> "He won't know where I went."

The resident remained beside him.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> "We'll make sure he knows where you are."

Jake did not appear reassured.

He continued watching the doorway.

# 3. PHYSICAL CONDITION

C.O.R.E. Medical was notified immediately.

The initial assessment documented the following observations:

- Persistent shivering.
    
- Marked muscular tension.
    
- A rigid, guarded posture.
    
- Unusually limited spontaneous movement.
    
- Increased vigilance toward nearby doors and approaching personnel.
    
- Strong reluctance to leave the location where he had been waiting.
    
- Tight, sustained contact with his teddy bear.
    
- Minimal verbal communication.
    

The cause of the shivering has not yet been conclusively established.

Possible contributing factors include environmental cold, prolonged immobility, exhaustion, and acute psychological distress.

The cafeteria's overnight temperature and ventilation records are to be reviewed.

Personnel must not assume that the shivering was purely emotional or purely environmental.

Jake has a history of anomalous physiology and recent severe injuries. His physical condition requires an appropriate medical assessment regardless of the apparent cause.

The Medical Division has been instructed to assess:

1. Body temperature and signs of cold exposure.
    
2. Hydration and nutritional status.
    
3. Cardiovascular stability.
    
4. Exhaustion and possible sleep deprivation.
    
5. Any recurrence of symptoms associated with his previous medical deterioration.
    

Jake's teddy bear was not removed during the assessment.

No attempt was made to separate him from it for convenience.

# 4. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

The immediate concern is not simply that Jake spent a night alone.

It is the context in which the incident occurred.

Jake had recently survived an assault in which an individual resembling Aries stabbed him and inflicted a wound to his neck.

He had subsequently developed severe separation distress.

He could tolerate only a brief period alone before panic might overwhelm him.

He had begun requiring continuous observation and explicit caregiver handovers.

These requirements were known to personnel.

Despite that knowledge, Jake was left unattended for eight hours and thirty-five minutes.

The incident therefore occurred during a period when his vulnerability to abandonment-related distress was already established.

## Likely psychological impact

C.O.R.E. considers the following responses plausible and worthy of monitoring:

- Intensified fear when trusted people leave.
    
- Increased checking of doors and entrances.
    
- Difficulty believing that someone will return.
    
- Greater reluctance to sleep.
    
- Increased physical attachment to Aries, Clover, Bao, or other trusted residents.
    
- Panic when a caregiver leaves without an explicit explanation.
    
- Transformation into a cat when overwhelmed.
    
- Hiding or fleeing when he believes he has been forgotten.
    
- Reluctance to leave a location because he fears missing someone's return.
    

These are risk indicators, not a definitive diagnosis.

The full impact cannot be established from one observation.

However, Jake's current behavior warrants immediate support rather than waiting for symptoms to become more severe.

---

# 5. WHY THE CAFETERIA MATTERS

The location is significant.

Jake was not left alone in his bedroom with his teddy bear, familiar belongings, and an established bedtime routine.

He was left in a communal space.

The cafeteria would ordinarily be expected to fill with residents the following morning. At night, however, it can become quiet and largely empty.

Jake may have understood that people would eventually arrive.

That does not mean he understood when they would arrive, whether Aries would return first, or whether anyone knew he was missing.

The cafeteria also offered no clear explanation for Aries' absence.

There was no message.

No confirmed return time.

No caregiver handover.

No one telling Jake that his brother had gone elsewhere and that another trusted person would stay with him.

From Jake's perspective, the available information may have been brutally simple:

**Aries left. Aries did not come back. Nobody came to get him.**

Whether Jake interpreted this as intentional abandonment remains unknown.

His distress must not be dismissed merely because the abandonment was accidental.

An explanation given afterward cannot retroactively make those eight hours safe.

# 6. INTERVIEW WITH JAKE

**INTERVIEW STATUS:** Welfare conversation only  
**FORMAL QUESTIONING:** Suspended  
**PRESENT:** C.O.R.E. Welfare Officer, Medical representative

Jake was moved to a quiet, warm room after medical clearance for transport.

He initially resisted leaving the cafeteria.

He agreed only after being told that Aries would be informed of his location and that a trusted resident would remain with him.

During the subsequent welfare conversation, Jake was asked what he remembered.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Can you tell me what happened last night?"

Jake looked at his teddy bear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "We went to get food."

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "You and Aries?"

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "He left."

The officer waited.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I thought he was coming back."

A pause.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "How long did you wait?"

Jake shook his head.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I don't know."

He rubbed one hand over the teddy bear's ear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It got dark."

The officer did not correct him.

The cafeteria had already been dark when the incident began, but Jake was describing his experience of time rather than providing a literal account of the lighting.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then it was still dark."

His voice became quieter.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then people came."

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "And someone found you."

Jake nodded.

Several seconds passed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Did I do something wrong?"

The officer immediately answered.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "No."

Jake looked up.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "You did nothing wrong."

Jake's eyes began to water.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then why didn't he come back?"

The officer did not attempt to defend Aries or speculate about his intentions.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "I don't know exactly what happened. But it was not your fault."

Jake lowered his head.

A tear fell against the teddy bear's fabric and crystallized into a small diamond.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...I waited."

The conversation was paused.

No further questions were asked.

# 7. ARIES AKANA — ACCOUNT AND RESPONSE

Aries was informed of the incident after Jake had been located and medically assessed.

His initial response was consistent with genuine alarm.

He had not realized Jake remained in the cafeteria.

He had assumed the outing had concluded and returned to his own activities without confirming Jake's location.

No evidence currently indicates that he intended to abandon his younger brother.

Nevertheless, his failure to confirm Jake's whereabouts directly resulted in a prolonged period without supervision.

The distinction between intention and consequence must remain explicit.

Aries was subsequently permitted to see Jake after the Welfare Division determined that the meeting could be conducted without overwhelming him.

Aries entered the room slowly.

Jake was sitting on the bed, wrapped in a blanket, holding his teddy bear.

When he saw Aries, he froze.

Aries stopped near the doorway.

> **ARIES:**  
> "...Jake."

Jake stared at him.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Aries did not approach immediately.

> **ARIES:**  
> "I'm sorry."

Jake's expression crumpled.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You left."

Aries swallowed.

> **ARIES:**  
> "I did."

> **JAKE:**  
> "You didn't come back."

> **ARIES:**  
> "I know."

Jake looked down at the teddy bear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I waited for you."

Aries took a small step forward, then stopped again.

> **ARIES:**  
> "I forgot you were still there."

Jake went very still.

Aries appeared to realize how the statement sounded.

He did not attempt to retract it or replace it with a more comfortable explanation.

> **ARIES:**  
> "I should have checked. I should have made sure you were with me. I didn't, and I'm sorry."

Jake's fingers tightened around the bear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...You forgot me?"

Aries closed his eyes briefly.

> **ARIES:**  
> "Yes."

A long silence followed.

> **ARIES:**  
> "I didn't mean to leave you there. But I did leave you there, and that was wrong."

Jake began to cry.

Aries remained where he was.

> **ARIES:**  
> "Can I sit with you?"

Jake did not answer immediately.

Then, very quietly:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Don't leave."

Aries sat beside him.

> **ARIES:**  
> "I won't leave you alone."

Jake reached for his sleeve and held it tightly.

Aries did not ask him to let go.

He did not tell Jake that he needed to be more independent.

He did not ask Jake to forgive him.

He simply remained beside him while Jake cried.

# 8. RESIDENT WELFARE DIRECTIVES

The following requirements are effective immediately.

### 8.1 Continuous supervision remains mandatory

Jake must have a designated, trusted caregiver present.

No individual is permitted to assume that another resident is watching him without explicit confirmation.

### 8.2 All departures require handover

If a caregiver must leave, another approved person must accept responsibility before the first person departs.

The handover must be explicit.

"Someone is probably nearby" is not an acceptable substitute.

### 8.3 Cafeteria visits require direct accountability

Jake must not be left in the cafeteria or another communal space without a confirmed caregiver.

When leaving a shared location, the responsible resident must verify Jake's physical location before departing.

### 8.4 No reliance on the five-minute threshold

Jake's established tolerance for solitude is approximately five minutes at most.

This is not a permitted absence interval.

It is a warning that distress may develop rapidly.

### 8.5 No forced reconciliation

Jake is not required to accept Aries' apology immediately.

He is not required to sit with Aries, hold his hand, or demonstrate forgiveness.

If Jake wants Aries nearby, the interaction should be supported safely.

If Jake needs space from Aries while remaining accompanied by another trusted person, that choice must also be respected.

### 8.6 No guilt-based reassurance

Personnel must not tell Jake that Aries is devastated, that he should forgive him, or that his brother needs him to feel better.

Jake is the person who was left alone.

He must not be made responsible for comforting the person who forgot him.

---

# 9. SECURITY AND PROCEDURAL REVIEW

C.O.R.E. Security has been instructed to determine why no one noticed Jake's prolonged absence earlier.

The review will examine:

- cafeteria access and surveillance records;
    
- overnight resident movement;
    
- whether Jake's location was recorded or checked at any point;
    
- why no missing-resident alert was initiated;
    
- whether the current handover protocol was followed;
    
- and whether the incident exposed weaknesses in the existing welfare-monitoring process.
    

The investigation is not limited to determining why Aries forgot.

It must also determine why a vulnerable child could remain unaccounted for throughout the night without the system identifying the failure.

Aries' responsibility is real.

So is the institutional failure to detect that Jake was missing.

C.O.R.E. will not use one to conceal the other.

---

# 10. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake was left alone from 23:00 until 07:35.

He was not equipped to manage that period of isolation safely.

His recent assault had already left him frightened of being abandoned. His distress threshold was known. His need for continuous supervision had been documented.

Despite these precautions, no one returned for him until morning.

The available evidence indicates that Aries forgot Jake was still in the cafeteria rather than deliberately leaving him there.

This reduces the likelihood of intentional abandonment.

It does not reduce the duration.

It does not erase Jake's fear.

And it does not make the incident acceptable.

Jake's current presentation suggests that the event may reinforce his existing belief that even the people he trusts most can disappear without warning.

That belief must be addressed carefully.

Personnel should not insist that Jake immediately understand Aries' intentions. Understanding that an action was accidental does not automatically restore the sense of safety lost when it occurred.

Aries must demonstrate reliability through consistent behavior, not repeated promises.

C.O.R.E. must demonstrate reliability through supervision systems that do not depend on any single person's memory.

Jake must be allowed to recover without being pressured to make the incident easier for everyone else.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

At 23:00, Jake was waiting for a snack.

He was with his older brother.

There was no reason for him to believe he was in danger.

There was no reason for him to expect that he would still be in the cafeteria when the sun rose.

He waited through the night.

He watched the entrance.

He held his teddy bear.

And when the cafeteria remained empty, he had no way of knowing whether Aries was coming back, whether someone knew where he was, or whether anyone would notice that he had disappeared from his usual place.

At 07:35, someone finally found him.

By then, Jake was shivering and deeply distressed.

He had already survived an attacker who looked like his brother.

Now, another experience had taught him that his brother could disappear too.

The circumstances were different.

The emotional consequence may not be.

Aries did not intend to hurt him.

But Jake cannot recover from an intention.

He has to recover from what happened.

That will take time.

It will take consistency.

It will take adults and residents who remember to check, to communicate, to hand over responsibility, and to come back when they say they will.

Most importantly, it will require that nobody ask Jake to prove his trust by waiting alone again.

**Jake was forgotten once.**

**The system responsible for his safety must ensure that it does not happen again.**
