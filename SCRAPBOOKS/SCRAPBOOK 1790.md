# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake no longer wears his monocle.

He no longer wears his top hat.

The monocle is no longer medically necessary. Jake's visual condition has changed, and current examinations indicate that his eyes can now focus comfortably without the previous correction.

The top hat, meanwhile, has simply been retired.

Jake has offered no elaborate explanation.

He put both accessories away.

And that was that.

For most residents, this would be an unremarkable change in personal attire.

For Phoenix, it has been considerably more difficult.

The little business kid is gone.

Not Jake himself.

Just the particular combination of a glowing blue ribbon, a tiny top hat, a monocle, and a teddy bear sitting beside an aggressively serious miniature accountant.

Phoenix has been attempting to adjust.

She has not been particularly successful.

Meanwhile, Jake has spent considerably more time in his room, usually in the company of Clover.

He appears comfortable with this arrangement.

Clover does not demand conversation, does not insist on entertaining him, and does not treat his quietness as a problem to solve.

Sometimes they read.

Sometimes Jake works through mathematical problems.

Sometimes Clover shows him something in her notebook.

Sometimes they do absolutely nothing.

Jake seems perfectly content.

Phoenix, however, has begun to miss him.

More specifically, she misses seeing him.

And, although she is reluctant to admit it, she misses the little business kid.

---

# 1. ACCESSORY DISCONTINUATION

## 1.1 — The Monocle

Jake's monocle has been removed from routine use following a medical reassessment.

His previous nearsightedness no longer requires the same corrective arrangement.

C.O.R.E. Medical has confirmed that Jake is comfortable without it and can perform ordinary visual tasks without the previous difficulties.

The monocle has been cleaned and placed in storage alongside his other personal belongings.

Jake has not expressed any particular attachment to it.

When asked whether he intended to wear it again, he shrugged.

**JAKE:**  
“Maybe.”

**MEDICAL OFFICER:**  
“Do you miss it?”

**JAKE:**  
“Not really.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Does everything look clearer now?”

Jake looked around the room.

**JAKE:**  
“It's fine.”

He then returned to his book.

The examination concluded without further discussion.

---

## 1.2 — The Top Hat

The top hat has also been retired.

The black hat, previously distinguished by its luminous azure ribbon, now rests in Jake's room.

It has not been damaged.

The ribbon continues to glow.

Jake simply no longer wears it.

There was no ceremony.

No announcement.

No farewell to the residents who had grown accustomed to seeing him walk around SAFEHOUSE looking like a tiny executive who had come to negotiate a merger.

He took it off.

Put it away.

And continued with his day.

### Observation transcript

**CLOVER:**

**JAKE:**  
“No.”

**CLOVER:**  
“Why?”

**JAKE:**

“Can I borrow it?”

Jake considered the question.

**JAKE:**  
“For what?”

**CLOVER:**  
“Business.”

Jake stared at her.

Clover grinned.

**JAKE:**  
“No.”

**CLOVER:**  
“Worth asking.”

Jake returned to his book.

The hat remained on the shelf.

# 2. PHOENIX — A SMALL BUT NOTICEABLE DISAPPOINTMENT

Phoenix noticed the change almost immediately.

At first, she assumed Jake had simply left the hat in his room.

Then she saw him again.

No hat.

No monocle.

Just Jake.

White hair.

Azure shooting-star eyes.

Feline ears.

Tail.

Wings.

Teddy bear.

The same little boy she knew.

He looked perfectly normal by his own standards.

Phoenix looked at him for several seconds.

Jake noticed.

**JAKE:**  
“What?”

Phoenix hesitated.

**PHOENIX:**  
“Nothing.”

Jake tilted his head.

**JAKE:**  
“You're looking at me.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“I can look at you.”

**JAKE:**  
“Okay.”

He continued walking.

Phoenix watched him go.

She had no reasonable complaint.

The monocle had been a medical device, and she was genuinely glad he no longer needed it.

The hat had been his choice, and he was equally entitled to stop wearing it.

She understood both things.

She was not upset that Jake was healthier.

She was not upset that he had changed his appearance.

She was simply sad that something she had quietly come to associate with him was gone.

The little hat had become part of a familiar picture.

Jake sitting at a table.

His wings folded neatly behind him.

His teddy bear beside a ledger.

His monocle catching the light.

His tiny, serious voice explaining why a column of numbers did not match.

It had been ridiculous.

It had been adorable.

It had been _Jake._

Now the hat was sitting on a shelf.

Phoenix missed it.

She felt rather silly about that.

Which did not make her miss it any less.

# 3. PHOENIX'S PRIVATE OBSERVATION

C.O.R.E. Welfare personnel encountered Phoenix sitting alone in the lounge.

She was looking at a photograph taken several weeks earlier.

The image showed Jake at the dining table, wearing his top hat and monocle while checking a ledger.

His teddy bear sat beside him.

The azure ribbon glowed softly beneath the room's overhead lighting.

Phoenix enlarged the photograph.

Then reduced it.

Then enlarged it again.

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
“Something wrong?”

**PHOENIX:**  
“No.”

The officer glanced at the photograph.

**OFFICER:**  
“Jake?”

Phoenix nodded.

**OFFICER:**  
“He seems to be doing well.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“I know.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Then what's bothering you?”

Phoenix looked at the photograph for a moment longer.

**PHOENIX:**  
“I liked his hat.”

The officer waited.

**PHOENIX:**  
“And the monocle.”

A pause.

**PHOENIX:**  
“I liked how serious he looked when he wore them.”

She smiled faintly.

**PHOENIX:**  
“He looked like he was about to fire someone for miscalculating a budget.”

The officer almost laughed.

Phoenix did not.

Not because she was angry.

Because the memory was making her miss something that no longer existed in quite the same way.

**PHOENIX:**  
“It's stupid, isn't it?”

**OFFICER:**  
“No.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“He doesn't need the monocle anymore. That's good.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Yes.”

**PHOENIX:**  
“And the hat was his choice.”

**OFFICER:**  
“Correct.”

Phoenix looked down.

**PHOENIX:**  
“I know.”

She closed the photograph.

**PHOENIX:**  
“I just miss it.”

The officer did not attempt to convince her otherwise.

There was nothing unreasonable about missing a small, familiar thing.

Even when the reason it disappeared was good news.

# 4. THE ROOMMATE ARRANGEMENT

Jake has increasingly chosen to spend his free time in his own room, frequently with Clover.

This is not a newly imposed restriction.

No one has instructed him to remain indoors.

No medical order requires him to avoid the common areas.

He simply seems to prefer his room at present.

Clover's presence appears to make the arrangement especially comfortable.

Their interactions are usually quiet.

They share space without requiring constant engagement.

Clover may sit at her desk while Jake reads on his bed.

Jake may work through mathematical problems while Clover writes in her notebook.

Sometimes Clover talks.

Sometimes Jake answers.

Sometimes neither says anything for an hour.

Neither appears troubled by the silence.

### Typical afternoon

14:07 — Jake enters his room with a book and his teddy bear.

14:09 — Clover asks whether he wants the desk or the bed.

14:09 — Jake chooses the bed.

14:10 — Clover returns to her own activity.

14:32 — Jake asks a mathematical question.

14:34 — Clover offers an explanation.

14:36 — Jake points out a simpler method.

14:37 — Clover tells him to stop being smug.

14:37 — Jake says he is not being smug.

14:38 — Clover tells him that denying it makes him sound even smugger.

14:38 — Jake turns a page.

15:12 — Clover offers him a snack.

15:13 — Jake accepts.

15:14 — Both resume their respective activities.

16:03 — No further conversation has occurred.

Both remain in the room.

**Assessment:** Normal, voluntary companionship.

# 5. WHY JAKE PREFERS CLOVER'S COMPANY

C.O.R.E. does not consider Jake's increased time in his room inherently concerning.

He continues to engage in his interests.

He continues to communicate when he wants to.

He continues to seek assistance when necessary.

His behavior suggests that he is choosing a quieter environment rather than withdrawing from everyone indiscriminately.

Clover provides a particularly suitable form of companionship for him.

She does not treat silence as rejection.

She does not demand that he perform his feelings for her.

She does not insist that he participate in every conversation.

She can be present without making her presence a task.

For a child who values both independence and familiar company, this arrangement appears beneficial.

# 6. PHOENIX'S VISITS

Phoenix has not been excluded from Jake's room.

She remains welcome to visit.

However, Jake has not been spending as much time in shared spaces, and Phoenix has been reluctant to interrupt his time with Clover.

She occasionally passes his door.

Sometimes she hears them talking.

Sometimes she hears Clover laughing.

Sometimes she hears nothing at all.

On one occasion, she stopped outside the room with a small box in her hands.

Inside was a photograph frame.

She had intended to give it to Jake so he could display the old photograph of himself wearing his top hat and monocle.

She stood outside for nearly a minute.

Then she reconsidered.

She did not want to make him feel obligated to preserve an appearance he had chosen to leave behind.

She placed the box on a nearby table with a note.

> _For whenever you want it. No pressure._

Jake found it later.

He looked at the photograph.

Then at the empty space on his shelf where the hat had been.

He did not put the photograph in the frame immediately.

He carried it to his desk instead.

The next morning, Phoenix found a small note outside her own room.

> **Thank you. I like the picture.**

Nothing else was written.

Phoenix kept the note.

# 8. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. recognizes two simultaneous circumstances.

First, Jake is comfortable spending more time in his room with Clover.

Second, Phoenix is experiencing a small but genuine sense of loss regarding a familiar aspect of Jake's appearance and routine.

Neither circumstance requires correction.

Jake should not be encouraged to resume wearing accessories he no longer wants simply because another resident misses them.

Phoenix should not be made to feel foolish for missing something that brought her happiness.

The appropriate response is to allow both feelings to exist.

Personnel are advised:

- Do not pressure Jake to wear the hat for Phoenix.
    
- Do not suggest that he should wear the monocle when it is no longer medically necessary.
    
- Do not frame the change as Jake becoming less himself.
    
- Do not treat his time with Clover as exclusion of other residents.
    
- Do not encourage Phoenix to conceal her feelings indefinitely.
    
- Do not make Jake responsible for resolving Phoenix's disappointment.
    
- Continue allowing Phoenix to visit when Jake is comfortable with visitors.
    
- Continue respecting Jake's preference for quiet companionship.
    

Most importantly, **Jake does not owe anyone a particular appearance.**

He may wear the hat again someday.

He may never wear it again.

Both outcomes are acceptable.

The same applies to the amount of time he chooses to spend outside his room.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Phoenix misses the hat.

She misses the monocle.

She misses the tiny business kid who looked like he was about to purchase an entire corporation with pocket money and a ledger.

But she does not want Jake to be uncomfortable for her sake.

She does not want him to wear something he no longer needs.

She does not want him to become a memory she can preserve by asking him to remain unchanged.

She wants him to be happy.

Even if that means seeing him less often.

Even if it means that, lately, his favorite place is his room.

Even if it means that Clover gets to spend most afternoons with him while Phoenix only catches glimpses of him in the hallway.

And perhaps that is the difficult part of caring for someone.

Sometimes, the things you miss are not things you should ask to have back.

Sometimes, you simply let them go.

Then you knock on the door.

And hope that, when it opens, the person you missed is still there.
