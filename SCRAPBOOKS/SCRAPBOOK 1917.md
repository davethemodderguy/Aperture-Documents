## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At approximately one day after the incident documented in CORE-SH-INC-176, Sora left his designated room without authorization or supervision.

He was subsequently observed running through the snow.

Blood was seen slowly dripping from his injuries and disappearing into the white snow beneath him.

The observation is especially concerning because Sora remains in critical medical condition. His anomalous inability to die does not provide protection against blood loss, pain, or permanent physical deterioration. His body does not naturally heal. Every minute spent outside medical supervision increases the likelihood of further complications.

However, the medical emergency is only one part of the incident.

Sora's departure appears consistent with his increasingly entrenched belief that he is worthless, that he has disappointed Mia, and that his presence is a burden to others.

He is not simply attempting to leave the SAFEHOUSE.

He appears to be running away from the people trying to help him.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet establish whether Sora intended to reach a particular destination. His recent statements about wanting to be left in the snow, combined with his self-blame and the severity of his injuries, require staff to treat this as an immediate threat to his welfare.

**Sora is not to be considered safe merely because he cannot die.**

## 2. INCIDENT CHRONOLOGY

**Date:** Following day, exact time pending reconstruction.

Sora had remained under observation following the discovery of his injuries.

His condition had not been declared stable. Medical personnel continued to monitor his bleeding and provide care. No recovery had been established, and no assurance could be made that his condition would improve without continued treatment.

At some point, Sora left the area in which he was being monitored.

The departure was not immediately detected.

He was later seen outside, moving through the snow.

A staff member noticed a trail of blood behind him.

The blood was not flowing rapidly. It fell slowly, drop by drop, marking the otherwise undisturbed snow.

Sora continued running.

He did not stop when called.

No confirmed destination was identified.

## 3. MEDICAL ASSESSMENT — THE DANGER OF CONTINUED EXERTION

Sora's current medical condition makes unsupervised movement exceptionally dangerous.

The following facts remain applicable:

- Sora cannot die through conventional means.
    
- He can sustain severe physical injuries.
    
- He experiences pain.
    
- He can bleed.
    
- He does not naturally regenerate or heal.
    
- His body does not automatically replace lost blood.
    
- His current injuries have not been confirmed as resolved.
    

His ability to remain alive does not mean he can withstand indefinite blood loss.

The cold presents an additional concern. Sora has already demonstrated a tendency to seek isolation in snowy environments, and his current psychological state makes it difficult to rely on him voluntarily returning for assistance.

His hemophobia also remains a major consideration.

Sora is terrified of blood. He may be confronted with the sight of his own injuries while simultaneously experiencing the physical consequences of them. Staff must not assume that his continued movement indicates that he is coping with either.

**Medical Directive:** Recovery personnel must prioritize locating Sora, preventing further exertion where safely possible, and returning him to medical care. No member of staff is to delay intervention because of assumptions regarding his immortality.

---

## 4. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT — A CHILD WHO BELIEVES HE SHOULD DISAPPEAR

The most concerning aspect of Sora's departure is its apparent connection to his recent statements.

Before leaving, Sora expressed several beliefs:

- He believed he was worth nothing.
    
- He blamed himself for making Mia angry.
    
- He questioned how he could be a good older brother after hurting her.
    
- He asked to be left in the snow.
    

These statements must not be treated as ordinary expressions of sadness or frustration.

Sora is six years old. He has experienced severe trauma, prolonged isolation, the loss and return of his sister, and repeated disruptions to his sense of safety.

His recent argument with Mia appears to have intensified an existing pattern of self-blame.

He may have interpreted her anger as confirmation that he is a failure.

That interpretation is not true.

Mia's anger does not determine Sora's worth. Their argument does not invalidate their relationship. Sora is not responsible for maintaining his sister's happiness, and he does not need to earn the right to be cared for.

C.O.R.E. further emphasizes that Sora's departure must not be interpreted as an inconvenience caused to staff.

He is a child in crisis.

He needs help, even if he cannot currently accept it.

## 5. RECOVERY DIRECTIVES

<text>All available personnel are to assist with the recovery operation.</text>

### A. Search and recovery

1. Establish Sora's last confirmed location and direction of travel.
    
2. Search nearby snow-covered areas and previously identified places where Sora has sought isolation.
    
3. Follow any visible blood trail as an indicator of his route, while avoiding unnecessary disturbance of the area.
    
4. Maintain communication between search personnel and medical staff.
    
5. Do not allow the search to become a pursuit that further frightens or exhausts him.
    

### B. Contact protocol

If Sora is located:

- Approach slowly and announce one's presence.
    
- Avoid surrounding him with multiple personnel.
    
- Do not shout, threaten, or demand that he return.
    
- Do not make his injuries a subject of accusation.
    
- Do not tell him that he has frightened or inconvenienced everyone.
    
- Reassure him that medical assistance is there to help, not punish him.
    

If he refuses to move, personnel must coordinate a medically appropriate recovery rather than abandoning him.

### C. Medical intervention

Sora must receive immediate assessment upon recovery.

His bleeding, existing injuries, exposure to cold, and overall condition must be evaluated independently. His inability to die must not be used as a substitute for treatment.

### D. Family support

Mia, Jake, and other children are not to be assigned responsibility for locating or retrieving Sora.

They may be informed of his condition in an age-appropriate manner, but they must not be expected to resolve the crisis.

In particular, Mia must not be told that Sora's condition is her responsibility.

The adults must handle the emergency.

## 6. INTERNAL WELFARE NOTE — THE SNOW IS NOT A SAFE PLACE FOR HIM

There is a recurring detail in Sora's behavior that C.O.R.E. cannot ignore.

When overwhelmed, he seeks places where nobody can reach him.

His original maintenance locker. His den. The snow outside the Blacksite.

These locations have become associated with isolation, not recovery.

Sora has previously survived alone in conditions that no child should have endured. He may therefore perceive isolation as familiar, even when it is actively harming him.

The current incident suggests that this pattern has returned under extreme psychological distress.

Personnel must not mistake familiarity for safety.

The snow is not where Sora needs to be.

He needs warmth, medical attention, and people who will remain nearby without demanding that he explain himself.

---

## 7. CLOSING NOTE — FOLLOW THE FOOTPRINTS

The witness described a small figure running through the snow.

Behind him, there was a trail of blood.

It is difficult to reconcile that image with the fact that Sora cannot die.

People hear the word _immortal_ and imagine someone who cannot be harmed. They imagine a child who can fall, bleed, and suffer without consequence.

That is not Sora.

He can be hurt.

He can be frightened.

He can lose blood.

And he can remain alive through suffering that should never have been allowed to continue.

Yesterday, he asked to be left in the snow.

Today, he ran there himself.

C.O.R.E. will not interpret this as a request that should be honored. It is a warning that a six-year-old child no longer believes he deserves to be helped.

We do not need him to explain why he ran.

We do not need him to apologize for leaving.

We do not need him to promise that he will never do it again.

We need to find him.

We need to bring him somewhere warm.

And when he is finally found, nobody is to tell him how much trouble he has caused.

He already believes he is worthless. We will not give that belief another reason to survive.

**Follow the footprints. Follow the blood. Bring the boy home.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
