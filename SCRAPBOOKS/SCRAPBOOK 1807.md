# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has provided his first coherent verbal account of the assault that nearly killed him.

The account introduces a significant new lead.

According to Jake, the assailant **resembled his older brother, Aries Akana.**

The individual wore a cloak bearing an unidentified symbol. He approached Jake, stabbed him, and continued the assault after Jake attempted to fight back. The attacker subsequently inflicted a wound to Jake's neck.

The assailant fled after hearing Clover knock on the door.

Jake's statement does not establish the attacker's identity. However, the resemblance to Aries raises the possibility that the perpetrator deliberately exploited Jake's trust, or intended to create suspicion toward someone Jake loves.

Neither possibility has been confirmed.

A second concern has become equally urgent.

Jake's current tolerance for being left alone is approximately **five minutes at most** before he may enter an acute panic response, transform into a cat, and attempt to flee or hide.

This is no longer merely a preference for company.

It is an immediate welfare and missing-resident risk.

C.O.R.E. has consequently issued two primary directives:

- The assault investigation must prioritize the assailant's resemblance to Aries and the symbol on the cloak.
    
- Jake must remain under continuous, coordinated observation. Personnel must not deliberately leave him alone to test his reaction.

# 3. THE ARIES RESEMBLANCE

The most consequential detail in Jake's account is not simply that the attacker wore a cloak.

It is that **Jake believed the attacker was Aries.**

Jake's trust in his older brother made the resemblance potentially significant.

Investigators are considering several possibilities:

- **Deliberate impersonation:** The assailant intentionally resembled Aries to approach Jake without immediately provoking suspicion.
    
- **Anomalous disguise:** The assailant used an unknown ability or device to alter their appearance.
    
- **Partial resemblance:** The attacker may have shared enough visible characteristics with Aries to mislead Jake in a stressful situation.
    
- **Misidentification under distress:** Pain, fear, and the suddenness of the attack may have affected Jake's initial perception.
    

These are investigative hypotheses, not findings.

C.O.R.E. will not treat Aries as a suspect solely because the assailant resembled him.

Aries' whereabouts, access records, and available footage will be reviewed as part of standard evidence verification. This procedure is not an accusation.

Aries has been informed of this distinction.

He accepted it without objection.

His only request was that investigators avoid repeatedly asking Jake to relive the attack unless additional information is genuinely necessary.

The request has been approved.

# 4. THE CLOAK AND ITS SYMBOL

The cloak represents a potentially valuable investigative lead.

Jake remembers a symbol on its surface, but has not yet provided a sufficiently detailed description to permit reliable identification.

Investigators are instructed to avoid showing Jake large collections of disturbing imagery or asking him to select a symbol under pressure.

Instead, the process will proceed in stages.

1. Review all recovered scene photographs and available security footage.
    
2. Search for evidence of unusual clothing, fabric, fibers, or anomalous residue.
    
3. Compare the reported symbol against documented markings associated with known hostile groups, including SPAWN, without assuming a connection.
    
4. If Jake volunteers additional details, record them in his own words.
    
5. Offer a neutral drawing sheet only if Jake wishes to reproduce the symbol himself.
    

**No symbol is to be declared demonic or ritualistic solely because it looks unfamiliar.**

The presence of a sacrificial dagger and a marked cloak may suggest deliberate symbolism, but neither establishes the attacker's affiliation.

C.O.R.E. will investigate evidence rather than manufacture a conclusion.

# 5. ACUTE SEPARATION DISTRESS

Jake's psychological response has deteriorated following the assault.

Previously, Jake could tolerate brief periods of solitude under ordinary circumstances.

That is no longer a safe assumption.

Current observations indicate that Jake can tolerate **approximately five minutes alone at most** before panic may overwhelm his ability to remain in place.

The five-minute interval is an observed upper limit, not a guaranteed safe window.

His distress may begin earlier.

When Jake believes he has been abandoned or that nobody is coming back, the progression can be rapid:

- He repeatedly checks the room and doorway.
    
- He attempts to establish whether another person is still nearby.
    
- He becomes increasingly distressed when he cannot confirm someone's presence.
    
- He may stop responding to reassurance.
    
- He may transform into a cat.
    
- He may flee, hide, or remain concealed in a location that is difficult to search.
    

The transformation is not the primary danger.

The danger is what happens when a frightened six-year-old, already traumatized by a near-fatal assault, disappears from the people responsible for keeping him safe.

In feline form, Jake is small, quiet, and capable of fitting into spaces that are difficult to inspect. If frightened, he may not respond to his name or approach even a familiar person.

**Personnel must not interpret his disappearance as a deliberate attempt to evade care or as a harmless game.**

## If Jake transforms and hides

Personnel are to avoid rushing toward him, surrounding him, or attempting to drag him from a hiding place merely to end the incident quickly.

One familiar person should approach the area, remain calm, and speak in a quiet, ordinary voice.

Suitable statements include:

> "Jake, I'm here."

> "You're not in trouble."

> "You don't have to come out right this second."

> "I'm staying nearby."

Personnel should maintain awareness of the surroundings and coordinate the search if his location is uncertain. Reassurance does not replace the missing-resident protocol.

Once Jake is located, the priority is safety and restoration of trust, not punishment for having hidden.

# 7. DESIGNATED SUPPORT PERSONNEL

No single resident should be expected to provide uninterrupted care indefinitely.

Continuous observation requires a coordinated roster, appropriate rest periods, and explicit handovers.

### Aries Akana — Older Brother

Aries remains one of Jake's strongest sources of reassurance.

His presence may be especially important because the attacker exploited a resemblance to him.

However, this creates a difficult emotional situation for both brothers.

Jake may seek Aries because he feels safe with him, while simultaneously remembering that the person who attacked him looked like Aries.

Personnel must not pressure Jake to resolve that contradiction.

Aries must not be required to prove that he is the real Aries every time Jake becomes frightened.

Instead, he should remain consistent, patient, and honest.

### Clover — Witness and Trusted Companion

Clover's knock interrupted the attacker's actions, according to Jake's recollection.

Clover is also the person who discovered Jake afterward.

This makes Clover's presence potentially reassuring, but may also associate door sounds or unexpected arrivals with the incident.

Personnel should observe Jake's reactions to knocking, opening doors, and sudden movement without deliberately provoking them.

### Bao — Maternal Support

Bao remains an appropriate source of familiar care and emotional reassurance.

Jake calls her "Mom."

Her involvement should continue when available, but she must be included in the coordinated handover system rather than being expected to remain beside him without relief.

### Phoenix — Additional Support

Phoenix may assist with familiar routines, food, and companionship.

No support person should force Jake to speak, eat, drink, or explain his fear as a condition of receiving comfort.

**Care must remain available even when Jake cannot reciprocate.**

# 8. MEDICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Jake's external wounds have healed, but his recent refusal to eat or drink remains unresolved.

The assault account does not diminish that concern.

His anomalous physiology may alter ordinary expectations, but it does not justify assuming that prolonged refusal of fluids or food is harmless.

The Medical Division is instructed to:

- reassess hydration, nutrition, and physiological stability;
    
- determine whether pain, nausea, fear, or another factor is contributing to the refusal;
    
- offer familiar food and fluids without coercion;
    
- monitor for renewed deterioration;
    
- coordinate any examination with the welfare team so that necessary care does not leave Jake feeling abandoned.
    

His teddy bear must remain within reach. Its heartbeat is Jake's actual heartbeat, and separating him from it is prohibited except where an immediate medical necessity requires otherwise and an appropriate plan is in place.

No force-feeding, threats, or conditional promises of companionship are permitted.

Jake must not be made to believe that eating, drinking, or behaving calmly is the price of having someone remain with him.

Jake continued holding his hand.

Bao remained beside them.

For the moment, he had what he needed:

People he recognized.

A hand he could hold.

His teddy bear.

And no empty room waiting for him when he looked up.

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's statement provides a clearer sequence of events but does not yet identify the perpetrator.

The resemblance to Aries may have been intentional. If so, the attacker selected a disguise that could exploit a child's trust in his own family.

That possibility is deeply concerning.

However, the immediate welfare risk is no longer limited to the original assault.

Jake now appears to associate solitude with the possibility of being attacked again, abandoned, or left without help.

His current five-minute tolerance must not be treated as a countdown personnel can safely use.

It is an observed upper limit.

A frightened Jake may transform sooner.

A transformed Jake may hide.

A hidden Jake may be difficult to locate.

And an apparently quiet room may conceal a child in acute distress.

The response must therefore prioritize **continuous supervision, predictable companionship, and a coordinated search plan** while the Medical and Security Divisions continue their respective work.

Jake must not be confined merely because he can transform.

He must not be punished for fleeing.

He must not be forced to relive the attack to satisfy investigative curiosity.

And he must not be left alone in the hope that he will eventually learn to tolerate it.

Recovery cannot be built by recreating the condition he is afraid of.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake remembers someone who looked like his brother.

He remembers the cloak.

The symbol.

The knife.

He remembers fighting back.

He remembers the wound to his neck.

And he remembers the attacker running when Clover knocked.

He survived.

But survival has not made him feel safe.

Now, when the room becomes quiet, he watches the door.

When someone stands up, he watches them.

When a familiar person leaves, he needs to know who will remain.

And if he cannot find that reassurance, he may disappear into a form so small that even the people searching for him could walk past without noticing.

C.O.R.E. must not allow that to become another incident report.

**A child who has learned to fear an empty room should not have to disappear before anyone realizes he is frightened.**

Jake is not required to recover on a schedule.

He is not required to stop being afraid because his wounds have closed.

And he is not required to prove that he trusts us by enduring the very thing that terrifies him.

Until further assessment establishes otherwise:

**Maintain continuous observation.**

**Keep his teddy bear within reach.**

**Coordinate every handover.**

**If Jake transforms and cannot be located, begin the missing-resident protocol immediately.**

**Never deliberately leave Jake alone to test his tolerance.**

The assailant took away Jake's sense of safety.

C.O.R.E. will not ask Jake to rebuild it in an empty room.
