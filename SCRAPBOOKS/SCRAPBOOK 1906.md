## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Sora's withdrawal from social activities and continued refusal to use the replacement whiteboard, Mia and Jake independently decided to make something for him.

The result was a white wool scarf, knitted by the two children.

Their stated intentions were straightforward:

- Cover the visible wound and injuries around Sora's neck.
    
- Keep his neck warm.
    
- Give him something made specifically for him.
    

The scarf was not intended to replace his lost whiteboard, resolve his communication difficulties, or persuade him to resume social interaction.

It was simply a gift.

The significance of the gesture lies in its simplicity. Sora has spent considerable time inside his den, largely separated from the two children with whom he previously shared nearly every day. Mia and Jake did not attempt to force him out or demand that he respond to them.

Instead, they made something with their own hands and brought it to him.

C.O.R.E. considers this a positive welfare development, provided that the scarf is worn only when medically appropriate and does not place pressure on his healing neck.

---

## 2. THE MAKING OF THE SCARF

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE common room  
**Participants:** Mia, Jake  
**Observation:** Unscheduled

The two children were observed sitting together in the common room with a collection of white wool yarn and knitting materials.

Jake was having considerable difficulty keeping the yarn organized.

**JAKE:** Wait. That's the wrong one.

**MIA:** You're holding the right one.

**JAKE:** Then why does it look wrong?

**MIA:** Because you tangled it.

Jake looked down.

A substantial portion of the yarn had become tangled around his hands.

**JAKE:** Oh.

Mia patiently helped him untangle it.

They continued working.

The scarf was not perfectly uniform. Some sections were tighter than others, and the edges were uneven. Jake's contributions were particularly inconsistent, although Mia made an effort to incorporate them rather than undo all his work.

**JAKE:** Is it long enough?

**MIA:** Not yet.

**JAKE:** How much longer?

**MIA:** A little more.

**JAKE:** How much is a little?

**MIA:** Jake.

**JAKE:** Okay.

They continued.

At one point, Jake held up the unfinished scarf.

**JAKE:** Do you think Sora will like it?

Mia examined their work.

**MIA:** I hope so.

**JAKE:** It's white.

**MIA:** I know.

**JAKE:** He has white hair.

**MIA:** I know.

**JAKE:** So it'll match.

Mia smiled faintly.

**MIA:** That's why we picked it.

Jake returned to knitting.

Neither child suggested that Sora owed them a response for their effort.

---

## 3. PRESENTATION TO SORA

**Location:** Sora's Den, Floor 7  
**Observation:** Supervised

Mia and Jake approached Sora's den carrying the finished scarf.

Sora was inside.

He did not initially approach the entrance.

Mia sat down nearby, leaving enough space for him to remain comfortable.

**MIA:** Sora?

There was no immediate response.

**JAKE:** We made something.

Sora slowly looked toward them.

Jake held up the scarf.

**JAKE:** Look!

Mia took the scarf and gently unfolded it.

**MIA:** It's for you.

Sora stared at the white wool.

His eyes moved from the scarf to Mia, then to Jake.

He did not reach for it.

**JAKE:** We made it together.

**MIA:** It's a scarf. It'll keep your neck warm.

She paused.

**MIA:** And it'll cover the marks on your neck, if you want it to.

Sora lowered his eyes.

His hand moved toward his neck before stopping.

Mia noticed.

**MIA:** You don't have to wear it right now.

Jake nodded enthusiastically.

**JAKE:** You can wear it whenever!

He looked at Mia.

**JAKE:** Right?

**MIA:** Right.

Mia placed the scarf beside the entrance to Sora's den, within his reach but not on top of him.

**MIA:** It's yours.

Sora looked at it for a long time.

Then he slowly reached out and touched the wool with his fingertips.

Jake immediately brightened.

**JAKE:** He touched it!

**MIA:** Jake.

**JAKE:** Sorry.

He lowered his voice.

**JAKE:** I hope you like it.

Sora continued holding a small section of the scarf.

He did not write anything.

He did not need to.

For the remainder of the observation, the scarf remained beside him.

## 4. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

The scarf represents a meaningful attempt by Mia and Jake to reconnect with Sora without demanding communication.

Several aspects warrant attention.

### A. The gift does not demand a response

Sora was not required to write a thank-you message, smile, hug either child, or immediately wear the scarf.

This was appropriate.

His inability or unwillingness to communicate must not be interpreted as ingratitude.

### B. The scarf provides practical comfort

The wool may provide warmth around his neck and offer some coverage for visible injuries.

However, C.O.R.E. medical personnel must verify that it does not:

- Put pressure on healing tissue.
    
- Restrict neck movement.
    
- Interfere with breathing or swallowing.
    
- Conceal injuries that require medical observation.
    
- Become entangled or otherwise create a physical hazard.
    

The scarf is a comfort item, not a medical dressing or substitute for treatment.

### C. The scarf carries personal significance

Unlike the replacement whiteboard, the scarf does not attempt to recreate something Sora has lost.

It is something new, made specifically for him by two children who care about him.

C.O.R.E. will not assume that this distinction makes the scarf easier for Sora to accept. His reaction must be allowed to develop naturally.

### D. The children require support, too

Mia and Jake have demonstrated considerable concern for Sora.

Their affection is welcome, but they must not become responsible for restoring his emotional stability.

Adults remain responsible for his care, medical supervision, communication accommodations, and psychological support.

The children are his siblings and friends.

They are not his treatment plan.

---

## 5. PERSONNEL DIRECTIVES

1. The scarf is to remain Sora's personal property.
    
2. Staff must not force him to wear it or remove it without a legitimate medical or safety reason.
    
3. Medical personnel must assess whether wearing it is appropriate given his previous cervical injuries.
    
4. Mia and Jake must not be pressured to produce additional gifts or activities to encourage Sora's recovery.
    
5. Staff must continue offering accessible communication methods without repeatedly insisting that Sora use the replacement whiteboard.
    
6. His withdrawal must continue to be monitored, even if he begins accepting small gestures of affection.
    

**Important:** Acceptance of the scarf must not be recorded as evidence that Sora has recovered from the psychological consequences of losing his original whiteboard.

---

## 6. CLOSING NOTE — SOMETHING MADE BY HAND

The original whiteboard was something Jake gave Sora.

It carried Jake's signature on the back. Sora carried it everywhere because it allowed him to communicate with a world he could not speak to.

It is gone.

No replacement can restore the exact object, the time spent carrying it, or the certainty Sora once had that it would always be there.

Mia and Jake cannot bring it back.

So they made something else.

A white wool scarf.

It is uneven. It is not particularly sophisticated. Some of the stitches are noticeably different from one another, and the finished product bears unmistakable evidence of Jake's involvement.

But both children worked on it.

They wanted Sora's neck to be warm. They wanted the injuries to be less visible. They wanted him to have something comfortable.

Most importantly, they wanted to give him something without asking him to give anything back.

Perhaps Sora will wear it tomorrow.

Perhaps he will leave it beside his den for several days.

Perhaps he will hold it without ever putting it on.

C.O.R.E. will not assign a deadline to any of these possibilities.

For now, two children have made their quiet way of saying that they still want him beside them.

And they have left him the choice of when to come closer.

**The scarf cannot give Sora his voice back. It cannot restore the board. But it can keep his neck warm, and it can remind him that someone thought of him while he was away from everyone else.**

Sometimes, that is where care begins.

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
