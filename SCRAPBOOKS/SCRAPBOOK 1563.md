# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The third week has concluded.

There has been **almost no change.**

Jake remains on his floor.

He rarely leaves.

No resident has visited him.

No member of the Obol Squad has visited him.

No family member has visited him.

No meaningful conversation concerning Jake has occurred among the residents.

More broadly, there has been very little social interaction between residents in general.

The SAFEHOUSE population remains occupied with ongoing work.

Research continues.

Operations continue.

Medical duties continue.

L.S.I.N. assignments continue.

KCA and CED responsibilities continue.

Engineering continues.

Administrative work continues.

People arrive.

People leave.

People work.

Then they sleep.

Then they work again.

Jake's floor remains quiet.

C.O.R.E. recorded no major activity on Jake's floor during the third week.

No anomalous incident occurred.

No environmental breach occurred.

No notable manifestation event occurred.

The grass continued growing.

The flowers remained.

Jake remained there.

---

# 2. JAKE'S SLEEPING PATTERN

One change has become increasingly consistent.

Jake now frequently sleeps **face down** on his old mattress.

He does not use a conventional pillow.

His face is usually turned slightly to one side.

His teddy bear is commonly underneath one arm.

Grass occasionally grows onto the edge of the mattress.

Several plushies are usually nearby.

There is nothing particularly abnormal about the sleeping position itself.

What is notable is how long Jake remains asleep.

He sometimes sleeps for extended periods.

When awake, he is generally quiet.

---

# 3. MORNING

Jake wakes.

There is no alarm.

No one wakes him.

He simply eventually opens his eyes.

He remains face down for several seconds.

Then he reaches for his teddy bear.

He sits up.

Looks around.

And begins his day.

There is rarely anyone else awake on his floor.

---

# 4. DAYTIME

Jake's daytime activity has become repetitive.

He reads.

He draws.

He eats.

He drinks.

He walks around his floor.

He occasionally lies down again.

Sometimes he watches the flowers.

Sometimes he does nothing.

There is no evidence that he is attempting to attract attention.

There is also no evidence that he expects anyone to visit.

---

# 5. THE REST OF SAFEHOUSE

The absence of Jake-related conversation is not isolated.

Residents have become unusually focused on their respective responsibilities.

Conversations increasingly consist of:

> "The report is finished."

> "The equipment arrived."

> "Where is the keycard?"

> "The meeting starts in ten minutes."

> "I need those samples."

> "We're leaving."

> "I'll be back later."

Then people leave.

There is little casual conversation.

Very few meals are shared.

Few people linger in common areas.

Everyone appears to be operating under the same assumption:

> **Work first.**

---

# 6. JAKE'S NAME

During the third week, Jake's name appears infrequently in routine conversation.

Not because anyone has decided he is unimportant.

There has simply been very little reason for anyone to mention him.

Work does not require it.

Reports do not require it.

Operations do not require it.

The Blacksite continues functioning without needing his participation.

The absence is subtle.

And therefore easy to overlook.

---

# 7. OBL SQUAD

The Obol Squad has also remained occupied.

Their usual visits to Jake's room have stopped.

Not permanently.

There is no decision to stop visiting him.

They simply have not had the time.

Their schedules have remained full.

The same is true of everyone else.

Jake is not being deliberately avoided.

He is simply not being encountered.

---

# 8. BAO

Bao has not visited Jake.

There has been no conversation between them during the observation period.

Bao remains occupied with her own responsibilities.

Jake has not gone looking for her.

No message has been sent.

No request for company has been recorded.

---

# 9. NERI

Neri has also remained occupied.

Her own work has continued.

She has not visited Jake's floor.

Jake has not visited hers.

There have been no exchanges between them.

This is notable primarily because both normally tolerate long periods of silence together.

In the current circumstances, even that quiet companionship has disappeared.

---

# 10. THE SILENCE

The most notable characteristic of Week Three is not Jake's behavior.

It is the silence surrounding him.

There is no argument.

No rejection.

No confrontation.

No deliberate avoidance.

No dramatic event.

Just absence.

The residents are working.

Jake is alone.

Everyone assumes there will eventually be time.

Nobody knows exactly when.

---

# 11. WELFARE OBSERVATION

Jake remains physically stable.

He continues to eat and drink.

His anomalous characteristics remain unchanged.

His stellar glow remains present.

His teddy bear's cardiac activity remains normal.

His room remains physically safe.

No immediate intervention is required.

However, the duration of social isolation is now significant.

Three weeks have passed.

Jake has not complained.

That remains insufficient evidence that the situation has no emotional effect.

His established behavior strongly favors silent adaptation over requesting assistance.

Accordingly, C.O.R.E. continues passive observation.

---

# 12. NIGHT

At night, Jake usually stops drawing.

He puts his pencil down.

His books remain open.

His teddy bear rests beside him.

The flowers continue to move.

The grass remains still.

Jake lies down.

Sometimes he faces the wall.

Sometimes he curls around the teddy bear.

Increasingly, he simply lies face down.

No one comes to check on him.

No one knocks.

The floor remains silent.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Three weeks.

No incident.

No emergency.

No confrontation.

No meaningful conversation.

No visit.

The SAFEHOUSE continues functioning.

C.O.R.E. continues functioning.

L.S.I.N. continues functioning.

KCA continues functioning.

CED continues functioning.

Everyone has work.

Everyone has somewhere to be.

And because everyone has something that needs doing, almost nobody has had time to simply sit with anyone else.

Not Jake.

Not each other.

The common rooms are quieter.

Meals are shorter.

Conversations are shorter.

People pass one another in hallways with folders, equipment, reports, keycards, and tired expressions.

Then they continue walking.

Jake's floor is different.

Nothing moves except the grass.

Nothing speaks except Jake, occasionally, when he speaks to himself.

Tonight, he finishes a drawing.

He places the pencil beside it.

He picks up his teddy bear.

He lies down on the old mattress.

Face down.

One arm around the bear.

A few sketched flowers sway beside him.

Jake closes his eyes.

There is no knock.

No footsteps.

No voice calling his name.

Downstairs, work continues.

Upstairs, Jake sleeps.

And for the third consecutive week,

**nobody comes.**
