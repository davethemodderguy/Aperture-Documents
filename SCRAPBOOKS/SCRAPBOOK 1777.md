# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the stabilization of Jake's voluntary, permanent transformation, C.O.R.E. has identified a consistent pattern in his daily activities.

Jake is frequently seen near three residents:

1. **Phoenix** — his most frequent companion;
    
2. **Clover Luminare** — his housemate and a familiar presence in his private living space;
    
3. **Bao** — a source of comfort, food, and quiet maternal care.
    

The distribution is not equal.

Phoenix is, by a considerable margin, the person Jake most frequently chooses to spend time with.

He may sit beside her, sleep on her lap, follow her through the SAFEHOUSE, or simply occupy the same room without interacting.

Clover and Bao remain important presences, particularly when Jake wants familiar surroundings or a quieter form of companionship.

However, C.O.R.E. has observed another development.

Jake has become increasingly comfortable exercising his right to be a cat.

Not merely to behave like one while humanoid.

He may voluntarily transform into his full feline form simply because he feels like it.

He may then spend several hours doing absolutely nothing of particular importance.

He may nap.

He may loaf.

He may groom himself.

He may sit in a sunbeam.

He may climb onto Phoenix and fall asleep before she has finished saying hello.

He may walk into a room, inspect everyone present, and leave without explanation.

**No task is required. No purpose is necessary. No productivity is expected.**

Jake is allowed to be careless with his time.

He is allowed to be silly.

He is allowed to enjoy himself without turning the experience into an accomplishment.

For someone whose life has repeatedly been shaped by fear, medical emergencies, and the need to survive, this is considered a meaningful improvement in his day-to-day quality of life.

---

# 1. PHOENIX — PRIMARY COMPANION

**OBSERVATION FREQUENCY:** VERY HIGH

Phoenix is now the resident most frequently found near Jake.

This is not the result of a formal assignment.

No one instructed Phoenix to remain beside him.

No one instructed Jake to seek her out.

The arrangement developed naturally.

Phoenix likes cats.

Jake can be a cat.

Phoenix loves Jake.

Jake appears comfortable around Phoenix.

The resulting situation has been described by one anonymous observer as:

> "A completely predictable disaster for Phoenix's ability to behave normally."

The description was considered accurate.

### Typical interactions

Jake may be found:

- Sleeping on Phoenix's lap while she reads;
    
- Sitting beside her on the couch;
    
- Following her into the common room;
    
- Curling up against her arm;
    
- Asking for attention by rubbing his cheek against her hand;
    
- Grooming his hands while sitting beside her in humanoid form;
    
- Transforming into a cat and immediately occupying the nearest comfortable surface;
    
- Or doing nothing whatsoever while Phoenix quietly keeps him company.
    

Phoenix has learned not to interpret every visit as a request for interaction.

Sometimes Jake wants to be petted.

Sometimes he wants to sleep near her.

Sometimes he wants to occupy her lap while she does something else.

And sometimes he simply wants to know that she is there.

Phoenix has become reasonably good at recognizing the difference.

Her restraint is particularly notable given her enthusiasm.

## INCIDENT 01 — THE UNANNOUNCED LAP OCCUPATION

**LOCATION:** COMMON ROOM  
**TIME:** 11:23

Phoenix was sitting on the couch, reading.

Jake entered in humanoid form.

His ears were relaxed, and his tail moved lazily behind him.

He looked at Phoenix.

She looked up.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Hi, Jake."

**JAKE:**  
"Hi."

He approached.

Phoenix moved her book aside.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Want to sit?"

Jake nodded.

He climbed onto the couch, sat beside her, and leaned against her arm.

For approximately thirty seconds, nothing happened.

Then Jake's body shimmered with pale blue light.

His humanoid form disappeared.

A small white cat occupied the space beside Phoenix.

Phoenix looked down.

The cat looked up.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Oh. Cat mode?"

Jake meowed.

**PHOENIX:**  
"All right."

Jake walked across her lap, turned twice, tucked his paws beneath himself, and closed his eyes.

Phoenix resumed reading.

Three minutes later, she noticed that one of his ears was pressed against her sleeve.

She carefully adjusted her arm to give him more room.

Jake purred.

Phoenix smiled at her book.

She did not announce the incident to the entire SAFEHOUSE.

This was considered progress.

# 2. THE RIGHT TO BE A CAT

C.O.R.E. has revised its terminology regarding Jake's transformation.

Previous documentation primarily described the feline form in terms of anomalous physiology, transformation mechanics, and recovery.

Those details remain relevant to medical staff.

They do not, however, describe the entirety of Jake's current experience.

The feline form is not merely a condition to monitor.

It is a form Jake actively enjoys.

He can choose it.

He can leave it.

He can return to it.

And he can remain in it simply because he would prefer to spend the afternoon as a cat.

There is no requirement for him to justify that preference.

### Observed behavior

Jake has voluntarily transformed into a cat to:

- Sleep in a warm patch of sunlight;
    
- Curl up on Phoenix's lap;
    
- Rest beside Clover while she works;
    
- Sit near Bao in the kitchen;
    
- Investigate a paper bag;
    
- Watch dust move through a shaft of light;
    
- Groom his hands;
    
- Play with a loose blanket thread under supervision;
    
- Occupy a windowsill;
    
- And avoid participating in a conversation he found uninteresting.
    

The final incident was not considered a behavioral concern.

Jake was not required to attend the conversation.

He was not a staff member.

He was not an employee.

He was not an anomalous research instrument.

He was a child who decided that being a cat sounded more appealing than listening to three adults discuss maintenance schedules.

C.O.R.E. considers this an entirely reasonable decision.

# 3. PHOENIX'S CAT POLICY

Phoenix has informally established several principles regarding Jake's feline form.

They are not official SAFEHOUSE regulations.

They are, however, sensible.

**PHOENIX'S RULES FOR JAKE:**

- If he approaches, let him approach.
    
- If he wants pets, give him pets.
    
- If he wants to sleep, let him sleep.
    
- If he wants to leave, let him leave.
    
- If he transforms into a cat, do not make it a dramatic event.
    
- If he chooses a comfortable surface, check that it is safe.
    
- If he wants to play, play with him.
    
- If he does not want to play, find something else to do.
    
- Do not give him cat food.
    
- Do not pull his tail.
    
- Do not assume he is being difficult when he is simply being a cat.
    
- Do not turn every cute thing he does into a public announcement.
    

The last rule was added after Phoenix repeatedly violated it.

She has since improved.

Occasionally.

### Recorded exchange

**PHOENIX:**  
"Jake, you're so—"

Jake's ears flattened.

Phoenix stopped.

**PHOENIX:**  
"—comfortable."

Jake looked at her suspiciously.

**PHOENIX:**  
"You're so comfortable."

Jake slowly relaxed.

He returned to grooming one paw.

Phoenix nodded to herself.

**PHOENIX:**  
"I'm learning."

From across the room, Aeterna replied:

**AETERNA:**  
"Slowly."

# 4. CLOVER — FAMILIARITY WITHOUT PRESSURE

Jake continues to spend time in the residence he shares with Clover.

Clover does not attempt to compete with Phoenix for his attention.

She does not interpret Jake's frequent visits to Phoenix as rejection.

She understands that people can feel comfortable with different companions in different circumstances.

Her own relationship with Jake is quieter.

She is often present when he wants to read, rest, or spend time in familiar surroundings.

Jake may sit beside her desk while she draws.

He may curl up on his old blanket.

He may climb onto her lap without warning.

He may also remain on the opposite side of the room for hours.

Clover accommodates all four possibilities.

### INCIDENT 02 — THE DRAWING SESSION

Clover was sketching at her desk when Jake entered.

He was in humanoid form.

He approached the desk and examined the drawing.

**CLOVER:**  
"Do you like it?"

Jake nodded.

He watched her draw for another minute.

Then he transformed into a cat.

Clover paused.

The small white cat jumped onto the nearby cushion, turned twice, and loafed.

Clover looked at him.

**CLOVER:**  
"Are you going to supervise?"

Jake closed his eyes.

Clover continued drawing.

Twenty minutes later, she looked over again.

Jake had rolled onto his side.

One paw rested against the edge of the cushion.

His tail twitched occasionally.

He was asleep.

Clover quietly placed a folded blanket beside him.

Jake shifted onto it without waking fully.

She returned to her drawing.

The session continued.

No conversation was required.

No explanation was requested.

Jake had chosen to spend time with her.

That was enough.

# 5. BAO — THE KITCHEN CAT

Bao remains another person Jake frequently seeks out.

His visits often coincide with meals, although C.O.R.E. cannot conclusively determine whether this is due to affection, familiarity, hunger, or the fact that Bao's kitchen is a particularly pleasant place to be.

All four explanations are considered plausible.

Jake is not fed commercial cat food.

His meals continue to consist of appropriate food prepared according to his current medical and nutritional needs.

His feline form does not invalidate those requirements.

### INCIDENT 03 — QUALITY CONTROL

Bao was preparing dinner when Jake entered in feline form.

He jumped onto an approved padded chair beside the kitchen counter.

Bao glanced over.

**BAO:**  
"Hello, sweetheart."

Jake meowed.

Bao continued preparing the meal.

Jake watched her.

She placed a small portion of plain cooked chicken into his dish, checked that it had cooled sufficiently, and set it within reach.

Jake sniffed it.

He ate.

Bao resumed cooking.

After finishing, Jake washed his face with one paw.

Then he tucked his paws beneath himself and loafed on the chair.

Bao looked over.

**BAO:**  
"Finished?"

Jake's ear turned toward her.

**BAO:**  
"All right. I'll clean that up later."

She did not immediately remove the dish while he was still using the area.

Jake closed his eyes.

For the next fifteen minutes, Bao cooked while a small white cat rested nearby.

At no point did Jake attempt to help.

No one expected him to.

# 6. THE THREE-PERSON PATTERN

C.O.R.E. has reviewed Jake's recent movement and companionship patterns.

The following summary is qualitative rather than a precise statistical measurement.

|Resident|Typical interaction|
|---|---|
|**Phoenix**|Frequent voluntary contact, lap sleeping, petting, play, shared quiet time|
|**Clover**|Familiar surroundings, reading, drawing, quiet companionship, resting together|
|**Bao**|Meals, kitchen visits, gentle care, familiar routines, comfortable proximity|

These categories overlap.

Jake may spend the morning with Phoenix, return to his room with Clover, and visit Bao before dinner.

He may spend an entire day with Phoenix.

He may spend a day mostly alone.

He may transform into a cat in the middle of any of these arrangements.

No fixed schedule has been imposed.

The pattern is not a hierarchy of affection.

Phoenix simply happens to be the person Jake most frequently seeks out.

Clover and Bao remain important in their own ways.

And Jake is permitted to choose.

# 7. WHEN JAKE DOES ABSOLUTELY NOTHING

A separate observation has been added to the welfare file.

Jake is increasingly comfortable doing things that have no obvious purpose.

He may spend twenty minutes watching sunlight move across the floor.

He may sit on a windowsill with his tail curled around his paws.

He may groom his hands.

He may nap.

He may wake up, change into a cat, and immediately go back to sleep.

He may walk into the common room, sit beside Phoenix, and remain there without asking for anything.

He may play with a blanket for several minutes before losing interest.

He may then decide that the blanket is beneath his dignity and fall asleep on it anyway.

Personnel have been reminded that these activities do not require intervention.

Jake does not need to be constantly entertained.

He does not need to demonstrate improvement through activity.

He does not need to prove that he is grateful for his current circumstances.

And he does not need to turn his free time into a recovery exercise.

### Welfare interpretation

Jake's history includes prolonged fear, serious medical crises, and incidents in which his safety was compromised.

His present willingness to relax is encouraging.

The ability to be idle without anticipating punishment or danger may be particularly valuable for him.

C.O.R.E. will not assume that a few peaceful afternoons have resolved his underlying distress.

However, personnel should not undermine those afternoons by treating every quiet moment as a problem to solve.

**Sometimes the correct welfare intervention is to let a child have a lazy day.**

---

# 8. MEDICAL LIMITATIONS STILL APPLY

Jake's freedom to be a cat does not remove the need for reasonable safety precautions.

His current condition is much improved, but his anomalous physiology remains under observation.

Personnel should continue to ensure that:

- He has access to fresh water and suitable food;
    
- His resting areas are clean and safe;
    
- He cannot accidentally fall from an unsafe height;
    
- His teddy bear remains accessible;
    
- His medical needs are addressed when necessary;
    
- And his voluntary transformation is not interrupted without a valid reason.
    

Ordinary care should remain ordinary wherever possible.

If Jake wants to sleep on Phoenix's lap, there is no need to turn it into a medical examination.

If he wants to curl up beside Clover while she draws, there is no need to record every movement.

If he visits Bao's kitchen, there is no need to interpret his presence as a symptom.

Observe when observation is necessary.

Intervene when intervention is necessary.

Otherwise, allow Jake to live.

# 9. FINAL OBSERVATION — HIS CHOICE

**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — COMMON ROOM  
**TIME:** 21:16

Phoenix was sitting on the couch.

Clover was working at her desk in the adjoining room.

Bao was preparing something in the kitchen.

Jake entered in humanoid form.

He looked toward Phoenix.

She looked up.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Hey."

**JAKE:**  
"Hey."

He walked over and sat beside her.

For a while, they watched the television.

Jake groomed his hands absently.

Phoenix continued watching the screen.

His ears turned toward the kitchen when Bao opened a cupboard.

His tail curled around his leg.

After several minutes, he leaned against Phoenix's arm.

She rested a hand lightly against his shoulder.

Jake closed his eyes.

Then, without warning, his form shimmered.

A small white cat occupied the space beside her.

Phoenix glanced down.

**PHOENIX:**  
"Comfortable?"

Jake meowed.

He climbed onto her lap, turned twice, and tucked himself into a loaf.

Phoenix adjusted the blanket around him.

She did not ask why he had changed.

She did not ask whether he intended to change back.

She did not tell him how adorable he looked.

She simply resumed watching television, one hand resting gently against his back.

A few minutes later, Clover entered the common room.

She saw Jake.

She smiled.

**CLOVER:**  
"He's a cat tonight?"

**PHOENIX:**  
"Looks like it."

Clover nodded and sat nearby.

Bao eventually joined them.

She brought a drink for herself and left fresh water within Jake's reach.

Nobody asked him to change back.

Nobody needed him to.

At 22:03, Jake woke up.

He stretched.

He jumped down from Phoenix's lap and walked toward Clover.

He sat beside her chair.

Clover offered him a hand.

He rubbed his cheek against it.

Then he continued toward the kitchen.

Bao looked down as he arrived.

**BAO:**  
"Hello again."

Jake meowed.

He sat beside her feet for a while, then wandered back toward the common room.

No one called him back.

No one worried that he was choosing someone else.

No one demanded that he stay.

He was free to move between the people he liked.

He was free to rest alone.

He was free to be a cat.

And tomorrow, he might wake up and choose to be a little boy with white hair, azure eyes, feline ears, and a tail.

Either way, the SAFEHOUSE would still be there.

Phoenix would still be delighted to see him.

Clover would still leave his side of the room untouched.

Bao would still make sure he had something suitable to eat.

And Jake would still be allowed to decide where he wanted to spend his time.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake is most frequently found near Phoenix.

He is also frequently found near Clover and Bao.

The reasons vary.

Affection.

Familiarity.

Food.

Comfort.

A warm lap.

A quiet room.

The absence of any obligation to explain himself.

All are acceptable.

The most important change is not that Jake has become more sociable.

It is that he appears increasingly comfortable making choices without feeling that each choice must serve a purpose.

He can seek company without owing anyone a conversation.

He can accept affection without repaying it.

He can spend time with one person without rejecting another.

He can transform into a cat without treating it as an event.

He can be playful, sleepy, lazy, curious, or completely uninterested in whatever everyone else is doing.

He can simply exist.

And if he decides that the most important task of the afternoon is to curl up on Phoenix's lap and sleep for three hours?

That is his decision.

No report is required.

No productivity target applies.

No one needs to make him useful.

**Jake is allowed to be a child.**

**Jake is allowed to be a cat.**

**Jake is allowed to be both, in whichever form he chooses.**

And for once, nobody is asking him to be anything else.
