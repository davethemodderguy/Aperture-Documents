# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Clover Luminare has confirmed that the two-dimensional flowers manifested by Jake inside his room are **edible.**

The flowers retain their original sketched appearance.

They remain visibly flat, cartoon-like, and inconsistent with conventional three-dimensional plant morphology.

Despite this, they possess physical mass, can be picked up, and can be consumed.

Their taste has been described as:

> **"Edible crayons."**

No toxic effects have been observed.

Jake has apparently known this for some time.

C.O.R.E. was not informed.

---

# 1. DISCOVERY

Clover was sitting in Jake's room while completing paperwork.

Jake was lying on the grass-covered floor nearby.

Several blue flowers were growing among the grass.

Clover had previously assumed they were decorative manifestations.

She picked one up.

The flower remained completely flat in her fingers.

She examined it.

Then looked at Jake.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Can you eat these?"

Jake looked up.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

Clover paused.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...You've eaten them?"

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Sometimes."

Clover looked at the flower again.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Why didn't you tell anyone?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Nobody asked."

---

# 2. INITIAL TASTE TEST

Clover hesitated.

She took a very small bite.

The flower made a faint paper-like sound.

It did not tear like paper.

It behaved more like edible material attempting to imitate a drawing of paper.

Clover chewed.

She stopped.

Jake watched her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Good?"

Clover considered the question carefully.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...It tastes like a crayon."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Why?"

> **JAKE:**  
> "That's what they taste like."

Clover looked at the flower.

Then took another bite.

---

# 3. PHYSICAL PROPERTIES

The flowers possess several unusual characteristics.

They are:

- visibly two-dimensional;
    
- physically tangible;
    
- detachable from their stems;
    
- edible;
    
- apparently non-toxic;
    
- capable of growing from Jake's manifested grass;
    
- capable of being handled without losing their anomalous properties.
    

When consumed, the flower does not behave like ordinary paper.

It is metabolized sufficiently to provide at least minimal nutritional value.

C.O.R.E. has not yet determined how.

---

# 4. THE FLOWERS ARE STILL DRAWINGS

Despite being edible, the flowers retain their drawn appearance.

A flower placed flat against a table appears almost completely two-dimensional.

When picked up, it does not naturally unfold into a conventional three-dimensional object.

Its stem remains visually thin.

Its petals remain visually flat.

Its internal structure appears to possess depth only when physically interacted with.

This suggests that Jake's manifestation ability does not simply create ordinary objects.

It appears to create:

> **Physical objects according to the logic of a drawing.**

---

# 5. JAKE'S REACTION

Jake appeared completely unsurprised by Clover's discovery.

He continued lying in the grass.

Clover looked at him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You eat these?"

> **JAKE:**  
> "Sometimes."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "How many?"

Jake shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Some."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "That's not a number."

Jake thought about it.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...A lot?"

Clover stared at him.

Jake stared back.

Neither spoke.

---

# 6. CLOVER'S COOKING EXPERIMENTS

Clover immediately became interested in the culinary potential.

This was not approved as a formal research project.

It happened because Clover is Clover.

She picked several flowers.

She placed them beside ordinary ingredients.

Jake watched.

> **JAKE:**  
> "What are you doing?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Cooking."

> **JAKE:**  
> "With my flowers?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Experimenting."

Jake thought for a moment.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Okay."

No anomalous reaction occurred when the flowers were incorporated into ordinary food.

The resulting dish remained edible.

The flavor was described as:

> **"Still like crayons."**

Clover elected not to serve the result to anyone.

---

# 7. NUTRITIONAL VALUE

Preliminary testing indicates that the flowers provide some nutritional value.

However, C.O.R.E. strongly recommends against treating Jake's manifested flora as a replacement for conventional food.

They are anomalous manifestations.

Their nutritional composition may vary according to:

- Jake's emotional state;
    
- artistic intent;
    
- drawing style;
    
- manifestation stability;
    
- environmental conditions;
    
- and other unknown factors.
    

Jake is still expected to eat normal food.

---

# 8. THE GARDEN

Clover's real four-leaf clovers continue to grow alongside Jake's blue flowers.

The distinction is now considerably more obvious.

Clover's clovers:

**Three-dimensional.**

Jake's flowers:

**Two-dimensional.**

Clover's clovers:

**Normal plant material.**

Jake's flowers:

**Edible crayons.**

Despite the differences, the two continue growing together.

Clover has decided that this makes the garden more interesting.

Jake agrees.

# 10. C.O.R.E. SAFETY NOTE

The flowers are currently considered safe for incidental consumption.

However:

**Do not encourage Jake to replace meals with them.**

**Do not harvest large quantities without permission.**

**Do not attempt to cultivate them outside Jake's established manifestation environment.**

**Do not assume every object Jake draws is edible.**

The fact that a flower can be eaten does not establish that:

> **Everything Jake draws can be eaten.**

This distinction has been explicitly added following a suggestion from Alice to determine whether a drawn axe would taste like wood.

The suggestion was rejected.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Clover had originally thought Jake's room contained a collection of strange drawings that happened to become real.

She now understands that the room is slightly stranger than that.

The grass is real.

The flowers are real.

The clovers are real.

And apparently, some of the flowers are lunch.

That evening, Clover was sitting on the floor doing paperwork.

Jake was sitting on her lap.

He reached down, picked a small blue flower from the grass, and held it up.

Clover looked at it.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You're not eating another one."

Jake paused.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Why?"

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You already had dinner."

Jake looked at the flower.

Then at Clover.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Dessert?"

Clover sighed.

She took the flower from him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Fine."

She handed it back.

Jake smiled faintly and took a bite.

Clover returned to her paperwork.

Apparently, being a caretaker now involved preventing a six-year-old star from eating his own artwork between meals.
