# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### ANOMALOUS RESIDENT & PSYCHOLOGICAL WELFARE DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-WEL-4C-JAKE-436  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — RESIDENT WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Jake  
**EVENT:** Historical Trauma / Post-Mortem Psychological Distress

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has been deceased for **more than seventy-five years.**

His physical death occurred long before Aperture Industries constructed the current Blacksite on the surrounding property.

His spirit remained.

His memories remained.

And, significantly:

**The psychological consequences of his death remained as well.**

Initial observations suggested that Jake had accepted his death.

He has previously described the event with unusual calmness:

> **“I'm very hungry, so I sleep it off. The next day I woke up I am a ghost and my body is on the ground. I cried, but I got over anyways.”**

Further observation indicates that this statement was incomplete.

Jake did not "get over it."

He learned how to talk about it.

There is a substantial difference.

---

# 2. THE LAST DAY

Jake was approximately six years old when he died.

Available evidence indicates that he had been alone and severely hungry.

At some point, he went to sleep.

He did not wake up alive.

He woke as a spirit.

His body was still present.

He cried.

Nobody came.

Nobody found him.

Nobody answered.

Eventually, he stopped crying.

The event ended.

The memory did not.

---

# 3. SEVENTY-FIVE YEARS LATER

Jake continues to demonstrate behavioral patterns directly associated with his death.

Most notably:

- searching for food at night;
    
- staring into occupied buildings;
    
- lingering near cafeterias;
    
- watching people eat;
    
- repeatedly asking about food;
    
- appearing particularly interested in anything that resembles something edible;
    
- remaining near populated areas without entering;
    
- becoming quiet when asked about his death;
    
- avoiding discussion of the circumstances surrounding his final day.
    

These behaviors initially appeared to be simple manifestations of his death by starvation.

They are now understood to be more complicated.

Jake is not merely hungry.

He remembers **being hungry and having nobody come.**

---

# 4. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN MEMORY AND EXPERIENCE

C.O.R.E. Psychological Welfare personnel initially assumed that the seventy-five-plus-year interval would have reduced the emotional impact of the event.

This assumption was incorrect.

Jake does not appear to experience the passage of time in the same manner as living residents.

His body did not age.

His life did not continue.

His environment changed around him.

A field became a Blacksite.

A building appeared where there had once been nothing.

Generations passed.

People came and went.

Jake remained six years old.

The event that ended his life therefore remains one of the defining experiences available to him.

There was no later childhood.

No adolescence.

No adulthood.

No subsequent life experience capable of naturally contextualizing the memory.

From Jake's perspective:

> **He was six.**

Then he died.

Then he continued existing.

---

# 5. NIGHTTIME BEHAVIOR

Jake manifests only at night.

This was initially considered a property of his anomalous state.

It may also have psychological significance.

Jake died after going to sleep.

Consequently, darkness and sleep appear repeatedly in his recollections.

During observation, Jake has occasionally stopped walking when the corridor lights transition into nighttime illumination.

He does not panic.

He simply becomes quiet.

On one occasion, Nora asked:

> **Nora:**  
> “Are you okay?”

Jake answered:

> **Jake:**  
> “Yeah.”

A few seconds passed.

> **Jake:**  
> “I don't like bedtime.”

Nora did not ask why.

She simply stayed beside him.

---

# 6. THE BODY

The most severe element of Jake's residual trauma appears to involve his own corpse.

Jake has never voluntarily approached the location where his body was originally discovered.

When shown archival reconstruction material depicting the area, Jake immediately became distressed.

He identified the location.

> **Jake:**  
> “That's where I was.”

Personnel asked:

> “Where you died?”

Jake shook his head.

> **Jake:**  
> “Where my body was.”

The distinction was noted.

Jake does not appear to conceptualize his death solely as:

> **“I died there.”**

He remembers:

> **“My body was there and nobody found it.”**

---

# 7. THE MOST CONCERNING STATEMENT

During a welfare interview, Jake was asked what he remembered immediately after dying.

He initially gave his usual answer.

Then he became silent.

After approximately thirty seconds:

> **Jake:**  
> “I thought someone would come.”

The interviewer did not interrupt.

> **Jake:**  
> “I waited.”

Pause.

> **Jake:**  
> “Then I waited more.”

Jake looked toward the floor.

> **Jake:**  
> “Nobody came.”

The interviewer asked whether he remembered how long he waited.

Jake shook his head.

> **Jake:**  
> “A long time.”

Then:

> **Jake:**  
> “I was still hungry.”

---

# 8. WHY THE BULLSEYE MATTERS

Jake's fixation on the Tachyon Merch Store has previously been documented as curiosity.

This remains partially true.

However, personnel have identified another possible reason.

The store is:

- brightly illuminated;
    
- visibly occupied during the day;
    
- associated with food brought by Nora;
    
- and surrounded by people.
    

Jake does not attempt to enter the store.

He watches from outside.

He stands behind the security glass.

He observes people moving around inside.

He waits.

This behavior is remarkably similar to his repeated nighttime wandering near the cafeteria.

He sees food.

He sees people.

He remains outside.

---

# 9. NORA'S RESPONSE

Nora appears to have noticed the pattern before most personnel.

During one nighttime walk, Jake stopped outside the cafeteria.

He watched through the glass.

Nora stood beside him.

> **Nora:**  
> “You can stay with me.”

Jake looked at her.

> **Jake:**  
> “Why?”

Nora shrugged.

> **Nora:**  
> “Because.”

Jake stared at her.

> **Jake:**  
> “That's not an answer.”

Nora smiled.

> **Nora:**  
> “I know.”

Jake remained beside her.

He did not ask for food.

He did not ask to leave.

He simply stayed.

---

# 10. AETERNA'S OBSERVATION

Aeterna has now directly observed Jake's nighttime behavior.

Her assessment was brief.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “He is still waiting.”

Nora looked at her.

> **Nora:**  
> “For what?”

Aeterna looked toward Jake.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “Someone to come.”

No further explanation was provided.

---

# 11. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's condition should not be reduced to:

> **“He is sad because he died.”**

The evidence indicates a more specific and persistent trauma.

Jake experienced:

**hunger → isolation → sleep → death → awakening alone → waiting → no rescue.**

The sequence appears to have become psychologically fixed.

His continued existence does not appear to have erased it.

Neither has the passage of seventy-five years.

He can laugh.

He can talk.

He can make friends.

He can become interested in objects.

He can call something "cool."

He can accept cake even though he cannot eat it.

None of these behaviors indicate that the underlying trauma has disappeared.

They indicate that Jake is capable of experiencing things **in addition to it.**

---

# 12. IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Jake is not constantly distressed.

He does not spend every night crying.

He does not appear to be permanently trapped in the moment of his death.

He can function.

He can socialize.

He can explore.

He can form attachments.

However, certain circumstances reliably bring aspects of the memory back.

Food.

Darkness.

Sleeping.

Being alone.

Waiting.

Empty rooms.

Being unable to physically interact with the living.

These are not necessarily triggers in the conventional sense.

They are simply reminders of the world in which Jake died.

---

# 13. CURRENT SUPPORT

Personnel are instructed not to force Jake to discuss his death.

Do not repeatedly ask:

> “What happened to you?”

Do not tell him:

> “It was a long time ago.”

Do not tell him:

> “You should be over it by now.”

Do not treat his calm description of the event as evidence that it no longer affects him.

Instead:

Allow Jake to choose when to speak.

Remain available.

Include him in ordinary activities where possible.

Do not make his death the defining feature of his existence.

If he wants to talk about it:

**Listen.**

If he does not:

**Stay anyway.**

---

# 14. CURRENT RESIDENT RESPONSE

Nora has continued accompanying Jake during nighttime hours.

Aeterna has begun accompanying them as well.

This appears to have had an unexpected effect.

Jake no longer spends the entirety of his nighttime wandering alone.

He now has two people who can simply remain nearby.

Neither requires him to explain himself.

Neither requires him to stop being a ghost.

Neither requires him to pretend that seventy-five years erased what happened.

They simply walk with him.

---

# 15. CLOSING OBSERVATION

During a recent observation, Jake stood outside the cafeteria.

The lights were on.

Someone inside was eating cake.

Jake watched through the glass.

Nora stood beside him.

Aeterna stood behind both of them.

After several minutes, Jake quietly said:

> **Jake:**  
> “I used to wait by myself.”

Nora looked at him.

> **Nora:**  
> “You don't have to anymore.”

Jake was silent.

Then he nodded.

He did not smile.

He did not cry.

He simply remained there.

This was considered significant.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

More than seventy-five years have passed since Jake died.

To everyone else, that is an extraordinary amount of time.

To Jake, it is not necessarily distance.

He remembers being six.

He remembers being hungry.

He remembers going to sleep.

He remembers waking up dead.

He remembers looking for someone.

He remembers nobody coming.

And somewhere along the way, he learned to say:

> **“I got over it.”**

Perhaps he did.

Perhaps that is how a six-year-old ghost learned to survive seventy-five years.

But surviving something is not the same as forgetting it.

Jake can still laugh.

He can still make friends.

He can still stand outside the Tachyon Merch Store and decide that a strange lever-action rifle is cool.

He can still walk beside Nora at midnight.

He can still look up at Aeterna and ask questions.

He is still capable of being a child.

But there is a six-year-old part of him that is still standing somewhere in the dark—

**waiting for somebody to come.**

This time,

somebody did.
