# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. — CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE WELFARE & ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOR DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** CORE-SH-INC-184  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — CHILD WELFARE / PSYCHOLOGICAL DISTRESS  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE OBSERVATION  
**SUBJECT:** Sora — Withdrawal Following Discovery  
**RELATED PERSONNEL:** Mia, Jake Akana, Bao, Guiding Light  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7 — SORA'S DEN  
**PRIORITY:** HIGH — CONTINUED WELFARE CONCERNS

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Sora's unexplained return to his den, C.O.R.E. has observed two developments.

First, Mia has stopped visiting him.

Second, Jake has begun spending time beside him.

Neither development represents a resolution to Sora's condition.

Mia is reportedly unable to bring herself to enter Sora's den. She believes the events leading to his injuries and subsequent deterioration were her fault. The argument, her words, and the discovery of Sora's condition have become inseparable in her mind.

She has not forgotten Sora.

She is afraid to face him.

Jake, meanwhile, has been observed beside Sora in his weakened state. During one particularly quiet interaction, Sora weakly reached toward his own tail, briefly touched it, then let go. He subsequently raised his hand and gently ruffled his ears.

The movement was slow and visibly tiring.

It was not an indication of recovery.

Sora remains severely weakened, withdrawn, and unable or unwilling to speak. His wounds remain bandaged. His nutritional and physical recovery cannot be assumed complete.

The sight of him engaging in such a small, familiar activity has nevertheless drawn considerable attention from staff.

For the first time in some time, he appeared to be doing something without being prompted.

That alone should not be mistaken for improvement.

---

## 2. MIA — THE SISTER WHO CANNOT ENTER

Mia has not entered Sora's den since his most recent return.

She has remained elsewhere on Floor 7, occasionally asking staff whether he is eating, drinking, or moving. When asked whether she wishes to visit, she consistently declines.

Her explanations have been brief.

**MIA:** I can't.

**STAFF MEMBER:** You don't have to go in right now.

**MIA:** I hurt him.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Mia—

**MIA:** I made him think he wasn't worth anything.

She reportedly became distressed during this conversation and withdrew before the staff member could continue.

### 2.1 Assessment

Mia's guilt is understandable, but it is becoming another obstacle to the siblings' recovery.

She is six years old.

She is not equipped to process the consequences of the argument, Sora's self-inflicted injury, his subsequent disappearance, and the months of neglect that followed. She should not be expected to independently understand the distinction between her responsibility for hurtful words and the adults' responsibility for maintaining Sora's welfare.

Her actions during the argument hurt Sora. That must not be dismissed.

However, the subsequent failure to consistently monitor a critically vulnerable child was an institutional failure. It was not Mia's responsibility to discover his deterioration, maintain his medical care, or ensure that he ate and drank.

C.O.R.E. will not allow the full weight of that failure to settle on another six-year-old.

Mia does not need to force herself into Sora's den to prove that she cares.

Nor should Sora be expected to forgive her simply because she is ready to apologize.

**Recommendation:** Continue offering Mia age-appropriate emotional support. Do not compel visits, demand apologies, or use Sora's condition to pressure her into approaching him.

The possibility of reconciliation must remain open without becoming an obligation imposed on either child.

---

## 3. JAKE — A QUIET VISIT

Jake has continued visiting Sora.

Unlike previous visits, he has not attempted to initiate games or encourage conversation. He generally stays nearby, allowing Sora to remain in his den without demanding a response.

During one observation, Jake sat beside the opening of the doghouse-like enclosure.

Sora was lying inside.

His white fur had been cleaned. His bandaged wrist remained visible. He appeared exhausted, with little energy for movement.

Jake watched him for a while.

**JAKE:** Hi, Sora.

No response.

Jake did not repeat himself.

He shifted closer, carefully avoiding Sora's injured wrist.

Sora's tail moved slightly.

Jake noticed.

He did not immediately reach for it.

Instead, he waited.

After a while, Sora weakly lifted one hand toward his fluffy white tail. His fingers reached it, rested there briefly, and then released it.

He did not seem to have the strength or inclination to continue.

His hand moved upward.

He gently ruffled his own ears.

Jake watched quietly.

**JAKE:** Your ears are fluffy.

Sora did not answer.

Jake remained beside him.

There was no attempt to make him smile, no insistence that he play, and no demand that he acknowledge the visit.

The interaction continued in silence.

### 3.1 Assessment

The movement was minor, but worth documenting.

Sora's brief contact with his tail and subsequent ear-ruffling may represent a small instance of self-directed activity. It does not establish that his strength, appetite, mobility, or psychological condition has meaningfully improved.

Staff should also avoid interpreting the behavior as an invitation for physical contact.

Jake did not pull Sora's tail, handle his ears, or attempt to reposition him.

He simply stayed.

That restraint was appropriate.

Jake is also six years old. His continued companionship is appreciated, but he must not become Sora's primary caregiver or be expected to compensate for failures in adult supervision.

---

## 4. THE EMPTY SPACE BETWEEN THEM

The current situation has created an uncomfortable separation.

Mia wants to know whether Sora is safe, but cannot bear to see him.

Sora remains in his den, where he has withdrawn from nearly everyone.

Jake visits, but cannot make him well through companionship alone.

These are three children, each carrying a different part of a situation that should never have been theirs to carry.

Mia believes she caused everything.

Sora has previously expressed the belief that he was a failure as an older brother and that everyone would be better without his voice.

Jake has witnessed the consequences and continues to try to stay close, despite having no power to undo what happened.

C.O.R.E. must prevent these children from becoming responsible for one another's survival.

Mia is not required to enter the den.

Sora is not required to receive visitors.

Jake is not required to keep watch.

The adults are required to provide care regardless.

---

## 5. UPDATED WELFARE DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

1. **Medical monitoring remains mandatory.** Sora's weakened state, previous blood loss, nutritional deterioration, and existing injuries require continued professional assessment. His inability to die must never be used to justify reduced care.
    
2. **Food and water must be monitored directly.** Meals and drinks should be offered in a manner that does not pressure or frighten him. Staff must document actual intake rather than assuming that food left nearby has been consumed.
    
3. **Visits must remain voluntary.** Jake, Mia, Guiding Light, and other familiar individuals may be offered opportunities to visit, but Sora's refusal or lack of response must be respected.
    
4. **Mia must receive independent support.** Her guilt must be addressed without making her responsible for Sora's recovery. She must not be pressured to apologize in person.
    
5. **Jake must retain his childhood.** Staff must not assign him monitoring duties, emotional responsibilities, or expectations of constant companionship.
    
6. **The den must remain accessible and safe.** Its familiar structure should be preserved where possible. It is a retreat, not a substitute for medical care or a justification for leaving Sora isolated.
    
7. **The unexplained relocation remains under investigation.** No punitive action is authorized against Sora. His return to the den does not establish misconduct.
    

---

## 6. CLOSING NOTE — A LITTLE MOVEMENT

The observation lasted only a few minutes.

Sora reached for his tail.

He let go.

Then he ruffled his ears.

That was all.

Jake did not celebrate. He did not tell anyone that Sora was getting better. He understood, in the limited way a six-year-old could, that his friend was tired.

So he stayed nearby.

Elsewhere, Mia remained unable to visit. She wanted her brother back, but the thought of seeing him reminded her of what she had said and what followed.

She is hurting, too.

But Sora cannot be asked to comfort her. Jake cannot be asked to fix either of them. And Mia cannot be asked to repair an injury by simply wishing she had never caused it.

There are adults in this facility. There are medical personnel, welfare officers, supervisors, and an entire organization that claims responsibility for protecting the children under its care.

They must act like it.

Sora's tail is still fluffy. His ears are still there. He can still reach for them, even if only briefly.

Those small movements deserve to be noticed.

But they do not erase the months he spent deteriorating without adequate care.

**We will not mistake a child finding the strength to move his hand for a child no longer needing help.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
