### MEDICAL RESEARCH & TREATMENT EXECUTIVE

**SECONDARY SUBJECT:** Clover Luminare

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A recreational activity between **Jake** and **Clover Luminare** resulted in an unexpected medical emergency.

Jake and Clover were playing catch with Jake's teddy bear.

Clover threw the bear.

The throw was comparable in speed and force to an ordinary baseball being thrown against a wall.

The bear struck the wall.

Jake immediately experienced the impact through the previously documented sensory connection between himself and the teddy bear.

The resulting pain was severe.

Jake subsequently experienced:

- widespread pain;
    
- severe chest pain;
    
- significant cardiac pain;
    
- repeated coughing;
    
- difficulty breathing;
    
- impaired speech;
    
- and extreme physical distress.
    

Jake was unable to communicate normally.

Clover immediately recognized that something was seriously wrong.

She has since displayed significant guilt regarding the incident.

This guilt is being compounded by her established survivor's guilt surrounding the death of her father.

---

# 1. THE GAME

The activity began normally.

Jake suggested playing catch.

Clover agreed.

The teddy bear was thrown back and forth between them.

There was no indication that either participant understood the degree to which the bear's sensory feedback had become integrated with Jake's physical experience.

Previous testing had established that Jake could feel:

- being touched through the bear;
    
- pressure against the bear;
    
- the bear's movement;
    
- impacts against the bear;
    
- and environmental contact.
    

The possibility of a high-force impact had not been encountered during ordinary play.

---

# 2. THE IMPACT

Clover threw the bear.

The throw was intended to be routine.

The bear struck the wall.

The impact was substantially stronger than the previous incidental contacts documented during testing.

Jake stopped moving.

Clover initially believed he had simply been startled.

Then Jake bent forward.

His hands went immediately to his chest.

---

# 3. ACUTE RESPONSE

Clover approached him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Jake?"

Jake attempted to answer.

Nothing intelligible came out.

He tried again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...ah—"

He coughed.

Then again.

The coughing continued.

Jake struggled to draw a full breath.

His entire body appeared tense from the pain.

Clover immediately understood.

The bear was still on the floor.

She picked it up.

Jake reacted again.

The movement of the bear produced another sensation.

Clover immediately placed it down.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Okay. Okay. I'm not touching it."

Jake remained hunched over.

His breathing was shallow and irregular.

---

# 4. COMMUNICATION FAILURE

Jake attempted to speak several times.

He could not form complete sentences.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It... hurts..."

Cough.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Chest..."

Another cough.

He attempted to breathe.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Can't..."

The remainder of the sentence was lost to coughing.

Clover stopped asking questions.

She recognized that Jake did not have enough breath or concentration to answer them.

She stayed beside him and called for medical assistance.

---

# 5. MEDICAL RESPONSE

Medical personnel arrived shortly afterward.

Jake remained conscious.

He was, however, in considerable distress.

The primary concerns were:

**Respiratory difficulty.**

**Severe chest pain.**

**Cardiac pain.**

**Widespread pain consistent with the transmitted impact.**

The teddy bear was secured without further unnecessary movement.

Jake was kept still.

Medical personnel focused on stabilizing him rather than questioning him.

---

# 6. THE TEDDY BEAR

The bear itself showed no catastrophic physical damage.

This was considered particularly significant.

The physical injury to the bear did not correspond to the severity of Jake's subjective experience.

The sensory connection does not appear to require proportional visible damage.

In other words:

> **The bear does not need to look badly damaged for Jake to experience severe pain.**

This finding has been added to Jake's welfare precautions.

---

# 7. CLOVER

Clover remained at the scene.

She was visibly shaken.

She repeatedly looked between Jake and the teddy bear.

At one point she whispered:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I did that."

No one answered immediately.

Clover continued:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I threw it."

Her hands were shaking.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I knew it was connected to him."

She stopped.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I didn't know it would..."

She could not finish.

---

# 8. SURVIVOR'S GUILT RESPONSE

C.O.R.E. Medical personnel identified a secondary psychological concern.

The incident appears to have activated Clover's pre-existing survivor's guilt.

Clover has historically struggled with the belief that surviving her father's death makes her responsible for what happened.

Her luck protected **her** during the accident.

It did not protect her father.

She was subsequently blamed for surviving.

The current incident has created a familiar internal pattern:

> **Someone was hurt.**

> **Clover was involved.**

> **Clover survived.**

> **Therefore, Clover believes she should have prevented it.**

This interpretation is not supported by the circumstances.

The incident was accidental.

Clover did not know the throw would produce this degree of sensory feedback.

No malicious intent was present.

---

# 9. CLOVER'S GUILT

While Jake was receiving treatment, Clover remained nearby.

She repeatedly apologized.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'm sorry."

Pause.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'm really sorry."

Another pause.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I should've known."

A medical staff member attempted to reassure her.

Clover shook her head.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "That's what everyone says."

She looked toward Jake.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "But someone still gets hurt."

This statement is consistent with her established survivor's guilt.

---

# 10. JAKE'S CONDITION

Jake eventually became capable of speaking again, although only minimally.

His voice remained extremely weak.

Clover approached him carefully.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Jake?"

Jake looked at her.

She hesitated.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Are you angry?"

Jake shook his head.

Clover looked down.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You don't have to forgive me."

Jake attempted to speak.

His voice was barely audible.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Not mad."

Clover's eyes watered.

Jake coughed again.

Clover immediately moved closer.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Don't talk."

Jake nodded.

---

# 11. BAO'S RESPONSE

Bao arrived shortly afterward.

She immediately recognized Jake's condition.

She approached him carefully.

> **BAO:**  
> "Mom's here."

Jake looked toward her.

He did not answer.

Bao sat beside him.

She did not ask what happened.

She did not ask whether he was okay.

She simply stayed.

Clover remained nearby.

Bao eventually looked at her.

Clover expected anger.

Instead:

> **BAO:**  
> "It was an accident."

Clover looked down.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "But Jake got hurt."

> **BAO:**  
> "Yes."

Pause.

> **BAO:**  
> "And now we're taking care of him."

Clover said nothing.

Bao continued:

> **BAO:**  
> "That's what happens after accidents."

---

# 12. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri arrived later.

She looked at Jake.

Then at the teddy bear.

Then at Clover.

She understood what had happened.

Neri approached Clover.

She did not say anything at first.

Then:

> **NERI:**  
> "Accident."

Clover nodded.

Neri looked at Jake.

Then back at Clover.

> **NERI:**  
> "He's here."

Clover's eyes lowered.

Neri remained beside her.

She did not attempt to make Clover feel better.

She simply stayed.

---

# 13. CLOVER'S BREAKDOWN

Later, when Jake was stable, Clover finally broke down.

She sat on the floor beside the wall the bear had struck.

She stared at it.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "My luck saved me."

Pause.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "It always saves me."

Her voice became quieter.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "But it doesn't save anyone else."

She pressed her hands against her face.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "My dad..."

She stopped.

She did not finish the sentence.

She didn't need to.

The association was obvious.

---

# 14. MEDICAL PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. considers Clover's guilt understandable but disproportionate.

The incident does **not** indicate:

- negligence;
    
- malicious intent;
    
- deliberate recklessness;
    
- failure to care about Jake;
    
- or responsibility for the severity of the anomalous feedback.
    

The sensory connection was recently discovered.

Its response to high-force impact had not been established.

Clover made a reasonable assumption based on ordinary play.

The resulting injury was an anomalous consequence that neither participant anticipated.

---

# 15. UPDATED TEDDY BEAR PROTOCOL

The teddy bear is now classified as an extension of Jake's sensory system.

Personnel are prohibited from treating it as an ordinary plush object.

Specifically:

> **DO NOT THROW THE BEAR.**

Not during games.

Not as a joke.

Not for testing.

Not to see what Jake feels.

Not because someone "forgot."

The bear may still be hugged, carried, held, or gently interacted with.

Jake may still play with it.

The difference is force.

---

# 16. CLOVER'S STATUS

Clover remains Jake's primary day-to-day caretaker.

This incident does **not** constitute grounds for removing her from the role.

However, C.O.R.E. recommends that Clover be temporarily relieved of physically strenuous activities involving Jake's teddy bear until she is comfortable again.

This is not punishment.

It is intended to prevent guilt-driven avoidance from developing.

Jake continues to trust her.

Clover continues to care about him.

Neither fact has changed.

---

# 17. FINAL OBSERVATION

Jake eventually reached for Clover.

She hesitated.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Are you sure?"

Jake nodded.

Clover carefully took his hand.

Jake squeezed it weakly.

Neither spoke.

The teddy bear rested safely beside them.

For several minutes, Clover simply held Jake's hand.

She did not apologize again.

Jake did not ask her to.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Clover's luck has always protected Clover.

It saved her when she was a child.

It could not save her father.

That fact has remained with her for most of her life.

Now she has accidentally hurt someone she cares about.

The circumstances are entirely different.

Her mind does not care.

It has already begun assembling the same conclusion:

> **"I survived again. Someone else got hurt again. Therefore, it must be my fault."**

It isn't.

Jake was hurt.

That matters.

Clover's pain matters too.

But an accident is not a repetition of the past simply because it feels familiar.

Jake eventually managed to speak.

Barely.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Still here."

Clover looked at him.

Jake squeezed her hand again.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You're here too."

Clover cried.

This time, she did not apologize.
