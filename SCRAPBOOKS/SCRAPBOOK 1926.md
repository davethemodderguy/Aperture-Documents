## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Mia reported an unusually vivid dream involving her permanently lost brother, Sora.

The dream took place on a floating island suspended high above the clouds. A cherry blossom tree stood near its center.

The tree was significant to Mia.

It was her favourite.

Underneath it, she found Sora.

Not a photograph. Not a recording. Not a written description.

Her brother, as she remembered him.

The two spent what felt like hours together. They talked, shared time, and experienced a reunion that Mia had been unable to have in waking life.

For the first time since Sora's disappearance, Mia could speak to him without having to explain who she was.

She could talk to her big brother.

Then she said something that changed the entire dream.

**MIA:** I can't believe I found you, Sora.

Sora went silent.

His response was not the one she had hoped to hear.

**SORA:** I'm not Sora.

He continued:

**SORA:** I'm just how you see him.

**SORA:** He is gone. He was at your hand and you let him disappear.

**SORA:** I'm just your mind's desperate attempt to remember, something that you forgotten.

Mia woke abruptly.

The dream ended.

No further conversation was possible.

---

## 2. DREAM RECONSTRUCTION

The following account is reconstructed from Mia's recollection immediately after waking. Minor details may be incomplete.

### 02:?? — The Floating Island

Mia found herself standing on an island in the sky.

There was no visible ground beneath its edges. Clouds drifted below the island, and the surrounding sky appeared calm and bright.

A cherry blossom tree stood nearby.

Its branches extended over the grass, with petals falling gently around its trunk.

Mia recognized the place as somewhere she would have liked to visit.

She approached the tree.

Someone was sitting underneath it.

White hair.

Familiar features.

A little boy.

Sora.

**MIA:** ...Sora?

He looked up.

**SORA:** Hi.

Mia immediately ran toward him.

She stopped just before reaching him, as though afraid that touching him might make him disappear.

**MIA:** You're here!

**SORA:** Yeah.

**MIA:** You're really here!

**SORA:** I think so.

Mia sat beside him.

For a while, neither said anything.

There was no urgency. No medical emergency. No adults calling for assistance. No den with its dark entrance.

Just the two of them beneath a cherry blossom tree.

### 3. THE CONVERSATION

The conversation reportedly continued for hours.

Mia could not recall every subject they discussed. She remembered talking about the farm, their grandmother, the doghouse, and the things they used to do together.

She remembered laughing.

She remembered Sora listening.

Most importantly, she remembered that he seemed familiar.

He did not ask her who she was.

He did not need to be introduced.

He did not look at her as though her face were covered in black scribbles.

For those hours, Mia was not explaining their relationship to someone who could not remember it.

She was simply his little sister.

Eventually, she looked at him and spoke.

**MIA:** I can't believe I found you, Sora.

Sora stopped responding.

His expression changed.

The silence lasted long enough for Mia to notice.

**MIA:** Sora?

He looked away.

**SORA:** I'm not Sora.

Mia blinked.

**MIA:** What?

**SORA:** I'm just how you see him.

**MIA:** But...

**SORA:** He is gone.

A pause.

**SORA:** He was at your hand and you let him disappear.

Mia's expression fell.

**MIA:** No. I—

**SORA:** I'm just your mind's desperate attempt to remember, something that you forgotten.

The dream ended immediately afterward.

### 4. AWAKENING

Mia snapped awake.

She was no longer beneath the cherry blossom tree.

There was no floating island.

No familiar white-haired boy beside her.

Only the room.

She had dreamed of finding Sora.

And then, even in the place where she had found him, she had been told that he was gone.

## 5. C.O.R.E. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

The dream must not be treated as evidence that Sora's consciousness persists elsewhere, nor as confirmation that he has communicated with Mia.

No anomalous activity has been established.

The experience is consistent with a vivid grief-related dream, although C.O.R.E. cannot determine its precise psychological origin from a single account.

The most concerning element is not the setting or the apparent recreation of Sora.

It is the accusation.

> "He was at your hand and you let him disappear."

This statement places responsibility for Sora's loss on Mia.

That accusation is not supported by the available evidence.

Mia is six years old.

She did not have the authority, medical knowledge, resources, or adult responsibility necessary to manage Sora's condition. She was also experiencing her own distress and memory difficulties during the period in which Sora deteriorated.

She could not reasonably have been expected to compensate for the failures of an entire residential institution.

**Mia did not let Sora disappear.**

The adults responsible for the SAFEHOUSE failed to maintain adequate care and oversight. That responsibility cannot be reassigned to a child simply because she was the last person who remembered him.

The dream's words may reflect Mia's own fear and guilt, rather than any truth about Sora.

C.O.R.E. must be particularly careful not to reinforce that guilt.

---

## 6. REQUIRED WELFARE RESPONSE

1. Mia must be allowed to discuss the dream without being told that it was meaningless or that she should simply forget it.
    
2. Staff must not confirm that the dream was an actual encounter with Sora.
    
3. Staff must explicitly reassure Mia that Sora's disappearance was not her responsibility.
    
4. Mia must not be pressured to describe the dream repeatedly.
    
5. The cherry blossom tree and floating island should not be treated as evidence of a recoverable location or destination.
    
6. Any further dreams involving Sora should be documented only with Mia's consent and without encouraging her to seek him through sleep.
    

The most important immediate measure is to ensure that Mia understands one thing:

**She was his little sister. She was not his caretaker.**

---

## 7. CLOSING NOTE — UNDER THE CHERRY BLOSSOM TREE

There is something especially cruel about the way this dream ended.

For hours, Mia had him back.

She could talk to him.

She could laugh with him.

She could sit beneath her favourite tree and spend time with the brother she had been missing.

She did not need to explain the farm.

She did not need to explain the doghouse.

She did not need to remind him that she was Mia.

She had her brother.

And then he told her that he wasn't real.

Perhaps the dream was only her mind trying to reconstruct someone she could no longer find. Perhaps it was grief giving a familiar face to an unbearable absence.

C.O.R.E. cannot know.

But the words he spoke deserve particular scrutiny.

_He was at your hand and you let him disappear._

No.

Mia did not make the medical decisions.

Mia did not control the SAFEHOUSE.

Mia did not oversee the welfare reports.

Mia was not responsible for noticing every missed meal, every unattended injury, every day spent alone in that little den.

She was six.

She was his little sister.

And she loved him.

If Sora had been able to choose what Mia should remember, perhaps it would have been the farm. Perhaps it would have been their grandmother. Perhaps it would have been the days when he chased wolves away while she stayed safely inside the doghouse.

Perhaps he would have wanted her to remember that she was never a burden to him.

We cannot put words into his mouth.

We cannot promise that the boy beneath the cherry blossoms was really Sora.

But we can refuse to let a dream convince a little girl that she caused her brother's loss.

The tree was her favourite.

For a few hours, she got to sit beneath it with him.

When she woke, he was gone again.

**She deserves to remember the hours they spent together without having to carry the blame for how they ended.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
