### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / ENGINEERING & PERSONNEL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — ASSISTIVE MOBILITY DEVICE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 6 — INTERNAL PERSONNEL  
**Designer:** Neko Lucia Rodreguez  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Device:** SAFEHOUSE Electric Mobility Scooter  
**Status:** **OPERATIONAL / UNDERUTILIZED**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Following repeated incidents involving Alice attempting to walk independently and subsequently collapsing, Neko developed a solution.

A small electric scooter was constructed specifically for Alice.

It was not designed for speed.

It was not designed for exploration.

It was not designed to facilitate escape.

It was designed for one purpose:

> **Let Alice get around without having to hurt herself.**

---

# **DESIGN PARAMETERS**

The scooter was constructed around Alice's extremely fragile condition.

Features include:

- Extremely low maximum speed
    
- Automatic emergency braking
    
- High-stability chassis
    
- Cushioned seating
    
- Soft-contact handlebars
    
- Automatic speed reduction around obstacles
    
- Fall detection
    
- Automatic shutdown after collision
    
- Medical alert system
    
- Adjustable support frame
    
- Easy-access controls
    
- Low center of gravity
    
- Reinforced protective enclosure around critical components
    

The scooter also possesses an integrated SAFEHOUSE boundary system.

It **cannot leave the Blacksite.**

Any attempt to cross the authorized perimeter causes the vehicle to automatically stop.

The system is physically incapable of being overridden by Alice.

Neko specifically designed it this way.

---

# **THE REASON**

Neko had watched Alice repeatedly attempt to walk.

He had watched her legs give out.

He had watched her fall.

He had watched medical personnel treat another injury.

And eventually he decided:

> **"If she can't walk safely, then I'll make something that can carry her."**

He did not tell Alice that.

He simply finished the scooter.

---

# **PRESENTATION**

Neko brought the finished device to Alice.

Alice stared at it.

One eye visible beneath her eyepatch.

> **"What's that?"**

Neko answered:

> **"Yours."**

Alice tilted her head.

> **"Mine?"**

> **"Yeah."**

He showed her the controls.

> **"You can use this to get around."**

Alice looked at the scooter.

Then at Neko.

> **"...Why?"**

Neko paused.

> **"Because walking hurts you."**

Alice looked down.

> **"Oh."**

---

# **FIRST RIDE**

Neko helped Alice onto the seat.

The scooter automatically adjusted itself to her weight.

Alice carefully placed her hands on the handlebars.

The machine moved forward.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

Alice traveled approximately several meters.

She stopped.

> **"It works."**

Neko smiled.

> **"Of course it works."**

Alice smiled faintly.

> **"Thank you."**

For a moment, Neko was genuinely happy.

---

# **THE PROBLEM**

Alice barely uses it.

The scooter remains parked near her usual resting area.

Sometimes she uses it to move from one room to another.

Usually, she doesn't.

If someone asks:

> **"Why don't you use Neko's scooter?"**

Alice answers:

> **"Don't need."**

This is demonstrably untrue.

She does need it.

She simply doesn't want to move.

---

# **NEKO'S DISAPPOINTMENT**

Neko initially assumed there was something wrong with the design.

He checked everything.

Battery.

Controls.

Seat.

Suspension.

Safety systems.

Speed limiter.

Navigation.

Medical monitoring.

Nothing was malfunctioning.

The scooter worked perfectly.

Eventually he asked Alice directly:

> **"Do you not like it?"**

Alice immediately shook her head.

> **"No, like it."**

> **"Then why don't you use it?"**

Alice thought.

> **"Alice can just stay here."**

Neko became quiet.

That answer wasn't what he wanted.

---

# **THE REAL PROBLEM**

Neko had built Alice a machine that solved a physical problem.

He had not accounted for the psychological one.

Alice does not avoid walking because she lacks transportation.

She avoids movement because she has become accustomed to making herself as small and unobtrusive as possible.

The scooter gives her mobility.

Alice doesn't necessarily want mobility.

She wants to avoid being a problem.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle loves the scooter.

She thinks it's adorable.

She has repeatedly encouraged Alice to ride it.

> **"Come on! Let's go!"**

Alice sometimes agrees.

Other times:

> **"Maybe later."**

Later rarely comes.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin understands immediately.

The scooter cannot solve what is happening inside Alice.

Still, she encourages Alice to use it whenever she wants to move.

Not:

> **"You need to use it."**

Instead:

> **"If you want to come with me, we can take Neko's scooter."**

Sometimes Alice follows.

Sometimes she doesn't.

Corin never forces her.

---

# **Neko's FINAL ASSESSMENT**

Neko eventually made one modification.

He added a small compartment to the front.

Inside:

A water bottle.

A small snack.

A medical communication button.

A blanket.

And a carrot.

Alice no longer remembers why she likes carrots.

Neko put one there anyway.

When Alice noticed it, she picked it up.

> **"What's this?"**

Neko answered:

> **"Carrot."**

Alice looked at it.

> **"...Oh."**

She took a bite.

Neko smiled.

He did not explain anything else.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Neko built Alice a scooter because he wanted her to stop falling.

He wanted her to be able to visit the kitchen.

The garden.

The living room.

Belle.

Corin.

Everyone.

He wanted Alice to have a little bit of freedom without putting her body at risk.

The machine worked perfectly.

Alice simply didn't use it very much.

Neko was disappointed.

Not because his invention was ignored.

But because he realized what the scooter could never fix.

Alice's problem wasn't:

> **"I can't get around."**

It was:

> **"I don't think I should."**

The scooter could carry Alice anywhere inside the Blacksite.

Neko just wished Alice wanted to go somewhere.
