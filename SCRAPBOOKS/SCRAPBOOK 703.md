### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — REGENERATION & FAMILY RESPONSE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / CONTINUOUS OBSERVATION**

---

# **REGENERATIVE CAPABILITY**

Alice possesses a natural regenerative ability.

Under normal circumstances, this would be considered a significant biological advantage.

Alice's body can repair damaged tissue at a rate far exceeding normal human physiology.

However, regeneration does not create matter from nothing.

Repair requires resources.

Calories.

Protein.

Water.

Nutrients.

Energy.

Alice currently has insufficient reserves to comfortably sustain even her ordinary physiological functions.

Her regeneration therefore represents a paradox:

> **Her body can repair itself.**

> **Her body does not have enough resources to keep repairing itself indefinitely.**

---

# **CURRENT MEDICAL CONCERN**

Alice's regenerative system appears to be compensating for damage while simultaneously struggling with severe nutritional deprivation.

The result is an unstable equilibrium.

Her body repairs what it can.

Then requires additional resources.

Those resources are not adequately supplied.

Her condition worsens.

The regenerative system activates again.

The cycle continues.

Medical personnel have described it as:

> **"A body attempting to rebuild a house while the construction materials are being depleted faster than they can be replaced."**

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE KNOWS**

Everyone knows Alice is sick.

Everyone knows she is fragile.

Everyone knows she is not eating enough.

But knowing it and watching it happen are two different things.

The SAFEHOUSE has become unusually quiet around her.

Nobody wants to frighten her.

Nobody wants to make her feel watched.

Nobody wants to make her feel like a patient.

But everyone is watching.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle has been among the most visibly affected.

She checks on Alice constantly.

She brings food.

She brings water.

She sits nearby even when Alice doesn't speak.

Sometimes she brings carrots.

Alice no longer remembers why she likes them.

Belle brings them anyway.

One evening Belle placed a carrot beside Alice.

Alice looked at it.

> **"What's that?"**

Belle smiled despite the tears beginning to form.

> **"A carrot."**

Alice picked it up.

She took a tiny bite.

Belle looked away.

She did not want Alice to see her crying.

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi remembers Alice differently.

She remembers the little rabbit who was always the weakest of the group.

The one who got sick first.

The one everyone had to look after.

The one who would struggle to keep up.

But she also remembers Alice drawing.

Badly.

Very badly.

Lumi still remembers telling her:

> **"You don't have a natural for arts."**

Alice had laughed.

Lumi had laughed too.

Now Lumi cannot remember the last time Alice laughed like that.

She sometimes sits outside Alice's room without saying anything.

She simply stays there.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin refuses to leave Alice alone for extended periods.

She has become intensely attentive to every change.

A quieter voice.

A slower movement.

A missed meal.

A longer sleep.

A tremor.

Anything.

Corin knows what it looks like when Alice is trying to pretend she's okay.

She has seen it before.

When Alice says:

> **"Alice fine."**

Corin does not automatically believe her.

She answers:

> **"Okay."**

And stays.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has become increasingly protective.

She has reviewed Alice's medical information repeatedly.

Not because she wants to know how bad things are.

She already knows.

She wants to know what can still be done.

She has instructed personnel to avoid discussing Alice's condition within Alice's hearing.

Orphie does not want Alice hearing words like:

> **"critical."**

> **"malnutrition."**

> **"organ failure."**

> **"permanent damage."**

Alice does not need those words.

She needs to know:

> **"You're safe."**

---

# **THE REGENERATION INCIDENT**

During one medical examination, a small injury was observed on Alice's hand.

Within a short period, the wound began closing.

The medical team initially considered this encouraging.

Then they noticed Alice becoming visibly exhausted.

Her breathing became shallow.

She became lethargic.

The regenerative process slowed.

The medical team immediately recognized the problem.

Her body had spent resources repairing itself that it could barely afford to spend.

The regeneration stopped once the injury had stabilized.

Alice simply fell asleep afterward.

---

# **MEDICAL CONCLUSION**

Alice's regeneration is **not a substitute for nutrition.**

It is not an infinite healing mechanism.

It cannot be relied upon to compensate for starvation, dehydration, systemic illness, or developmental deprivation.

Most importantly:

> **Alice cannot regenerate what her body does not have the resources to rebuild.**

---

# **FAMILY RESPONSE**

The SAFEHOUSE has quietly developed a routine.

Someone prepares food.

Someone prepares water.

Someone stays nearby.

Someone checks her temperature.

Someone checks her breathing.

Someone makes sure she rests.

Nobody calls it a duty.

Nobody calls it a burden.

They simply do it.

Because Alice is family.

---

# **ALICE**

Alice herself seems unaware of how frightened everyone is.

She sometimes looks around the room and asks:

> **"Why everyone here?"**

Corin answers:

> **"Because we like being with you."**

Alice thinks about this.

> **"...Oh."**

Then she leans against Corin.

Sometimes she falls asleep there.

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice can heal.

That should have been reassuring.

Instead, everyone has learned that watching her heal can be frightening too.

Because every repaired wound costs something.

Every recovery demands resources.

Every day Alice remains undernourished is another day her body has to choose where its limited energy goes.

And everyone in SAFEHOUSE knows it.

Belle worries.

Lumi worries.

Corin worries.

Orphie worries.

Everyone worries.

But Alice doesn't need to see their fear.

She doesn't need another reason to believe that her existence is causing problems.

So when Alice looks up at them with tired eyes and asks:

> **"Alice okay?"**

Corin answers first.

> **"You're here."**

Alice blinks.

Corin gently fixes her blanket.

> **"That's enough for tonight."**

And everyone stays.
