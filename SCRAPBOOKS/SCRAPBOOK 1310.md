# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### RESIDENT WELFARE & ANOMALOUS PSYCHOLOGY DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-WEL-4C-JAKE-157  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — EXISTENTIAL WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE  
**SUBJECT:** Jake — Persistent Resident / Social Obscurity  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE / SECTOR 4-C

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

C.O.R.E. has identified an unusual development concerning Jake.

Nobody remembers him.

Not consistently.

Not deliberately.

There is no evidence of a memory-erasing anomaly.

No hostile effect has been detected.

No individual has reported forgetting Jake immediately after seeing him.

Instead, the SAFEHOUSE population has gradually returned to treating Jake as though he were simply part of the background.

People see him.

They speak to him.

They may even interact with him.

And then, later, they forget that he was there.

Jake has noticed.

He does not appear distressed by it.

In fact:

> **Jake appears to have accepted it.**

---

# 1. THE OBSERVATION

Jake continues to physically reside within Sector 4-C.

He walks through the corridors.

He eats in the cafeteria.

He sits beneath trees.

He reads.

He carries his teddy bear.

He occasionally watches residents working.

He is physically present.

But socially—

He has almost disappeared.

---

# 2. MEMORY AUDIT

C.O.R.E. conducted informal interviews with SAFEHOUSE residents.

The results were unusual.

### Neko

> **"Jake?"**

Pause.

> **"I know that name."**

Another pause.

> **"He's the ghost kid, right?"**

Neko was shown a photograph.

> **"Oh."**

Pause.

> **"Right. Jake."**

Five minutes later, Neko asked:

> **"Why is there a photo of some kid in the report?"**

---

### Miss Neko

Miss Neko remembered that Jake existed.

She did not remember when she had last spoken to him.

She initially estimated:

> **"Yesterday."**

Security footage indicated:

**Eleven days ago.**

---

### Alice

Alice remembered Jake's face.

She remembered his teddy bear.

She remembered that he liked cake.

She could not remember the last time she had actually spoken to him.

When shown footage of herself sitting beside Jake:

> **"Oh! I was with Jake!"**

Pause.

> **"I forgot."**

---

### Bao

Bao remembered that Jake was a resident.

She remembered that he had once been a ghost.

She remembered his heart was inside the teddy bear.

But when asked where he currently spent most of his time:

> **"I... don't know."**

Jake was standing approximately four meters away.

Bao had not noticed him.

---

# 3. NORA

Nora's response was different.

She remembered Jake more strongly than most.

However, even Nora occasionally lost track of him.

She would enter a room.

Talk to someone.

Leave.

Then suddenly stop in the hallway.

> **"Jake."**

She would turn around.

Sometimes he was there.

Sometimes he was not.

Nora eventually stopped trying to determine whether she had forgotten him or simply failed to notice him.

She simply began checking.

---

# 4. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake was asked whether this bothered him.

He shook his head.

> **"No."**

The welfare officer asked:

> **"You're okay with people forgetting you?"**

Jake nodded.

> **"They're busy."**

Pause.

> **"It's okay."**

He then returned to reading.

---

# 5. THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Jake does not appear to interpret being forgotten as abandonment anymore.

This is a significant change.

Previously:

> **Someone leaves → Jake waits → Jake becomes afraid → Jake assumes he has been abandoned.**

Currently:

> **Someone forgets → Jake continues what he was doing.**

He does not chase them.

He does not ask them to remember.

He does not become angry.

He does not demand recognition.

He simply continues existing.

---

# 6. "I'M STILL HERE"

During one observation, a Security Officer walked past Jake without noticing him.

Jake was sitting against a wall.

The Officer continued down the corridor.

Jake watched him leave.

Then looked down at his teddy bear.

The bear's heartbeat remained steady.

Jake quietly said:

> **"I'm still here."**

No one else heard him.

---

# 7. THE PRISONER PROBLEM

Jake has made an observation that C.O.R.E. considers particularly important.

He understands that he cannot leave.

He cannot return to the Starlands.

He cannot restore his wings.

He cannot reverse his Weeping Angel condition.

There is no known route home.

The SAFEHOUSE is therefore not merely where Jake lives.

It is where Jake is **permanently contained by reality itself.**

Jake once described it as:

> **"A place I can't leave."**

The welfare officer asked:

> **"Does that make you feel trapped?"**

Jake thought for several seconds.

> **"Yes."**

Pause.

> **"But it's my home."**

---

# 8. THE CHAIN

Jake does not possess a literal physical chain.

The chain is existential.

His connection to this world is permanent.

He cannot simply walk through a door and continue onward into another existence.

He cannot choose to return to the Starlands.

He cannot die again and expect somewhere else to receive him.

He is bound here.

A prisoner without a cell.

A resident without an exit.

A child who cannot leave the place that eventually became his home.

---

# 9. JAKE'S ATTITUDE

Despite this, Jake does not describe himself as angry.

When asked whether he wanted freedom:

> **"I don't know where I'd go."**

When asked whether he wanted to leave Earth:

> **"No."**

When asked whether he wanted to return to the Starlands:

Jake shook his head.

> **"I can't."**

When asked whether he wanted the SAFEHOUSE destroyed:

Jake looked confused.

> **"Why?"**

He then returned to reading.

---

# 10. THE STRANGE NORMALITY

Jake has developed an unusual routine.

He wakes.

He eats.

He walks.

He reads.

He wanders.

He occasionally sits somewhere for hours.

He watches residents pass by.

Sometimes someone notices him.

Sometimes nobody does.

He goes to bed.

Then repeats the process.

There is no grand purpose.

No mission.

No destination.

No escape plan.

Jake simply exists.

---

# 11. THE ACCIDENTS CONTINUE

The strange irony is that being forgotten has not made Jake safer.

Because people forget he is there, the usual accidents continue.

Doors still hit him.

People still walk into him.

Someone occasionally moves a chair while he is sitting down.

Someone may leave something in his path.

Jake generally reacts with:

> **"Ow."**

Then continues.

One incident involved a door striking him directly in the forehead.

The responsible resident apologized.

Jake nodded.

Ten minutes later, the resident had apparently forgotten the incident.

Jake had not.

He simply did not care enough to mention it.

---

# 12. THE TEDDY BEAR

The teddy bear remains Jake's constant companion.

Unlike the people around him, it never forgets him.

Its arms remain around his shoulders.

Its heartbeat remains synchronized with him.

When Jake sleeps, it stays with him.

When Jake walks, it walks with him.

When Jake sits alone, it sits with him.

When Jake is forgotten by everyone else:

**The bear remains.**

C.O.R.E. considers this psychologically significant.

---

# 13. A QUESTION FROM C.O.R.E.

Jake was eventually asked:

> **"Do you wish people remembered you?"**

Jake looked at the welfare officer.

Then at his teddy bear.

Then around the room.

He answered:

> **"Sometimes."**

Pause.

> **"But it's okay."**

The officer asked:

> **"Why?"**

Jake shrugged.

> **"I'm used to it."**

# 15. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jake is not currently exhibiting significant distress over being forgotten.

This should not be interpreted as evidence that forgetting him is harmless.

It simply means Jake has adapted.

He has already experienced something far more extreme than social obscurity.

He was abandoned.

He died.

He waited.

He remained alone for decades.

He lost his wings.

He lost his path to the Starlands.

He was forgotten beneath a tree for two weeks.

At some point, being overlooked became ordinary.

---

# FINAL OBSERVATION

Jake was observed sitting beneath a tree this afternoon.

The weather was clear.

His teddy bear was wrapped around him.

He was reading.

A group of residents walked past.

None noticed him.

Jake looked up briefly.

Then returned to his book.

A few minutes later, he closed it.

He looked toward the SAFEHOUSE.

Then at the teddy bear.

> **"I guess I'm staying here."**

The bear's heartbeat remained steady.

Jake leaned back against the tree.

He did not look sad.

He did not look happy.

He simply looked tired.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake is, in many ways, the strangest resident in Sector 4-C.

Not because he is a ghost.

Not because his heart is inside a teddy bear.

Not because he cannot return to the Starlands.

But because he has somehow become comfortable with a truth that would terrify almost anyone else:

**He is not leaving.**

There is no door waiting at the end of the hallway.

No road back.

No second destination.

No release.

The world has effectively placed a chain around him and thrown away the key.

Yet Jake does not spend his days pulling against it.

He sits beneath trees.

He reads.

He eats cake.

He carries his teddy bear.

He watches everyone else live.

Sometimes they remember him.

Sometimes they don't.

Jake is fine with either.

Because even if nobody remembers that he is here—

**he knows that he is.**

And for now,

that is enough.
