**STATUS:** ACTIVE MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Phoenix Raffedella  
**SECONDARY SUBJECT:** Jake  
**EVENT:** Delayed emotional response to cardiac-core separation

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Phoenix has experienced a delayed emotional reaction to Jake's previous cardiac-core separation incident.

The incident occurred when Jake's teddy bear was lost for approximately **five hours**.

Because the teddy bear contains Jake's actual heart and stellar cardiac core, the prolonged separation caused Jake to progressively deteriorate.

Phoenix was holding him when his condition became critical.

She believed he was dying in her arms.

Neko eventually located the teddy bear near the washing machine and restored Jake's cardiac activity.

Jake survived.

Phoenix did not.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

The event appears to have left a significant psychological imprint.

---

# 2. THE MEMORY

Phoenix was in the medical area when the memory resurfaced.

There was no immediate trigger.

Jake was nearby.

He was alive.

His teddy bear was securely attached to him.

Nothing was wrong.

Phoenix looked at him.

Then remembered.

Jake's fading eyes.

His weakening breathing.

His body becoming limp.

The teddy bear missing.

Her inability to restore him.

The fear that she was going to watch another person die while she could do nothing.

Phoenix began crying.

---

# 3. DELAYED REACTION

Phoenix initially attempted to suppress it.

She wiped her eyes.

Took a breath.

Returned to her work.

Then stopped.

Her hands began shaking.

She sat down.

The memories continued.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Five hours.”

She repeated it quietly.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Five hours...”

She covered her face.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “I thought he was gone.”

---

# 4. WHY IT AFFECTED HER

Phoenix has witnessed death repeatedly.

She has personally experienced death thousands of times.

She has treated catastrophic injuries.

She has watched patients die despite treatment.

She has survived situations that should have killed her permanently.

Death itself is therefore not unfamiliar to Phoenix.

Jake was different.

She was holding him.

She was watching him deteriorate.

And there was nothing she could do because the thing keeping him alive was somewhere else.

Her medical knowledge could not solve the problem.

Her Transcendense could not solve it.

Her experience could not solve it.

She could only hold him and wait.

That helplessness appears to be what remained with her.

---

---

# 6. THE TEDDY BEAR

Phoenix's relationship with Jake's teddy bear has changed since the incident.

Previously, she understood it as his emotional comfort object and cardiac component.

Now she also associates it with the memory of almost losing him.

When Phoenix sees the bear lying somewhere unattended, she may immediately check whether Jake is nearby.

If the bear is not attached to him, she becomes visibly concerned.

On one occasion, she asked:

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Where's Jake?”

The resident answered:

> “He's upstairs.”

Phoenix immediately looked at the teddy bear.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Okay.”

She remained visibly tense until Jake returned.

---

# 7. JAKE NOTICES

Jake eventually noticed Phoenix crying.

He approached her.

> **JAKE:**  
> “Phoenix?”

She wiped her face.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “I'm okay.”

Jake looked at her.

He knew she was not.

> **JAKE:**  
> “You're crying.”

Phoenix nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “I remembered something.”

Jake sat beside her.

> **JAKE:**  
> “The bear?”

Phoenix looked at him.

Her expression immediately broke again.

She nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Yeah.”

Jake hugged his teddy bear.

Then looked at Phoenix.

> **JAKE:**  
> “I'm here.”

Phoenix covered her face.

She began crying harder.

---

# 8. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Jake did not attempt to explain the incident.

He did not tell Phoenix to stop crying.

He did not tell her it was okay.

He simply sat beside her.

After several seconds, he held his teddy bear toward her.

Phoenix looked at it.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Your heart?”

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> “Safe.”

Phoenix carefully touched the bear.

Her hand was still shaking.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Good.”

Jake leaned against her.

> **JAKE:**  
> “I'm not going anywhere.”

Phoenix closed her eyes.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> “Thank you.”

---

# 9. MEDICAL / WELFARE ASSESSMENT

The emotional response is considered consistent with a delayed traumatic reaction to a severe medical emergency involving someone Phoenix cares about.

The event combined several factors particularly difficult for Phoenix:

- perceived imminent death;
    
- inability to medically intervene;
    
- prolonged uncertainty;
    
- responsibility for another person's safety;
    
- physical proximity to the dying individual;
    
- fear of permanent loss;
    
- and her existing history of survivor's guilt.
    

Phoenix should not be expected to simply "get over it" because Jake survived.

The outcome was positive.

The experience was still frightening.

Both statements can be true.

---

# 10. CURRENT OBSERVATION

Phoenix continues working.

She continues treating residents.

She continues cooking.

She continues helping others.

However, her reaction to Jake's teddy bear indicates that the incident remains emotionally active.

She may become anxious if:

- Jake is separated from his teddy bear;
    
- the bear cannot immediately be located;
    
- Jake appears unusually tired;
    
- his glow becomes weak;
    
- his breathing changes;
    
- or she sees him unconscious.
    

Personnel should avoid deliberately testing this response.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Phoenix has died thousands of times.

She knows what death looks like.

She knows what a failing pulse feels like beneath her fingers.

She knows the moment breathing becomes too shallow.

She knows what it means when a body stops responding.

But there is a difference between knowing death...

and holding someone you love while believing they are dying.

For five hours, Jake's heart was somewhere else.

Phoenix held the rest of him.

She watched him fade.

She begged him to stay awake.

She thought she was going to lose him.

Neko found the teddy bear.

Jake came back.

The crisis ended.

But memories do not always end when emergencies do.

Sometimes they wait.

Sometimes they return when everything is quiet.

And sometimes a medical assistant looks at a six-year-old boy who is perfectly alive, sees the teddy bear strapped safely to his back, and remembers the moment she thought she was about to watch him die.

Phoenix cried today.

Not because Jake was dying.

Because she remembered when she thought he was.

And this time, there was nothing she could medically do about the memory.

She simply needed to let herself cry.
