**File Designation:** SAFE-NERI-MED-008

---

# 1. OVERVIEW

Neri is prone to episodes of anemia associated with her unusual physiology and recurring requirement for supplemental blood.

One of the most reliable external indicators is a noticeable change in her skin coloration.

When Neri becomes sufficiently anemic, her normally healthy skin tone becomes visibly pale.

The change can be surprisingly dramatic.

Personnel familiar with Neri have learned to recognize it quickly.

> **Archivist Note:**  
> If Neri suddenly looks like someone turned the saturation slider down, check her blood status.

---

# 2. PROGRESSION

Neri's pallor generally becomes more apparent as her condition worsens.

Early signs may include:

- Slightly paler skin.
    
- Reduced energy.
    
- Quiet behavior.
    
- Mild dizziness.
    
- Reduced physical activity.
    

More significant anemia may produce:

- Pronounced pallor.
    
- Weakness.
    
- Lightheadedness.
    
- Difficulty maintaining balance.
    
- Reduced stamina.
    
- Shortness of breath.
    
- Increased likelihood of fainting.
    

Severe anemia may contribute to the cardiovascular crashes already documented in Neri's medical record.

---

# 3. WHY IT MATTERS

Neri's heart already has a limited tolerance for physical and emotional stress.

Reduced oxygen-carrying capacity from anemia places additional strain on an already compromised system.

This can create a dangerous progression:

**Blood levels decrease**

↓

**Skin becomes pale**

↓

**Oxygen delivery becomes less efficient**

↓

**Fatigue and weakness increase**

↓

**Cardiovascular strain increases**

↓

**Risk of collapse increases**

This is why Neri's pallor is treated as a legitimate medical warning rather than merely an aesthetic change.

---

# 4. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri is not particularly good at reporting when she feels unwell.

She frequently insists that she is fine.

This is especially problematic because she has historically normalized physical discomfort.

A typical interaction may therefore proceed as follows:

> **Medical Staff:** "Neri, you're pale."

> **Neri:** "No I'm not."

> **Medical Staff:** "You are."

> **Neri:** "I'm fine."

> **Medical Staff:** "You're sitting on the floor."

> **Neri:** "...The floor is comfortable."

---

# 5. SUPPLEMENTAL BLOOD

When clinically indicated, Neri may require supplemental blood.

Bao remains the only confirmed compatible donor currently documented.

The procedure has repeatedly produced positive results without significant adverse effects to either resident.

Personnel should contact Bao rather than attempting to improvise an alternative.

---

# 6. SUGAR

Sugar may also be useful when Neri is experiencing an energy-related decline.

Cake, candy, sweet drinks, and similar foods are readily accepted by the subject.

However:

> **Sugar is not a substitute for blood.**

If Neri is pale because she is anemic, cake may make her feel better temporarily but does not correct the underlying blood deficit.

This distinction has been added after several residents apparently misunderstood the medical instructions.

---

# 7. VISUAL MONITORING

Because Neri wears round prescription glasses, personnel should not mistake her glasses for a change in facial coloration.

The relevant indicator is the skin itself.

Particular attention should be paid to her face and other normally exposed areas.

Neri's oversized light-azure coat covers much of her body, making the face one of the easiest locations for visual monitoring.

---

# 8. RELATIONSHIP TO FAINTING

Neri's anemia and fainting episodes are considered potentially related.

Not every fainting episode is necessarily caused by anemia.

Neri can also collapse due to:

- Excessive stamina expenditure.
    
- Severe stress.
    
- Cardiovascular instability.
    
- Multiple factors occurring simultaneously.
    

Nevertheless, noticeable pallor before a collapse should be treated as an important warning.

---

# 9. SAFEHOUSE RESPONSE

If Neri becomes visibly pale:

**1. Ask if she feels unwell.**

**2. Reduce physical exertion.**

**3. Have her sit or lie somewhere safe.**

**4. Notify medical personnel.**

**5. Assess whether supplemental blood is required.**

**6. Contact Bao when appropriate.**

**7. Monitor her cardiovascular condition.**

**8. Do not allow her to immediately return to strenuous activity simply because she says she feels better.**

---

# 10. ARCHIVIST OBSERVATION

The pallor has become one of the easiest ways to tell when Neri is running out of physiological reserves.

This is particularly useful because Neri herself is remarkably willing to ignore those limits.

She will continue working.

She will continue walking.

She will continue protecting people.

She will continue insisting:

> **"I'm fine."**

Until somebody notices that she is visibly pale and makes her sit down.

Fortunately, SAFEHOUSE now has the ability to recognize it.

And when Neri needs help, she has people who will notice.
