# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — VOCAL AND SOCIAL PROGRESS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — PERSONNEL CARE  
**Status:** **IMPROVING**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice has been talking more.

What initially consisted of a few quiet words has gradually become short conversations.

She still speaks softly.

She still has to repeat herself occasionally.

But she is talking.

Voluntarily.

More importantly—

she is beginning to **start conversations herself.**

Medical and behavioral personnel consider this remarkable progress.

---

# **VOCAL PROGRESS**

Alice's voice remains small and gentle.

However, she is using it significantly more often.

Previously:

> "Hi."

Now:

> "Hi, Big sis. What are you doing?"

Previously:

> "Hungry."

Now:

> "Can Alice have some soup?"

Previously:

> "Okay."

Now:

> "Okay! Alice understands."

The change is subtle.

But everyone notices.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has probably noticed the change the most.

Alice now occasionally approaches her without being prompted.

> "Big sis?"

> "Yeah?"

> "Can Alice help?"

Corin smiles.

> "You can sit with me."

Alice:

> "Okay."

She sits nearby.

Sometimes she talks about absolutely nothing.

Corin doesn't mind.

---

# **STAR**

Star's grammar lessons have become much easier.

Alice still makes mistakes.

Quite a lot of them.

But she is now willing to correct herself.

Star:

> "Try that sentence again."

Alice:

> "Alice... don't know."

Star:

> "You can say, 'I don't know.'"

Alice:

> "I don't know."

Star:

> "Perfect."

Alice:

> "I don't know why grammar is so mean."

Star:

> "...That's progress."

---

# **BELLE**

Alice has started talking to Belle while riding her scooter.

> "Belle!"

> "Yeah?"

> "Look!"

Alice rides past.

Belle watches.

> "Very impressive."

Alice beams.

> "Alice is fast!"

Belle:

> "Very fast."

Alice:

> "Faster than Big sis?"

Corin, from across the hallway:

> "No."

Alice giggles.

---

# **MORE SOCIAL INTERACTION**

Alice is increasingly comfortable approaching people.

She may now:

- Say hello to employees.
    
- Ask simple questions.
    
- Comment on things she sees.
    
- Ask where someone is going.
    
- Tell people about something she found.
    
- Ask to join activities.
    
- Say when she is tired.
    
- Say when she wants to rest.
    
- Ask for help.
    

The last item is considered particularly significant.

---

# **SHE ASKS FOR HELP**

Alice has started saying:

> "Can you help Alice?"

Rather than silently attempting something herself.

This has already prevented several minor accidents.

One employee found Alice standing beside a high shelf.

She was looking at something she wanted.

Before attempting to climb it, Alice turned around.

> "Can you get that for Alice?"

The employee immediately did.

Alice smiled.

> "Thank you."

---

# **LESS FORGOTTEN**

The new supervision procedures have also been effective.

Alice is being forgotten significantly less often.

The previous pattern of:

> _"I thought someone else had her."_

has become much rarer.

Personnel now actively confirm who is responsible for Alice.

When she goes somewhere with someone, the person knows she is with them.

When she returns to SAFEHOUSE, someone knows she has arrived.

When she goes outside, someone accompanies her.

---

# **THE DIFFERENCE**

Alice has noticed.

She doesn't fully understand the new procedures.

But she notices that people check for her.

She notices that someone follows her.

She notices that doors don't simply close behind her anymore.

She notices people asking:

> "Where's Alice?"

And then finding her.

---

# **ONE SMALL MOMENT**

Alice was walking down a hallway with an employee.

The employee stopped.

> "Alice?"

Alice looked up.

> "Hm?"

> "You're still with me, right?"

Alice nodded.

> "Yes."

The employee smiled.

> "Okay."

Alice thought for a moment.

Then quietly said:

> "You remember Alice."

The employee paused.

> "...Yeah."

Alice smiled.

> "Good."

---

# **CORIN'S OBSERVATION**

Corin recorded:

> "She's talking more."

> "She's asking for things."

> "She's actually telling us when she needs something."

> "And we're not losing track of her nearly as often."

She paused before adding:

> "I think she's starting to believe we'll come back."

---

# **THE MOST IMPORTANT PROGRESS**

Alice has not simply regained her voice.

She is beginning to understand something much more important:

**She is allowed to use it.**

She can ask.

She can interrupt.

She can say no.

She can tell someone she's tired.

She can tell someone she's uncomfortable.

She can ask someone to stay.

She does not have to silently endure everything.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

**Speech:** Significantly improved.

**Vocabulary:** Expanding.

**Social interaction:** Increasing.

**Self-advocacy:** Improving.

**Supervision:** Stable.

**Unintentional isolation:** Significantly reduced.

**Anomalous right eye:** Covered voluntarily.

**General physical condition:** Fragile, but improving.

**Overall assessment:** **REMARKABLE PROGRESS.**

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice still has her quiet moments.

She still sometimes sits silently.

Her ears still fall naturally under gravity.

She still gets tired easily.

She still needs people watching over her.

But now—

sometimes she walks into the SAFEHOUSE and announces:

> "Alice is here!"

Someone will answer:

> "We know."

And Alice smiles.

Because this time—

**they actually do.**
