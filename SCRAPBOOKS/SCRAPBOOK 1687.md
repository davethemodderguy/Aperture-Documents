# 1. OBSERVATION

A peculiar psychological phenomenon has been repeatedly documented among SAFEHOUSE residents.

**Picking up Jake feels strangely satisfying.**

This is difficult to quantify.

There is no anomalous compulsion involved.

Jake does not manipulate the emotions of people carrying him.

The sensation appears to originate entirely from the absurdity of the situation.

Because when someone picks up Jake:

> **They are holding a star.**

Not metaphorically.

Not symbolically.

Literally.

---

# 2. PHYSICAL EXPERIENCE

Jake is approximately the size and weight of a small six-year-old boy.

He can therefore be carried normally.

However, knowledge of his true nature produces an unusual psychological disconnect.

A resident may have:

- Jake's legs over one arm;
    
- his back supported by the other;
    
- his teddy bear pressed against his chest;
    
- his white hair visible over their shoulder;
    

while simultaneously knowing:

> **This child is a literal stellar entity.**

The contradiction produces what several residents have independently described as a feeling of accomplishment.

---

# 3. BAO

Bao is particularly fond of carrying Jake.

She can lift him without difficulty.

Jake usually allows it.

During one observation, Bao picked him up from the floor.

Jake's feet left the grass.

He looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "What?"

Bao smiled.

> **BAO:**  
> "Nothing."

She continued carrying him.

After several seconds:

> **BAO:**  
> "I'm holding a star."

Jake blinked.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yeah."

Bao laughed.

> **BAO:**  
> "That's still funny."

Jake rested his head against her shoulder.

---

# 4. JANE DOE

Jane has developed an entirely different reaction.

She picks Jake up whenever he is in a convenient location.

Once, she lifted him from the floor while he was reading.

Jake continued reading.

Jane looked at him.

Then at the book.

Then back at him.

> **JANE:**  
> "I'm holding a star."

> **JAKE:**  
> "You're also blocking my light."

Jane adjusted him.

> **JANE:**  
> "Sorry."

Jake continued reading.

Jane remained visibly pleased with herself.

---

# 5. NORA

Nora considers the experience extremely entertaining.

She has repeatedly asked whether she can carry Jake.

Jake usually agrees.

Nora once held him under his arms and lifted him slightly.

> **NORA:**  
> "I'M HOLDING A STAR!"

Jake covered one ear.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You're loud."

> **NORA:**  
> "SORRY."

Pause.

> **NORA:**  
> "...I'm holding a star."

Jake sighed.

# 6. NERI

Neri is considerably less dramatic.

She picked Jake up once after finding him sitting on the floor.

She carried him approximately three meters.

Then put him down.

No comment.

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Why'd you pick me up?"

Neri shrugged.

> **NERI:**  
> "Wanted to."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Okay."

Several seconds later, Neri quietly added:

> **NERI:**  
> "You're lighter than I thought."

Jake looked offended.

# 8. CLOVER

Clover's reaction was different.

She already spends time sitting on the floor of Jake's room.

One afternoon, Jake became tired and simply raised his arms toward her.

Clover understood.

She picked him up.

He rested against her shoulder.

Clover looked down.

White hair.

Blue constellation eyes.

Teddy bear.

Heartbeat.

She smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "You're really a star."

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Yep."

Clover looked toward the ceiling.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I used to look for you up there."

Jake was quiet.

Then:

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm here now."

Clover hugged him slightly tighter.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Yeah."

# 10. PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

C.O.R.E. believes the sensation of accomplishment comes from the juxtaposition between:

**Jake's appearance:**

A small, harmless-looking child.

and

**Jake's actual nature:**

A literal stellar entity possessing the capabilities of a Blue Dwarf Star.

The human brain expects something resembling a star to be:

- enormous;
    
- distant;
    
- unreachable;
    
- impossibly hot;
    
- visible only from afar.
    

Instead, Jake can be:

> picked up.

He can sit on someone's shoulders.

He can fall asleep against someone's chest.

He can ask for cake.

He can complain when someone walks him into a wall.

He can hold his teddy bear.

The absurdity is difficult to ignore.

---

# 11. IMPORTANT WELFARE NOTE

Personnel are reminded:

Jake is still a six-year-old child.

The fact that he is a star does not make him:

- a trophy;
    
- a novelty object;
    
- a research specimen;
    
- a portable power source;
    
- or a demonstration of physical strength.
    

He may be carried **because he wants to be carried.**

If Jake reaches for someone:

They may pick him up.

If he does not want to be picked up:

Do not.

# CLOSING OBSERVATION

There is something profoundly strange about holding Jake.

His body is small.

His hair is soft.

His teddy bear has a tiny heartbeat.

His wings fold against his back.

His eyes look like someone put an entire night sky inside them.

And somewhere inside that little body is a star.

A real one.

Something that should be impossibly distant.

Something that should be millions of kilometers away.

Yet here he is.

In your arms.

Looking at you.

Probably asking whether you have snacks.

The strange feeling of accomplishment is therefore understandable.

Because for a few seconds:

> **You are holding the universe in your hands.**

And the universe is probably wondering why you're holding him like a sack of potatoes.
