# Executive Summary

Nora's physical illness has begun to improve.

Her recovery, however, has not been straightforward.

During the acute and early recovery periods, Nora experienced **frequent fainting episodes** associated with her weakened physical state, prolonged sleep deprivation, reduced food and fluid intake, and general exhaustion.

The episodes have made independent movement temporarily unsafe.

As a result, Nora has spent considerably more time in bed than she normally would.

There has also been an unexpected development.

Nora has received a large number of:

> **"GET WELL SOON!"**

cards.

A very large number.

The cards have accumulated around her bedside to the point that C.O.R.E. Welfare has formally documented them as part of her recovery environment.

Nora has read nearly all of them.

She has kept nearly all of them.

---

# 1. RECURRENT FAINTING

Nora has fainted repeatedly throughout the illness.

The episodes generally occur after periods of weakness or attempted activity.

Observed circumstances have included:

- standing after prolonged bed rest;
    
- attempting to walk unassisted;
    
- becoming physically exhausted;
    
- insufficient food intake;
    
- insufficient fluid intake;
    
- attempting to move too quickly;
    
- and, on several occasions, apparently without sufficient warning for Nora herself to recognize what was happening.
    

The episodes have been brief.

However, their frequency has resulted in a temporary rule:

> **Nora is not to assume that she is capable of walking simply because she currently feels capable of walking.**

She has not been particularly enthusiastic about this rule.

---

# 2. NORA'S RESPONSE

Nora's response to fainting is unusual.

She does not appear particularly embarrassed.

She is more annoyed by the interruption.

After regaining consciousness, she will often look around and ask:

> **"What happened?"**

Once informed that she fainted, her usual response is approximately:

> **"...Oh."**

Then:

> **"Can I get up?"**

The answer has frequently been:

> **"No."**

Nora generally looks offended by this.

She remains in bed anyway.

---

# 3. AETERNA'S RESPONSE

Aeterna has become extremely familiar with the sequence.

When Nora begins to lose consciousness, Aeterna intervenes immediately.

Afterward, she checks Nora, ensures she remains in a safe position, and waits for her to fully recover.

Aeterna does not panic.

She does, however, become significantly less tolerant of Nora attempting to immediately resume normal activity.

# 4. THE GET-WELL CARDS

The first card was delivered by Alice.

It was brightly decorated and contained considerably more glitter than C.O.R.E. Welfare considers medically necessary.

The message read:

> **GET WELL SOON NORA!!!**

Below it was a drawing of two rabbits, one shark, and something that appeared to be an extremely large cake.

Nora stared at it for several seconds.

Then quietly said:

> **"Thank you."**

Alice immediately became extremely happy.

This resulted in more cards.

# 6. NERI'S CARD

Neri's card was considerably smaller than most.

It contained only a few words.

> **"Get better soon, Nora."**

Below that was a small drawing of a shark beside a seal.

Nora looked at it for a long time.

She then placed it carefully beside her pillow.

She did not say anything.

Later that evening, Aeterna noticed Nora checking whether the card was still there.

---

# 7. BAO'S CARD

Bao's card was handwritten.

It contained considerably more text.

The central message was:

> **"You don't have to hurry.  
> You don't have to be strong today.  
> Rest until your body is ready.  
> Mom is here."**

Nora read it twice.

Then folded the card open again.

She kept it on the bedside table.

Nora stared at the drawing.

Alice answered:

Nora considered this.

# 9. JANE'S CARD

Jane's card contained significantly less decoration.

It read:

> **"Hey, kiddo.  
> Stop scaring everybody.  
> Get some rest.  
> Cheeseburger when you're better."**

Nora read the last line twice.

She looked toward Aeterna.

> **NORA:**  
> "She bribed me."

Aeterna looked at the card.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "It appears so."

Nora nodded.

> **NORA:**  
> "Good."

---

# 10. THE CARD WALL

The number of cards eventually became large enough that they could no longer comfortably remain on Nora's bedside table.

C.O.R.E. Welfare therefore placed a small display area beside the bed.

Nora's cards were arranged there.

The result has informally become known as:

> **THE NORA CARD WALL**

Nora appears to like it.

She occasionally reads the cards when she cannot sleep.

This has proven particularly useful during the night.

Rather than staring at the ceiling, Nora can look toward the wall and see evidence that people are thinking about her.

No one is requiring her to respond.

No one is asking her to become cheerful.

The cards simply remain there.

---

# 11. ANOTHER FAINTING EPISODE

During the sixth day of recovery, Nora attempted to retrieve a card from the far side of the room.

She stood.

Walked approximately four steps.

Stopped.

Her vision became unstable.

She reached toward the wall.

Aeterna caught her before she reached the floor.

Nora briefly lost consciousness.

Upon waking:

> **NORA:**  
> "...The card."

Aeterna was holding it.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "I have it."

Nora looked at the card.

> **NORA:**  
> "Okay."

Pause.

> **NORA:**  
> "Can I have it?"

Aeterna handed it to her.

Nora returned to bed.

The card was placed beside her pillow.

---

# 12. MEDICAL RESPONSE

Because the fainting episodes have been recurrent, C.O.R.E. medical personnel have increased observation during Nora's recovery period.

Nora is currently encouraged to:

- remain seated or lying down when weak;
    
- request assistance when moving;
    
- maintain reasonable fluid intake;
    
- eat when able;
    
- rise slowly;
    
- avoid unnecessary exertion;
    
- and immediately report unusual symptoms rather than attempting to ignore them.
    

The last instruction has required repeated reinforcement.

Nora has a demonstrated tendency to decide that something is:

> **"Probably fine."**

before anyone else has had an opportunity to evaluate it.

This behavior is currently being monitored.

# 14. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

The cards appear to have had an unexpected psychological benefit.

Nora has not become suddenly energetic.

Her nightmares have not disappeared.

Her sleep remains inconsistent.

Her fainting episodes remain concerning.

However, she has begun asking questions such as:

> **"Who made this one?"**

and:

> **"When did Alice bring this?"**

She has also started reading them aloud occasionally.

Very quietly.

This is a significant change from the earliest days of the illness, when she often refused interaction altogether.

---

# 15. AETERNA'S OBSERVATION

Aeterna was asked whether she believed the cards were helping.

She looked toward the wall.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Yes."

> **C.O.R.E. WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Why?"

Aeterna paused.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "Nora believes she has been absent."

Pause.

> **AETERNA:**  
> "The cards demonstrate that she has been missed."

---

# Final Assessment

Nora remains in a temporary recovery period.

The acute illness is improving.

Her strength is gradually returning.

However, recurrent fainting remains a significant concern, and her sleep deprivation has left her unusually vulnerable to further exhaustion.

For now, she is being permitted to recover at her own pace.

The cards remain.

The fainting is being monitored.

Aeterna remains nearby.

Neri visits when chemistry permits.

Bao continues checking on her.

Alice continues producing increasingly elaborate cards.

Jane has promised a cheeseburger upon recovery.

Nora has not forgotten that promise.
