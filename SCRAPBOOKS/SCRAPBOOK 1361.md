# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A commonly repeated observation among SAFEHOUSE residents is:

> **“Phoenix is so sweet. How can you genuinely make her mad?”**

The answer is surprisingly simple.

**Injure yourself.**

Preferably in the stupidest way imaginable.

Phoenix can tolerate insults.

She can tolerate people being annoying.

She can tolerate chaos.

She can tolerate dying.

She has, unfortunately, become extremely accustomed to that last one.

What she apparently cannot tolerate is someone deliberately or carelessly hurting themselves over something completely avoidable.

Her medical instincts activate immediately.

Her patience does not.

---

# 1. THE INCIDENT

The incident began with a SAFEHOUSE resident attempting to retrieve a cup from a kitchen shelf.

The shelf was approximately one meter above the floor.

There was a chair directly beside it.

The resident chose not to use the chair.

Instead, they attempted to reach the cup by climbing onto the kitchen counter.

Phoenix entered the room halfway through this decision.

She stopped.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “What are you doing?”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “Getting a cup.”

Phoenix looked at the chair.

Then the resident.

Then the chair again.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “There's a chair.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I know.”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Why aren't you using it?”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I can reach.”

Phoenix stared.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Get down.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I'm fine.”

The resident stretched farther.

The counter shifted slightly.

Phoenix immediately stepped forward.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Get. Down.”

The resident reached anyway.

Their foot slipped.

They fell.

They hit the floor.

Silence.

Phoenix closed her eyes.

---

# 2. PHOENIX'S REACTION

The resident immediately attempted to stand.

Phoenix caught their shoulder.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Don't.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I'm okay.”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “You fell off a counter.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “It's not that high.”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “There was a chair.”

The resident looked at the chair.

Phoenix pointed at it.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “It was RIGHT THERE.”

The resident remained silent.

Phoenix checked them for injuries.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Where does it hurt?”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “My ankle.”

Phoenix's expression changed immediately.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Of course it does.”

---

# 3. THE MEDICAL EXAMINATION

Phoenix began examining the injury.

The resident attempted to apologize.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “Sorry.”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Don't apologize.”

Pause.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Just explain why you thought climbing onto the counter was a better idea than using a chair.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I thought I could reach.”

Phoenix stared at them.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “You could reach.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “Yeah.”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “The cup.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “Yeah.”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Not the floor.”

The resident stopped speaking.

Phoenix sighed.

---

# 4. THE ACTUAL SOURCE OF ANGER

C.O.R.E. analysis indicates that Phoenix's anger was not caused by the injury itself.

Phoenix has experienced significantly worse injuries.

Repeatedly.

Her reaction was caused by the fact that the injury was:

**avoidable.**

The resident had access to:

- a chair;
    
- stable flooring;
    
- functioning legs;
    
- common sense;
    
- and approximately three seconds of additional decision-making time.
    

They used none of them.

Phoenix reportedly considers this offensive.

---

# 5. SECOND INCIDENT

A separate resident later attempted to open a sealed package using a pair of scissors.

The scissors were pointed toward themselves.

Phoenix noticed.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Stop.”

The resident stopped.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Turn it around.”

They turned the scissors around.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Good.”

Pause.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Why did I have to tell you that?”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I wasn't thinking.”

Phoenix slowly lowered her head.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Apparently.”

---

# 6. THIRD INCIDENT

The most severe reaction occurred when a resident attempted to carry six mugs simultaneously.

They dropped one.

The mug shattered.

The resident immediately attempted to pick up the pieces with bare hands.

Phoenix appeared behind them.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “No.”

The resident froze.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Do not.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “I can—”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “No.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “But—”

> **Phoenix:**  
> “You already broke one thing. Please don't add your fingers to the list.”

The resident slowly stood up.

Phoenix picked up the cleaning equipment.

She was visibly irritated.

---

# 7. WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM PHOENIX'S OWN INJURIES

There is a peculiar irony to Phoenix's behavior.

Phoenix herself possesses notoriously poor self-preservation.

She has:

- died from explosions;
    
- been shot;
    
- fallen down stairs;
    
- suffered catastrophic injuries;
    
- accidentally injured herself while exhausted;
    
- and repeatedly experienced Transcendense.
    

Yet she becomes genuinely angry when someone else performs an obviously avoidable act of self-injury.

The distinction appears to be:

> **Phoenix has accepted what happens to herself.**

She has **not** accepted watching someone else get hurt.

# 9. PHOENIX'S MEDICAL ASSISTANT VOICE

Observed example:

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Please sit down.”

This is considered normal.

However:

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Please sit down.”

_Pause._

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Before you make this worse.”

This indicates elevated irritation.

The highest recorded level:

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Sit.”

No further explanation is generally required.

---

# 10. IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Phoenix does **not** become angry at people for being injured accidentally.

Accidents happen.

She understands this better than almost anyone.

She becomes angry when someone:

1. knowingly takes an unnecessary risk;
    
2. ignores an obvious safer alternative;
    
3. gets hurt;
    
4. then says:
    

> **“I'm fine.”**

That phrase has been identified as a particularly effective way to make Phoenix stop being sweet.

---

# C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Question:

> **“How can you genuinely make Phoenix mad?”**

Answer:

Do not insult her.

Do not threaten her.

Do not yell at her.

Do not even necessarily argue with her.

Instead:

**Do something unbelievably stupid that results in an entirely preventable injury.**

Phoenix will treat the injury.

Phoenix will make sure you're okay.

Phoenix will probably give you something for the pain.

Phoenix will remain beside you until she's satisfied you're safe.

And then:

She will lecture you.

At length.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Phoenix is sweet.

She is patient.

She is compassionate.

She has an almost absurd tolerance for chaos.

She has personally experienced thousands of deaths and still spends her time trying to keep other people alive.

But there is apparently one reliable way to make her genuinely angry.

Get hurt.

Not because you were attacked.

Not because you were unlucky.

Not because something was beyond your control.

But because there was a chair.

And you decided:

> **“Nah, I'll climb the counter.”**

Phoenix will save you.

Phoenix will treat you.

Phoenix will make sure you're okay.

And then she will stare at you for approximately thirty seconds and ask:

> **“Why?”**

There is no good answer.
