# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Week Five has produced a marked deterioration in Jake's behavior.

He remains alive.

He continues to consume enough food and water to sustain himself.

He remains physically capable of movement.

His stellar core remains active.

His anomalous manifestation remains stable.

Beyond those basic functions, however, Jake has almost completely withdrawn from normal activity.

He has stopped drawing.

He has stopped reading.

He has stopped playing with his plushies.

He rarely leaves his mattress.

He has stopped speaking.

And most significantly:

**Jake is no longer sleeping.**

He appears unable to sleep while alone for prolonged periods.

The longer the isolation continues, the less willing he becomes to close his eyes.

His current routine has therefore become remarkably narrow:

> **Eat. Drink. Remain awake. Remain alive.**

Nothing else.

---

# 1. LOSS OF NORMAL ACTIVITY

During the first weeks, Jake continued maintaining some semblance of his normal routine.

He drew.

He read.

He manifested flowers.

He explored his floor.

He occasionally left his residence.

Those activities have now almost completely stopped.

The pencil remains where he left it.

His books remain open to pages he has not turned.

His plushies remain scattered across the mattress.

Several drawings remain unfinished.

No new manifested flowers have been deliberately created.

Jake appears to have lost interest in making anything.

---

# 2. SPEECH

Jake has stopped talking.

This is different from his normal infrequent verbal behavior.

Previously, Jake could go hours without speaking and then quietly say something when necessary.

During Week Five, there have been extended periods in which he produces **no speech at all.**

When personnel have attempted remote welfare checks, Jake has not answered verbally.

He may acknowledge something with a movement.

Sometimes he does not respond at all.

His voice remains physically functional.

There is no indication of another loss of speech capability.

He simply does not use it.

---

# 3. SLEEP

This is the most significant change.

Jake cannot sleep when he has been alone for too long.

The behavior was initially mistaken for an irregular sleep schedule.

It is not.

Jake appears to require a sense of nearby presence before he can relax enough to sleep.

When completely alone, he remains awake.

Hours pass.

He continues lying on the mattress.

His eyes remain open.

Eventually he sits up.

He looks toward the doorway.

Nothing is there.

He lies down again.

The cycle repeats.

---

# 4. NIGHTTIME OBSERVATION

One observation lasted approximately six hours.

Jake remained on his mattress throughout the entire period.

01:13 — Awake.

02:04 — Awake.

03:17 — Awake.

04:42 — Awake.

05:31 — Awake.

06:08 — Awake.

He did not appear restless.

He did not attempt to entertain himself.

He did not read.

He did not draw.

He simply remained there.

At approximately 06:11, Jake pulled his teddy bear closer.

He closed his eyes.

For approximately eight seconds.

Then opened them again.

He looked toward the doorway.

Nothing had changed.

He remained awake.

---

# 5. THE TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear has become his primary source of physical comfort.

He keeps it against his chest while lying down.

He carries it when he leaves the mattress.

He rarely allows it to remain more than an arm's reach away.

During one observation, Jake placed the teddy bear beside him and attempted to sleep.

He lasted approximately two minutes.

He then pulled it against himself again.

His eyes remained open.

---

# 6. THE ROOM

Jake's room has not changed significantly.

The grass remains.

The flowers remain.

The plushies remain.

The mattress remains.

The room is still messy.

It is still almost completely unfurnished.

It is still unmistakably his.

But the room no longer appears lived in the same way.

The objects are present.

Jake simply does not interact with them.

A plushie can remain beside him for hours without being touched.

A book can remain open for days.

A flower can sway directly beside him without receiving any attention.

The room has become less of a home and more of a place where Jake remains.

---

# 7. FOOD

Jake continues eating.

This is one of the few routines he has maintained.

Meals are simple.

He eats enough.

He drinks enough.

He does not appear interested in taste.

He does not request particular foods.

He does not seek cake.

He does not make coffee.

He does not appear to care what he is eating.

The objective appears to be purely functional:

> **Stay alive.**

---

# 8. MOVEMENT

Jake still moves when necessary.

He gets up to eat.

He gets up to drink.

He occasionally uses the bathroom.

He occasionally moves to another part of his floor.

Otherwise, he remains on the mattress.

There is no evidence that he lacks the physical ability to move.

He simply does not appear to see a reason to.

---

# 9. DRAWING ACTIVITY

**Previous baseline:** Frequent.

**Week Four:** Reduced.

**Week Five:** Almost completely absent.

Jake's drawing materials remain accessible.

He has not destroyed them.

He has not discarded them.

He simply does not pick them up.

One unfinished drawing remains on the floor.

It depicts a field of flowers.

Six figures are drawn in the distance.

Jake's figure is absent.

The drawing has remained unfinished for six days.

---

# 10. MANIFESTED FLOWERS

No significant new flower manifestations have been recorded.

Existing flowers remain physically stable.

The grass continues its anomalous growth.

Several flowers have begun growing around Jake's mattress.

Jake does not remove them.

He does not interact with them.

They simply grow around him.

During one observation, a flower bent toward Jake's face.

Jake opened his eyes.

He looked at it.

He did not move.

After several seconds, the flower slowly returned upright.

---

# 11. SOCIAL CONTACT

There has still been no meaningful social contact.

Jake's family has not visited.

His friends have not visited.

The Obol Squad has not visited.

No resident has had a conversation with him.

The ongoing workload remains unresolved.

Everyone remains busy.

The absence continues.

---

# 12. JAKE'S CURRENT UNDERSTANDING

Jake still knows why nobody is visiting.

He has not forgotten.

He has not suddenly decided that everyone hates him.

He continues to understand:

> **"They're busy."**

But the distinction between understanding and feeling has become increasingly severe.

He knows they are busy.

He still feels abandoned.

And now he appears to have stopped expecting that feeling to change.

---

# 13. PSYCHOLOGICAL PRESENTATION

Jake's current presentation is characterized by:

- near-total withdrawal;
    
- loss of recreational activity;
    
- loss of verbal communication;
    
- inability to sleep while alone;
    
- reduced movement;
    
- emotional flatness;
    
- persistent isolation;
    
- and continued basic self-maintenance.
    

There is little outward expression of distress.

That should not be interpreted as improvement.

Jake has simply stopped expressing most things.

---

# 14. CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Jake is still taking care of himself.

But he is no longer **living normally.**

There is a significant difference between:

> **"Jake is doing okay by himself."**

and:

> **"Jake is doing just enough to remain alive by himself."**

Current observations are substantially closer to the second.

He eats because he needs food.

He drinks because he needs water.

He remains awake because he cannot sleep alone.

He moves when necessary.

Everything else has gradually disappeared.

---

# 15. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. now considers Jake's prolonged isolation a **critical resident welfare concern.**

His physical stability is currently maintained.

His emotional and behavioral state is not.

The primary concern is no longer simply loneliness.

It is **functional withdrawal.**

Jake has stopped participating in activities that previously gave him comfort.

He has stopped talking.

He has stopped sleeping.

He has stopped creating.

He has stopped expecting company.

The current state cannot reasonably be considered sustainable.

---

# 16. FINAL OBSERVATION — END OF WEEK FIVE

At 03:48, Jake was lying face down on his mattress.

His teddy bear was beneath one arm.

His eyes were open.

The room was dark.

The grass was still.

The flowers barely moved.

A dozen plushies surrounded him.

He did not touch any of them.

His pencil was on the floor.

His books were beside it.

Everything he normally used to occupy himself was still there.

Jake simply did not want any of it.

He remained awake.

He turned his head slightly toward the doorway.

Nothing.

He waited.

Nothing.

He closed his eyes.

Eight seconds.

They opened again.

Jake pulled the teddy bear closer.

No words were spoken.

No tears were visible.

He simply remained there.

Alive.

Awake.

Alone.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Five weeks ago, Jake was alone because everyone was busy.

Four weeks ago, he began feeling abandoned.

Three weeks ago, he still drew.

Two weeks ago, he still occasionally went outside.

One week ago, he still had enough energy to wait for someone.

Now he waits for nothing.

He eats.

He drinks.

He remains physically alive.

And then he lies down and stares at the ceiling until morning.

He cannot sleep when he is alone for too long.

So he does not sleep.

The flowers continue growing around him.

The plushies remain untouched.

The drawings remain unfinished.

The old mattress remains his entire world.

And the little star who once filled that room with drawings, flowers, books, and quiet curiosity has become almost completely still.

There is no dramatic incident.

No attack.

No anomaly.

No breach.

Just a child who has been alone for too long.

And, at the end of Week Five,

Jake is still there.

Still awake.

Still breathing.

Still alive.

And still completely alone.
