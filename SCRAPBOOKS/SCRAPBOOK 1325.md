# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Something has changed.

Jake has started trusting people again.

Not completely.

Not immediately.

Not everyone.

But enough that C.O.R.E. can now document a measurable change in his behavior.

For the first time since the series of incidents that caused his severe trust deterioration, Jake has begun allowing himself to believe that the people around him may actually return.

He is no longer checking every door.

He is asking fewer questions.

He is beginning to accept invitations without demanding exact schedules.

He occasionally leaves his room without his teddy bear.

He has started approaching residents voluntarily.

Most importantly:

> **Jake has started believing people when they tell him they will come back.**

---

# 1. THE FIRST SIGN

The change was initially so subtle that nobody noticed.

Neri was sitting outside Jake's room reading.

She eventually stood up.

> **Neri:**  
> “I'm going to the cafeteria.”

Jake was sitting on his bed.

Previously, this would have prompted:

> “When will you come back?”

Instead:

Jake simply nodded.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

Neri paused.

She looked at him.

> **Neri:**  
> “I'll be back later.”

Jake nodded again.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

Neri left.

Jake did not follow.

He did not watch the clock.

He did not stand beside the door.

He simply returned to his book.

---

# 2. NERI RETURNED

Approximately thirty minutes later, Neri came back.

She knocked.

Jake opened the door.

> **Neri:**  
> “Hi.”

Jake looked at her.

> **Jake:**  
> “Hi.”

Neri held up a small plate.

Cake.

Jake looked at it.

> **Jake:**  
> “For me?”

Neri nodded.

Jake accepted it.

There was no question about why she returned.

There was no question about whether she actually would.

He simply accepted the cake.

---

# 3. BAO

Bao noticed the change next.

Jake had already treated Bao as his mother.

Now he began trusting her in ways he had previously been unable to.

One afternoon:

> **Bao:**  
> “Jake, I'm going to the kitchen.”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay, Mom.”

Bao paused.

> **Bao:**  
> “I'll be back.”

Jake smiled faintly.

> **Jake:**  
> “I know.”

Bao stopped walking.

She looked back.

Jake was already reading.

---

# 4. NORA

Nora's relationship with Jake changed more slowly.

She had always been one of his closest companions.

However, Jake's fear of being left behind had made him unusually cautious around her.

One evening, Nora stood at the elevator.

> **Nora:**  
> “Jake, I'm going to the store.”

Jake looked up.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

> **Nora:**  
> “Want anything?”

Jake thought.

> **Jake:**  
> “Cake.”

Nora grinned.

> **Nora:**  
> “I'll get you some.”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

Nora left.

Jake did not ask when she would return.

---

# 5. THE CAKE

Nora returned forty minutes later.

Jake was sitting in the common room.

He looked up.

Nora held up the cake.

> **Nora:**  
> “Told you I'd bring it.”

Jake smiled.

A genuine, brief smile.

> **Jake:**  
> “You came back.”

Nora blinked.

> **Nora:**  
> “I said I would.”

Jake looked at the cake.

Then at Nora.

> **Jake:**  
> “Yeah.”

He took it.

> **Jake:**  
> “Thanks.”

# 6. AETERNA

Aeterna approached the change differently.

She never asked Jake to trust her.

She continued doing exactly what she had always done.

If she needed to leave:

She said so.

If she returned:

She returned.

If she did not know when she would return:

She said that.

Eventually Jake stopped asking.

One evening:

> **Aeterna:**  
> “I need to leave.”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

Aeterna waited.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “Do you want to know when?”

Jake shook his head.

> **Jake:**  
> “You'll come back.”

Aeterna remained silent for several seconds.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “Yes.”

Jake nodded.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

---

# 7. THE DOOR

The most significant change concerns Jake's door.

Previously, unexpected movement around his room caused immediate anxiety.

Now, when someone knocks:

Jake does not always freeze.

Sometimes he simply says:

> **“Come in.”**

This is particularly significant because the door represents one of the strongest boundaries in his life.

He is beginning to associate:

**Knock → Someone is outside → They are asking permission → I am allowed to decide.**

The door is no longer exclusively a barrier.

It has become a boundary he controls.

---

# 8. THE SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTS

Jake has begun approaching residents more frequently.

Not constantly.

Not dramatically.

Just enough to be noticeable.

He may walk up to Frost and sit beside her.

He may ask Alice what she is doing.

He may bring a book to Belle.

He may sit near Endmin while she works.

He may ask Neko what he is building.

He may quietly watch Mart read.

He may sit with Taiga.

He may ask Astral whether she is making tea.

He may even wander into a room and simply say:

> **“Hi.”**

Then stay.

---

# 9. THE FIRST UNEXPECTED HUG

One afternoon, Frost was sitting in the lounge.

Jake walked over.

He stopped in front of her.

Frost looked up.

> **Frost:**  
> “Hey, Jake.”

Jake hesitated.

Then he hugged her.

Frost froze.

Jake released her almost immediately.

> **Jake:**  
> “Sorry.”

Frost shook her head.

> **Frost:**  
> “You don't have to apologize.”

Jake looked at her.

> **Frost:**  
> “You can hug me.”

Jake nodded.

He hugged her again.

The teddy bear's heartbeat remained steady.

---

# 10. ALICE

Alice noticed something even stranger.

Jake approached her carrying his teddy bear.

> **Jake:**  
> “Alice?”

> **Alice:**  
> “Yeah?”

> **Jake:**  
> “Can I sit here?”

Alice moved over.

> **Alice:**  
> “Sure.”

Jake sat beside her.

They remained there for several minutes.

Alice eventually asked:

> **Alice:**  
> “Want to play?”

Jake considered it.

> **Jake:**  
> “Okay.”

Alice smiled.

Jake smiled too.

Neither of them seemed particularly concerned about whether the other would suddenly disappear.

For once, they simply played.

---

# 11. THE TEDDY BEAR

Even Jake's relationship with his teddy bear has changed slightly.

Previously, he held it constantly.

Now he occasionally puts it down.

This is significant.

The bear remains extremely important.

It is still his core.

It still contains his heart.

Jake still retrieves it immediately if he becomes frightened.

But he has begun allowing himself to move several steps away from it.

C.O.R.E. interprets this not as reduced attachment, but as increased environmental confidence.

Jake is beginning to believe:

> **“I can put this down and nothing bad will happen.”**

---

# 13. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jake's trust has not returned to its previous state.

It may never return in exactly the same form.

That is not necessarily the objective.

The current change is healthier.

Jake is learning that trust does not have to mean:

> **“Nobody will ever leave.”**

It can instead mean:

> **“They left before, and they came back.”**

That distinction is important.

He is no longer demanding certainty about the future.

He is beginning to rely on observed patterns.

People leave.

People return.

Doors close.

Doors open.

Someone can go away without disappearing forever.

Someone can say goodbye without meaning goodbye forever.

Someone can say:

> **“I'll be back.”**

And actually come back.

# FINAL OBSERVATION

Jake spent a long time believing that trusting someone meant giving them the ability to hurt him.

Now he is beginning to learn something different.

Trust does not mean nothing bad will ever happen.

It means that someone can leave the room—

and still come back.

Someone can close a door—

and open it again.

Someone can say goodbye—

without disappearing.

And perhaps most importantly:

Someone can say:

> **“I'm here.”**

And Jake can finally begin to believe them.

Not because they said it.

Because they kept showing him.

Again.

And again.

And again.

Until one evening, Jake walked into the common room, sat down among everyone else, put his teddy bear beside him—

and didn't check the exits.

For the first time in a very long time,

Jake simply stayed.
