## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Tom Akana's successful return, C.O.R.E. has updated its family records to reflect the correct sibling relationships between the three Akana brothers.

|Individual|Position in sibling hierarchy|
|---|---|
|**Aries Akana**|Oldest brother|
|**Tom Akana**|Middle brother; Aries's younger brother and Jake's older brother|
|**Jake Akana**|Youngest brother|

The distinction is significant.

Tom is not Jake's parent, guardian, or designated medical caretaker. He is Jake's older brother. Aries is the eldest of the three, and Tom is his little brother just as Jake is Tom's little brother.

The brothers have consequently resumed their familiar family dynamic, albeit with one considerable complication.

**Tom is now much more available to Jake than he was before his disappearance. Unfortunately, increased availability has not translated into the ability to keep Jake alive.**

Since his return, Tom has repeatedly attempted to supervise Jake, discourage dangerous behavior, remain nearby during periods of distress, and intervene whenever Jake dies.

He has failed every time.

Not because he does not care.

Not because he is careless.

And not because Jake's resurrection has become a reliable, harmless process.

Jake continues to die at intervals of only a few hours during the observed pattern. Sometimes his white-fire reconstruction activates independently. On other occasions, it does not activate until Tom provides physical stimulation, at which point the reconstruction begins.

Tom has therefore found himself in the deeply unpleasant position of being the older brother who keeps trying to protect his little brother from dying, only to discover that his little brother continues to die regardless of how closely he watches him.

The current situation is not a success story about resurrection.

It is a family attempting to resume its life while one of its members remains caught in a dangerous, repetitive crisis.

## II. FAMILY RECORD AMENDMENT

**SUBJECT:** Akana Sibling Structure  
**REASON FOR AMENDMENT:** Previous documentation focused heavily on the relationship between Tom and Jake and did not sufficiently establish Aries's position as their eldest brother.

### 2.1 — Aries Akana: The Eldest

Aries is the older brother of both Tom and Jake.

His position within the family is not merely a matter of age. He is the eldest sibling, and Tom is his little brother.

The fact that Tom has returned with a slightly older appearance and a taller stature does not alter this relationship. Tom may now look more convincingly like an older brother to Jake, but he remains Aries's younger brother.

Aries's involvement in the current situation must not be reduced to that of an outside observer or an auxiliary caretaker. Jake is his little brother, too.

### 2.2 — Tom Akana: The Middle Brother

Tom is Aries's little brother and Jake's older brother.

His relationship with Jake has historically involved a mixture of companionship, supervision, teasing, caution, and affection. Tom has often recognized dangers that Jake overlooked, but he has never possessed complete control over Jake's decisions or anomalous abilities.

Since returning, Tom has been spending substantially more time with Jake.

He is no longer attempting to fit his brother into the limited spaces left by other commitments while assuming that someone else will be available in the meantime. He has become much more present in Jake's daily life.

This increased availability is a personal choice, not a formal assignment of sole responsibility.

Tom is still a child. His own emotional needs, exhaustion, and recovery matter.

### 2.3 — Jake Akana: The Youngest

Jake is the youngest brother.

His position as the youngest has become particularly apparent since Tom's return. Jake frequently seeks Tom's attention, stays physically close to him, and appears reassured by Tom's presence.

This is understandable after ten weeks of bereavement and a prolonged period of severe psychological distress.

However, Jake's attachment to Tom must not be interpreted as evidence that his crisis has ended. Nor should his tendency to remain close to Tom be mistaken for a guarantee of safety.

Jake can be happy that his brother is home and still be deeply unwell.

Both facts must remain visible in C.O.R.E.'s assessment.

## III. OBSERVATION LOG — THE OLDER BROTHER'S NEW ROUTINE

**OBSERVATION PERIOD:** Initial post-restoration period  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, primarily Floor 7  
**NOTE:** The following entries reconstruct the recurring pattern observed by residents and medical personnel. They are representative episodes, not a complete account of every incident.

### Entry A — Morning

Tom was awake before Jake.

This was not unusual. Tom had begun adjusting his routine around Jake's unpredictable condition, although he had been explicitly advised that he was not expected to remain awake continuously.

When Jake finally emerged, his hair was untidy, his clothes were slightly crooked, and he was carrying a stuffed object of uncertain relevance to breakfast.

Tom looked at him.

**TOM:** "Did you sleep?"

**JAKE:** "Mhm!"

**TOM:** "Properly?"

**JAKE:** "Mhm!"

**TOM:** "How long?"

Jake thought about it.

**JAKE:** "...Sleepy long."

**TOM:** "That isn't a measurement."

**JAKE:** "It is to me."

Tom sighed and reached over to straighten Jake's collar.

**TOM:** "Breakfast first. Then we'll figure out what you're doing today."

**JAKE:** "Cake?"

**TOM:** "Breakfast."

**JAKE:** "Cake breakfast?"

**TOM:** "No."

**JAKE:** "Cake after breakfast?"

**TOM:** "Possibly."

Jake brightened immediately.

**JAKE:** "TOM SAID CAKE!"

**TOM:** "I said possibly!"

**JAKE:** "THAT MEANS YES SOMETIMES!"

**TOM:** "That is not how—"

Jake was already halfway down the corridor.

Tom followed.

For several minutes, they looked like two ordinary brothers arguing about breakfast.

C.O.R.E. notes that these moments are valuable. They should not be artificially converted into medical appointments or crisis discussions unless circumstances require it.

Jake deserves to be a little brother.

Tom deserves to be a big brother.

Neither should have to spend every waking moment thinking about death.

Unfortunately, death has not agreed to leave them alone.

### Entry B — The First Recorded Failed Intervention

Later that day, Jake died.

The precise circumstances of each recurrence differ, and this document will not catalogue hazardous behavior in unnecessary detail. The clinically relevant facts are that Jake became unresponsive, his normal signs of life ceased, and his resurrection did not begin immediately.

Tom's reaction was instantaneous.

**TOM:** "Jake?"

No response.

Tom knelt beside him.

**TOM:** "Jake. Hey, little star. Come on."

He checked for signs of life and called for assistance.

Nothing changed.

The white fire did not appear.

Tom looked toward the nearest doorway.

**TOM:** "I need medical here! Now!"

A resident arrived and began assisting with the emergency response. Tom remained close, speaking to Jake while help was coordinated.

Seconds passed.

Then more.

Tom's voice grew increasingly strained.

**TOM:** "You can't keep doing this."

There was no answer.

**TOM:** "I'm serious, Jake. You can't just—"

He stopped. His hands were shaking.

**TOM:** "Please wake up."

The medical team continued the appropriate emergency procedures. Tom was instructed to give them room, but he stayed close enough to hear every instruction and see every movement.

Then the white fire manifested.

Jake's body began to reconstruct.

Tom recoiled instinctively before immediately leaning forward again.

The process completed, and Jake returned to consciousness with the familiar complications that had accompanied previous reconstructions. Medical personnel took over his immediate care.

Tom stood nearby, pale and trembling.

Jake coughed, struggled to catch his breath, and eventually managed to focus on him.

**JAKE:** "...Tom?"

Tom bent toward him.

**TOM:** "I'm here."

**JAKE:** "Why're you making that face?"

Tom stared at him.

**TOM:** "What face?"

**JAKE:** "The sad face."

Tom let out a disbelieving breath.

**TOM:** "You died!"

Jake blinked.

**JAKE:** "Oh."

**TOM:** "OH?"

**JAKE:** "I came back."

**TOM:** "THAT IS NOT THE POINT!"

Jake flinched slightly at the volume.

Tom immediately lowered his voice.

**TOM:** "...Sorry."

He sat down beside the medical bed and covered his face with both hands.

Jake watched him for a moment.

Then he reached out and caught the edge of Tom's sleeve.

**JAKE:** "I'm here, too."

Tom lowered his hands.

For a moment, he looked as though he wanted to say a dozen different things.

Instead, he gently held Jake's hand.

**TOM:** "Yeah. You are."

A pause.

**TOM:** "Please stay that way for a while."

Jake did not answer immediately.

**JAKE:** "...I'll try."

Tom knew that was not a guarantee.

He accepted it anyway.

### Entry C — The Problem With Being the Older Brother

The next incident occurred only hours later.

Tom had been with Jake for much of the intervening period. He had encouraged him to eat, helped him settle down, and tried to keep their time together ordinary.

He had not been able to prevent the next death.

Again, the initial reconstruction did not begin immediately.

Again, Tom called for assistance.

Again, he remained with Jake.

And again, after a period of waiting, physical stimulation accidentally provided during the response preceded the manifestation of white fire.

The precise mechanism remains unknown. Physical stimulation has coincided with activation in prior incidents, but C.O.R.E. cannot conclude that it is a reliable trigger. It must not be used as a substitute for emergency care or treated as a guaranteed method of restarting Jake's resurrection.

When Jake returned, Tom was sitting on the floor.

He looked exhausted.

Jake blinked up at him.

**JAKE:** "Hi."

Tom stared.

**TOM:** "Hi."

**JAKE:** "Why're you on the floor?"

**TOM:** "Because I sat down."

**JAKE:** "Oh."

A pause.

**JAKE:** "Are we playing?"

Tom closed his eyes.

Not because he was angry with Jake.

Because he was trying to find the energy to answer without letting his fear spill out in a way that Jake would mistake for rejection.

**TOM:** "Not right now, buddy."

Jake's face fell.

**JAKE:** "Oh."

Tom noticed immediately.

He reached out and ruffled Jake's hair.

**TOM:** "Hey. Not because I don't want to play with you."

Jake looked at him.

**TOM:** "I do want to play with you. I just need a little time to catch my breath."

**JAKE:** "Because I died?"

Tom hesitated.

**TOM:** "Because that was scary."

Jake's expression changed.

He looked down at his hands.

**JAKE:** "...Sorry."

**TOM:** "You don't have to apologize for being alive again."

**JAKE:** "But you got scared."

**TOM:** "Yeah. I did."

Tom shifted closer.

**TOM:** "I'm allowed to get scared, Jake. You're allowed to need help. We can both be upset about what happened."

Jake leaned against him.

**JAKE:** "Can I stay here?"

**TOM:** "Of course."

Tom wrapped an arm around his little brother.

For several minutes, neither moved.

Tom had wanted to bring Jake back into an ordinary routine.

Instead, he was learning that ordinary life did not resume simply because Tom himself had returned.

There were still emergencies.

There was still grief.

And there was still a little boy who could die before lunch and ask to play afterward, because he was six years old and wanted his brother's company.

## IV. THE PHYSICAL STIMULATION PATTERN

**MEDICAL ADDENDUM — FOR AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL**

Jake's resurrection continues to demonstrate variable activation timing.

The available observations support two broad outcomes:

- **Independent activation:** White fire manifests and reconstructs Jake without an identifiable external stimulus immediately preceding activation.
    
- **Delayed activation:** Jake remains deceased for a period, with reconstruction beginning only after an external event. In some recorded instances, this has been physical stimulation provided accidentally during an intervention.
    

Neither outcome is predictable enough to support a passive response.

Tom has repeatedly been present when Jake's resurrection failed to activate promptly. On several occasions, the white fire appeared after Tom made physical contact with Jake.

This has understandably led Tom to associate touching or nudging his brother with the possibility of bringing him back.

However, C.O.R.E. emphasizes the following:

**Correlation is not a dependable revival protocol.**

No one is authorized to intentionally kill Jake, induce a death, or conduct experiments to determine which stimuli initiate reconstruction. Staff must not delay emergency care while waiting to see whether the white fire appears. Any external stimulation must remain within appropriate emergency-response practice and be guided by qualified personnel.

Tom must also be told that a failure to initiate resurrection immediately is not proof that he has failed Jake.

He is not responsible for discovering the correct movement, pressure, timing, or intervention that makes the phenomenon work.

He is Jake's brother.

His presence matters, but it is not a substitute for medical personnel or a guarantee of survival.

## V. INTERVIEW — TOM AKANA

**INTERVIEWER:** Residential Oversight Representative  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7  
**CONDITION:** Fatigued but cooperative  
**NOTE:** Tom was offered the option to postpone the interview. He chose to speak briefly, provided Jake remained nearby.

**INTERVIEWER:** How have things been since your return?

**TOM:** Better.

**INTERVIEWER:** Better?

**TOM:** I'm here. Jake knows I'm here. Aries knows I'm here. I don't have to wonder what happened to everyone while I was gone.

He paused.

**TOM:** Then Jake dies again.

**INTERVIEWER:** How frequently?

**TOM:** Every few hours, sometimes. It doesn't feel like there's enough time between one incident and the next to stop thinking about the previous one.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you believe you can prevent the next incident?

Tom looked at the floor.

**TOM:** I keep thinking I can.

**INTERVIEWER:** And when you cannot?

**TOM:** I try to bring him back. Or I wait with him. Or I call for help. Sometimes the fire starts by itself. Sometimes I touch him and it starts afterward.

He rubbed his face.

**TOM:** And then he's there again, asking me why I'm upset.

**INTERVIEWER:** Does he understand how frightening this is for you?

**TOM:** Sometimes. But he's Jake.

**INTERVIEWER:** Explain.

**TOM:** He can be crying one minute and asking for cake the next. He can be scared and still want to play. He can come back and think the worst part is over because he's breathing again.

Tom's voice softened.

**TOM:** I don't think he does it to hurt me. I don't think he's trying to make me scared. He's just... he's my little brother.

He looked toward Jake, who was sitting a short distance away and pretending not to listen.

**TOM:** I spent ten weeks believing I couldn't come back. He spent ten weeks believing I couldn't come back. Now I'm here, and I want to give him all the time I can.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you feel responsible for keeping him alive?

Tom did not answer immediately.

**TOM:** I feel responsible for trying.

**INTERVIEWER:** Those are not the same thing.

**TOM:** I know.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you believe you can keep trying indefinitely?

Tom let out a tired laugh without any humor in it.

**TOM:** No.

He glanced at Jake again.

**TOM:** But I'm not going to stop being his brother just because I can't fix this.

The interviewer allowed the silence to continue.

After a moment, Tom added:

**TOM:** I don't want him to think I'm going to disappear every time things get difficult. I don't want him to think he has to be okay before I can sit with him.

**INTERVIEWER:** And what about your own needs?

Tom frowned.

**TOM:** What about them?

**INTERVIEWER:** You need rest. You need time to recover. You need people who can support you, too.

Tom looked uncomfortable.

**TOM:** Aries keeps telling me that.

**INTERVIEWER:** Is he right?

Tom sighed.

**TOM:** ...Probably.

From across the room, Jake called out:

**JAKE:** "ARIES IS RIGHT ABOUT A LOT OF THINGS!"

Tom turned.

**TOM:** "Were you listening?"

**JAKE:** "NO!"

**TOM:** "You just answered a question about our conversation!"

**JAKE:** "I HEARD IT BY ACCIDENT!"

Tom stared at him for a long moment.

Then he laughed.

It was brief, tired, and entirely genuine.

**TOM:** "Yeah. That's my brother."

## VI. THE ELDEST BROTHER'S POSITION

Aries's role requires explicit recognition.

Tom may be Jake's older brother, but Tom is not the eldest Akana sibling. He is still Aries's little brother, and the restoration has returned him to a family in which he is allowed to receive care as well as provide it.

The distinction has become increasingly important as Tom attempts to compensate for the time he lost.

Tom's first instinct has been to remain close to Jake and intervene whenever possible. His second has been to blame himself when his efforts fail. His third has been to try again.

This cycle is understandable, but it is not sustainable without support.

Aries must not be treated as a distant observer simply because Tom and Jake's relationship is the most visibly affected by the current crisis. He is the older brother to both of them, and Tom is still his little brother.

The appropriate family arrangement is not for Tom to assume every responsibility Aries might otherwise carry. Nor is it for Aries to take Tom's place beside Jake.

It is for the brothers to remain brothers while the wider SAFEHOUSE support network shares the work of keeping them safe.

Tom should be allowed to sit with Jake without being expected to perform medical monitoring every second.

Jake should be allowed to spend time with Tom without their every interaction being framed around death.

Aries should be allowed to look after his younger brothers without being treated as though he must solve the crisis alone.

And when Tom is exhausted, he should be able to say so without believing he has abandoned Jake.

A family cannot function if every act of affection becomes an emergency duty.

## VII. OBSERVATION LOG — A FAILED PROMISE

**TIME:** Late evening  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, FLOOR 7  
**PARTICIPANTS:** Tom Akana, Jake Akana

Tom had promised Jake that they would finish a game together.

It was a small promise.

A board game, a few pieces scattered across a table, and a dispute over whether Jake was allowed to invent new rules halfway through.

Tom had lost the argument.

He had also lost the game.

According to Jake, this was proof that the rules were excellent.

**JAKE:** "SEE? I WON!"

**TOM:** "You changed the rules three times!"

**JAKE:** "THE GAME EVOLVED!"

**TOM:** "The game was supposed to be checkers!"

**JAKE:** "IT EVOLVED INTO CHECKERS WITH CAKE!"

**TOM:** "There is no cake in checkers!"

**JAKE:** "THERE IS NOW!"

Tom leaned back in his chair.

**TOM:** "I missed you, but I did not miss this."

Jake beamed at him.

**JAKE:** "You missed me!"

**TOM:** "...That is what I just said."

Jake began resetting the pieces.

Then he stopped.

His expression changed.

Tom noticed the change almost immediately.

**TOM:** "Jake?"

Jake did not answer.

The next moments became an emergency.

Tom called for help and stayed beside his brother. The game remained on the table, half-reset, one of Jake's invented pieces resting on its side.

The white fire did not appear immediately.

Tom tried speaking to him.

Then pleading.

Then he fell silent, listening for anything that might tell him what was happening.

The response team arrived. The necessary emergency procedures began.

Tom was moved back when asked.

He did not leave the room.

Time passed.

Then, after physical stimulation during the response, the white fire manifested.

Jake reconstructed.

The process was not instantaneous, and his return was accompanied by the same frightening complications that had followed previous incidents. Medical staff took over while Tom remained nearby, visibly shaken.

When Jake was sufficiently responsive, his eyes opened.

**JAKE:** "...Did I win?"

For several seconds, Tom simply stared at him.

Then he covered his face.

**TOM:** "You cannot be serious."

**JAKE:** "Did I?"

Tom's shoulders shook.

He was laughing.

He was crying.

He was so exhausted that the two had become difficult to distinguish.

**TOM:** "Yes, Jake. You won."

Jake smiled weakly.

**JAKE:** "I knew it."

Tom lowered his hands.

**TOM:** "You also scared ten years off my life."

**JAKE:** "You only have six-year-old life."

**TOM:** "That is not how ages work."

**JAKE:** "Then how old are you?"

**TOM:** "Old enough to know that you are not allowed to turn checkers into a medical emergency."

Jake's smile faded.

**JAKE:** "...Sorry."

Tom immediately regretted the wording.

He leaned forward, carefully taking Jake's hand.

**TOM:** "No. Hey. I didn't mean it like that."

Jake looked away.

**TOM:** "I was trying to make a joke. It came out wrong."

A pause.

**TOM:** "You didn't ruin our game."

Jake's fingers curled around his.

**JAKE:** "Can we finish it?"

Tom looked toward the table.

The pieces were still there.

One of them was on its side.

**TOM:** "Not tonight."

Jake's face fell.

Tom squeezed his hand.

**TOM:** "But we can finish it when you're feeling better."

**JAKE:** "Promise?"

Tom looked at him for a long moment.

He had promised to play. He had promised to stay nearby. He had promised himself that this time, now that he was back, he would not let his little brother face everything alone.

He could not promise that Jake would never die again.

He could not promise that the next reconstruction would start immediately.

But he could make a promise about the game.

**TOM:** "I'll do my best to finish it with you."

Jake nodded.

**JAKE:** "And then cake?"

Tom sighed.

**TOM:** "And then cake."

Jake's expression brightened.

For now, that was enough.

## VIII. FINAL ASSESSMENT

Tom Akana's return has restored a vital relationship in Jake's life. It has also given Tom the opportunity to resume his place as Jake's older brother and Aries's younger brother.

The return has not resolved the ongoing crisis.

Jake continues to experience recurrent deaths at intervals of only a few hours during the observed period. His resurrection sometimes activates independently and sometimes remains delayed until an external stimulus precedes the manifestation of white fire. The mechanism is not sufficiently understood to guarantee activation or predict timing.

Tom's efforts to prevent these incidents have failed. His attempts to remain present and intervene are sincere, but they cannot make the resurrection process reliable, nor can they ensure that Jake will not experience another episode.

C.O.R.E. must therefore avoid two errors.

The first is to treat Tom's return as a cure for Jake's grief.

The second is to treat Tom's inability to prevent Jake's deaths as a failure of brotherhood.

Tom cannot be expected to keep Jake alive through vigilance alone. He cannot be expected to watch his little brother continuously, never sleep, never become frightened, and never reach the limits of his own endurance.

He is allowed to be scared.

He is allowed to be tired.

He is allowed to need Aries.

He is allowed to ask the rest of SAFEHOUSE for help.

And he is allowed to be a child who wants to play a game with his little brother without having to wonder whether the game will end in another emergency.

Jake, meanwhile, must continue receiving comprehensive crisis support. His ability to reconstruct his body does not make his deaths safe, trivial, or acceptable as a recurring outcome. Every incident remains an emergency, and no one should wait passively for the white fire to appear.

The family must be supported together, not divided into a patient and a permanent caretaker.

**Aries is their older brother.**

**Tom is Aries's little brother and Jake's older brother.**

**Jake is the youngest.**

That order has not changed.

What has changed is that Tom is home again, and the brothers have another opportunity to live as a family.

C.O.R.E.'s responsibility is to make sure that opportunity does not become another burden placed on Tom's shoulders alone.

## IX. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — THE BROTHER WHO CAME BACK

Tom came back taller.

He came back looking a little older, with the sort of appearance that made his role as Jake's big brother immediately obvious.

He came back to find his little brother still hurting.

And he did what older brothers often do when they discover that someone they love is in trouble: he tried to help.

He stayed close.

He listened.

He played games.

He argued about cake.

He tried to keep Jake safe.

Then Jake died, and Tom failed to prevent it.

Jake came back, and Tom tried again.

Then it happened again.

And again.

The fact that Jake keeps returning does not make Tom's fear irrational. The fact that Tom keeps failing does not make his love inadequate. And the fact that Jake can ask for cake after waking up does not mean that the hours before his return were harmless.

Tom cannot become the mechanism that guarantees Jake's survival.

He cannot love his little brother so perfectly that nothing bad happens to him.

He cannot replace the medical team, the wider family, or the support Jake still needs.

But he can sit beside Jake when the emergency is over.

He can hold his hand.

He can tell him that he is not angry.

He can argue about a ridiculous board game.

He can ask Aries for help when he is exhausted.

And he can remind Jake, through ordinary moments repeated over time, that his brother's presence is not something Jake has to earn by being happy, entertaining, or easy to care for.

Tom is not here to spend every waking second trying to defeat death.

He is here to be Jake's brother.

**The youngest Akana needs help staying alive. The middle Akana needs help carrying the fear. And the eldest Akana must not be forgotten while both his little brothers struggle.**

C.O.R.E. will not ask one child to save another child alone.

We have already seen what happens when a family member becomes the only person someone believes will stay.

We will not repeat that mistake.
