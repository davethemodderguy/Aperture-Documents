Week 3 has concluded.

Fifteen remain.

---

# 2. WHAT CHANGED?

The answer is simple.

### The weather.

Week 3 introduced substantially harsher outdoor conditions.

The environment that had become manageable during the first two weeks became considerably more demanding.

The comfortable outdoor camp established during Week 2 began deteriorating.

Shelters required constant maintenance.

Supplies became more difficult to manage.

Sleep became less reliable.

Movement became increasingly exhausting.

The participants who had treated the competition as extended camping were forced to remember:

> **This is an endurance challenge.**

---

# 3. THE FIRST WAVE OF ELIMINATIONS

The majority of Week 3's eliminations occurred during the first several days.

Several operators voluntarily withdrew after determining that continuing was no longer worth the physical strain.

Others simply recognized that they had reached their personal endurance limits.

There were no disciplinary eliminations.

There were no combat eliminations.

There were no injuries requiring emergency evacuation.

Most simply walked back toward the Blacksite.

The moment they crossed the designated boundary:

> **ELIMINATED.**

---

# 4. THE CAMP

The once-busy outdoor camp is now considerably quieter.

Fifty operators had once occupied the area.

Now only fifteen remain.

Large sections of the original camp have been abandoned.

Empty shelters remain scattered across the competition zone.

Some contain personal belongings that were collected and returned to MSAC after their owners withdrew.

One operator described the scene as:

> **"A ghost town."**

Another replied:

> **"It's been three weeks."**

The first operator:

> **"Exactly."**

---

# 5. THE FIFTEEN

The remaining participants have become noticeably more disciplined.

They no longer waste supplies.

They no longer maintain unnecessary structures.

They no longer attempt to make the camp comfortable simply for comfort's sake.

Everything now has a purpose.

### Current priorities:

1. Shelter.
    
2. Food.
    
3. Water.
    
4. Sleep.
    
5. Physical condition.
    
6. Remaining outside.
    

Everything else is secondary.

---

# 6. COOPERATION REMAINS

Despite the dramatic reduction in participants, cooperation has not disappeared.

Quite the opposite.

The remaining fifteen have developed an extremely efficient communal system.

Participants continue to:

- share food;
    
- repair shelters;
    
- assist exhausted operators;
    
- maintain common areas;
    
- rotate sleeping periods;
    
- monitor one another's condition;
    
- distribute supplies fairly.
    

The competitive aspect remains.

Everyone knows that eventually only one person can win.

Nobody appears interested in watching another participant collapse simply to improve their own odds.

One operator summarized the situation:

> "We're competitors."

Pause.

> "We're also the only fifteen idiots still out here."

---

# 7. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri has continued observing the challenge.

She was informed that thirty-five operators had withdrawn.

Her response was a simple:

> **"Oh."**

She then asked how the remaining fifteen were doing.

Personnel reported that they were adapting well.

Neri nodded.

She then left.

Approximately twenty minutes later, several boxes of supplies appeared at the edge of the competition zone.

No explanation accompanied them.

---

# 8. THE FIFTEEN DISCOVER THE SUPPLIES

The boxes contained:

- food;
    
- water;
    
- blankets;
    
- medical supplies;
    
- replacement shelter materials;
    
- coffee.
    

One operator stared at the supplies.

> **"She's helping us."**

Another replied:

> **"She's not helping us win."**

Pause.

> **"She's helping us survive."**

No one disagreed.

---

# 9. MEDICAL OVERSIGHT

Although the challenge is intentionally difficult, C.O.R.E. continues monitoring participants remotely.

The competition does not override emergency medical intervention.

Any participant experiencing a genuine medical emergency may be removed from the competition and treated.

Such removal constitutes withdrawal rather than a competition victory.

Neri has specifically refused to allow the challenge to become a test of who can ignore medical problems the longest.

A note found attached to the medical supply box read:

> **"Don't be stupid."**

A second line had been added underneath:

> **"If you're actually hurt, go inside."**

---

# 10. THE PSYCHOLOGICAL SHIFT

Week 1 was characterized by confidence.

Week 2 was characterized by adaptation.

Week 3 is characterized by determination.

The remaining fifteen understand that they are no longer participating casually.

They have survived longer than seventy percent of the original field.

Every remaining participant now has a realistic possibility of receiving one of the three remaining Night Stalkers.

The atmosphere has therefore become considerably more serious.

---

# 11. MSAC INTERNAL DISCUSSION

One surviving operator reportedly asked:

Another answered:

> "Yeah."

> "Three Night Stalkers."

> "Yeah."

> "So we're basically fighting for three slots."

> "No."

Pause.

> "We're fighting to be the last one standing."

The first operator considered this.

---

# 12. CURRENT STANDING

### **15 / 50 REMAINING**

The remaining fifteen operators are now officially classified as the **Final Fifteen**.

No individual ranking has been assigned.

No advantage has been awarded based on previous performance.

All fifteen remain equally eligible.

The challenge continues.

---

# 13. WEEK 4 WARNING

Neri has not announced what will happen during Week 4.

When asked whether the conditions would become harder, she shrugged.

When asked whether the challenge would end soon, she shook her head.

When asked whether there would be another surprise, she smiled.

C.O.R.E. considers the smile concerning.

Fifty entered.

Fifteen remain.

The outdoor camp that once looked like a small MSAC settlement now contains only scattered shelters and fifteen exhausted operators.

Three Night Stalkers remain available.

The margin for error is getting smaller.

And the competition has finally reached the point where everyone understands what Neri meant when she said:

> **"Last man standing."**
