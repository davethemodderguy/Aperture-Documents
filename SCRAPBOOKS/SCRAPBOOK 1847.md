## I. INITIAL ALERT

**07:16:09 — SAFEHOUSE RESIDENTIAL WING**

The first report was submitted by Clover.

Jake's room was empty.

His usual sleeping area showed no sign of recent disturbance. There was no note, no recorded departure, and no indication that he had been escorted elsewhere by authorized personnel.

His teddy bear was missing with him.

This detail immediately escalated the incident beyond an ordinary missing-person search. Jake's teddy bear contains his actual heart and is essential to his continued existence. Its absence from the room was not evidence that the bear had been abandoned. It meant the search team had to locate Jake himself.

Clover checked the adjoining rooms.

Then the corridor.

Then the places Jake normally visited when he wanted somewhere quiet.

Nothing.

At 07:19, Clover contacted Phoenix.

At 07:21, Phoenix initiated a Blacksite-wide search request.

At 07:23, Aries was notified.

At 07:25, Bao arrived at the residential wing after hearing the alert.

By 07:27, four people who loved Jake were searching for him.

By 07:29, the incident commander had authorized an immediate welfare response.

Jake Akana, six years old, had disappeared from a secured residential facility without a known destination.

No one knew whether he was frightened, injured, lost, or simply somewhere the initial search had not yet reached.

No one knew whether he had left voluntarily.

No one knew how long he had been missing.

And no one could afford to assume that he would return on his own.

---

## II. THE FOUR PEOPLE WHO NOTICED

### 07:24 — ARIES AKANA

Aries arrived in the residential wing before the first search teams had finished dividing the floor into sectors.

He asked three questions.

“Who last saw him?”

“Was his bear with him?”

“Has anyone checked the exits?”

All three questions were answered incompletely.

The last confirmed sighting was still being verified. The bear was not in Jake's room. The exits showed no immediately obvious sign of unauthorized access, but that did not establish that Jake had remained inside.

Aries went quiet.

It was not the quiet of someone who had accepted the situation.

It was the quiet of someone trying to prevent fear from interfering with the next decision.

> **ARIES:** “Check every place he knows. Then check every place he doesn't.”
> 
> **SECURITY OFFICER:** “We're already covering the residential wing.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “Then cover it again.”

Aries personally joined the search.

He did not delegate Jake's welfare to the response team and wait for a report. He searched the rooms, checked the corridors, and called Jake's name at every location where a six-year-old might reasonably hide.

There was no response.

At 07:34, Aries asked whether Camelia had seen Jake that morning.

No one answered immediately.

That pause would later become significant.

### 07:26 — CLOVER

Clover was the first person to realize Jake was missing.

He was also the first to begin blaming himself.

He retraced his steps from the previous evening, trying to remember whether Jake had mentioned wanting to go somewhere. He searched the shared recreational spaces, the quieter corners of the residential wing, and the places where Jake sometimes sat with his teddy bear.

He found a few ordinary traces of Jake's presence.

A chair that had not been pushed back beneath a table.

A small item left where it belonged.

Nothing that indicated where Jake had gone.

Every familiar place that turned up empty made the situation worse.

> **CLOVER:** “He would've told me.”
> 
> **PHOENIX:** “Maybe.”
> 
> **CLOVER:** “He would've told someone.”

Phoenix did not correct him.

There was no point arguing about what Jake might have done under circumstances none of them understood.

Clover continued searching.

At 07:38, he returned to Jake's room for the third time.

The teddy bear was still missing.

Clover stood in the doorway for several seconds, staring at the empty space.

> **CLOVER:** “His heart is with him.”

It was not reassurance.

It was a reminder of what they stood to lose if the search failed.

### 07:28 — PHOENIX

Phoenix arrived with the expression of someone who had already begun calculating every possible disaster.

Jake was alive. Jake had been restored to life by Guiding Light. Jake was supposed to be safe.

None of those facts guaranteed that he was safe now.

Phoenix immediately requested confirmation of all recorded departures, authorized movements, and unusual access events. Security personnel were instructed not to dismiss a camera gap, an incomplete log, or an apparently harmless door opening.

Then Phoenix joined the physical search.

Phoenix did not ask the others to calm down.

There was nothing unreasonable about their fear.

> **PHOENIX:** “He's six.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “I know.”
> 
> **PHOENIX:** “He shouldn't have to be missing for us to realize something's wrong.”

Aries looked at Phoenix.

Neither of them said anything else.

They both knew that Jake had been hurt before. They both knew that he could be quiet when distressed, and that his tendency to withdraw made it possible for a serious problem to remain unnoticed until someone actively looked for him.

This was not the time to determine why he had disappeared.

It was the time to find him.

### 07:31 — BAO

Bao arrived after hearing that Jake could not be found.

Her first reaction was to ask where he was.

Her second was to ask who had last seen him.

Her third was to begin searching.

Bao checked the residential areas and the routes between them. She asked staff to look in every place where Jake might have stopped because he was tired, frightened, or unsure which way to go.

She did not assume he was deliberately running away.

She did not assume he was merely hiding.

She did not assume that his small size meant he could not have traveled farther than expected.

> **BAO:** “If he got scared, he'd look for somewhere he knows.”
> 
> **CLOVER:** “We've checked those places.”
> 
> **BAO:** “Then we check them again.”

Bao's voice broke on the final word.

She turned away before anyone could respond and continued searching.

## III. CAMELIA CANDLEHEART

**07:36 — Residential Common Area**

Camelia was found sitting in the common area.

She appeared calm.

She had her notebook with her. She was reviewing a written reminder when a member of the response team approached her.

> **OFFICER:** “Camelia, have you seen Jake this morning?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Jake?”
> 
> **OFFICER:** “Jake Akana. Your younger brother.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “No. Why?”

The officer informed her that Jake was missing and asked when she had last seen him.

Camelia turned to her notebook.

She checked the pages containing names, relationships, and daily reminders.

She checked again.

Then she looked up.

> **CAMELIA:** “I don't remember.”

The officer asked whether Jake had seemed upset the previous evening.

Camelia's expression shifted.

> **CAMELIA:** “Was he?”

She could not supply a reliable answer.

She did not remember the argument.

She did not remember telling Jake that she did not want to be his older sister.

She did not remember the morning when he had told her to go away.

She did not remember the distance he had begun placing between them.

To Camelia, the missing child was her little brother, someone she knew she loved and someone she expected to see again.

She had no accessible memory of the events that might have led him to leave without telling her.

She looked back at the officer.

> **CAMELIA:** “Did I forget something important?”

The officer did not answer immediately.

Camelia gripped her notebook.

> **CAMELIA:** “Please tell me if I did.”

The officer had to make a decision.

The search for Jake took priority. There was no evidence that Camelia knew his location, and her memory impairment made her recollection unreliable. Keeping her available for further questions was appropriate, but repeatedly pressing her for memories she did not have would not help locate him.

> **OFFICER:** “Right now, we need to find Jake.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I know.”
> 
> **OFFICER:** “If you remember anything about where he might go, tell us immediately.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I will.”

She lowered her eyes to the notebook.

After a moment, she asked the question again.

> **CAMELIA:** “Is Jake missing?”

The officer confirmed that he was.

Camelia went very still.

She read the words she had just written into her notebook.

**JAKE AKANA — LITTLE BROTHER.**

Then she added another line beneath them.

**MISSING. ASK STAFF FOR HELP.**

She underlined it twice.

It was the only thing she could think to do with information she was afraid her mind would take away.

## IV. SEARCH OPERATIONS

**07:40 — RESPONSE COORDINATION**

C.O.R.E. authorized the following actions:

- A complete search of Jake's residential wing, common areas, recreational spaces, and other accessible internal locations.
    
- Review of available security footage and access records, with particular attention to the last confirmed sighting.
    
- Immediate checks of authorized exits, transport points, and routes leading away from the residential complex.
    
- Notification to relevant personnel to report any sighting of a six-year-old boy with white hair, blue constellation-like eyes, cat ears, wings, or a white cat matching his known form.
    
- Verification that Jake's teddy bear remained with him, wherever he was found.
    
- A review of the circumstances surrounding his disappearance without presuming that any individual was responsible.
    

Search personnel were instructed to consider both human and cat forms.

Jake's ability to change forms complicated visual identification. A report of a small white cat could not be dismissed as unrelated, particularly if the animal appeared to be carrying or accompanied by his teddy bear.

The search team was also instructed to avoid loud, uncontrolled searches that might frighten Jake if he was hiding nearby.

**No confirmed sighting was available at the time of this report.**

The available evidence did not establish whether Jake had left the Blacksite, where he was going, or whether he had intended to disappear.

## V. THE QUESTION NOBODY COULD ANSWER

**07:52 — SAFEHOUSE Residential Wing**

Aries returned to the common area.

Clover was checking his phone for messages. Phoenix was coordinating with the search team. Bao was speaking to personnel who had been assigned to the exterior routes.

Camelia sat at a table with her notebook open.

Aries stopped in front of her.

> **ARIES:** “Did Jake tell you he was going somewhere?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “No.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “Did you see him last night?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I think so.”
> 
> **ARIES:** “You think so?”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “I know he's my brother.”

Her voice had become defensive, though she did not appear angry.

> **CAMELIA:** “I know that much.”

Aries closed his eyes briefly.

He was frightened. He was exhausted. His little brother was missing, and every passing minute made it harder to remain composed.

But Camelia was not the person he needed to interrogate.

He pulled out a chair and sat opposite her.

> **ARIES:** “I'm not asking whether he's your brother.”
> 
> **CAMELIA:** “Then what are you asking?”
> 
> **ARIES:** “I'm asking if you know where he went.”

Camelia shook her head.

> **CAMELIA:** “No.”

Aries waited.

She looked down at the notebook again.

> **CAMELIA:** “I wish I did.”

He believed that she meant it.

That did not make the situation easier.

Across the room, Clover suddenly stood up after receiving a notification. Phoenix turned toward him immediately.

It was a message from a search team.

Not a sighting.

A request for additional information about Jake's last known movements.

Clover's shoulders sank.

Bao pressed a hand against her mouth.

Aries looked toward the corridor.

Camelia watched all of them.

She could see their fear. She could hear the urgency in their voices. She understood that Jake was missing and that everyone was terrified something might happen to him.

But she could not remember the last conversation that had changed the way Jake saw her.

She could not remember the words that might have explained why he had stopped seeking her out.

And she could not know whether his disappearance had anything to do with her.

For the moment, the truth was that no one knew where Jake was.

Anything else was a theory.

## VI. C.O.R.E. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

The current incident must not be prematurely classified as an act of deliberate flight, self-harm, or retaliation.

Jake may have left because he wanted to be alone. He may have been frightened, confused, or pursuing a destination he had not shared with anyone. He may have encountered circumstances beyond his control.

His emotional withdrawal from Camelia is documented, but the relationship between that withdrawal and his disappearance remains unconfirmed.

Likewise, Camelia's failure to remember the recent argument is consistent with her established impairment. It does not establish that she caused the disappearance, knew of Jake's intentions, or deliberately withheld information.

The search will proceed on evidence.

**Priority One:** Locate Jake alive.

**Priority Two:** Ensure the integrity of his heart-bearing teddy bear and assess his physical condition immediately upon recovery.

**Priority Three:** Establish what happened without placing responsibility on either child before the facts are known.

**Priority Four:** Once Jake is found, allow him to speak privately with a trusted adult before asking him to explain his actions to the people searching for him.

No one is to demand that he apologize for frightening the household before confirming that he is safe.

No one is to use this incident to force him back into contact with Camelia.

And no one is to assume that, because Camelia has forgotten the argument, Jake has forgotten it too.

### VII. INCIDENT COMMANDER'S CLOSING NOTE

At 08:03, the search remained active.

Aries was still looking.

Clover was still calling.

Phoenix was still coordinating the response.

Bao was still checking every place where a frightened child might have stopped to rest.

Camelia sat with her notebook open, waiting for a memory that might never arrive.

Every few minutes, someone entered the common area with an update.

Every few minutes, someone left again to search.

No one had found Jake.

The teddy bear had not been found separately, either.

That was the one small piece of information the responders could hold onto: wherever Jake was, the last available evidence did not indicate that he had deliberately left his heart behind.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet determine whether Jake left because of the fracture between himself and Camelia. We will not write that conclusion into the record simply because the timing is frightening.

But the fracture exists.

Jake remembers a sentence that made him believe his sister did not want him.

Camelia does not remember saying it.

And now Jake is missing, while the people who love him search for a child who may not want to be found by anyone.

If he has gone somewhere alone because he believes he no longer belongs here, then the search is not only for a missing child.

It is for a child who may have decided that being unwanted is easier to endure when nobody can see him.

That remains a possibility, not an established fact.

Until we know otherwise, every corridor must be checked, every credible sighting investigated, and every minute treated seriously.

**Jake Akana remains missing.**

**Search operations continue.**
