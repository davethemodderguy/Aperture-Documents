## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Camelia Candleheart suffers from severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia.

Since her discovery at Heaven's Blacksite, it has become apparent that her memory loss extends considerably beyond the circumstances of her death. Camelia struggles to retain information about her surroundings, the people she meets, facility procedures, and other details that most residents can learn through ordinary experience.

To compensate, she maintains a dedicated notebook.

It is not a diary in the conventional sense. It is an external record of information she cannot reliably trust herself to remember.

The notebook contains residents' names, descriptions of who they are, directions around the Blacksite and SAFEHOUSE, basic protocols, restricted locations, and lists of personnel she can approach for assistance. It serves as a practical reference for navigating daily life in an environment where forgetting a single name or procedure can leave her confused, embarrassed, or dependent on someone else to explain things again.

C.O.R.E. considers this notebook an essential accessibility aid.

However, one aspect of Camelia's condition remains particularly notable.

Despite the severity of her memory impairment, **she almost never forgets Jake Akana.**

She remembers Jake. She recognizes him. His identity appears to remain unusually persistent in her memory, even when other information becomes difficult for her to retain.

Camelia has also become Jake's unofficial older sibling.

The relationship has not been formally assigned, nor does it require a biological connection. It has developed through their own interactions, their shared ghostly appearance, and the particular significance Jake holds in Camelia's life.

For a girl who struggles to remember so much of the world around her, Jake is one of the few people she can almost always find her way back to.

## II. CLINICAL AND ANOMALOUS PROFILE

**Name:** Camelia Candleheart  
**Preferred name:** Camelia  
**Apparent age:** Approximately sixteen  
**Nature:** Ghost  
**Condition:** Severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia  
**Primary accommodation:** SAFEHOUSE Residential Complex  
**Memory support:** Personal reference notebook  
**Known close relationship:** Jake Akana — unofficial younger brother

### Condition Overview

Camelia's memory impairment is severe enough that ordinary repetition and familiarity cannot be assumed to produce reliable long-term retention.

She may learn information and later find that she cannot recall it. Details about people, places, procedures, and her own past can become inaccessible to her, requiring her to consult written records or seek assistance from someone she trusts.

The condition has substantial practical consequences.

A resident who forgets where a room is located may need directions. A resident who forgets a person's name may need to ask again. A resident who forgets a safety procedure may be placed in danger if expected to perform it without assistance.

For Camelia, these are not isolated inconveniences. They are recurring challenges that affect her ability to navigate her environment independently.

Her condition is designated **severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia** in her current resident record.

C.O.R.E. will not assume that the designation explains every aspect of her memory loss. The precise anomalous mechanism, its limitations, and its long-term prognosis remain insufficiently understood.

In particular, Camelia's unusual retention of Jake's identity warrants observation without premature conclusions.

It is not currently known whether this represents selective preservation, an emotional association, an anomalous exception, or a combination of factors.

What is known is that Jake occupies a remarkably stable place in her recollection.

## III. THE NOTEBOOK

Camelia's notebook is among her most important personal possessions.

She carries it as a practical necessity, using it to record information she may otherwise forget. The contents are organized around the ordinary requirements of daily life rather than a chronological account of her experiences.

Its purpose is straightforward:

**If Camelia cannot reliably remember something, she needs a dependable way to find it again.**

### Recorded Sections

**1. People**

Names, identifying descriptions, relationships, and brief reminders of who each resident is.

These entries help Camelia recognize the people around her and understand how they relate to one another.

**2. Directions**

Routes through SAFEHOUSE and Heaven's Blacksite, including landmarks and instructions for reaching familiar destinations.

The information allows her to navigate without needing someone to accompany her everywhere.

**3. Basic Protocols**

Practical instructions for common situations, including the procedures she is expected to follow as a resident.

These notes are particularly important when forgetting a rule could have consequences beyond simple embarrassment.

**4. Restricted Locations**

A list of places she should not enter, along with reminders about access restrictions and areas where she must seek authorization or assistance.

**5. People to Ask for Help**

A short reference list identifying whom she can approach when she is lost, confused, or uncertain what to do.

This section ensures that forgetting a procedure does not automatically become a crisis.

**6. Personal Reminders**

Additional information Camelia considers useful for maintaining her independence and avoiding repeated confusion.

The exact contents may change as she becomes more familiar with her surroundings.

### Welfare Assessment

The notebook should not be regarded as evidence that Camelia's condition is improving or that her memory loss is insignificant.

Quite the opposite.

Its existence demonstrates how much effort she must expend to accomplish tasks that other residents can often perform without conscious preparation.

A person who can remember a route does not ordinarily need to consult written instructions every time they travel.

Camelia may.

A person who has met someone several times can usually expect their name to become familiar.

Camelia cannot safely rely on that expectation.

The notebook compensates for these difficulties, but it does not eliminate them.

C.O.R.E. recommends that Camelia be permitted to maintain and update her notes freely. Staff should help her verify important information when requested, ensure that critical safety procedures remain accessible, and avoid treating repeated questions as deliberate carelessness.

She is not refusing to learn.

She is attempting to live in a world that she cannot reliably remember.

## V. THE EXCEPTION: JAKE AKANA

Camelia's relationship with Jake presents a striking contrast to her broader memory difficulties.

She may struggle to retain names. She may need to consult her notes to remember where she is going. She may forget details about a conversation or the proper procedure for entering a particular area.

Yet Jake is unusually persistent in her memory.

She almost never forgets him.

This does not mean that every detail involving Jake is guaranteed to remain accessible. C.O.R.E. cannot establish that she will remember every conversation, every shared experience, or every promise they make.

The distinction is more specific: Jake himself, as a person and as someone important to her, appears to remain unusually recognizable and memorable.

He is not merely an entry in her notebook.

He is someone she knows.

### The Unofficial Older Sibling

Camelia has come to occupy the role of Jake's older sibling in practice, despite there being no established biological relationship between them.

She is approximately sixteen years old. Jake is six.

Their relationship therefore carries a natural difference in age, but the defining feature is not the numerical gap. It is the way they have come to regard one another.

Jake found Camelia alone in an unused equipment room.

He spoke to her gently.

He introduced himself rather than treating her as something to fear.

And he brought her to SAFEHOUSE, where she could live among people who were willing to welcome her.

For Camelia, who has lost access to so much of her former life, that encounter appears to have become an unusually enduring point of reference.

She knows Jake as the boy who found her.

She knows him as the little ghost with the blue flame.

She knows him as someone who made room for her when she had been living alone.

And she has come to treat him as her little brother.

C.O.R.E. recognizes this as an informal familial bond, not a formal designation requiring administrative approval.

No one needs to assign Camelia the title of older sister.

She has already made it her own.

## VI. OBSERVED INTERACTION: THE NAME SHE KNOWS

**RECORD TYPE:** Resident Welfare Observation  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE Common Room  
**PARTICIPANTS:** Camelia Candleheart and Jake Akana

During an ordinary interaction, Camelia was observed consulting her notebook while attempting to recall information about the day's activities.

She reviewed several entries, turned a page, and paused.

Jake was nearby.

**CAMELIA:** "Jake?"

**JAKE:** "Mhm?"

**CAMELIA:** "What were we supposed to do today?"

**JAKE:** "I think Clover wanted to show you something."

**CAMELIA:** "The clovers?"

**JAKE:** "Yeah."

Camelia wrote a short reminder in her notebook.

**CAMELIA:** "Okay. Lucky clovers. Clover. Show me clovers."

She checked the entry again.

**CAMELIA:** "I should write down where we're meeting, too."

**JAKE:** "You can ask her."

**CAMELIA:** "I know. But if I forget again, I'll have it written down."

**JAKE:** "Okay."

Camelia closed the notebook.

For a moment, she looked at Jake.

**CAMELIA:** "I don't need to write you down, though."

Jake tilted his head slightly.

**JAKE:** "Why?"

**CAMELIA:** "Because I know you."

**JAKE:** "You know everyone here."

**CAMELIA:** "Not always."

Her answer was quiet, matter-of-fact.

**CAMELIA:** "Sometimes I look at someone and I know I've met them before, but I can't remember their name. Or I remember their name but forget what they do. Sometimes I have to read my own notes just to figure out where I was going."

She rested a hand on the notebook.

**CAMELIA:** "But I remember you."

Jake was silent.

**CAMELIA:** "I remember finding you—or, well, you finding me. I remember your name. I remember that you're my little brother."

**JAKE:** "I'm your little brother?"

**CAMELIA:** "Unofficially."

**JAKE:** "Oh."

A brief pause followed.

**JAKE:** "Okay."

Camelia looked at him.

**CAMELIA:** "That's all you have to say?"

**JAKE:** "Do I need to say something else?"

**CAMELIA:** "...No."

**JAKE:** "Okay."

Camelia gave a small, amused shake of her head.

**CAMELIA:** "You're strange."

**JAKE:** "You're a ghost with three flames."

**CAMELIA:** "And you're a ghost with one."

**JAKE:** "See? We're both strange."

**CAMELIA:** "That doesn't mean you have to agree with me."

**JAKE:** "Okay."

Camelia looked down at her notebook again, smiling faintly.

Then she wrote one more line beneath Jake's name.

**JAKE IS MY LITTLE BROTHER.**

She closed the notebook.

She did not need to check the entry again.

## VII. MEMORY SUPPORT AND RESIDENT WELFARE DIRECTIVES

In view of Camelia's severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia, C.O.R.E. recommends the following accommodations.

**1. Preserve access to the notebook.**

Camelia must be permitted to carry, update, and consult her notebook as needed. It should be treated as an essential personal aid, not an optional convenience.

**2. Make critical information easy to retrieve.**

Important safety procedures, directions, and contact information should be written clearly and organized so that Camelia can locate them quickly.

**3. Do not punish repetition.**

If Camelia asks the same question multiple times, staff should respond patiently. Repetition should not be interpreted as disrespect or deliberate noncompliance.

**4. Confirm important instructions.**

Where safety is concerned, personnel should not assume that an instruction given once will remain accessible to Camelia later.

**5. Respect her privacy.**

Her notebook may contain personal information about her condition, her relationships, and her daily life. It should not be examined without appropriate permission or a legitimate safety justification.

**6. Do not make Jake responsible for her care.**

Their familial bond is meaningful, but Jake is himself a six-year-old child with his own needs. Camelia may rely on him emotionally, and he may provide companionship, but adult residents and C.O.R.E. personnel remain responsible for ensuring that both children receive appropriate support.

**7. Recognize the importance of their relationship.**

Camelia's unusual retention of Jake's identity should be acknowledged without pressuring her to explain it. Their bond may be a source of comfort and continuity, but it must not be treated as a substitute for broader memory support.

## VIII. INVESTIGATIVE CONSIDERATIONS

Camelia's condition raises several unanswered questions.

Why does she retain Jake so consistently when so much other information is difficult to recall?

Does the persistence reflect emotional significance, an anomalous property of her memory, or a mechanism that has not yet been identified?

Can her memory loss fluctuate depending on context?

Can written records reliably preserve information over time, or does she sometimes lose the ability to connect recorded information with the experiences it describes?

At present, C.O.R.E. cannot answer these questions conclusively.

The fact that Camelia remembers Jake should not be treated as evidence that her amnesia is selective by choice, nor should her ability to recognize him be interpreted as proof of a specific supernatural bond.

There is no need to invent an explanation where observation is still ongoing.

For welfare purposes, the immediate priority is more practical.

Camelia needs a stable environment, dependable records, patient assistance, and the freedom to build relationships without being expected to remember everything perfectly.

Her notebook can help her find a room.

It can remind her who to contact.

It can tell her which doors she should not enter.

But it cannot replace the experience of being welcomed by someone who knows her.

And, for reasons still unknown, Jake appears to be one of the people she can almost always recognize without needing the notebook to tell her who he is.

## IX. CLOSING NOTE — THE NAME SHE DOESN'T NEED TO LOOK UP

Camelia writes things down because she cannot always trust her memory.

She writes down names.

She writes down directions.

She writes down who works where, which places she should avoid, whom she can ask for help, and what she should do when she gets lost.

Her notebook is a map of a world that does not reliably stay in her head.

There is something painful about that.

Imagine meeting someone, speaking with them, perhaps even beginning to like them—and later discovering that the memory has slipped away. Imagine having to look up a person's name in your own handwriting because you cannot be certain you will remember it otherwise.

Imagine being unable to rely on familiarity.

For Camelia, this is not an occasional inconvenience. It is part of living with severe Etheric Retrograde Amnesia.

She has learned to compensate.

She prepares.

She records.

She checks her notes.

She tries again.

And then there is Jake.

She almost never forgets Jake.

We do not know why. We should not pretend that we do.

Perhaps he occupies a particularly important place in her life. Perhaps there is something about their encounter that has made his identity unusually persistent. Perhaps the answer lies in the anomalous nature of her condition.

For now, those remain possibilities.

What matters to resident welfare is that Camelia has someone she recognizes, someone she can call her little brother, and someone whose name she does not ordinarily need to look up.

Jake found her in an empty room.

He gave her a name to remember.

Not his own name—she knew that soon enough—but the name of a person she could begin to trust.

**Jake Akana.**

Her little brother.

And Camelia, for all the things she may forget, almost always remembers him.

_And Jake, whether either of them fully understands why, remains one of the clearest points of continuity in her life._

_We cannot promise Camelia that her memories will return._

_We can ensure she never has to face the uncertainty alone._
