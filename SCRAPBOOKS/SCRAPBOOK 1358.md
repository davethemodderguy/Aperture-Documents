# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Phoenix Raffedella is an exceptionally competent medical assistant.

She is also, when she is not working:

> **astonishingly clumsy.**

The distinction has become sufficiently consistent that C.O.R.E. now recognizes two observable Phoenix states.

### ON DUTY

Phoenix is:

- attentive;
    
- medically precise;
    
- highly knowledgeable;
    
- observant;
    
- capable of remaining calm under extreme conditions;
    
- and exceptionally reliable when treating another person.
    

### OFF DUTY

Phoenix is:

- sleepy;
    
- distracted;
    
- uncoordinated;
    
- frequently exhausted;
    
- prone to walking into things;
    
- prone to falling;
    
- occasionally incapable of noticing hazards directly in front of her;
    
- and apparently capable of dying from circumstances that most people would consider mildly inconvenient.
    

Her Transcendense condition has consequently created an unusual situation.

Phoenix is not particularly difficult to kill.

She is simply:

> **extremely difficult to keep dead.**

---

# 1. THE PATTERN

C.O.R.E. initially assumed Phoenix's repeated deaths were isolated incidents.

They are not.

A review of medical records identified a recurring pattern:

> **Phoenix stops working → Phoenix becomes relaxed → Phoenix becomes clumsy → Phoenix gets injured → Phoenix dies → Transcendense activates → Phoenix returns → Phoenix continues whatever she was doing.**

The interval between these events varies.

The general pattern does not.

---

# 2. EXAMPLE — THE STAIRCASE

The most recent confirmed example:

Phoenix was walking downstairs.

She tripped.

She fell.

She died.

Approximately four hours later, Transcendense restored her.

Her first response was:

> **"What happened?"**

When informed:

> **"You fell down the stairs."**

Phoenix replied:

> **"...Oh."**

She subsequently returned to sitting in the same general area.

C.O.R.E. has not determined why.

---

# 3. EXAMPLE — THE DOOR

Phoenix once attempted to leave a room while looking at her phone.

She walked directly into the closed door.

She paused.

Opened the door.

Walked through.

Three steps later, she turned around and walked back into the same door from the opposite direction.

The second impact was sufficient to incapacitate her.

She recovered shortly afterward.

When asked what she had been doing:

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Checking a message."

When asked whether she saw the door:

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "...No."

---

# 4. EXAMPLE — THE CHAIR

Phoenix attempted to sit down.

She misjudged the chair.

She missed it.

She fell.

She struck the floor.

She died.

The chair remained completely undamaged.

Phoenix revived approximately two hours later.

She looked at the chair.

Then sat in it.

A witness asked:

> **"Are you sure?"**

Phoenix nodded.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Yes."

She remained in the chair for approximately six minutes.

Then fell asleep.

---

# 5. EXAMPLE — COFFEE

Phoenix was carrying a cup of coffee.

She tripped over absolutely nothing.

The coffee remained in the cup.

Phoenix did not.

She fell forward.

The cup landed upright.

Phoenix landed directly beside it.

She sustained fatal injuries.

Transcendense activated.

Upon revival, Phoenix immediately looked for the coffee.

The coffee was still there.

She picked it up.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "Good."

---

# 6. EXAMPLE — THE STAIRCASE AGAIN

Personnel attempted to restrict Phoenix's access to the staircase following her first fatal fall.

Phoenix used a different staircase.

She fell down that one too.

She survived the initial fall.

She then stood up.

Took three steps.

Tripped over the bottom step.

Died.

The medical team documented:

> **"The staircase appears to have become personally significant to Phoenix."**

---

# 7. PHOENIX'S EXPLANATION

Phoenix was eventually asked why she appeared significantly more accident-prone when not working.

She considered the question.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "When I'm working, I'm paying attention."

> **C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
> "And when you're not?"

Phoenix shrugged.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "I'm tired."

Pause.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "And I don't have to think."

This explanation is consistent with observed behavior.

Phoenix spends much of her working life maintaining intense attention toward other people's safety.

When that responsibility disappears, her vigilance appears to drop considerably.

Unfortunately, so does her awareness of:

> **stairs.**

---

# 8. JANE DOE'S OBSERVATION

Jane has become particularly familiar with Phoenix's off-duty behavior.

> **JANE:**  
> "She's terrifying when she's working."

> **C.O.R.E. OFFICER:**  
> "And when she's not?"

Jane watched Phoenix attempt to open a cabinet.

Phoenix pulled the wrong door.

Then the wrong door again.

Then opened the correct one.

> **JANE:**  
> "She's a liability."

Phoenix looked over.

> **PHOENIX:**  
> "I heard that."

> **JANE:**  
> "Good."

---

# 9. MEDICAL IMPLICATION

Phoenix's immortality has created an unusual psychological and practical problem.

She can survive accidents that would ordinarily be fatal.

This may reduce the immediate consequence of clumsiness.

It does **not** eliminate the consequences entirely.

Transcendense does not necessarily remove:

- pain;
    
- exhaustion;
    
- disorientation;
    
- emotional distress;
    
- temporary incapacitation;
    
- or the inconvenience of dying.
    

Phoenix still experiences the event.

She simply returns afterward.

C.O.R.E. therefore does not consider:

> **"She'll come back."**

an acceptable safety procedure.

---

# 10. CURRENT WELFARE GUIDANCE

Personnel are not instructed to treat Phoenix as fragile.

They are instructed to remember that she is:

> **competent but catastrophically clumsy when relaxed.**

Recommended precautions include:

- keeping walkways clear;
    
- reminding Phoenix about open doors and stairs;
    
- avoiding leaving hazardous objects where she might trip over them;
    
- checking on her if she suddenly becomes unusually quiet;
    
- allowing her to rest when tired;
    
- and, when appropriate, simply asking:
    

> **"Phoenix, are you looking where you're going?"**

Her usual answer is:

> **"Yes."**

This answer should not necessarily be considered reliable.

---

# 11. THE IMMORTALITY PROBLEM

Phoenix's condition creates a strange contrast.

During emergencies:

> **Phoenix keeps other people alive.**

Outside emergencies:

> **Phoenix keeps accidentally dying.**

The irony is not lost on C.O.R.E.

Her name is Phoenix.

Her condition causes her to return from death.

She is a medical professional.

She understands exactly what happens when someone dies.

And yet, when she is off duty, she appears to possess approximately the spatial awareness of someone walking through a room for the first time.

---

# 12. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Phoenix's recurrent accidental deaths should not be interpreted as intentional self-harm.

There is no evidence that she deliberately causes these incidents.

The pattern is much simpler.

Phoenix becomes relaxed.

Phoenix becomes tired.

Phoenix stops paying attention.

Something goes wrong.

Transcendense activates.

Phoenix wakes up.

Then she continues her day.

The phenomenon is therefore classified as:

> **RECURRING ACCIDENTAL FATALITY — NON-INTENTIONAL**

with contributing factors including:

- narcolepsy;
    
- chronic fatigue;
    
- poor situational awareness while off duty;
    
- general clumsiness;
    
- and an unusual lack of concern regarding her own post-mortem recovery.
    

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Phoenix is one of the most medically capable people currently associated with the Blacksite.

Give her an injured person and she becomes focused.

Calm.

Precise.

Professional.

Take away the medical emergency?

She walks into doors.

She falls down stairs.

She misses chairs.

She trips over nothing.

She dies.

Then she wakes up several hours later and asks whether anyone saved her coffee.

The medical department has therefore reached a rather unusual conclusion:

> **Phoenix does not need to be protected from death.**

> **She needs to be protected from herself when she's bored.**
