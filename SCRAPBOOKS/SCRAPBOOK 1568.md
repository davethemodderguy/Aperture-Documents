# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Bao remembered Jake.

She remembered him suddenly.

Not because of a new anomaly.

Not because of a recovered record.

Not because someone told her his name.

She simply remembered.

The realization was immediate.

Bao stopped what she was doing.

She looked toward the empty space beside her.

And remembered Jake.

Everything followed.

His room.

His teddy bear.

His drawings.

His habit of sitting on the floor.

The way he used to follow people around.

The way he called her **Mom**.

The fact that she had not seen him in weeks.

Then months.

Bao's expression changed.

She immediately went looking for him.

And, when she found the others, she reminded them.

---

# 1. BAO

Bao entered the common area without announcing herself.

She looked at the residents present.

Her voice was quiet.

> **BAO:**  
> “Jake.”

Several residents looked up.

> **BAO:**  
> “Where's Jake?”

Silence.

Nobody answered immediately.

Bao's expression tightened.

> **BAO:**  
> “How long has it been?”

No one knew.

That answer was enough.

Bao began reminding them.

Not aggressively.

Not accusingly.

Just one person at a time.

> **BAO:**  
> “Jake.”

> **BAO:**  
> “The little star.”

> **BAO:**  
> “His teddy bear.”

> **BAO:**  
> “His room.”

> **BAO:**  
> “The flowers.”

> **BAO:**  
> “He lives here.”

One by one, the memories returned to the forefront.

Neri stopped moving.

Nora went silent.

Jane's expression changed immediately.

Aeterna's eyes brightened.

The Obol Squad abandoned their current conversation.

Others began asking the same question.

> **“Where is he?”**

---

# 2. THE DOOR

They reached Jake's room.

Bao knocked.

No response.

She knocked again.

> **BAO:**  
> “Jake?”

Nothing.

Jane tried the handle.

It did not move.

The door was locked.

From the inside.

C.O.R.E. security personnel confirmed that the lock had not malfunctioned.

No external override had been triggered.

The physical mechanism was functioning normally.

Jake had locked himself inside.

Bao rested her hand against the door.

> **BAO:**  
> “Jake?”

Silence.

No footsteps.

No movement.

Nothing.

---

# 3. NO FORCED ENTRY

Neko arrived shortly afterward.

He examined the access panel.

Then the mechanical lock.

Then the door.

> **NEKO:**  
> “It's locked from inside.”

Jane looked at him.

> **JANE:**  
> “Can you open it?”

Neko paused.

> **NEKO:**  
> “Yes.”

Another pause.

> **NEKO:**  
> “But I don't think we should.”

Bao nodded.

The decision was immediate.

Nobody would force the door open unless Jake's life was in immediate danger.

They could not undo twenty-five weeks of isolation by immediately taking away the one boundary Jake had chosen for himself.

---

# 4. THE ROOM

Bao remained outside.

The others gathered behind her.

No one spoke for several seconds.

Then:

> **BAO:**  
> “Jake?”

Nothing.

Bao slowly sat down outside the door.

She rested her back against the wall.

> **BAO:**  
> “I'm here.”

No response.

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't have to open the door.”

Still nothing.

> **BAO:**  
> “I'm staying.”

Jane sat down beside her.

Then Neri.

Then Nora.

Then Aeterna.

Eventually, the hallway became crowded.

Nobody demanded that Jake come out.

Nobody demanded that he talk.

They simply stayed.

---

# 5. THE ABSENCE

At approximately 01:14, Neri noticed something.

The hallway was quiet.

The door remained closed.

The room was silent.

Neri looked at the door.

Then at Bao.

She understood.

Jake had not simply been alone.

He had been alone long enough to make **being alone his normal state.**

Neri lowered her head.

She whispered:

> **NERI:**  
> “We were late.”

Bao closed her eyes.

> **BAO:**  
> “Yes.”

---

# 6. GRASSLAND

At 02:03, Aeterna noticed the missing elevator button.

U-01.

The access point to LSIN-001.

The Grassland.

Nobody needed to discuss it.

They went.

---

# 7. ENTRY

The elevator opened.

Everyone stepped through.

The Grassland was waiting.

But it was different.

The endless green field was under a darkened sky.

Rain fell continuously.

There was no sunlight.

No warm breeze.

No gentle music.

The horizon was obscured by rain.

The grass bent beneath the water.

And the flowers—

The flowers were dying.

---

# 8. THE DRAWN FLOWERS

The 2D flowers that Jake had once created were scattered across the Grassland.

They had always looked strange.

Crude.

Childlike.

Almost like drawings placed into reality.

Now they hung beneath the rain.

Their paper-like petals drooped.

Their colors had faded.

Some had collapsed completely into the wet grass.

Others remained standing only because their stems had not yet broken.

One flower lost a petal.

It fell into a puddle.

The ink bled.

---

# 9. REACTION

Nora stopped walking.

> **NORA:**  
> “They're dying.”

No one answered.

Neri crouched beside one.

She touched a wilted petal.

It felt damp.

Real.

She looked at her hand.

> **NERI:**  
> “Jake made these.”

Bao nodded.

> **BAO:**  
> “I know.”

Neri stared at the flower.

> **NERI:**  
> “They're supposed to be happy.”

Bao did not answer.

---

# 10. AETERNA

Aeterna looked toward the horizon.

Her celestial eyes illuminated the rain.

She scanned the Grassland.

Nothing.

No Jake.

No teddy bear.

No figure beneath the sky.

Only rain.

Aeterna's voice was unusually quiet.

> **AETERNA:**  
> “He's been here.”

Jane looked at her.

> **JANE:**  
> “How do you know?”

Aeterna pointed.

A small patch of grass near the center of the field had been flattened.

Beside it was a single blue flower.

Unlike the others, it had not wilted.

---

# 11. THE FLOWER

Bao approached.

She knelt.

The flower was one of Jake's.

Simple.

Crude.

Drawn with thick blue lines.

The rain struck its petals.

It remained standing.

Bao touched it.

> **BAO:**  
> “Jake?”

The flower did not respond.

Of course it did not.

It was only a flower.

But Bao remained there anyway.

---

# 12. THE GRASSLAND'S SILENCE

LSIN-001 had previously been classified as a restorative environment.

Warm.

Peaceful.

Safe.

Its primary anomalous effect restored memories.

It had once been where everyone went to find Jake.

Where they sat beside him.

Where they waited.

Where they remembered.

Now the same place felt abandoned.

The anomaly itself had not become hostile.

The Grassland was simply reflecting something different.

Or perhaps everyone was finally seeing it differently.

---

# 13. NERI

Neri walked farther into the field.

She looked at the rain.

Then at the wilted flowers.

Then toward the place where Jake had once sat with his knees against his chest.

Her voice was barely audible.

> **NERI:**  
> “He waited here.”

Bao looked at her.

Neri continued.

> **NERI:**  
> “We made him wait here.”

She looked down.

> **NERI:**  
> “Again.”

---

# 14. JANE

Jane stood in the rain.

Her clothes became soaked almost immediately.

She did not move.

> **JANE:**  
> “Jake.”

Nothing.

She looked across the field.

> **JANE:**  
> “It's me.”

Silence.

> **JANE:**  
> “You don't have to come back with us.”

Her voice softened.

> **JANE:**  
> “You don't even have to come out.”

She swallowed.

> **JANE:**  
> “We just want you to know we're here.”

---

# 15. BAO

Bao eventually sat down in the wet grass.

Rain ran through her hair.

She did not care.

She held the wilted flower in both hands.

> **BAO:**  
> “I'm sorry.”

No response.

> **BAO:**  
> “I remembered you.”

Pause.

> **BAO:**  
> “And then I realized how long it had been.”

Her voice shook.

> **BAO:**  
> “I should have remembered sooner.”

She looked at the flower.

> **BAO:**  
> “I'm sorry, Jake.”

---

# 16. NO RESPONSE

Nothing happened.

The rain continued.

The flowers continued wilting.

The grass moved beneath the wind.

Nobody heard Jake's voice.

Nobody saw his silhouette.

Nobody knew whether he was even inside the Grassland.

But nobody left.

---

# 17. RESIDENT RESPONSE

Eventually, everyone scattered throughout the field.

Not searching aggressively.

Not shouting.

Not forcing the anomaly to reveal him.

They simply remained.

Some sat beneath the rain.

Some stood.

Some crouched beside the flowers.

Some walked slowly across the field.

Nora held one of the wilted flowers carefully in both hands.

Aeterna stood nearby.

Jane sat in the grass.

Neri remained beside Bao.

Nobody spoke much.

There was nothing useful to say.

---

# 18. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

The significance of the Grassland had changed.

Previously:

> **The Grassland was where they found Jake.**

Now:

> **The Grassland was where they were waiting for him again.**

The difference was painful.

Previously, Jake had been waiting for them.

Now they were waiting for Jake.

---

# 19. 04:26

Rain continued.

Bao remained seated.

The flower in her hands had almost completely wilted.

She carefully placed it back into the soil.

Then she whispered:

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't have to forgive us.”

Pause.

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't have to make us feel better.”

Another pause.

> **BAO:**  
> “You don't have to come home tonight.”

Bao looked toward the empty horizon.

> **BAO:**  
> “Just... don't think nobody remembers you.”

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake's breakdown has exposed a fundamental failure in the SAFEHOUSE welfare structure.

Everyone remembered Jake.

But remembering someone and **being present for them** are not the same thing.

Bao remembered.

She reminded everyone.

They came.

But they arrived after twenty-five weeks of absence.

And when they finally reached his door, it was locked.

The Grassland offered no easier answer.

Only rain.

Wilted flowers.

And the quiet realization that the place where they had once found Jake waiting for them was now empty.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

At 05:02, the rain intensified.

Nora carefully picked up a fallen flower.

Its petals were almost entirely colorless.

She held it against her chest.

> **NORA:**  
> “He made these.”

Aeterna looked at the flower.

> **AETERNA:**  
> “Yes.”

Nora's voice became smaller.

> **NORA:**  
> “Then why are they dying?”

No one answered.

Bao looked across the Grassland.

For a moment, she thought she saw a small figure in the distance.

White hair.

A teddy bear.

A tiny silhouette beneath the rain.

She stood immediately.

But when lightning illuminated the field—

There was nothing there.

Only a wilted flower.

And the rain.
