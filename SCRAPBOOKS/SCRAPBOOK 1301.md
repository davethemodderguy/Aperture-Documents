**SUBJECT:** Jake — Voluntary Isolation Despite Fear of Solitude  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE / SECTOR 4-C

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has continued isolating himself.

This behavior initially appeared contradictory.

Jake has repeatedly demonstrated that he **hates being alone.**

He becomes frightened when left by himself for extended periods.

He seeks proximity to Nora.

He stays near Aeterna when she is available.

He will sit beside other residents without speaking simply to avoid being alone.

He has asked personnel:

> **"Can you stay?"**

And yet Jake continues deliberately removing himself from shared spaces.

He hides.

He wanders away.

He chooses empty rooms.

He sits behind furniture.

He avoids being found.

The contradiction is now considered a central component of Jake's current welfare state.

---

# 2. OBSERVED PATTERN

Jake's current behavior can be summarized as:

> **Jake does not want to be alone.**

followed by:

> **Jake repeatedly makes himself alone.**

He will leave a room after noticing people entering.

He will hide when someone begins looking for him.

He will decline invitations to sit with others.

He will sometimes walk away from Nora despite clearly wanting her company.

He may even hide while listening to people searching for him.

When eventually found, he may become visibly relieved.

This has occurred repeatedly.

---

# 3. INCIDENT — CAFETERIA

Jake entered the cafeteria at approximately 16:20.

Several SAFEHOUSE residents were present.

He stood near the entrance.

His teddy bear was wrapped around his shoulders.

He looked at the other residents.

Nobody appeared to be paying particular attention to him.

Jake remained there for approximately thirty seconds.

Then he left.

He walked down the corridor.

He entered an unused room.

He closed the door.

He sat on the floor.

Approximately eight minutes later, his bear's heartbeat became noticeably irregular.

Jake began crying.

---

# 4. WELFARE INTERVIEW

A welfare officer located him.

The officer did not enter immediately.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Jake?"

Silence.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Can I sit outside?"

A pause.

> **Jake:**  
> "...Yes."

The officer sat outside the doorway.

Several minutes passed.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Why did you leave?"

Jake did not answer.

Another minute passed.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Did you want to be alone?"

Jake shook his head.

> **WELFARE OFFICER:**  
> "Then why did you leave?"

Jake hugged his bear.

His voice was barely audible.

> **Jake:**  
> "I don't know."

---

# 5. SECOND OBSERVATION

Later that evening, Nora found Jake hiding beneath a table.

She sat on the floor nearby.

> **Nora:**  
> "Jake."

Jake looked at her.

> **Nora:**  
> "Do you want me to go?"

Jake immediately shook his head.

Nora remained.

A few seconds later:

> **Jake:**  
> "You can go."

Nora paused.

> **Nora:**  
> "Do you want me to?"

Jake shook his head again.

> **Nora:**  
> "Okay."

She stayed.

Jake looked down at his teddy bear.

The heartbeat gradually became steadier.

---

# 6. THE CONTRADICTION

C.O.R.E. initially considered two possibilities:

### Possibility A

Jake genuinely wanted solitude.

### Possibility B

Jake wanted companionship but was incapable of requesting it.

Neither explanation fully accounts for the observed behavior.

The current evidence suggests a third possibility:

> **Jake wants people near him, but does not believe he is allowed to ask them to stay.**

This would explain why he repeatedly isolates himself while becoming distressed by the resulting loneliness.

---

# 7. WHY JAKE LEAVES

Jake's behavior appears connected to several overlapping fears.

He may fear:

- becoming a burden;
    
- worrying people;
    
- being watched while distressed;
    
- having to explain himself;
    
- being asked about his mother;
    
- being asked about the Starlands;
    
- having someone see him flicker;
    
- someone taking his teddy bear;
    
- becoming dependent on another person;
    
- eventually losing that person.
    

The last concern appears particularly significant.

Jake has already experienced abandonment.

He waited for his mother.

She did not return.

He spent years alone after death.

He was eventually separated from his body.

He was returned from the Starlands.

His teddy bear was stolen.

And he has repeatedly experienced people and places disappearing from his life.

From Jake's perspective, closeness may therefore carry an additional risk:

> **If I become attached, I can lose them.**

---

# 8. THE TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear remains with him throughout these episodes.

This is important.

When Jake hides, he does not leave the bear somewhere safe.

He takes it with him.

When he becomes frightened, he holds it.

When he sleeps, he holds it.

When he is surrounded by people, he holds it.

When he isolates himself, he holds it even tighter.

The bear may therefore represent something Jake can keep close without having to ask it to stay.

Its presence is guaranteed.

People are not.

---

# 9. NERI'S OBSERVATION

Neri appears to understand Jake's behavior better than most residents.

She was asked why she thought Jake kept hiding despite clearly disliking loneliness.

Neri thought for several seconds.

Then answered:

> **Neri:**  
> "Safer."

The welfare officer asked:

> **"Safer than what?"**

Neri looked toward the hallway.

> **Neri:**  
> "Being wanted."

She did not elaborate.

# 11. AETERNA'S OBSERVATION

Aeterna has independently identified the same pattern.

She does not pursue Jake every time he leaves.

Instead, she has adopted a consistent response.

She remains somewhere Jake knows he can find her.

When Jake eventually returns, she does not ask why he left.

She simply remains present.

On one occasion, Jake returned after approximately forty minutes.

He stood beside Aeterna.

He said nothing.

Aeterna said nothing.

Jake eventually sat against her armor.

The bear's heartbeat slowed.

No conversation occurred.

---

# 12. JAKE'S FEAR

During one particularly severe episode, Jake finally explained part of the contradiction.

He was hiding in a storage room.

His body was flickering.

His teddy bear was held tightly against his chest.

> **Jake:**  
> "I don't like being alone."

Pause.

> **Jake:**  
> "But when people come..."

He stopped.

> **Jake:**  
> "I get scared."

**WELFARE OFFICER:**  
"Of them?"

Jake shook his head.

> **Jake:**  
> "No."

He looked at his bear.

> **Jake:**  
> "Of losing them."

---

# 13. C.O.R.E. INTERPRETATION

Jake's isolation is therefore not necessarily an expression of wanting solitude.

It may instead be a form of **preemptive abandonment.**

If Jake leaves first:

Nobody can leave him.

If Jake hides first:

Nobody can discover that he needs them.

If Jake refuses to ask someone to stay:

They cannot later disappoint him by leaving.

The strategy does not make Jake happier.

It does, however, reduce his immediate exposure to the possibility of another loss.

This may explain why the behavior persists despite its obvious negative effect.

---

# 14. PERSONNEL RESPONSE

Personnel are instructed not to forcibly prevent Jake from hiding.

Do not drag him into communal areas.

Do not surround him.

Do not repeatedly demand:

> "Why are you hiding?"

Do not tell him:

> "You have friends, so stop being lonely."

Instead, maintain **accessible presence.**

Appropriate responses include:

> "I'm nearby."

> "You don't have to come out."

> "You can sit here if you want."

> "I'll be around."

> "You don't have to talk."

If Jake eventually approaches:

Do not make his return into an event.

Let him simply return.

---

# 15. CURRENT STATUS

Jake remains severely distressed.

His cardiac rhythm remains irregular.

His manifestation continues to flicker intermittently.

His pain remains present.

His Starlands memories remain intrusive.

His grief regarding his mother remains unresolved.

He continues hiding.

But he also continues seeking people out after hiding.

This distinction is significant.

Jake is not withdrawing because he has stopped wanting connection.

He is withdrawing because connection has become frightening.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake hates being alone.

Everyone who has spent enough time with him knows this.

He likes having Nora nearby.

He likes sitting beside Aeterna.

He likes knowing other residents are awake.

He likes hearing people talking in the cafeteria, even when he does not participate.

He likes knowing that somebody is nearby.

And yet he keeps hiding.

Behind doors.

Under tables.

In empty rooms.

In dark corridors.

Holding the teddy bear that has never abandoned him.

Perhaps Jake's behavior makes more sense when viewed from his perspective.

He does not think:

> **"I want to be alone."**

He thinks:

> **"If I leave first, it won't hurt as much when they leave me."**

It does not work.

It has never worked.

He still becomes lonely.

He still becomes frightened.

He still eventually looks out from whatever hiding place he has chosen and hopes someone is there.

And increasingly, someone is.

Not forcing him out.

Not demanding an explanation.

Just waiting nearby.

Because Jake may keep hiding for a while.

But when he finally whispers:

> **"Can you stay?"**

the answer should remain the same.

> **"Yes."**
