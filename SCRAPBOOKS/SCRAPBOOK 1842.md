# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. — CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### SAFEHOUSE WELFARE & RESIDENTIAL OVERSIGHT DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** CORE-SW-CC-039  
**DOCUMENT TITLE:** _The Spoon She Went to Get_  
**CLASSIFICATION:** RESTRICTED — RESIDENTIAL WELFARE  
**STATUS:** ACTIVE — MEMORY RETENTION AND DAILY FUNCTIONING  
**SUBJECT:** Camelia Candleheart  
**RELATED RESIDENT:** Jake Akana  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE, Residential Wing and Kitchen

---

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

At approximately 09:12, Camelia Candleheart attempted to retrieve a spoon from the SAFEHOUSE kitchen to administer cough syrup to Jake Akana, who was experiencing a cough.

Camelia remembered the intended task when she began walking toward the kitchen. However, before reaching her destination, she passed a doorway and lost the purpose of her trip.

She remained in the corridor, uncertain why she had left the residential lounge.

Jake, currently in his white-cat form, accompanied her and subsequently reminded her of the intended task.

Upon receiving the reminder, Camelia recognized the purpose of the trip and continued toward the kitchen.

The incident provides a practical example of how Camelia's memory impairment affects ordinary caregiving. Her initial intention was clear, appropriate, and considerate. The difficulty arose not because she did not care about Jake, but because she could not reliably retain the task long enough to complete it.

**The intended action was remembered. The journey interrupted the intention. A reminder restored the immediate context.**

This event is consistent with the previously observed average interval of approximately four minutes and thirty seconds between memory lapses, though the precise timing of this particular lapse does not establish a universal interval.

### 2. EVENT LOG

**09:10 — Residential lounge**

Jake was resting on a cushion near Camelia. He had been coughing intermittently.

Camelia leaned closer to him, watching his small body rise and fall with each breath.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake? Are you coughing?”

Jake gave a small meow.

Camelia looked toward the table, where the cough syrup had been placed for the purpose of the incident. She checked the instructions and realized she needed a spoon.

**CAMELIA:** “Wait here, okay? I'll get a spoon.”

Jake lifted his head.

**CAMELIA:** “Then we can give you your medicine.”

She stood up and began walking toward the kitchen.

At this point, she was able to state her objective correctly. No confusion was apparent.

**09:11 — Residential corridor**

Camelia walked past the doorway connecting the lounge corridor to the central passage.

She slowed down.

Then she stopped.

She looked to her left, then to her right.

Her expression suggested uncertainty rather than alarm.

**CAMELIA:** “...Where was I going?”

Jake followed behind her on four white paws.

Camelia glanced at him.

**CAMELIA:** “Did I need something?”

Jake meowed.

She waited, apparently expecting the sound to clarify the situation.

It did not.

**CAMELIA:** “Sorry. I don't know.”

She looked back toward the lounge but did not immediately return. The purpose of her trip had become inaccessible.

Jake walked closer and nudged her ankle.

Camelia looked down.

**CAMELIA:** “Hm?”

Jake meowed again, then turned toward the kitchen and took a few steps in that direction.

Camelia watched him.

**CAMELIA:** “The kitchen?”

Jake looked back at her.

**CAMELIA:** “Oh!”

Her expression brightened with recognition.

**CAMELIA:** “The spoon! I was getting a spoon for your cough syrup!”

She crouched and gently stroked his head.

**CAMELIA:** “You remembered for me.”

Jake rubbed his cheek against her fingers.

**CAMELIA:** “Good cat.”

She resumed walking toward the kitchen, with Jake accompanying her.

### 3. TASK COMPLETION

**09:12 — SAFEHOUSE kitchen**

Camelia located a clean spoon and returned to the lounge with Jake.

Before administering the medicine, she checked the label and followed the provided dosing instructions. The administration was completed with assistance available if needed.

Jake remained in cat form throughout the interaction.

Afterward, Camelia sat beside him and watched him for a while.

**CAMELIA:** “I forgot what I was doing.”

Jake settled against her leg.

**CAMELIA:** “I was only going to get a spoon. It wasn't even difficult.”

She paused, rubbing the back of his head.

**CAMELIA:** “But I forgot anyway.”

Jake purred.

**CAMELIA:** “Thank you for reminding me.”

She leaned down and gave him a gentle kiss on the forehead between his ears.

**CAMELIA:** “You're a very good little brother.”

Jake's tail curled around her wrist.

Camelia remained beside him for several minutes.

### 4. CLINICAL AND FUNCTIONAL ASSESSMENT

This incident demonstrates a distinction that personnel must understand when assisting Camelia.

She was capable of:

- Recognizing that Jake needed care.
    
- Understanding the immediate task.
    
- Identifying the item needed to complete it.
    
- Beginning the journey independently.
    
- Recognizing the task again when given a contextual reminder.
    
- Completing the task once the immediate objective was restored.
    

The interruption occurred between intention and execution.

Passing through a doorway coincided with the loss of the task's purpose. This observation should not be interpreted as proof that doorways themselves cause Camelia's memory lapses. The available evidence establishes only that she forgot her objective while moving through the corridor.

The incident also demonstrates that Camelia can benefit from simple, timely reminders. Jake's meow and movement toward the kitchen appeared to help her reconstruct the immediate context.

This does not mean Jake has a reliable ability to restore her memories. He provided a cue that helped her remember what she had been doing; he did not cure the underlying impairment.

### 5. WELFARE CONSIDERATIONS

Camelia's willingness to care for Jake remains evident. Her condition can nevertheless introduce risks when a task involves multiple steps, especially where medication, food preparation, equipment, or other safety-sensitive activities are involved.

Accordingly:

1. Camelia may participate in ordinary caregiving when appropriate, but medication preparation and administration must follow the responsible caregiver's instructions.
    
2. Where a task requires several steps, written prompts or prepared materials should be available.
    
3. Staff should offer assistance without unnecessarily removing Camelia's independence.
    
4. If Camelia loses track of a task, personnel should calmly explain the immediate objective rather than reprimand her.
    
5. Jake must not be assigned responsibility for remembering instructions, supervising medication, or managing Camelia's condition. His reminder in this incident was a spontaneous act of companionship, not a substitute for adult oversight.
    

The goal is to preserve Camelia's ability to participate in family life while accounting for the practical limitations of her memory.

### 6. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

A later review of the incident recorded one detail that might otherwise have been overlooked.

When Camelia realized she had forgotten her objective, she did not become angry with Jake, blame the environment, or insist that she had never intended to visit the kitchen.

She apologized.

When Jake helped her recover the context, she thanked him.

Her first response to remembering was not embarrassment over the forgotten spoon. It was relief that she could finish what she had set out to do for someone she loved.

That distinction matters.

Camelia's memory impairment can interrupt her actions. It does not erase the care that motivated them.

### 7. CLOSING NOTE — THE THINGS HE CAN REMIND HER OF

Later that morning, Camelia opened her notebook and added a short entry:

_Jake was coughing today._

_I went to get a spoon for his medicine._

_I forgot why I was going to the kitchen._

_Jake reminded me._

She stared at the page for a moment before adding one more line.

_He helps me when I forget._

Then she looked down at the white cat sleeping beside her.

**CAMELIA:** “Jake?”

One ear twitched.

**CAMELIA:** “If I forget something again, can you tell me?”

Jake opened one blue eye.

Camelia smiled and scratched gently behind his ear.

**CAMELIA:** “Not everything, obviously. You're a cat.”

Jake gave her a small, indignant meow.

**CAMELIA:** “A very clever cat.”

He settled back down.

Camelia closed the notebook.

She might forget this conversation before the day was over. She might someday read the entry without remembering writing it. Even the fact that Jake had helped her might become inaccessible.

But the incident had still happened.

For a brief moment, a forgotten intention had been recovered, a task had been completed, and a little girl who struggled to keep hold of the world had been helped by someone who loved her.

**C.O.R.E. FINAL NOTE:**

We must be careful not to confuse Camelia's need for reminders with an inability to care for others.

She knew Jake needed medicine. She wanted to help him. She set out to do so.

Her memory failed her halfway down the corridor.

That is the tragedy of the condition: sometimes the distance between wanting to do something and actually doing it is only one doorway.

Today, Jake helped her cross it.

Tomorrow, the responsibility remains ours to ensure that neither child has to manage the other's needs alone.
