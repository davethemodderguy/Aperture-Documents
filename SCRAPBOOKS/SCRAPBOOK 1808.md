# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The organization suspected of involvement in the assault against Jake Akana has been identified as a **cult originating in the Banlands**.

This identification substantially changes the scope of the investigation.

The organization is not an obscure group operating outside established society. It is a legally recognized organization that has existed for more than **one thousand years**.

Its longevity has afforded it institutional protections, established legal standing, and the ability to operate within the limits of the law while remaining widely despised.

The organization has earned the hostility of virtually everyone in the Banlands.

Two particularly notable adversaries are:

- **T.O.P.S.**
    
- **Krampus**
    

Neither is willing to tolerate the cult's activities. Nevertheless, neither can simply eliminate the organization or secure its conviction through hostility alone.

The cult remains legally established.

Its members cannot be convicted merely because they are hated, feared, or suspected of wrongdoing.

**Clear, admissible evidence is required.**

This presents a significant obstacle for Aperture Industries.

Jake's testimony establishes that an individual resembling Aries attacked him while wearing a cloak bearing an unidentified symbol. His statement is important evidence, but further corroboration is necessary to establish the attacker's identity, the organization's involvement, and the responsibility of specific individuals.

C.O.R.E. must therefore investigate the cult without compromising the evidentiary standard required to bring a legitimate organization to justice.

The objective is not to prove that the cult is evil.

Its reputation already precedes it.

The objective is to prove **what it did, who did it, and whether the organization authorized or supported the act**.

# 1. ORGANIZATIONAL PROFILE

**DESIGNATION:** Banlands Cult  
**ORIGIN:** Banlands  
**ESTABLISHED:** More than 1,000 years ago  
**LEGAL STATUS:** Recognized organization  
**CURRENT THREAT ASSESSMENT:** High — specific threat activity under investigation  
**KNOWN ADVERSARIES:** T.O.P.S., Krampus, numerous Banlands entities and organizations

The cult has persisted through centuries of political change, territorial disputes, leadership transitions, and repeated conflicts with other Banlands entities.

Its continued existence is not evidence of innocence.

Nor is its reputation evidence of guilt in any individual case.

Its legal standing is the central complication.

The cult is not an organization that authorities can simply declare illegal because its beliefs are disturbing or because its members are universally disliked.

Any action against it must satisfy the relevant legal requirements.

This distinction has repeatedly frustrated its enemies.

For more than a millennium, the cult has survived in a position that appears almost paradoxical:

**It is a legitimate organization that has made itself an enemy of nearly everyone around it.**

C.O.R.E. has not yet established whether the cult's longevity is attributable to political maneuvering, legal protections, internal discipline, anomalous capabilities, or a combination of factors.

No single explanation is to be accepted without evidence.

---

# 2. WHY T.O.P.S. HAS NOT DESTROYED IT

T.O.P.S. is among the organization's most determined adversaries.

Its hostility is not a secret.

Nevertheless, hostility does not confer unlimited authority.

T.O.P.S. cannot lawfully dismantle a recognized organization merely because it believes the organization is dangerous.

Without sufficient evidence of a prosecutable offense, an attempted suppression could expose T.O.P.S. to legal consequences, undermine its credibility, and provide the cult with grounds to portray itself as the victim of persecution.

The cult's longevity further complicates the situation.

It has existed for more than a thousand years. It is not a newly established group whose legitimacy can be challenged through a simple administrative action.

Any serious attempt to prosecute it must distinguish between:

- the organization's established legal existence;
    
- its beliefs and general reputation;
    
- the conduct of individual members;
    
- actions performed under organizational authority;
    
- and any direct involvement by its leadership.
    

These are separate questions.

T.O.P.S. may despise the cult.

It may possess intelligence suggesting that the organization is dangerous.

It may even have encountered its members in previous conflicts.

But unless that information meets the applicable evidentiary requirements, it cannot automatically become the basis for a successful prosecution.

The result is a frustrating stalemate.

T.O.P.S. knows the organization is an enemy.

The organization knows T.O.P.S. is an enemy.

And both understand that an unlawful or poorly substantiated attack could benefit the very people T.O.P.S. intends to stop.

---

# 3. KRAMPUS — HOSTILITY WITHOUT A CONVICTION

Krampus shares T.O.P.S.'s hatred of the cult.

His position is no less constrained.

Krampus cannot treat his personal opposition to the organization as a substitute for proof of criminal conduct.

Even if he possesses substantial knowledge of the cult's history, that knowledge must be connected to the specific offense under investigation.

Historical misconduct does not automatically establish responsibility for a new assault.

Suspicion does not identify an individual perpetrator.

And an organization's reputation does not establish that its leadership ordered a particular crime.

Krampus therefore faces the same fundamental obstacle as T.O.P.S.

**He may hate the cult. He may distrust it completely. He may have excellent reasons for doing so. None of those facts, by themselves, proves who attacked Jake.**

C.O.R.E. considers this limitation strategically important.

The cult may expect its enemies to act out of anger.

It may be prepared to exploit any attempt to bypass the law.

An impulsive response would give the organization an opportunity to redirect attention away from the assault and toward the conduct of its accusers.

That outcome must be avoided.

---

# 4. THE THOUSAND-YEAR LEGAL PROBLEM

The cult's age is not merely an historical curiosity.

It is a source of institutional resilience.

An organization that has operated for more than a thousand years may possess extensive archives, established legal relationships, recognized property, documented leadership succession, and procedures for defending its institutional status.

C.O.R.E. has not confirmed which of these advantages the cult currently uses.

However, its legal recognition creates several investigative challenges.

### 4.1 Reputation is insufficient

Reports describing the cult as dangerous, malicious, or universally hated may provide useful context.

They cannot replace evidence of the specific offense.

### 4.2 Membership is insufficient

Proof that an individual belongs to the cult does not, by itself, establish that the individual attacked Jake.

### 4.3 Symbolism is insufficient

A cloak bearing a symbol associated with the organization could be significant.

It could also be copied, misidentified, or deliberately planted.

The symbol must be authenticated and connected to the perpetrator through corroborating evidence.

### 4.4 Institutional responsibility must be established

Even if a cult member is identified as the attacker, investigators must separately determine whether the act was authorized, coordinated, financed, concealed, or otherwise supported by the organization.

An individual crime and an organizationally directed operation are not interchangeable findings.

### 4.5 Evidence must survive scrutiny

The investigation must preserve original records, establish chain of custody, document witness statements accurately, and distinguish direct observations from inference.

The cult's lawyers, representatives, and allies must be assumed capable of challenging every significant evidentiary weakness.

C.O.R.E. will not provide them with an avoidable one.

# 5. CONNECTION TO THE JAKE AKANA ASSAULT

Jake's account is the central starting point.

During his recovery, he stated:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Someone looked like Aries came, he wears some sort of cloak with this weird symbol, then he stabbed me, I fought back but he sliced my neck, he ran the second he saw Clover knocking, then I'm here"

The statement contains several investigative leads.

|Evidence|Current significance|Limitation|
|---|---|---|
|Attacker resembled Aries|Possible deliberate impersonation|Resemblance alone does not establish identity or motive|
|Cloak bearing a symbol|Potential organizational identifier|Symbol remains to be authenticated|
|Sacrificial dagger recovered|Potential physical evidence of the assault|Definitive forensic correlation remains necessary|
|Clover's arrival coincided with the attacker's flight|May help establish a timeline|Does not independently identify the perpetrator|
|Jake's injuries|Corroborate that a serious assault occurred|Do not independently establish organizational responsibility|
|Possible spatial discontinuity|May explain how the assailant entered or escaped|Teleportation remains a hypothesis unless corroborated|

The identification of the Banlands cult provides a direction for further inquiry.

It does not, by itself, complete the evidentiary chain.

Investigators must determine whether the cloak's symbol corresponds to a verified organizational marking, whether any identifiable member had the opportunity to reach Jake, and whether independent records support Jake's account.

No one is to tell Jake that the cult has been conclusively proven responsible.

He deserves an honest account of what is known, what is suspected, and what remains uncertain.

---

# 6. THE ARIES IMPERSONATION QUESTION

The possibility that the attacker deliberately resembled Aries is especially concerning.

Jake is six years old.

He trusted his older brother.

An assailant who looked sufficiently like Aries may have been able to approach him before Jake recognized the danger.

If the resemblance was intentional, it could indicate planning rather than a spontaneous attack.

It could also have been intended to cause lasting psychological damage.

However, investigators must not assume that the cult was responsible for the resemblance merely because its involvement is suspected.

The investigation must establish:

1. Whether the resemblance was deliberate.
    
2. Whether the attacker used a disguise, anomalous transformation, or another mechanism.
    
3. Whether the cloak's symbol is genuinely associated with the cult.
    
4. Whether the individual can be identified through physical, digital, testimonial, or anomalous evidence.
    
5. Whether any organizational records connect the individual to the assault.
    

Aries is not to be treated as a suspect simply because he was impersonated.

Nor should Jake be repeatedly asked to compare the attacker with his brother.

The initial statement has been recorded. Additional questioning should occur only when necessary and under welfare supervision.

The purpose is to identify the attacker, not to make Jake relive the assault until he produces a detail investigators want to hear.

# 7. STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR APERTURE INDUSTRIES

Aperture occupies an unusual position.

It has the resources to conduct a serious investigation, access to advanced surveillance and forensic systems, and relationships with powerful entities across multiple realities.

However, those advantages do not remove the need for evidence.

If Aperture acts prematurely, the cult could challenge the investigation's legitimacy, dispute the handling of evidence, or portray the operation as an attack by a powerful institution against a lawful organization.

Such a response could complicate future cooperation with T.O.P.S., Krampus, and other Banlands authorities.

Worse, it could alert the actual perpetrator that investigators are approaching the truth.

C.O.R.E. therefore recommends a controlled strategy:

- preserve the evidence already recovered;
    
- investigate the symbol and cloak independently;
    
- obtain relevant records through lawful channels;
    
- coordinate with T.O.P.S. and Krampus where information can be exchanged appropriately;
    
- distinguish verified intelligence from longstanding hostility;
    
- avoid public accusations until the evidence supports them;
    
- and maintain Jake's protection independently of the prosecution's progress.
    

The last point is essential.

Jake's safety cannot depend on how quickly investigators solve the case.

If the investigation takes months, Jake still requires care.

If the cult contests every allegation, Jake still requires protection.

If the evidence eventually points toward an individual acting without organizational authorization, Jake still deserves justice.

And if the evidence establishes that the organization itself directed the assault, Aperture must be prepared to present that case with enough rigor that the cult cannot escape through procedural failures.

# 10. RESIDENT WELFARE — SEPARATE PRIORITY

The investigation does not supersede Jake's immediate welfare needs.

Jake remains acutely distressed when left alone. His tolerance may be as little as five minutes, and he may transform into a cat and flee or hide if panic overwhelms him.

Continuous observation remains mandatory.

A designated caregiver must be present, with explicit handovers between trusted residents. No investigative interview, evidence collection exercise, or meeting with Banlands representatives may proceed in a way that leaves Jake unattended.

The following are prohibited:

- using separation to test his recovery;
    
- bringing unfamiliar investigators into his room without preparation;
    
- showing him disturbing imagery without his agreement;
    
- asking him to identify a suspect in a crowded room;
    
- making him responsible for proving the cult's involvement;
    
- implying that his account is insufficient because he is a child;
    
- or promising immediate justice when the outcome is not yet known.
    

Jake is a witness and a victim.

He is not the investigation's evidence-gathering instrument.

His account matters. His wellbeing matters independently of the case's outcome.

# 11. FINAL ASSESSMENT

The Banlands cult is an established organization with more than a thousand years of history and a reputation that has made it an enemy of virtually everyone.

T.O.P.S. hates it.

Krampus hates it.

Neither can simply remove it from existence or secure a conviction without evidence.

That constraint is not a sign of weakness. It is the condition that separates a substantiated case from retaliation.

The assault against Jake provides a serious investigative lead.

The reported resemblance to Aries, the marked cloak, the recovered dagger, and the circumstances of the attack warrant a thorough inquiry into the cult's possible involvement.

They do not yet establish the culpability of the organization or any particular member.

C.O.R.E. must now pursue two parallel objectives:

**First:** protect Jake and support his recovery without forcing him to participate in every stage of the investigation.

**Second:** build an evidentiary case strong enough that, if the cult is responsible, its legal standing cannot shield the perpetrators from accountability.

The organization has survived for more than a thousand years.

Its enemies have not forgotten what they believe it has done.

But this case will not be decided by the weight of a thousand years of hatred.

It will be decided by what investigators can prove.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Some organizations survive because they are loved.

Others survive because they are feared.

And some survive because, no matter how many enemies they accumulate, nobody has yet assembled the evidence necessary to bring them down.

The Banlands cult appears to belong to the third category.

T.O.P.S. cannot simply destroy it.

Krampus cannot simply condemn it.

Aperture cannot simply declare it guilty.

Not yet.

But a six-year-old child has described someone who looked like his brother entering his life, wearing a marked cloak, and trying to kill him.

Jake fought back.

He survived.

And now he is afraid to be alone for five minutes.

That is the human cost beneath the legal problem.

The cult's history will not make Jake's wounds less real.

Its legitimacy will not make the attack acceptable.

And the difficulty of proving the truth will not make the truth less important.

**C.O.R.E. will not confuse hatred with evidence.**

**It will not confuse legal recognition with innocence.**

And it will not allow the investigation to forget whom it is meant to protect.
