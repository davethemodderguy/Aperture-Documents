# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The fourth week marks a significant change.

Jake remains physically stable.

No major medical event has occurred.

No anomalous breach has occurred.

His room remains unchanged.

The grass continues to grow.

The sketched flowers remain.

The plushies remain scattered across the old mattress.

Jake continues to eat, drink, read, draw, and sleep.

From an external perspective, almost nothing has changed.

Internally, however, something has.

Jake has begun to believe that he has been **abandoned.**

He knows that everyone is busy.

He understands that people have work.

He understands that the lack of visits is not intentional.

He has even stated this explicitly.

But understanding something intellectually has not prevented another conclusion from forming beneath it.

> **"They're busy."**

has gradually become:

> **"They're busy without me."**

And eventually:

> **"Maybe they don't need me anymore."**

Jake has not been told this.

Nobody has said it.

Nobody has implied it.

But four weeks of silence have given the thought enough room to grow.

---

# 1. TRUE ISOLATION

The first three weeks could reasonably be described as prolonged absence of social contact.

Week Four is different.

Jake has stopped expecting contact.

He no longer looks toward the hallway when he hears footsteps.

He no longer pauses when someone passes outside.

He no longer sits near his door.

He no longer checks the common areas hoping someone might be there.

He has adapted.

That adaptation is concerning.

He has begun behaving as though his isolation is the normal state of his life.

---

# 2. JAKE UNDERSTANDS

During an observation period, Jake was heard speaking quietly to himself.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They're busy."

Pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "It's okay."

Another pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They have work."

He looked toward the doorway.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I know."

Jake lowered his head.

Then, quietly:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...I know."

There was no apparent confusion about the circumstances.

He understands.

That is what makes the situation more difficult.

---

# 3. THE OTHER THOUGHT

Approximately twelve minutes later, Jake spoke again.

His voice was barely audible.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then why does it feel like they left?"

No response was expected.

None came.

Jake looked at his teddy bear.

He pulled it closer.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They didn't."

Pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Right?"

He waited.

Then answered himself.

> **JAKE:**  
> "No."

Another pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They're busy."

He repeated the explanation.

It did not appear to help.

---

# 4. MEMORY

Jake's current reaction appears connected to his previous experiences.

He has already experienced a period in which he was forgotten.

A period in which his existence disappeared from the memories and records of the people around him.

He eventually returned.

His relationships were restored.

Everyone remembered him.

But the emotional memory of being absent remains.

Four weeks of silence appear to have activated that old fear.

The current situation is objectively different.

Jake knows this.

His emotional response does not appear to care.

---

# 5. THE EMPTY HALLWAY

Jake occasionally stands in the hallway.

He does not appear to be going anywhere.

He simply stands there.

One observation lasted approximately three minutes.

Jake looked toward one end.

Then the other.

Nothing.

He eventually whispered:

> **JAKE:**  
> "Nobody's coming."

He returned to his room.

---

# 6. THE ROOM

Jake's room has become messier.

Not because he is neglecting himself.

Because he has stopped caring about the appearance of the room.

Grass covers most of the floor.

Flowers grow between the plushies.

Books are stacked wherever there is space.

Drawings are everywhere.

Some drawings have manifested.

Others remain unfinished.

The old mattress is still his primary sleeping surface.

Jake spends most of his time there.

---

# 7. DRAWINGS

The content of Jake's drawings has changed.

Earlier drawings frequently contained:

- flowers;
    
- stars;
    
- plushies;
    
- landscapes;
    
- simple figures;
    
- memories of the Grassland.
    

Recent drawings increasingly depict empty spaces.

A table with several chairs.

One occupied.

A hallway with nobody in it.

A doorway with no one standing behind it.

A group of small figures drawn together.

One figure drawn several steps away.

The isolated figure is consistently Jake.

In one drawing, the other figures have been drawn with their backs turned.

In another, Jake has drawn himself sitting on the floor while six empty circles hover above him.

No names are written.

No explanation is provided.

---

# 8. THE MANIFESTED FLOWERS

The flowers continue to appear.

However, Jake has begun drawing them farther apart.

Previously, his room was densely covered with them.

Now there are larger patches of empty grass.

One observation recorded Jake drawing a flower.

He finished.

It manifested beside him.

Jake looked at it.

He picked it up.

He placed it on the mattress.

Then he looked around the room.

He quietly said:

> **JAKE:**  
> "At least you're here."

He hugged the flower.

The flower remained physically present.

---

# 9. FOOD

Jake continues eating.

However, meals have become increasingly solitary.

He rarely leaves his floor to eat.

Instead, he prepares simple food and returns to his room.

There is no evidence that he is refusing food.

There is evidence that he no longer expects meals to be shared.

This distinction is significant.

---

# 10. SLEEP

Jake continues to sleep face down on the old mattress.

His teddy bear is usually beneath one arm.

His sleeping periods have become longer.

He sometimes remains asleep well into the day.

When awake, he appears tired.

No acute physiological abnormality has been detected.

The current pattern appears more consistent with emotional exhaustion than medical deterioration.

---

# 11. NO ANGER

Importantly, Jake does not appear angry with anyone.

There is no resentment toward Bao.

No resentment toward Neri.

No resentment toward Nora.

No resentment toward Aeterna.

No resentment toward Jane.

No resentment toward the Obol Squad.

He does not blame them.

In fact, he continues defending them.

> **JAKE:**  
> "They're busy."

When asked whether he thinks they are avoiding him:

> **JAKE:**  
> "No."

When asked whether he thinks they forgot him:

Jake immediately shook his head.

> **JAKE:**  
> "No."

Then, after a pause:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...I just miss them."

---

# 12. THE CONTRADICTION

Jake understands that he has not been abandoned.

At the same time, he feels abandoned.

Both statements currently exist simultaneously.

He can explain the situation rationally:

> Everyone is busy.

But another part of him continues asking:

> **If they still care, why has nobody come?**

There is no simple answer.

Work is real.

Their responsibilities are real.

Their absence is also real.

Jake experiences only the last one directly.

---

# 13. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. considers the current situation a significant psychological welfare concern.

Jake remains physically stable.

He remains capable of caring for himself.

He continues eating and drinking.

He continues engaging in creative activity.

He continues demonstrating anomalous stability.

However, the internal narrative forming around his isolation is increasingly concerning.

The primary concern is no longer:

> **"Jake is alone."**

It is:

> **"Jake has begun believing that being alone is what he deserves."**

This distinction requires continued monitoring.

---

# 14. PASSIVE OBSERVATION

No forced intervention has been authorized.

Jake's room remains private.

Personnel are not to enter without appropriate reason.

However, if the isolation continues, C.O.R.E. recommends that personnel remain aware of the possibility that Jake may not voluntarily request assistance.

His established behavior strongly favors concealment of distress.

Silence should therefore not automatically be interpreted as comfort.

---

# 15. END OF WEEK FOUR

At the end of the fourth week, Jake remained in his room.

He sat on the old mattress.

His teddy bear was beside him.

A small group of manifested flowers grew around the mattress.

The room was otherwise empty.

Jake looked toward the doorway.

He knew everyone was busy.

He knew they had not forgotten him.

He knew nobody had intentionally left him.

He knew all of this.

And still:

> **JAKE:**  
> "...I feel alone."

He lowered his head.

His voice became quieter.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I know they didn't leave."

A pause.

> **JAKE:**  
> "But it feels like they did."

Jake pulled the teddy bear against his chest.

He lay down.

Face down.

The flowers swayed around him.

The room remained silent.

---

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Four weeks ago, Jake was simply alone because everyone was busy.

Now he is beginning to construct an explanation for that loneliness.

It is not an explanation anyone gave him.

It is not supported by the facts.

It is simply what prolonged silence can do to someone who has already experienced being forgotten.

Jake knows the truth.

Everyone is busy.

Everyone still cares.

Everyone will eventually have time again.

But emotional memory does not operate on schedules.

It remembers the empty hallway.

The unanswered silence.

The door that never opened.

The days that passed.

The weeks that passed.

And eventually, it asks a question that logic cannot easily silence:

> **"If nobody has left me, why does being left behind feel exactly like this?"**

Jake has no answer.

So he closes his eyes.

Holds his teddy bear.

And waits for morning.

Alone.
