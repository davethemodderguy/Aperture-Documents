# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake's medical condition following the teddy bear impact incident is **improving steadily.**

The immediate cardiac and respiratory concerns have subsided.

His chest pain has decreased substantially.

Coughing has become less frequent.

His breathing has returned closer to his normal baseline.

His generalized pain is also diminishing.

Jake remains physically weaker than normal, but the recovery trend is positive.

There is currently no indication that the incident caused permanent damage.

---

# 1. CARDIAC CONDITION

Jake's cardiac symptoms have improved significantly.

The severe pain previously concentrated around his chest and heart has decreased to occasional discomfort.

Monitoring has shown increasing stability.

The teddy bear's heartbeat remains active and consistent.

No further acute cardiac episodes have been observed.

This is considered a major improvement.

---

# 2. RESPIRATION

Jake's breathing has also improved.

Previously, even attempting to speak could trigger coughing and discomfort.

He can now speak short sentences without significant respiratory distress.

His coughing has become occasional rather than continuous.

He is no longer struggling for every breath.

Medical personnel consider this a strong indicator of recovery.

---

# 3. MOBILITY

Jake is beginning to move independently again.

He can:

- stand without assistance;
    
- walk short distances;
    
- sit down and stand back up;
    
- reach for nearby objects;
    
- move around his room;
    
- and carry lightweight objects.
    

He still moves cautiously.

Pain can return if he moves too quickly.

Personnel therefore continue encouraging gradual activity rather than immediately returning him to his previous level of movement.

---

# 4. TEDDY BEAR

Jake has resumed carrying his teddy bear.

This initially caused concern among personnel.

However, the bear is no longer producing significant discomfort when handled normally.

Jake can hold it against his chest.

He can carry it.

He can gently pat its head.

The sensory connection remains active.

There is simply no longer a significant pain response to ordinary contact.

C.O.R.E. continues to prohibit throwing or otherwise violently impacting the bear.

---

# 5. CLOVER

Clover remains extremely careful around Jake.

Perhaps excessively careful.

She now handles the teddy bear as though it were made of glass.

Jake has noticed.

> **JAKE:**

Clover looked at the bear.

Then Jake.

> **CLOVER:**

Jake nodded.

> **JAKE:**

C.O.R.E. considers this understandable.

# 8. CLOVER'S GUILT

Although Jake's physical condition is improving, Clover's emotional response has not resolved as quickly.

She still occasionally watches Jake whenever he moves.

If he coughs:

She looks up immediately.

If he touches his chest:

She stops what she is doing.

If he stumbles:

She is beside him almost instantly.

Jake has begun reassuring her before she can ask.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm okay."

Clover usually answers:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

She is still learning to believe him.

---

# 9. RECOVERY MILESTONE

He held it under one arm.

Then walked across his room.

Clover watched.

Jake reached the other side.

Turned around.

Walked back.

Then looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "See?"

Clover smiled.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I see."

No pain response occurred.

He looked pleased.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake was sitting on the grass in his room.

His teddy bear was beside him.

Clover was working at her desk.

Jake picked up the bear.

He patted its head.

Then smiled faintly.

He looked at Clover.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Good."

Jake leaned back into the grass.

His teddy bear rested against his side.

The room was quiet.

And, for the first time in several days, quiet did not mean something was wrong.
