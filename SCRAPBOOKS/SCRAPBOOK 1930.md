## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

It has become normal to see Sora sitting in the hallways.

A small Dog Thiren with white fur, blue eyes, and a white scarf.

He usually sits against a wall, occasionally somewhere near a doorway. His head rocks slowly back and forth. He rarely appears to have a particular destination or activity in mind.

Sometimes he watches the floor.

Sometimes he looks toward passing residents.

Sometimes he simply rocks.

People have grown accustomed to seeing him there.

That normalization is itself a concern.

Sora's memory deterioration has progressed to an extraordinary degree. He has forgotten almost everything that once constituted his personal history.

He cannot reliably recognize faces.

He cannot reliably recognize voices.

He cannot remember the appearance of people he once trusted.

He cannot consistently recall his relationships, his childhood, his family, or the events that brought him to SAFEHOUSE.

He has even forgotten how to return to his own den.

The remaining information is exceptionally limited.

There are, however, a few things that persist.

His name.

Fragments of a past, now so blurred that he cannot reliably explain them.

And the underlying foundations of the person he once was.

C.O.R.E. considers these foundations significant.

They are not proof of intact autobiographical memory. They are the remaining elements through which Sora continues to exist as an individual, even when he can no longer explain who that individual is.

**He has forgotten almost everything about being Sora. But something of Sora remains.**

## 2. CURRENT OBSERVATION — THE HALLWAYS

**Observation period:** Continuous residential monitoring  
**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, primarily Floor 7

The following behavior has become increasingly commonplace:

Sora sits on the hallway floor.

His back rests against the wall, his legs positioned comfortably in front of him. His fluffy white tail occasionally moves beside him.

His white scarf remains around his neck.

His blue eyes are generally open, although he does not consistently focus on anything in particular.

His head rocks slowly back and forth.

The movement is repetitive and appears to provide some form of self-directed regulation. Its precise purpose has not been established.

He may remain in this position for extended periods.

When someone passes, he sometimes looks up.

He does not necessarily speak.

He does not necessarily recognize the person.

He may return to rocking after a few seconds.

## 3. THE REMAINING FOUNDATIONS

C.O.R.E. has revised its assessment of Sora's memory.

Previous reports attempted to document what he could no longer remember: faces, voices, relationships, locations, and personal experiences.

At this stage, that approach provides diminishing value.

The more important question is:

**What remains?**

### A. His name

Sora.

This is one of the few surviving pieces of personal information associated with him.

He may still respond to it, although responses are not entirely consistent.

When asked his name, he may answer:

**SORA:** Sora.

Sometimes he says it quietly.

Sometimes he repeats it.

Sometimes he appears uncertain.

Nevertheless, the name remains an important point of reference.

Staff must continue using it.

Not because he is expected to remember every person who says it, but because it belongs to him.

### B. His blurred past

Fragments of Sora's past occasionally remain accessible.

They are no longer organized into a reliable chronology.

He may remember that there was a place he once lived.

He may remember something about a doghouse.

He may retain the idea that someone was important to him without remembering their appearance, voice, or relationship.

These fragments are inconsistent.

They may disappear during conversation or become confused with unrelated recollections.

A representative exchange:

**STAFF MEMBER:** Do you remember where you lived before?

**SORA:** Home.

**STAFF MEMBER:** What was it like?

**SORA:** ...

**SORA:** Outside.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Did you have someone there?

**SORA:** Someone.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Who?

**SORA:** Don't know.

A pause.

**SORA:** Had someone.

The conversation ended there.

C.O.R.E. will not force these fragments into a complete narrative. They are not reliable enough to establish precise recollections, and repeated questioning may cause unnecessary distress.

### C. His underlying disposition

Despite the deterioration of his autobiographical memory, certain aspects of Sora's behavior remain recognizable.

He is generally quiet.

He remains hesitant around unfamiliar people.

He does not readily seek attention.

He may accept gentle assistance when it is explained beforehand.

He continues to display repetitive rocking behavior while seated.

These characteristics are observations, not proof that his personality or emotional life has remained unchanged.

C.O.R.E. cannot claim to know how much of the original Sora remains internally accessible.

However, the absence of recognizable memories does not make him an empty person.

He continues to have feelings, preferences, discomfort, and needs.

He continues to deserve dignity.

## 4. INCIDENT — "WHAT IS MY NAME?"

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, Floor 7  
**Time:** 19:24

A staff member found Sora sitting against the hallway wall.

He was rocking his head slowly.

The staff member sat nearby, leaving sufficient distance.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Hello, Sora.

No response.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Are you comfortable?

**SORA:** ...

**STAFF MEMBER:** Is your scarf okay?

Sora touched the fabric around his neck.

**SORA:** Yes.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Good.

A pause.

**SORA:** What...

**STAFF MEMBER:** What is it?

**SORA:** What my name?

The staff member paused.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Your name is Sora.

Sora looked down.

**SORA:** Sora.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Yes.

**SORA:** That's me?

**STAFF MEMBER:** Yes. That's you.

He repeated it quietly.

**SORA:** Sora.

Another pause.

**SORA:** Okay.

He resumed rocking.

The staff member remained nearby.

No further conversation occurred.

### Assessment

This exchange is particularly significant.

Sora was not asking someone to introduce themselves.

He was asking for his own name.

Even the basic information through which he identifies himself may now require external reinforcement.

Staff must answer calmly and without displaying alarm.

Sora should not be made to feel ashamed for needing to be reminded.

## 5. THE WHITE SCARF

Sora continues to wear his white scarf.

It is now one of the most consistently identifiable elements of his appearance.

The scarf was knitted by Mia and Jake.

It was originally intended to keep him warm and cover his neck.

Its uneven stitching and handmade construction were previously noted.

Sora's present ability to remember who made it is uncertain.

He may not remember Mia.

He may not remember Jake.

He may not remember the circumstances in which it was given to him.

Nevertheless, he continues to wear it.

C.O.R.E. will not claim that this demonstrates retained emotional recognition.

However, the scarf remains part of his familiar environment and should not be removed without necessity.

If it requires cleaning or adjustment, staff must explain the procedure beforehand.

**The scarf does not need to be remembered for its significance to remain.**

## 6. THE NORMALIZATION PROBLEM

Several residents and employees have become accustomed to seeing Sora sitting in the hallways.

They recognize the sight as ordinary.

A small white Dog Thiren.

Blue eyes.

White scarf.

Slowly rocking his head.

Sometimes someone greets him.

Sometimes someone sits nearby.

Sometimes people walk past.

C.O.R.E. is concerned that repeated exposure has reduced the perceived urgency of his condition.

The fact that Sora has become a familiar sight does not mean his condition has stabilized.

The fact that he rarely asks for help does not mean he does not need it.

The fact that he cannot explain what he has forgotten does not mean the loss is insignificant.

Staff are reminded that Sora remains a six-year-old child with severe neurological impairment and a history of significant physical and psychological trauma.

He requires active support.

He must not be left to navigate the SAFEHOUSE independently merely because his current behavior appears predictable.

### Mandatory measures

- Maintain supervised assistance with navigation throughout SAFEHOUSE.
    
- Provide a consistent resting location that Sora can access without relying on memory of complex directions.
    
- Continue monitoring nutrition, hydration, sleep, and physical condition.
    
- Explain every physical interaction before approaching or touching him.
    
- Never impersonate familiar individuals or deliberately test his recognition.
    
- Avoid repeatedly questioning him about people and events he cannot remember.
    
- Continue using his name.
    
- Preserve his scarf and other familiar belongings.
    
- Provide companionship without demanding conversation, recognition, or emotional responses.

## 7. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT — WHAT MAKES SOMEONE THEMSELVES?

There is a question that has become increasingly difficult to avoid.

If someone forgets their family, their childhood, their friends, their home, their experiences, and eventually even the faces and voices associated with those memories, what remains of the person they once were?

C.O.R.E. cannot provide a definitive philosophical answer.

Nor can it establish the exact neurological condition of every remaining memory.

But it can establish one thing:

Sora is still a person.

He is not an empty container waiting to be filled with new memories.

He is not an abandoned identity.

He is not a collection of missing information.

He is a child who cannot remember almost anything about his life.

That distinction matters.

His name remains.

His past remains in fragments.

His physical presence remains.

His needs remain.

And the people responsible for him must continue treating him as someone whose life has value, even when he cannot remember why.

C.O.R.E. further emphasizes that memory loss does not erase the responsibility of others to remember.

Mia does not need Sora to recognize her before she can be his sister.

Jake does not need Sora to remember their friendship before he can sit beside him.

Bao does not need Sora to remember calling her Mom before she can continue caring for him.

And Sora does not need to remember any of them to deserve their kindness.

## 8. CLOSING NOTE — THE FOUNDATIONS

There used to be so much to Sora.

A farm.

A grandmother.

A little sister.

A doghouse.

A boy who stood outside in the cold to protect the sheep.

A child who was frightened of people but still wanted someone to trust.

A whiteboard.

A scarf.

A friend named Jake.

A family he slowly found at SAFEHOUSE.

There were memories of laughter, fear, hunger, warmth, and loss.

Now, almost all of them are gone.

His face blindness means that faces are nothing but black scribbles.

His voice recognition has deteriorated until familiar voices are no longer reliable.

His memories of the people he loved have become blurred beyond recognition.

He cannot even remember how to find the little den built for him.

And so he sits in the hallways.

A little white dog boy with blue eyes and a white scarf.

Rocking his head slowly back and forth.

People walk past him.

Some say hello.

Some sit beside him.

Some do not know what to say.

He might look up.

He might answer.

He might ask what his name is.

And someone will tell him.

**Sora.**

That is what we can still give him.

Not his childhood.

Not the memories that have disappeared.

Not the faces he can no longer see.

But his name.

His almost completely blurred past.

And the foundations that made him who he is.

Perhaps those foundations are all that remain of the boy we knew.

But they are not nothing.

**He is Sora. And even if he forgets everything else, we have no right to forget him.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
