# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The second week has passed.

Jake remains on his floor.

He has rarely left it.

More accurately:

He has rarely had an opportunity to leave it.

The ongoing workload affecting SAFEHOUSE residents, C.O.R.E. personnel, KCA, CED, L.S.I.N., and associated organizations has continued.

The previous expectation of temporary absence has therefore remained in effect.

Jake's family and friends have still not visited.

Not Bao.

Not Neri.

Not Nora.

Not Aeterna.

Not Jane.

Not the Obol Squad.

Not any of the other residents who normally spend time with him.

There is no indication that they have stopped caring about him.

They simply remain occupied.

Jake has had almost no opportunity to encounter anyone.

His floor has become functionally isolated from the rest of SAFEHOUSE.

---

# 1. WEEK TWO — GENERAL PATTERN

Jake's routine has become extremely small.

He wakes.

He eats.

He reads.

He draws.

He sleeps.

Occasionally, he walks around his floor.

Occasionally, he sits beside the window.

Occasionally, he goes outside.

These outings are rare.

Not because Jake has been prohibited from leaving.

There is simply very little reason to go anywhere.

And even when he does leave, there is no guarantee that he will encounter anyone.

The timing of his movements rarely overlaps with anyone else's.

---

# 2. DAY EIGHT

Jake left his floor once.

He carried his teddy bear with him.

He walked through several otherwise empty corridors.

He reached the cafeteria.

Nobody was there.

Jake stood in the doorway for approximately twenty seconds.

He looked around.

Then turned around.

He returned upstairs.

No interaction occurred.

---

# 3. DAY NINE

Jake did not leave his floor.

He spent most of the day drawing.

Several new flowers manifested.

One drawing depicted a group of six figures sitting together.

The figures were surrounded by grass.

Jake left the drawing beside his mattress.

He did not attempt to manifest it.

---

# 4. DAY TEN

Jake briefly entered the hallway.

A security door closed somewhere in the distance.

Jake stopped.

He listened.

No footsteps followed.

He continued walking.

He eventually returned to his room.

No resident encounter occurred.

---

# 5. DAY ELEVEN

Jake remained inside.

He spent approximately three hours reading.

Later, he fell asleep on the mattress with his teddy bear.

Several manifested flowers had grown around the mattress.

One had appeared beside his pillow.

When Jake woke, he stared at it for several seconds.

Then quietly moved it away from his face.

He went back to sleep.

---

# 6. DAY TWELVE

Jake went downstairs.

This was the longest excursion of the week.

He walked through the SAFEHOUSE common areas.

No one was present.

He stopped outside the cafeteria.

The lights were on.

The room was empty.

Jake entered.

He prepared something to eat.

He sat at a table.

He ate alone.

After approximately ten minutes, he returned to his floor.

---

# 7. DAY THIRTEEN

Jake did not leave his floor.

He spent the afternoon lying on the mattress.

His eyes remained open.

His teddy bear was resting against his chest.

There was no apparent medical issue.

He simply did not move for a long time.

Eventually he sat up.

He took a pencil.

He began drawing.

---

# 8. DAY FOURTEEN

The second week ended without a single personal visit.

No one knocked.

No one entered.

No one called for him.

No one sat beside him.

No one brought him food.

No one asked him to come outside.

Jake did not complain.

He did not send a message.

He did not ask where everyone was.

He simply continued his routine.

---

# 9. THE FLOOR

Jake's floor has become increasingly recognizable as his own isolated environment.

Grass covers most of the residential space.

Sketched flowers grow throughout it.

Plushies are scattered across the old mattress.

Books lie open on the floor.

Drawings are everywhere.

Some remain paper.

Some have become physical objects.

The space is messy.

It is quiet.

It is almost entirely Jake's.

There are no conventional furnishings.

There is no reason to change that.

---

# 10. SOCIAL CONTACT

C.O.R.E. records **zero voluntary social interactions** with Jake during Week Two.

This does not mean Jake has rejected anyone.

No one has actually been available long enough for him to reject.

There have been no opportunities.

That distinction is important.

Jake cannot be described as choosing isolation when circumstances have provided almost no alternative.

His world has simply become very small.

---

# 11. FAMILY

Jake's relationship with his family remains unchanged.

Bao still cares about him.

Neri still cares about him.

Nora still cares about him.

Aeterna still cares about him.

Jane still cares about him.

The Obol Squad still considers him part of their social circle.

Nothing indicates otherwise.

But none of them have been able to visit.

Jake therefore experiences the only part of the situation that is immediately visible to him:

**They are not here.**

---

# 12. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN WEEK ONE AND WEEK TWO

During Week One, Jake occasionally appeared to wait.

He would look toward the hallway.

He would pause when hearing distant footsteps.

He would sometimes sit near his door.

During Week Two, this behavior became less frequent.

Jake has begun spending more time inside his own environment.

He draws more.

Reads more.

Sleeps more.

Moves less.

The change is subtle.

There has been no dramatic deterioration.

That makes it harder to notice.

---

# 13. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Jake remains physically stable.

He continues to:

- eat;
    
- drink;
    
- sleep;
    
- read;
    
- draw;
    
- move independently;
    
- maintain his anomalous manifestation abilities;
    
- and care for his teddy bear.
    

However, prolonged absence of meaningful social contact remains a welfare concern.

Jake has a documented history of emotional injury associated with being forgotten and left alone.

The present situation is fundamentally different in cause.

But from Jake's perspective, prolonged silence may still carry emotional significance.

C.O.R.E. therefore continues passive monitoring.

No forced social intervention is recommended.

Jake's private space remains private.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Week One was quiet.

Week Two is quieter.

Jake's floor has become a small world of its own.

Grass.

Flowers.

Plushies.

An old mattress.

Books.

Drawings.

And one little star.

He rarely goes outside.

Not because he is forbidden.

Not because he has nowhere to go.

He simply doesn't encounter anyone.

There is rarely anyone in the corridors when he walks through them.

Rarely anyone in the cafeteria when he visits.

Rarely anyone in the common areas.

And there has been nobody at his door.

Two weeks have now passed without a visit from his family or friends.

Jake has not complained.

He has not demanded anyone's attention.

He has not asked anyone to stop being busy.

He has simply adapted to the silence.

Tonight, he sits alone on his old mattress.

His teddy bear is beside him.

A small sketched flower grows beside his pillow.

Jake picks up his pencil.

He looks toward the empty doorway.

Nothing happens.

He lowers his eyes.

Then begins another drawing.

The floor remains quiet.

The rest of SAFEHOUSE remains busy.

And Jake remains upstairs,

**still alone.**
