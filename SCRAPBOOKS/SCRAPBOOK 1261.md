# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Nora's psychological condition has deteriorated beyond ordinary withdrawal.

Previous reports described Nora as believing that isolation would prevent others from worrying about her.

That assessment is no longer sufficient.

Nora has begun expressing a substantially more concerning belief:

> **Her death would make things easier for everyone.**

She has not made a direct attempt to harm herself.

She has not openly stated an intention to kill herself.

However, her attitude toward survival has changed.

Previously:

> **“I don't want everyone to worry about me.”**

Currently:

> **“If I'm gone, nobody has to worry.”**

This distinction represents a significant escalation.

Nora's previous enthusiasm for life has not merely diminished.

Her perception of the difference between **living** and **dying** appears to be becoming increasingly indistinct.

---

# 1. THE CHANGE IN NORA'S EYES

Nora's eyes have remained abnormal since the onset of her current illness.

The previous azure-and-white gradient has largely disappeared.

Her irises now appear predominantly hollow black.

Medical personnel initially considered this an anomalous physiological change.

It remains unexplained.

However, personnel have begun using an informal phrase to describe the expression accompanying the change:

> **“The line is gone.”**

Normally, Nora's behavior communicates a strong preference for life.

She wants to:

- play;
    
- work;
    
- explore;
    
- bother Alice;
    
- read her family journal;
    
- spend time with Neri;
    
- ask Aeterna to carry her;
    
- run the Merch Store;
    
- eat cake;
    
- discover new things.
    

Recently, those things have stopped carrying the same emotional weight.

Nora can still recognize them.

She simply no longer appears convinced that they matter.

---

# 2. THE FADING LINE

C.O.R.E. defines the current phenomenon as:

> **Faded Life-Death Distinction.**

This does not mean Nora is incapable of understanding that life and death are different states.

She understands the difference intellectually.

The concern is that she is beginning to assign them similar emotional value.

Previously:

> **Living = good.**

> **Dying = terrible.**

Currently:

> **Living = exhausting.**

> **Dying = quiet.**

And increasingly:

> **Living = everyone worries.**

> **Dying = everyone can stop worrying.**

The final association is considered the most dangerous.

---

# 3. NORA'S CURRENT LOGIC

Nora's reasoning has become increasingly circular.

She is sick.

Therefore, people worry.

People worry.

Therefore, she is causing them distress.

If she is causing distress:

> **She is a problem.**

If she is a problem:

> **Removing herself removes the problem.**

Therefore:

> **Nora disappearing would help everyone.**

This conclusion is false.

But Nora currently experiences it as logical.

---

# 4. OBSERVED STATEMENTS

Several statements have been recorded.

Nora was asked why she was not returning to the Merch Store.

She answered:

> **“It doesn't matter.”**

When asked whether she wanted to get better:

> **“I don't know.”**

When Aeterna told her that everyone wanted her to recover:

> **“Why?”**

Aeterna asked:

> **“Because we love you.”**

Nora was silent.

Then:

> **“Would it be easier if I wasn't here?”**

Aeterna did not answer immediately.

Nora continued:

> **“You wouldn't have to worry.”**

---

# 5. THE MOST CONCERNING STATEMENT

During a private conversation with Neri, Nora asked:

> **“Sis?”**

Neri looked at her.

> **“What?”**

Nora remained silent for several seconds.

Then:

> **“If I died...”**

Neri immediately became still.

Nora continued:

> **“...would everyone be okay?”**

Neri did not answer the question.

She asked:

> **“Why are you asking?”**

Nora looked down.

> **“Because everyone would stop worrying.”**

Neri responded:

> **“No.”**

Nora looked at her.

> **“You don't know that.”**

Neri answered:

> **“I do.”**

Pause.

> **“Because I'd still be here.”**

Nora did not respond.

---

# 6. NERI'S REACTION

Neri has displayed an unusual level of urgency.

She did not leave Nora alone after the conversation.

This is significant.

Neri normally respects Nora's solitude.

She understands it.

She recognizes the need to give her sister space.

On this occasion, however, Neri remained nearby.

She did not force Nora to talk.

She simply stayed.

At one point Nora asked:

> **“Why are you still here?”**

Neri answered:

> **“Because I want to be.”**

Nora looked at her.

> **“You don't have to.”**

Neri:

> **“I know.”**

Pause.

> **“I'm staying anyway.”**

---

# 7. AETERNA'S RESPONSE

Aeterna has significantly altered her behavior following the previous misunderstanding.

She no longer attempts to convince Nora that she is "needed."

Instead, she emphasizes that Nora is wanted.

When Nora said:

> **“Everyone would be happier if I wasn't sick.”**

Aeterna responded:

> **“Perhaps.”**

Nora looked up.

Aeterna continued:

> **“But that is not the same as being happier without you.”**

Nora remained silent.

Aeterna knelt.

> **“Your suffering is not the same thing as your existence.”**

Pause.

> **“We want the suffering to end.”**

Then:

> **“Not you.”**

Nora began crying.

Aeterna did not touch her until Nora moved closer herself.

---

# 8. ELSHER

Elsher was informed of Nora's statements.

His response was brief.

> **“She thinks she's doing us a favor.”**

C.O.R.E. asked whether he believed Nora understood the consequences of death.

Elsher answered:

> **“She understands death.”**

Pause.

> **“That's why this is dangerous.”**

He subsequently increased the frequency of his visits.

He does not attempt to entertain Nora.

He simply sits nearby.

Nora has recently stopped reading her family journal aloud.

This is considered significant.

The journal contains photographs and records of:

- Elsher;
    
- Neri;
    
- Nora;
    
- Bao;
    
- their family history;
    
- their reunion;
    
- and the events surrounding Nora's return.
    

One page is particularly important.

It reads:

> **WE FOUND EACH OTHER AGAIN.**

Nora previously read this page frequently.

She now avoids it.

When asked why, she answered:

> **“It already happened.”**

The statement has been flagged for continued observation.

---

# 10. WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM HER PREVIOUS WITHDRAWAL

Earlier:

> **Nora wanted to be alone so nobody would worry.**

Now:

> **Nora believes disappearing entirely would permanently solve the problem.**

The first is social withdrawal.

The second is passive acceptance of death.

C.O.R.E. therefore considers the current condition a significant escalation.

---

# 11. PERSONNEL RESPONSE

Nora should not be:

- guilted;
    
- threatened;
    
- told how devastated everyone would be;
    
- told that she is responsible for other people's happiness;
    
- told that she "has to stay strong";
    
- forced to perform happiness;
    
- overwhelmed by large groups;
    
- or told that she must stay alive because other people need her.
    

These responses may reinforce her belief that her existence is valuable only because of what she provides to others.

Personnel should instead communicate:

> **“You don't have to make anyone feel better.”**

> **“You don't have to earn your place here.”**

> **“You don't have to be useful.”**

> **“You don't have to be happy right now.”**

And:

> **“You are allowed to stay.”**

The distinction is deliberate.

Nora should not be made responsible for choosing life for everyone else.

She should be supported in choosing to remain herself.

---

# 12. MEDICAL PRIORITY

Nora's anemia remains medically significant.

Her physical condition and psychological condition are now directly interacting.

Physical weakness reduces her ability to engage with activities that previously provided positive reinforcement.

Reduced activity increases isolation.

Isolation increases rumination.

Rumination reinforces hopelessness.

Hopelessness reduces motivation to recover.

Reduced motivation makes physical recovery more difficult.

The cycle must therefore be interrupted medically **and** socially.

---

# 13. CURRENT SAFETY MEASURES

C.O.R.E. has authorized increased observation.

This does not mean Nora will be constantly surrounded.

Her privacy remains important.

However:

- significant deterioration must be reported;
    
- prolonged unexplained absence must be checked;
    
- medical symptoms must not be dismissed as "Nora being quiet";
    
- requests to be left completely unattended during periods of acute deterioration require review;
    
- Aeterna, Neri, Bao, and designated medical personnel remain available;
    
- Nora's food and hydration are to be monitored without turning meals into confrontations.
    

Most importantly:

> **Nora must not be left responsible for deciding whether her condition is serious enough to deserve assistance.**

---

# 14. CURRENT PSYCHOLOGICAL MODEL

Nora previously believed:

> **“I should not make people worry.”**

Then:

> **“I should stay away so nobody has to worry.”**

Then:

> **“I should need less.”**

Then:

> **“They would be better off if I disappeared.”**

The progression is clear.

The objective is not simply to convince her that she is loved.

She already knows people love her.

The problem is that she currently believes:

> **Her existence creates suffering for the people who love her.**

That belief must be addressed directly.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Nora was once extraordinarily positive about life.

She wanted everything.

She wanted to see what was behind every door.

She wanted to know how everything worked.

She wanted to run the Merch Store.

She wanted to play.

She wanted cake.

She wanted to be carried.

She wanted to bother her siblings.

She wanted tomorrow.

Now, when she thinks about tomorrow, she appears uncertain that tomorrow is worth reaching.

That does not mean the old Nora is gone.

It means the part of Nora that desperately wants to live is currently exhausted beneath:

**pain,**

**anemia,**

**sleep deprivation,**

**traumatic memories,**

**guilt,**

**and the belief that her disappearance would be an act of kindness.**

The distinction between life and death has become dangerously faint in her thinking.

But it has not disappeared completely.

There is still a line.

It is simply difficult to see.

And C.O.R.E.'s current objective is not to force Nora to become cheerful again.

It is to keep that line visible until she is strong enough to see it herself.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Nora believes death would make everyone else's lives easier.

She is wrong.

But telling her:

> **“You cannot die because we need you.”**

would only teach her that she must remain useful.

The message must be different.

Not:

> **“Stay because we need you.”**

But:

> **“Stay because you are allowed to.”**

Not:

> **“You make us happy.”**

But:

> **“You do not have to make us happy.”**

Not:

> **“Don't leave us.”**

But:

> **“You don't have to go anywhere.”**

Nora does not need another reason to perform.

She needs somewhere safe to stop performing entirely.

She needs to be sick without apologizing.

She needs to be frightened without hiding it.

She needs to be quiet without disappearing.

And most importantly:

She needs to learn that her life is not a service she provides to everyone else.

It is hers.

Even now.

Especially now.
