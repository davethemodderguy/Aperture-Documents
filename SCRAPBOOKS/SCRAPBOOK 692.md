### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — PERMANENT PHYSICAL LIMITATIONS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **PERMANENTLY PHYSICALLY FRAGILE**

---

# **MEDICAL DETERMINATION**

Alice's physical limitations have been determined to be **permanent**.

Her body survived more than eight months of extreme deprivation and environmental exposure at an age when she was barely five years old.

She survived.

But her body did not recover normally.

C.O.R.E. medical personnel have confirmed that Alice's baseline physical capabilities will remain significantly below those of an average individual.

This is not a temporary condition.

It is not something that can simply be trained away.

It is now considered part of Alice's permanent physiology.

---

# **PRIMARY LIMITATIONS**

Alice experiences severe limitations in several areas.

### **STAMINA**

Alice becomes exhausted quickly.

Even relatively short periods of walking can leave her tired.

Long-distance travel on foot is impractical.

She requires frequent rest.

---

### **SPEED**

Alice's running speed is unusually low.

She cannot maintain a fast pace for long.

Attempting to push herself beyond her limits rapidly causes fatigue.

Personnel are instructed not to encourage her to "keep up."

---

### **MUSCLE STRENGTH**

Alice possesses naturally weak musculature.

Carrying heavy objects is difficult.

Climbing and prolonged physical activity are similarly challenging.

She is capable of normal daily movement, but strenuous activity is not recommended.

---

### **BONE FRAGILITY**

Alice's skeletal structure is comparatively fragile.

Falls and impacts represent a greater risk to her than they would to most residents.

Care should therefore be taken when Alice is playing or moving through unfamiliar environments.

---

### **IMMUNE SYSTEM**

Alice's immune system remains unusually weak.

She is significantly more susceptible to illness.

Minor infections may affect her more severely than expected.

Cold exposure is particularly dangerous.

Her previous habit of sitting outdoors for extended periods is therefore strongly discouraged.

---

# **THE PIGGYBACK SYSTEM**

Because Alice cannot comfortably walk long distances, SAFEHOUSE residents have developed an informal transportation method.

Specifically:

**Piggyback rides.**

Her two most frequent carriers are:

### **Corin Lockwood**

Alice's preferred carrier.

When Alice becomes tired, she will usually walk over to Corin and raise her arms.

> **"Big Sis..."**

Corin already knows what this means.

> **"Tired?"**

Alice nods.

Corin crouches.

Alice climbs onto her back.

Problem solved.

---

### **Belle Merryweather**

Belle also frequently carries Alice.

Belle treats the arrangement as completely normal.

> **"Need a ride?"**

Alice:

> **"Yes."**

Belle:

> **"Hop on!"**

Alice climbs onto her back.

Belle walks away carrying the smallest resident in SAFEHOUSE.

---

# **THE SAFEHOUSE HAS ADAPTED**

Alice's limitations have become an ordinary part of daily life.

Residents naturally account for her pace.

If everyone is walking somewhere:

**Alice does not need to keep up.**

If everyone is going somewhere far:

**Someone carries her.**

If Alice becomes tired:

**They stop.**

If she becomes sick:

**She rests.**

There is no expectation that she should somehow overcome her limitations.

---

# **ALICE'S ATTITUDE**

Interestingly, Alice does not appear particularly embarrassed by being carried.

She seems to enjoy it.

When riding on Corin's back, she often rests her head against Corin's shoulder.

Sometimes she falls asleep.

When riding on Belle:

> **"Fast rabbit!"**

Belle laughs.

> **"I'm the fast rabbit?"**

Alice nods.

> **"Yes."**

Belle proudly walks faster.

Medical personnel immediately object.

> **"Not _that_ fast."**

Belle slows down.

---

# **A PERMANENT REALITY**

C.O.R.E. has emphasized that Alice's limitations should not be framed as a temporary setback.

There is no anticipated point at which she suddenly becomes physically strong.

She will likely remain:

- Slow.
    
- Easily exhausted.
    
- Physically weak.
    
- Vulnerable to illness.
    
- Vulnerable to injury.
    

Her body simply has limits.

The SAFEHOUSE's responsibility is not to pretend those limits don't exist.

It is to accommodate them.

---

# **CORIN'S VIEW**

Corin was asked whether Alice's physical weakness bothered her.

Her answer was immediate.

> **"No."**

> **"She doesn't have to be strong."**

After a short pause:

> **"She's Alice."**

That was considered sufficient.

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Alice doesn't walk as far as everyone else.

She doesn't run as fast.

She doesn't have much stamina.

She gets tired easily.

She gets sick easily.

Her bones are fragile.

Her muscles are weak.

Her body will likely remain this way permanently.

And that's okay.

Sometimes Corin carries her.

Sometimes Belle carries her.

Sometimes Alice walks until she's tired and simply raises her arms.

Someone always picks her up.

Alice doesn't need to prove that she can survive alone anymore.

She already proved that once.

She was never supposed to have to.

Now, when her little legs become tired, she can simply say:

> **"Big Sis, carry."**

And Corin will crouch down.

Alice climbs onto her back.

And they keep going.
