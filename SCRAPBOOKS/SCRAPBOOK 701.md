## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — INTERNAL DISTRESS**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / INTERNAL DISTRESS OBSERVED**

---

# **OBSERVATION**

Alice has begun exhibiting signs of persistent internal distress.

She rarely complains.

She rarely cries in front of others.

She rarely asks for anything.

Most of the time, she simply sits quietly.

Her body remains still while her hands occasionally tremble.

When asked if something hurts, her answer is usually:

> **"No."**

Medical personnel do not consider this answer reliable.

---

# **THE PAIN**

Alice appears to be experiencing significant discomfort throughout her body.

The exact sources are difficult to isolate.

Her current condition involves multiple simultaneous physiological problems.

Her body is exhausted.

Her nutritional reserves are severely depleted.

Her immune system remains compromised.

Her previous injuries have not been completely forgotten by her body simply because the wounds have closed.

And her continued lack of adequate nutrition prevents proper recovery.

The result is a constant background of discomfort.

Sometimes it becomes pain.

Sometimes it becomes weakness.

Sometimes it becomes a strange sensation Alice cannot name.

---

# **SHE DOESN'T KNOW HOW TO DESCRIBE IT**

When asked:

> **"Where does it hurt?"**

Alice points vaguely toward herself.

> **"Inside."**

The medic asks:

> **"Inside where?"**

Alice pauses.

She places both hands against her chest.

> **"Here."**

Then her stomach.

> **"Here too."**

Then her head.

> **"And here."**

She looks frustrated.

> **"Alice don't know."**

The examination continues.

Alice eventually whispers:

> **"Everything hurts."**

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has noticed something else.

Alice has stopped asking to be carried.

Previously, when she became tired, she would quietly approach someone.

Now she simply sits down wherever she is.

Corin asked:

> **"Alice, are you tired?"**

Alice shook her head.

> **"No."**

Corin waited.

Alice's hands were trembling.

Her ears were lowered.

Her eyes were half closed.

Corin asked again:

> **"Does something hurt?"**

A long pause.

> **"...Maybe."**

Corin sat beside her.

> **"You can tell me."**

Alice looked down.

> **"Alice don't know how."**

---

# **THE INTERNAL BATTLE**

Alice's body is sending signals that she cannot properly interpret.

Hunger.

Pain.

Fatigue.

Cold.

Weakness.

Fear.

Her mind attempts to make sense of them.

But her memories are deteriorating.

Words disappear.

Associations disappear.

Even familiar sensations can become unfamiliar.

Something that once meant:

> **"I'm hungry."**

may simply become:

> **"Something feels wrong."**

And Alice does not know what to call it.

---

# **SHE STILL SMILES**

When someone enters the room, Alice sometimes smiles.

It is small.

Weak.

Often delayed.

But it is there.

If someone asks:

> **"Are you okay?"**

She answers:

> **"Alice happy."**

The medical department has explicitly warned personnel not to interpret this as evidence that Alice is comfortable.

Alice has a long history of masking distress.

Her smile may mean:

> **"I don't want you to worry."**

Not:

> **"I'm okay."**

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie sat beside Alice one evening.

Neither spoke for several minutes.

Eventually Alice leaned against her.

Orphie asked:

> **"Does it hurt?"**

Alice nodded.

Orphie waited for an explanation.

None came.

Alice simply whispered:

> **"Inside."**

Orphie held her.

Alice remained still.

---

# **MEDICAL RESPONSE**

Alice's condition has been escalated for continuous monitoring.

Personnel are instructed to treat behavioral changes as possible indicators of pain.

Particular attention should be paid to:

- Increased silence
    
- Reduced movement
    
- Trembling
    
- Loss of appetite
    
- Difficulty sleeping
    
- Unusual posture
    
- Withdrawal
    
- Increased irritability
    
- Repeatedly touching one area of the body
    
- Sudden crying
    
- Refusal to communicate
    

Alice may not be able to explain what she feels.

The adults around her must therefore learn to recognize it.

---

# **CURRENT CONCERN**

The medical team is particularly concerned that Alice's nutritional decline is creating a vicious cycle.

**Insufficient nutrition**

↓

**Physical weakness**

↓

**Reduced activity**

↓

**Worsened physiological condition**

↓

**Increased discomfort**

↓

**Reduced appetite**

↓

**Further nutritional deficiency**

The cycle cannot be allowed to continue indefinitely.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice is in pain.

She does not always know where.

She does not always know why.

Sometimes she cannot even find the words.

So she says:

> **"Inside."**

And that is enough.

Nobody needs her to explain it perfectly.

Nobody needs her to prove that she's hurting.

Nobody needs her to smile convincingly.

If Alice says something hurts—

they believe her.

If she cannot say it—

they watch for the signs.

And if all she can do is quietly lean against someone and whisper:

> **"Alice hurts."**

Someone stays.
