## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana remains very seriously ill.

However, following the deterioration documented in CORE-SH-OBS-109, his condition has reached an unsettling plateau. He has neither noticeably improved nor deteriorated further.

His severe weakness persists. He still struggles to hold his head upright, moves very little, speaks infrequently, and retains the dimmed eyes that first alerted residents to his condition.

There is, at present, no clear sign of recovery.

There is also no observed progression of the symptoms already documented.

The medical team has classified his current course as **stable but seriously ill**. This designation describes the absence of obvious change; it does not mean that Jake is out of danger.

A secondary observation has been added to the record, following repeated comments from residents and staff:

Jake is, by almost universal agreement, _ridiculously adorable when he is sick._

C.O.R.E. recognizes the observation.

C.O.R.E. would prefer that everyone remember the first paragraph.

## 3. THE ADORABLENESS PROBLEM

The following observations were collected after several personnel independently described Jake as unusually cute during his illness.

### Physical presentation

Jake's usual energy has given way to small, slow movements. He sometimes lets his head droop against a pillow or another person's shoulder because holding it upright requires too much effort.

His quietness makes every word stand out.

His dimmed eyes, normally so lively and expressive, now look tired even when he is awake.

When someone speaks to him, he occasionally responds with a faint movement of his head or a small glance.

These changes have prompted an extraordinary number of residents to describe him using words such as _precious_, _tiny_, _sweet_, and _must be protected at all costs._

The medical department has requested that the phrase _“too cute to be sick”_ be removed from all future clinical notes.

It has also requested that nobody attempt to solve the problem by buying him twelve cakes.

The request has not been universally respected.

### Recorded exchange — SAFEHOUSE bedside

**ARIES:** “You know you're being very cute right now, don't you?”

Jake slowly looks at him.

**JAKE:** “...Mm?”

**ARIES:** “Nothing. You're just cute.”

Jake's head begins to droop. Aries gently helps him settle against the pillow.

**JAKE:** “Sleepy.”

**ARIES:** “Then rest. We've got you.”

A few minutes later, Tom enters carrying a fresh blanket.

**TOM:** “Did he say anything?”

**ARIES:** “He said he's sleepy.”

**TOM:** “That's all?”

**ARIES:** “That's all.”

Tom looks at Jake for a long moment.

**TOM:** “...He's so little.”

**ARIES:** “He's six.”

**TOM:** “I KNOW HOW OLD HE IS.”

Jake makes a tiny, tired sound that might be a laugh.

Both brothers immediately look at him.

**TOM:** “Was that a laugh?”

**ARIES:** “I think so.”

**TOM:** “Jake? Do it again.”

Jake closes his eyes.

**ARIES:** “Tom, don't make him perform.”

**TOM:** “I wasn't—”

**ARIES:** “You absolutely were.”

Jake remains still, resting comfortably while his brothers argue in whispers beside him.

For the first time that afternoon, the room feels a little less frightening.

His condition, however, has not changed.

## 4. TOM AKANA — OBSERVATION NOTE

Tom has spent much of the observation period close to Jake's bedside.

He has begun recognizing the smallest details of his brother's behavior: the way Jake turns toward a familiar voice, the brief movements of his fingers, and the way he tries to answer even when speaking seems to exhaust him.

Tom has not mistaken these small responses for recovery.

When asked how he was coping, he reportedly answered:

> “It's worse when nothing happens.”
> 
> “If he gets worse, we know something changed. If he gets better, we know he's getting better.”
> 
> “But when he stays like this... I keep waiting for something. Anything.”
> 
> “And he just looks at me with those little dim eyes like he wants to tell me something, but he's too tired.”
> 
> “I don't know what I'm supposed to do with that.”

No one offered him a convenient answer.

Aries sat beside him instead.

Tom was reminded that he does not need to keep Jake entertained, make him laugh, or discover the correct words to make him recover. His presence is valuable even when Jake cannot respond.

Jake is receiving medical care. Tom is allowed to be his brother.

## 5. MEDICAL STATUS AND CONTINUING PRECAUTIONS

**Current classification:** Serious illness, stable course, cause unknown.

The absence of worsening symptoms is encouraging only in the limited sense that no further decline has been observed. It does not establish that the underlying illness has resolved or that Jake is safe without continued care.

The medical team will continue to:

- Monitor his responsiveness, breathing, circulation, blood glucose, and other clinically indicated measurements.
    
- Review readings from his right-thigh monitor.
    
- Track changes in his eye brightness, strength, mobility, and ability to communicate.
    
- Continue investigating possible medical and anomalous causes.
    
- Escalate immediately if new symptoms appear or his condition worsens.
    

Personnel are reminded that Jake's quietness must not be interpreted as contentment simply because he appears peaceful.

Likewise, a small smile, a weak laugh, or a moment of affection is not sufficient evidence of physical recovery.

**C.O.R.E. clarification:** A patient may be adorable, loved, comfortable in the moment, and still critically unwell. These conditions are not mutually exclusive.

## 6. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — “THE WAITING”

The first day, everyone waited for Jake to get worse.

They watched his eyes. They listened for the monitor. They studied every movement and every breath, afraid that the next change would be the one they could not afford to miss.

Then the hours passed.

Jake remained weak.

His eyes remained dim.

He still could not hold his head upright for very long, and he still spoke in those tiny, tired fragments that made everyone lean closer to hear him.

Nothing got better.

Nothing got worse.

And somehow, the waiting became its own kind of exhaustion.

Tom sits beside him and tells him about things he cannot currently participate in. Aries brings news from the rest of SAFEHOUSE. Residents visit quietly, sometimes leaving small gifts that Jake may not have the strength to open yet.

Someone placed a little plush star beside his pillow.

Someone else brought cake, despite the medical department's very explicit instructions to consult the attending team before offering him food.

Jake looked at the cake.

He looked at Tom.

**JAKE:** “...Mine?”

Tom had to look away for a moment before answering.

**TOM:** “We'll make sure you get some when it's safe for you to eat.”

Jake gave a faint nod and settled back against his pillow.

That was all.

No miraculous recovery. No sudden worsening. No explanation for why he had become so sick.

Just a little boy who wanted cake, whose eyes were too dim, and whose family was trying very hard not to let him see how frightened they were.

C.O.R.E. cannot promise that tomorrow will be better.

But tomorrow, the team will check his readings again. Someone will sit beside him. Someone will answer when he speaks.

And if Jake wakes up and can only manage one quiet word, the people who love him will still listen.

**He does not have to get better today to deserve care today.**

He does not have to smile. He does not have to speak. He does not have to make anyone feel better about how sick he is.

He only has to be Jake.

For now, that is enough.

_— C.O.R.E., SAFEHOUSE Resident Care & Monitoring Division_
