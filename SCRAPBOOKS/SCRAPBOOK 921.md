# SAFEHOUSE PSYCHOLOGICAL OBSERVATION

## NERI — CHILDLIKE PRESENTATION DURING MEDICAL DISTRESS

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE INTERNAL  
**Resident:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Subject:** Behavioral presentation during spontaneous anemia episodes  
**Status:** Active observation

---

## 1. GENERAL OBSERVATION

Neri is exceptionally intelligent.

She possesses advanced chemical reasoning, anomalous molecular restructuring capabilities, extensive practical knowledge, and combat abilities far beyond what would normally be expected of a child.

None of this changes her age.

**Neri is ten years old.**

She can discuss chemistry with Neko at a level that can leave experienced researchers struggling to keep up.

She can then become frightened because she cannot see properly.

This contrast is not contradictory.

It is one of the most important things SAFEHOUSE personnel need to understand about her.

---

# 2. NERI IS STILL A CHILD

Neri's intelligence can sometimes cause adults to forget that she is still developing emotionally.

She may:

- become excited over something she finds interesting,
    
- get distracted,
    
- become stubborn,
    
- pout,
    
- cling to people she trusts,
    
- become upset when someone leaves,
    
- seek reassurance,
    
- misunderstand complicated emotional situations,
    
- become frightened without knowing how to explain why,
    
- and occasionally behave much younger than her intellectual capabilities would suggest.
    

This is normal for Neri.

Her unusual intelligence does not make her an adult.

---

# 3. THE DIFFERENCE BECOMES MORE OBVIOUS DURING ANEMIA

Neri's usual composure can disappear rapidly when an episode begins.

She does not think:

> _My hemoglobin concentration is declining._

She thinks:

> **“Something's wrong.”**

Then:

> **“Everything's blurry.”**

Then:

> **“I can't breathe.”**

And eventually:

> **“I fell.”**

The language is simple because **she is a child experiencing something terrifying that she does not fully understand.**

---

# 4. BEHAVIOR DURING AN EPISODES

When her condition deteriorates, Neri may become noticeably more childlike.

She may:

- look for someone familiar,
    
- ask where Bao is,
    
- reach toward someone she trusts,
    
- become scared when left alone,
    
- speak in shorter sentences,
    
- become confused,
    
- ask simple questions repeatedly,
    
- cling to someone's sleeve or hand,
    
- cry without being able to explain exactly why,
    
- or simply sit there looking frightened.
    

She may also insist that she is fine shortly before collapsing.

This should not be interpreted as stubbornness.

She may genuinely not understand how serious the situation is.

---

# 5. TRUSTED PEOPLE

Neri's response is particularly noticeable around people she considers family.

**Bao** is the clearest example.

When Neri is frightened and recognizes Bao, her behavior can become markedly younger.

She does not care about the scientific explanation.

She wants her mom.

She may simply say:

> **“Mom...”**

or:

> **“Bao...”**

and reach for her.

Bao understands what this means.

She does not expect Neri to explain the medical problem.

She simply helps her.

---

# 6. WHY THIS MATTERS

Neri's competence creates a dangerous misconception:

> **“If she is smart enough to understand chemistry, she should be able to handle herself.”**

This is incorrect.

A ten-year-old can be exceptionally intelligent and still need an adult when frightened.

A ten-year-old can understand complicated chemistry and still not understand why her body suddenly cannot breathe.

A ten-year-old can fight extremely well and still want someone to hold her hand when she is scared.

Neri's abilities do not remove her need for care.

---

# 7. STAFF INSTRUCTION

When Neri becomes symptomatic, personnel should **not** switch into a cold or overly technical manner simply because she is normally capable of understanding complex subjects.

Keep instructions simple.

> **“Sit down.”**

> **“You're safe.”**

> **“We're here.”**

> **“Bao is coming.”**

> **“Don't try to stand.”**

If Neri becomes frightened:

> **“You don't have to figure this out. Let us help.”**

This last statement is considered particularly important.

Neri has spent much of her life believing that she had to solve everything herself.

She doesn't.

---

# 8. POST-EPISODE BEHAVIOR

After recovering, Neri may return to her normal personality surprisingly quickly.

She might:

- ask for cake,
    
- complain about being hungry,
    
- make a joke,
    
- ask what happened,
    
- become embarrassed about having cried,
    
- or immediately become interested in whatever she was doing before the episode.
    

Staff should not tease her for becoming frightened.

Nor should they infantilize her afterward.

The appropriate approach is simply:

**She was scared. She needed help. She received help.**

That is all.

---

# 9. C.O.R.E. REMINDER

Neri can be:

**A brilliant chemist.**

**A dangerous combatant.**

**A molecular restructuring anomaly.**

**A knife enthusiast.**

**A remarkably capable researcher.**

And simultaneously:

**A ten-year-old girl who gets scared when everything goes blurry.**

These descriptions do not contradict each other.

They describe different parts of the same child.

---

### FINAL NOTE

One Archivist summarized Neri's condition particularly well:

> **“Don't confuse the fact that she can understand something with the fact that she should have to handle it alone.”**

This has been added to Neri's permanent care guidance.
