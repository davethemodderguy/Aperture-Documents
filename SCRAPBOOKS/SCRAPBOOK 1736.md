# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake is no longer smiling.

This observation was initially recorded as a temporary behavioral change following his recovery from severe hypothermia.

Further observation indicates that this interpretation was insufficient.

Jake is exhibiting signs of **profound psychological distress, pervasive fear, emotional withdrawal, and a significant loss of his previously carefree behavior.**

He no longer appears comfortable in environments he previously considered safe.

He startles at unexpected sounds.

He watches doors.

He becomes visibly distressed when he cannot immediately locate the people around him.

He hesitates before accepting food, blankets, assistance, and affection.

He has begun treating ordinary, everyday uncertainties as potential threats.

A closed door may mean he has been abandoned.

An empty room may mean nobody is coming back.

A person approaching too quickly may mean he is about to be hurt.

A promise that someone will return may mean very little.

Even when surrounded by people who genuinely care about him, Jake may remain frightened that something terrible is about to happen.

**Jake is afraid of almost everything.**

And the most concerning aspect of his condition is that he no longer seems to believe the world will become safe simply because someone tells him it is.

---

# 1. THE DISAPPEARANCE OF JAKE'S SMILE

Before the recent incidents, Jake smiled frequently.

His happiness was often expressed through small things.

A new book.

A flower growing beside a walkway.

A particularly comfortable blanket.

Someone remembering what cake he liked.

A friend sitting beside him without asking him to do anything.

He laughed when something surprised him.

He became excited when someone showed him something interesting.

He smiled when Aeterna visited.

He smiled when Bao brought him food.

He smiled when Clover showed him something she had painted.

Sometimes, he smiled for no particular reason at all.

That behavior has almost entirely disappeared.

Personnel have attempted to reproduce familiar circumstances in the hope of reassuring him.

His favorite books have been returned to his room.

Flowers have been placed beside his window.

Bao has prepared familiar meals.

Clover has brought him drawings.

Nora has attempted to entertain him.

Aeterna has remained nearby.

Jake acknowledges these gestures.

He looks at the flowers.

He turns the pages of his books.

He accepts the food.

He recognizes his friends.

But he does not smile.

On several occasions, he has appeared to make a conscious attempt.

The corners of his mouth move slightly.

Then stop.

His expression returns to its previous state.

It is not clear whether Jake is physically incapable of producing a smile or whether his current emotional condition makes the expression inaccessible to him.

What is clear is that **he no longer experiences safety and happiness in the effortless way he once did.**

A member of the Welfare Division asked him whether he still liked flowers.

Jake looked at the rose beside his bed.

> **JAKE:** "Yes."

"Do you want to keep it?"

> **JAKE:** "Yes."

"Does it make you happy?"

Jake remained quiet.

After a long pause, he answered:

> **JAKE:** "I don't know."

He carefully moved the vase farther from the edge of the table.

Then he returned to bed.

He did not look at the rose again.

# 2. A WORLD THAT NO LONGER FEELS SAFE

Jake's current fear does not appear limited to one location or one person.

It has spread into ordinary activities.

## Doors

Jake watches doors whenever he enters a room.

He prefers to know whether they are open, closed, or locked.

If a door closes unexpectedly, he may freeze.

If someone leaves without telling him where they are going, he sometimes watches the doorway long after they have disappeared.

On one occasion, a door closed behind a medical staff member.

The sound was ordinary.

Jake reacted as though something had gone terribly wrong.

He immediately turned toward the exit.

His breathing became irregular.

He began looking around the room, searching for another way out.

When the staff member reopened the door, Jake did not relax immediately.

He stared at the gap.

Then at the staff member.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "It's okay, Jake. The door isn't locked."

Jake looked at the handle.

> **JAKE:** "Can it lock?"

The staff member hesitated.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "Yes. But nobody is going to lock you outside."

Jake's expression did not change.

> **JAKE:** "It already happened."

The staff member had no immediate answer.

Jake sat down beside his teddy bear.

He kept watching the door.

## Footsteps

Jake has become sensitive to approaching footsteps.

Heavy footsteps make him tense.

Fast footsteps make him look for somewhere to move.

Unexpected footsteps behind him may cause him to flinch sharply.

He sometimes turns around before the person reaches him.

He does not always recognize the person immediately.

Even familiar residents may frighten him if they approach without warning.

On one occasion, Frost approached him from behind to offer a blanket.

Jake recoiled so suddenly that he nearly fell from his chair.

Frost immediately stopped.

She placed the blanket on a nearby surface and stepped back.

> **FROST:** "It's just me, Jake."

Jake looked at her.

His eyes moved to the blanket.

Then back to Frost.

> **JAKE:** "I know."

He sounded frightened despite recognizing her.

Frost did not approach again.

She remained where she was until Jake eventually reached for the blanket himself.

## Unexpected Touch

Jake has become reluctant to be touched without warning.

A hand reaching toward his shoulder can make him recoil.

Someone attempting to straighten his clothes may cause him to pull away.

He sometimes stiffens when someone hugs him, even when the person is someone he previously approached for affection.

This does not necessarily mean he no longer wants comfort.

He may want to be held and simultaneously fear what will happen if he allows someone close enough to touch him.

Personnel have been instructed to ask before touching him, explain necessary medical procedures beforehand, and allow him to refuse nonessential physical contact.

His reactions are not to be treated as misbehavior.

He is frightened.

That is the relevant observation.

# 3. THE PEOPLE HE LOVES

Jake's fear is particularly severe around the possibility of losing the people closest to him.

He still recognizes them as his friends and family.

He remembers their voices.

He remembers their habits.

He remembers the kindness they showed him before the incidents.

He has not simply forgotten that he loves them.

The difficulty is that his memories of being cared for now coexist with memories of being left outside, being injured, and coming dangerously close to dying.

Those experiences have changed the meaning of familiar relationships.

Someone can love him and still fail to notice that he is missing.

Someone can promise to protect him and still be the person who accidentally hurts him.

Someone can sincerely believe he is safe when he is not.

Jake appears increasingly unable to separate a person's good intentions from the possibility that their actions may hurt him.

He does not necessarily believe that his friends want him dead.

He seems to be struggling with something more frightening:

**They might love him and still be unable to keep him safe.**

And if that is true, he has no clear idea whom he can trust.

## Bao

Bao remains one of the people Jake associates with comfort.

However, he has become hesitant about asking her for anything.

If she offers him food, he sometimes looks at her before accepting it.

If she brings him a blanket, he may ask whether she needs it herself.

If she leaves the room, he watches her go.

On one occasion, Bao told him she would return shortly.

Jake nodded.

He waited.

When she returned, he was still sitting in the same position.

Bao asked whether he had been waiting for her.

> **JAKE:** "You said you'd come back."

> **BAO:** "I did, sweetheart."

Jake looked down at his teddy bear.

> **JAKE:** "I thought you might not."

Bao sat beside him, leaving enough space that he could move away if he wanted.

> **BAO:** "I'm sorry you had to wonder."

Jake said nothing.

Bao did not ask him to forgive her for something she had not personally done in that moment.

She simply remained beside him.

After several minutes, Jake leaned against her arm.

He did not smile.

Bao did not treat the gesture as proof that he was better.

She understood that accepting comfort once did not mean the fear had disappeared.

## Aeterna

Aeterna's presence has historically been reassuring to Jake.

Her appearance, her voice, and her willingness to remain with him have become familiar sources of stability.

Now, even with Aeterna nearby, Jake sometimes checks that she is still there.

If she moves out of his direct line of sight, he may turn toward the sound of her armor.

If she stands near the door, he watches her.

If she tells him she will return, he may ask when.

Aeterna has begun giving him concrete answers instead of vague reassurances.

"I'll be back soon" has become:

"I am going to the medical station. I will return after I speak with Bao."

When circumstances change, she tells him.

She does not promise that nothing bad will ever happen.

She knows she cannot honestly guarantee that.

Instead, she tells him what she is doing and follows through whenever possible.

One evening, Jake was sitting on his bed with his teddy bear.

Aeterna stood beside the doorway.

> **AETERNA:** "Would you like me to stay?"

Jake nodded.

She entered and sat beside him.

Several minutes passed.

> **JAKE:** "You won't leave?"

Aeterna answered carefully.

> **AETERNA:** "I am staying with you tonight."

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:** "Promise?"

> **AETERNA:** "Yes."

He lowered his eyes.

> **JAKE:** "What if something happens?"

Aeterna did not tell him that nothing could happen.

> **AETERNA:** "Then I will tell you what I know, and I will do everything I can to help you."

Jake held his teddy bear more tightly.

> **JAKE:** "That's not the same."

> **AETERNA:** "No. It isn't."

He looked away.

Aeterna remained beside him.

For Jake, the distinction mattered.

He was no longer reassured by certainty that sounded too easy.

He needed someone willing to acknowledge that the world was frightening without pretending otherwise.

## Neri

Neri's role in Jake's fear is particularly complicated.

She was the last person Jake saw before the door closed.

She believed he was returning inside early.

Jake did not understand that.

From his perspective, he had been left outside while the person he had last seen went somewhere he could no longer reach.

Neri did not intend to abandon him.

But Jake's memory of the event does not contain her intention.

It contains the door.

The snow.

The cold.

The waiting.

And the fact that nobody came.

Neri has become aware that Jake is frightened around her.

When she enters a room, he sometimes watches her hands.

When she approaches a door, he may look toward it.

If she offers to help him, he may hesitate before accepting.

On one occasion, Neri sat several feet away from him.

She did not approach.

She did not demand an explanation.

After a while, she quietly said:

> **NERI:** "I thought you were going inside."

Jake looked at her.

Neri's voice remained soft.

> **NERI:** "I didn't know you were still outside."

Jake stared at the floor.

> **JAKE:** "I knocked."

Neri went still.

> **JAKE:** "I waited."

A pause.

> **JAKE:** "Nobody came."

Neri did not attempt to explain away those words.

She could explain what she had believed.

She could explain what she had failed to realize.

But none of that could change the hours Jake had spent outside.

> **NERI:** "I'm sorry."

Jake looked at her.

For a moment, he appeared as though he might say something else.

Instead, he pulled his teddy bear closer.

Neri remained where she was.

She did not ask him to forgive her.

She did not ask him to smile.

She did not tell him that he should know she would never hurt him.

She allowed the silence to remain.

At present, that may be the most respectful thing she can offer.

# 4. THE QUESTIONS JAKE NO LONGER ASKS ALOUD

Jake has previously asked:

> **"Why does everyone forget me behind?"**

> **"Why I am locked outside?"**

> **"Are you trying to kill me?"**

Personnel initially recorded these statements as expressions of confusion and distress.

They should now be understood in the context of his broader psychological deterioration.

Jake is struggling to reconcile the affection people express with the harm he has experienced.

He has been left alone in dangerous conditions.

He has been injured during an incident involving Anby's sleepwalking.

He has nearly been poisoned.

He has now experienced severe hypothermia after being unable to get back inside.

Each incident had different circumstances.

Each involved different people or failures.

Jake may not yet be able to process those distinctions consistently.

To him, the incidents may be accumulating into a single conclusion:

_People say they care about me._

_People say I'm safe._

_But I keep getting hurt._

_And sometimes nobody notices until it's almost too late._

This is not evidence that everyone around him is malicious.

It is evidence that Jake no longer experiences their good intentions as sufficient protection.

The distinction is essential.

C.O.R.E. must not respond by insisting that Jake is wrong to be afraid.

His fear may be disproportionate to the immediate circumstances, but it has not appeared without cause.

# 5. MEDICAL WARD INCIDENT — THE DROPPED TRAY

**LOCATION:** Medical Ward  
**TIME:** 14:26

A medical staff member dropped a metal tray.

The impact produced a sudden, sharp noise.

Jake was awake in bed.

He immediately curled around his teddy bear.

His shoulders rose toward his ears.

His wings folded tightly against his back.

He attempted to move away from the sound despite having nowhere to go.

His breathing became rapid.

Cold vapor escaped his mouth in short, irregular bursts.

A localized temperature decrease was subsequently recorded around his bedding.

The staff member approached instinctively.

Jake recoiled.

> **JAKE:** "Don't!"

The staff member stopped immediately.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "Okay. I'm stopping."

Jake continued staring at them.

His hands were trembling.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "I'm not going to touch you."

Jake looked toward the tray.

Then toward the door.

> **JAKE:** "Are you angry?"

The staff member appeared confused.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "No, Jake. I'm not angry."

> **JAKE:** "Are you going to make me go outside?"

The room went quiet.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "No."

Jake did not relax.

The staff member moved the tray away without approaching him again.

Bao was called to assist.

When she arrived, she did not immediately touch Jake.

She sat where he could see her.

She told him what had happened.

She explained that the noise came from the tray, that nobody was angry with him, and that he was not being sent anywhere.

She repeated the information without demanding that he believe her immediately.

It took several minutes for Jake's breathing to slow.

Even afterward, he continued watching the doorway.

The incident did not cause a new physical injury.

It demonstrated how readily Jake's fear can be activated by an unexpected event.

**Clinical observation:** Jake may react to sudden sounds and movements as though they signal an imminent threat. Staff must stop the triggering action where possible, reduce environmental stimulation, and allow him to regain a sense of control before resuming nonurgent care.

# 6. HE IS FRIGHTENED OF BEING LEFT ALONE

Jake's behavior when alone has changed substantially.

Previously, he could spend hours reading or drawing without requiring company.

Solitude was often enjoyable.

He liked quiet rooms.

He liked looking through windows.

He liked having time to himself.

Now, the same quiet may become frightening when he is uncertain whether anyone knows where he is.

He sometimes leaves a light on.

He may position himself where he can see the doorway.

He occasionally asks who will be nearby while he sleeps.

On particularly difficult evenings, he becomes reluctant to let a caregiver leave even briefly.

Yet he may also become overwhelmed when too many people gather around him.

This creates a difficult contradiction.

Jake wants someone close enough to notice if something happens.

He does not necessarily want people close enough to surprise, touch, question, or crowd him.

He wants company.

He fears what company might bring.

He wants to be left in peace.

He fears what might happen if everyone leaves.

**He is no longer certain that either being alone or being with people is safe.**

C.O.R.E. has therefore discontinued the assumption that Jake's preference for quiet automatically means he wants to be alone.

Personnel should ask whether he wants company and respect his answer.

If he requests someone to remain nearby, the arrangement should be made as predictably as possible.

If a caregiver must leave, Jake should be told who will take over and when the change will occur.

No one should promise uninterrupted personal presence if they cannot reliably provide it.

A reliable handover is preferable to a comforting promise that is later broken.

---

# 7. THE TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear remains central to his ability to feel secure.

It is his cardiac vessel.

It is also a sensory extension of himself.

The bear's condition directly affects Jake's own experience.

Personnel have observed Jake holding it against his chest, covering it with blankets, and checking its position when he becomes distressed.

He sometimes examines its limbs after an unexpected movement.

On one occasion, a staff member offered to hold the bear while adjusting Jake's bedding.

Jake immediately pulled it away.

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "I was just going to move it for a moment."

Jake shook his head.

> **JAKE:** "No."

> **STAFF MEMBER:** "All right. You can keep it."

Jake held the bear against himself.

> **JAKE:** "It stays with me."

The staff member agreed.

The bear was not taken from him.

This response is now standard procedure.

The bear is not an ordinary toy that can be removed for convenience.

It is a source of familiarity, physical comfort, and sensory continuity.

Given Jake's current fear, separating him from it without compelling medical necessity could intensify his distress.

Personnel should not use the bear to persuade him to cooperate, threaten to take it away, or treat his attachment as childish stubbornness.

It is one of the few things Jake can hold without having to wonder whether it will suddenly decide to leave.

# 8. THE LOSS OF PLAY

The change is also visible in Jake's interaction with things he once enjoyed.

His books remain beside his bed.

He reads occasionally, but he sometimes loses his place and stares at the same paragraph for several minutes.

His drawing materials remain available.

He has produced fewer drawings.

When he does draw, the subjects are often simple: windows, doors, flowers, and small figures standing beside other figures.

He has not recently attempted to bring a drawing to life.

When offered a new toy, he thanked the person who brought it.

Then he set it beside his bed.

He did not play with it.

Nora attempted to show him a game she thought he would enjoy.

Jake watched her demonstrate it.

When she finished, he nodded.

> **NORA:** "Do you want to try?"

> **JAKE:** "Not now."

Nora paused.

> **NORA:** "Later?"

Jake looked at the game.

> **JAKE:** "Maybe."

Nora did not insist.

She placed it within reach and returned to Bao.

Jake did not touch the game that afternoon.

This does not establish that he has permanently lost interest in play.

It indicates that his present distress is interfering with activities that previously came naturally to him.

Personnel are instructed not to turn recreation into a test of recovery.

Jake does not need to laugh, play, or smile to reassure the people around him.

He should be allowed to enjoy things in whatever limited way he can currently manage.

And if he cannot enjoy them today, the activity should still be available tomorrow.

# 9. WELFARE ASSESSMENT

C.O.R.E. considers Jake's current condition a **critical psychological welfare concern**, compounded by his recent severe hypothermia and ongoing anomalous temperature regulation.

The following observations are significant:

- Persistent fear in familiar environments.
    
- Increased startle responses.
    
- Heightened vigilance toward doors, footsteps, and unexpected movements.
    
- Reluctance to accept physical contact.
    
- Fear of abandonment and separation.
    
- Reduced engagement with previously enjoyable activities.
    
- Loss of spontaneous smiling and laughter.
    
- Increased dependence on predictable routines and familiar objects.
    
- Difficulty accepting reassurance.
    
- Repeated questions concerning whether others intend to hurt him or leave him behind.
    

These observations are not sufficient to establish a formal psychiatric diagnosis.

They are, however, sufficient to require immediate, sustained support.

Jake should receive assessment from an appropriately qualified mental-health professional experienced in childhood trauma, alongside continued medical monitoring.

His physical recovery and psychological recovery must be treated as interconnected concerns.

Fear can disrupt rest, eating, cooperation with care, and willingness to report symptoms.

His inability to feel safe may therefore create additional risks while his body remains medically vulnerable.

The objective is not to make Jake behave as he did before.

It is to help him regain a sense of safety without requiring him to deny what happened.

# 10. RESPONSE PROTOCOL — REVISED

All personnel interacting with Jake are instructed to follow these measures.

### A. Predictability

- Announce your presence before approaching.
    
- Explain what you intend to do before touching him.
    
- Tell him when you need to leave.
    
- Identify who will remain with him.
    
- Inform him promptly when plans change.
    
- Follow through on commitments whenever possible.
    

### B. Choice

- Offer simple choices whenever medically appropriate.
    
- Do not force nonessential physical affection.
    
- Do not insist that he participate in recreation.
    
- Allow him to keep his teddy bear with him unless a specific medical necessity prevents it.
    
- Give him time to answer.
    

### C. Environmental Safety

- Keep his immediate environment calm.
    
- Avoid unnecessary loud noises and sudden movements.
    
- Ensure that doors and access arrangements are clear to him.
    
- Do not lock him outside or leave him in an unmonitored area.
    
- Do not assume that an apparently ordinary environment feels safe to him.
    

### D. Emotional Support

Do not say:

> "You know we would never hurt you."

Jake's recent experiences make absolute assurances difficult for him to trust.

Do not say:

> "You need to stop being scared."

Fear is not a voluntary behavior he can simply switch off.

Do not say:

> "You're safe now, so forget what happened."

Safety must be demonstrated repeatedly, and what happened cannot be undone.

Instead, personnel should acknowledge the experience without exaggerating or minimizing it.

Appropriate responses include:

> "That was frightening. I'm here with you."

> "You don't have to trust me immediately."

> "I'll tell you before I move."

> "You can say no."

> "I made a mistake. I'm going to take responsibility for it."

> "You don't have to make me feel better about what happened."

Most importantly, personnel must accept that **Jake may remain afraid even when they do everything correctly.**

A single good interaction cannot erase a history of frightening experiences.

Consistency matters more than dramatic gestures.

# 11. OBSERVATION — 21:40

Jake was sitting on his bed.

His teddy bear rested in his lap.

The room was quiet.

Bao was seated nearby, reading.

Aeterna stood outside the open doorway, having told Jake that she would remain within sight while she spoke briefly with the medical team.

Jake looked toward her.

Then at Bao.

Then toward the hallway.

> **JAKE:** "Is everyone here?"

Bao looked up.

> **BAO:** "The people who told you they would be here are here."

Jake considered this.

> **JAKE:** "What about the others?"

> **BAO:** "They're elsewhere in SAFEHOUSE."

Jake lowered his head.

> **JAKE:** "Are they coming back?"

Bao closed her book.

> **BAO:** "Some of them will see you tomorrow. Some may come by tonight. I'll tell you if anyone makes a plan to visit."

Jake's fingers tightened around the bear.

> **JAKE:** "What if they forget?"

Bao did not answer too quickly.

> **BAO:** "Then we need to make sure you aren't left waiting without knowing what's happening."

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:** "That's not what I asked."

Bao's expression changed.

She understood.

Jake was not asking for a schedule.

He was asking whether the people he loved might forget that he existed.

> **BAO:** "I can't promise that nobody will ever make another mistake, sweetheart."

Jake looked down again.

> **BAO:** "But I can promise that we will take this seriously. And if someone says they'll be with you, we need to make sure there's a plan for you if they can't stay."

He did not respond.

Bao continued reading, leaving the conversation open.

Several minutes later, Jake spoke again.

> **JAKE:** "I don't want to go outside."

> **BAO:** "You don't have to go outside tonight."

> **JAKE:** "Ever."

Bao set the book down.

She did not tell him that he would eventually get over it.

She did not tell him that the snow was beautiful, or that flowers needed winter, or that he had once liked looking at the sky.

> **BAO:** "We don't have to decide about forever tonight."

Jake's eyes remained fixed on the bear.

> **JAKE:** "Okay."

He did not look relieved.

He did not smile.

Bao stayed beside him.

At 22:03, Jake fell asleep sitting against the pillows, still holding the bear.

Bao carefully adjusted the blanket around him without moving the bear from his arms.

Aeterna remained visible from the bed.

Neither spoke.

# 12. FINAL ASSESSMENT — THE CHILD WHO IS AFRAID

Jake is a star.

He is a being whose existence extends far beyond the small body he currently inhabits.

He possesses anomalous abilities that C.O.R.E. cannot fully explain.

He has wings.

His eyes contain patterns resembling distant constellations.

His tears can become diamonds.

He has done things that no ordinary child could do.

None of these facts make him less vulnerable to fear.

His present physical form is approximately that of a six-year-old child.

He experiences cold.

He experiences pain.

He becomes exhausted.

He needs sleep.

He needs food.

He needs other people.

And now, he is frightened of what other people might do, even when those people are the ones he most desperately wants to trust.

His loss of smiling is not the sole measure of his condition.

Some people smile very little without being unhappy. Some recover slowly. Some need time.

Jake's situation is more concerning because the absence of his smile is accompanied by a pervasive change in how he experiences the world.

He no longer appears to expect good things to happen.

He appears to expect something to go wrong.

When a door closes, he anticipates being trapped outside.

When footsteps approach, he anticipates being startled or hurt.

When someone leaves, he anticipates being forgotten.

When someone offers kindness, he may wonder what will happen afterward.

Even a quiet room can become frightening when he is unsure whether anyone will return.

The world has not become entirely hostile.

But Jake no longer knows how to tell which parts of it are safe.

And he is too frightened to find out by simply trusting.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake used to smile at flowers.

He used to smile when someone brought him cake.

He used to smile when Aeterna entered his room.

He used to smile when Bao called him sweetheart.

He used to smile when Clover showed him something she had drawn.

He used to smile at things that were small, ordinary, and kind.

Now, he looks at the same flowers without smiling.

He accepts the same cake without excitement.

He watches Aeterna enter as though he needs to confirm that she is really there.

He listens to Bao's voice while keeping one hand wrapped around his teddy bear.

He looks at Clover's drawings and sometimes cannot find the words to tell her what he thinks.

He is still Jake.

He remembers his friends.

He remembers his family.

He remembers the things he loves.

But the memories that once made the world feel warm are now accompanied by memories of cold, pain, waiting, and fear.

He cannot simply return to the child he was before.

Not because that child has disappeared.

But because that child learned something he should never have had to learn:

That someone can be loved and still be left behind.

That someone can be surrounded by people and still be in danger.

That someone can call for help and still have to wait.

And that being important to someone does not automatically mean they will notice when something is wrong.

Jake is not refusing to smile to punish anyone.

He is not withholding happiness as a test.

He is not being difficult.

**He is shattered, and he is frightened of almost everything.**

He may laugh again someday.

He may play again.

He may look at a flower and feel something other than the memory of being cold.

But none of those things should be demanded of him.

For now, he needs people who will announce themselves before entering, explain before touching, return when they say they will, and take responsibility when they fail.

He needs to discover, through ordinary repeated experiences, that a closed door does not always mean abandonment.

That footsteps do not always mean danger.

That someone leaving a room does not mean they have forgotten him.

That accepting a blanket does not create a debt.

That he can be frightened without being scolded.

That he can be angry without losing his place.

And that he does not have to smile to convince anyone that he is worth protecting.

Because Jake was worth protecting before he became afraid.

He was worth protecting before he became ill.

He was worth protecting before anyone knew what he was.

And he remains worth protecting now, when the child who once smiled at the stars can barely look at the doorway without wondering whether everyone is about to disappear.

**He does not need to become the old Jake to deserve a safe place in this world.**

He needs the world around him to become safe enough that, one day, he might want to smile again.

Until then, C.O.R.E. will not measure his recovery by the return of his smile.

We will measure it by whether he can sleep without watching the door.

Whether he can accept help without fearing its cost.

Whether he can be alone without believing he has been forgotten.

And whether, when he asks if someone is coming back, he can eventually believe the answer.
