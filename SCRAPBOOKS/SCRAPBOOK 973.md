# SAFEHOUSE INCIDENT SUPPLEMENT

## SUBJECT: NERI

### TITLE: PROGRESSIVE BACKGROUNDING — SECONDARY INCIDENTS

**Classification:** SAFEHOUSE Internal / Medical / Behavioral  
**Subject:** Neri  
**Age:** 10  
**Status:** Permanent Resident  
**Phenomenon:** Progressive social withdrawal / environmental non-recognition

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This document supplements the previous **“Background Effect”** report.

Since that report was issued, additional incidents have occurred in which Neri was physically present within ordinary SAFEHOUSE environments but was either:

1. not noticed by nearby personnel,
    
2. noticed only after an accident occurred,
    
3. or placed in a dangerous situation because surrounding personnel did not realize she was present.
    

None of the incidents indicate deliberate attempts to harm Neri.

Several appear to be ordinary accidents.

The concerning factor is the frequency with which **Neri is present but not accounted for.**

---

# 2. INCIDENT — STAIRWELL

Neri was walking down a SAFEHOUSE stairwell while carrying a chemistry book.

A group of workers was moving upward.

Neri stepped toward the side to allow them through.

The workers passed.

None appeared to notice her.

After they passed, Neri continued down the stairs.

She missed a step.

Neri fell several steps before stopping.

She sustained minor bruising and abrasions.

A worker further down the staircase heard the noise and investigated.

> **Worker:** “What happened?”

Neri, still sitting on the stairs:

> **Neri:** “I missed a step.”

> **Worker:** “Are you okay?”

> **Neri:** “Yeah.”

She then picked up her chemistry book.

The worker reportedly asked:

> **“Were you there the whole time?”**

Neri nodded.

---

# 3. INCIDENT — HEAVY SECURITY DOOR

Neri was following another employee through a heavy security door.

The employee entered.

Neri followed several seconds behind.

The employee released the door.

The door swung shut.

It struck Neri.

Neri was knocked backward and fell.

A nosebleed subsequently developed.

The employee initially continued walking.

Another employee eventually informed them that someone had been hit.

The first employee reportedly turned around and said:

> **“I didn't see anyone behind me.”**

Neri did not complain.

She simply wiped the blood from her nose and continued walking.

---

# 4. INCIDENT — BLAST DOOR

A more serious incident occurred near a blast-door access corridor.

Neri was standing near the doorway while personnel were preparing to close the blast door.

The door began its closing cycle.

Nobody noticed Neri.

A nearby worker eventually saw her and shouted.

The closure was stopped before contact.

Neri had been standing within the door's closing area.

The incident was classified as a **near-miss**.

When asked why she had not moved:

> **“I thought they saw me.”**

When asked why she had not spoken:

> **“They were busy.”**

This response has been repeatedly identified as characteristic of Neri's current behavior.

---

# 5. INCIDENT — BALCONY

Neri was observed walking along an upper-level balcony while reading.

She stopped near the railing.

She apparently became distracted by something below.

She leaned slightly over the edge.

Her footing slipped.

Neri fell forward.

She caught herself against the railing before going over.

A worker nearby saw the movement and immediately pulled her away from the edge.

No serious injury occurred.

The worker subsequently asked:

> **“What were you doing?”**

Neri answered:

> **“Looking.”**

The worker asked:

> **“Do you know how high that is?”**

Neri looked down.

Then back at the worker.

> **“Oh.”**

---

# 6. INCIDENT — CARGO CORRIDOR

Neri attempted to cross a cargo corridor while several workers were moving equipment.

Because of her small stature and quiet movement, personnel repeatedly failed to register her position.

One worker turned while carrying equipment.

Another shouted a warning.

Neri stepped backward.

The equipment passed within a dangerous distance of her.

The worker reportedly froze afterward.

> **Worker:** “I didn't even see you.”

Neri:

> **“It's okay.”**

The worker:

> **“No. It isn't.”**

Neri appeared confused by the response.

---

# 7. INCIDENT — ELEVATOR

Neri entered an elevator behind several employees.

Nobody noticed her.

The doors closed.

She remained inside while the others exited.

Neri eventually arrived at the wrong floor.

Instead of asking for directions, she left the elevator and attempted to find the correct route herself.

She was later found sitting outside the wrong laboratory reading her book.

When asked how long she had been there:

> **“I don't know.”**

---

# 8. INCIDENT — CAFETERIA

Neri's previous cafeteria emergency remains the most severe example.

She suffered an anemic shock while appearing to read normally.

She remained unnoticed for approximately four hours.

She eventually fell from her chair after a cafeteria worker accidentally bumped into her.

Neri required emergency cardiac treatment, including defibrillation and Bao's compatible blood.

This incident demonstrated that the Background Effect is not merely socially concerning.

It can directly increase the danger posed by her existing medical condition.

---

# 9. INCIDENT — OUTSIDE ACCESS

The earlier snow incident has also been reassessed.

Neri was locked outside after a guard failed to notice her entering behind him.

She had no keycard available.

Rather than alerting anyone, she waited beside the box of plant samples.

Four hours later, Bao discovered her covered in snow.

Neri had fallen asleep.

Again, the relevant behavior was not:

**“Neri was abandoned.”**

It was:

**“Neri assumed waiting was preferable to asking someone for help.”**

---

# 10. PATTERN ANALYSIS

The incidents appear increasingly interconnected.

### Neri's behavior:

She walks quietly.

She does not announce herself.

She rarely interrupts conversations.

She doesn't demand attention.

She doesn't complain when overlooked.

She assumes people are busy.

She assumes mistakes are her own responsibility.

She leaves dangerous situations only when she realizes they are dangerous.

And even then, she may not tell anyone afterward.

### Result:

Other people unconsciously stop accounting for her presence.

---

# 11. THE DANGEROUS PARADOX

The SAFEHOUSE is not ignoring Neri.

Many residents actively care about her.

The problem is that Neri behaves like someone who expects to be ignored.

This makes her easier to ignore.

The process therefore becomes self-reinforcing:

**Neri withdraws.**

↓

**People see less of Neri.**

↓

**People become less accustomed to checking for her.**

↓

**Neri receives less acknowledgement.**

↓

**Neri interprets this as confirmation that she isn't important.**

↓

**Neri withdraws further.**

---

# 12. PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCES

The Background Effect has now been associated with:

- Falls on stairs
    
- Facial impact from heavy doors
    
- Near-contact with blast doors
    
- Near-fall from elevated balconies
    
- Near-collision with moving equipment
    
- Being left outside in severe weather
    
- Becoming lost within Blacksite
    
- Unnoticed medical emergencies
    

None of these incidents were necessarily caused by malicious behavior.

That does not make them harmless.

A pattern of individually mundane accidents can become extremely dangerous when concentrated around one person who is already medically vulnerable.

---

# 13. NERI'S RESPONSE

Neri consistently minimizes these events.

Typical responses include:

> “I'm okay.”

> “It's fine.”

> “I wasn't hurt.”

> “They were busy.”

> “I didn't want to bother them.”

> “It was my fault.”

Even when an incident objectively required attention, Neri frequently attempted to return to whatever she had been doing beforehand.

This is considered abnormal self-assessment.

---

# 14. JANE DOE

Jane remains particularly concerned.

At 28 years old, Jane is old enough to recognize the difference between:

**a child being independent**

and

**a child having learned that asking for help is inconvenient.**

Neri increasingly demonstrates the latter.

Jane has invited Neri to her office.

Neri declined.

Jane has repeatedly told Neri that she can interrupt her.

Neri continues to assume Jane is too busy.

Jane has therefore changed her approach.

Instead of waiting for Neri to come to her, Jane has begun making deliberate check-ins when operationally possible.

Not because Neri is incapable.

Because Neri has demonstrated that she will often remain silent until someone else notices.

---

# 15. ALICE

Alice's reaction has also become increasingly apparent.

Alice invited Neri to the greenhouse with Bao.

Neri declined.

Alice's cheerful expression disappeared immediately.

She did not argue.

She simply left on her scooter.

Alice appears to understand that Neri is withdrawing from her.

However, Alice cannot force Neri to accept companionship.

The current recommendation is therefore simple:

**Keep inviting her.**

Even if she says no.

---

# 16. SAFEHOUSE CONCERN

Personnel should not interpret Neri's quietness as evidence that she does not want company.

She may genuinely want to be alone.

She may also believe she has no right to ask someone to stay.

Those two situations can look identical.

The distinction must therefore be determined through continued interaction rather than assumption.

---

# 17. CURRENT STATUS

Neri remains active within the SAFEHOUSE.

She continues studying.

She continues her chemistry interests.

She continues eating sweets.

She continues interacting with residents on occasion.

But her overall presence has diminished.

She is becoming someone people encounter rather than someone they expect to see.

That is the danger.

Because the SAFEHOUSE is full of doors.

Stairs.

Elevators.

Machinery.

Balconies.

Snow.

Security systems.

And hundreds of people moving quickly through them.

A person who expects to be noticed will usually move accordingly.

Neri doesn't.

She simply walks.

Quietly.

Behind everyone else.

---

# 18. FINAL ASSESSMENT

The phrase **“Background Character”** was initially used informally to describe Neri's increasing social withdrawal.

It is no longer considered entirely humorous.

Neri is not literally becoming a background character.

But she is increasingly behaving like one.

She waits at the edge of rooms.

She follows rather than leads.

She declines invitations.

She doesn't interrupt.

She doesn't complain.

She doesn't ask for help.

And when something goes wrong—

she often gets back up and continues as though nothing happened.

The SAFEHOUSE must not allow her ability to endure being overlooked to become an excuse for overlooking her.

**Neri doesn't need everyone watching her.**

**She needs people who remember that she's there.**
