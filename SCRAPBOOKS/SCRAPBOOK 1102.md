# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Neri has introduced a new Night Stalker authorization trial.

There is no contest registration.

There are no tickets.

There is no scheduled start time.

There is no announced location.

There is, however, one **Night Stalker.**

Technically.

Neri concealed a **plastic model** of a Night Stalker somewhere within the Blacksite.

The rules were subsequently communicated to MSAC personnel:

> **If an MSAC Operator finds it:**  
> **AUTHORIZATION GRANTED.**

> **If anyone else finds it first:**  
> **MSAC LOSES.**

The actual Night Stalker remains secured.

Neri has explicitly confirmed that she will **not** hide a functional firearm somewhere inside the Blacksite and expect employees to search for it.

The object being hidden is a harmless plastic model.

The authorization consequence, however, is real.

---

# 2. THE ANNOUNCEMENT

The first indication that anything had happened was a note placed on the MSAC briefing room door.

It read:

> **"I hid one."**

Below it:

> **"Find it."**

Below that:

> **"If MSAC finds it, it's yours."**

And beneath that:

> **"If someone else finds it first, you lose :P"**

No signature was necessary.

Everyone knew.

---

# 3. IMMEDIATE MSAC RESPONSE

MSAC personnel initially assumed the object had been hidden somewhere inside Sector 4-C.

They were incorrect.

Neri had hidden it somewhere within the broader Blacksite.

The search therefore expanded considerably.

Within twenty minutes, MSAC personnel could be observed searching:

- corridors;
    
- cafeterias;
    
- offices;
    
- meeting rooms;
    
- recreational areas;
    
- unused rooms;
    
- storage areas;
    
- administrative sections;
    
- approved common spaces.
    

They were extremely serious about this.

Perhaps too serious.

---

# 4. THE PROBLEM

The Blacksite contains a considerable number of people.

Many of them were not informed that a search was occurring.

This created an unusual situation.

An MSAC Operator would enter a room.

Search.

Check behind furniture.

Check shelves.

Leave.

Five minutes later, a completely unrelated employee would enter the same room.

Search for something entirely unrelated.

Leave.

The MSAC Operator would then return.

This repeated several times.

---

# 5. NERI'S ACTUAL STRATEGY

Neri was not trying to make the model impossible to find.

She was testing something else.

**Awareness.**

An MSAC Operator could not simply search the obvious locations and assume the object would be there.

They needed to notice the environment.

They needed to communicate.

They needed to avoid getting in each other's way.

They needed to understand that the Blacksite was full of people who were **not** participating.

And most importantly:

They needed to find the model before somebody else accidentally did.

---

# 6. THE FIRST NEAR-MISS

Approximately one hour into the search, a cafeteria employee moved a stack of empty containers.

The plastic Night Stalker was underneath them.

The employee looked down.

Paused.

Picked up the model.

### CAFETERIA EMPLOYEE:

“Uh, whose toy gun is this?”

Three MSAC Operators immediately turned around.

The employee looked at them.

Then at the model.

Then back at them.

### CAFETERIA EMPLOYEE:

“...Oh.”

An MSAC Operator approached.

### MSAC OPERATOR:

“Please give me that.”

### CAFETERIA EMPLOYEE:

“Why?”

### MSAC OPERATOR:

“It's complicated.”

The employee handed it over.

The trial remained active.

---

The trial quickly became informal but highly coordinated.

MSAC personnel began dividing the Blacksite into search zones.

Operators communicated findings.

Rooms already checked were marked.

Potential locations were reported.

Personnel were warned not to move suspicious objects without informing MSAC.

This last instruction produced several complaints.

One maintenance worker reportedly responded:

> “You people have turned finding a plastic gun into a military operation.”

The MSAC Operator replied:

> “It's a Night Stalker.”

The maintenance worker:

> “It's plastic.”

The MSAC Operator:

> “You don't understand.”
