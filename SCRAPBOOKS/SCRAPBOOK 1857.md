# DEATH IS APPARENTLY A MINOR INCONVENIENCE

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the initial manifestation of Phoenix-derived Transcendence, Jake Akana has experienced multiple additional deaths resulting from seemingly ordinary accidents.

The incidents include falling down the stairs, tripping while walking, and other mishaps associated with Jake's established tendency to become distracted, move without sufficient caution, or otherwise demonstrate an impressive lack of coordination for a child who possesses the ability to return from death.

In each documented instance, Jake underwent a resurrection event involving white fire and subsequently returned to life.

The repeated occurrences have provided additional evidence that the initial recovery from the gunshot wound was not an isolated anomaly.

**Jake can apparently revive himself after death, and he has now demonstrated this capability multiple times through accidental incidents.**

Unfortunately, the demonstrations have not been deliberate experiments.

They have been accidents.

Repeated accidents.

Accidents that have caused considerable distress to Tom Akana, who remains profoundly unhappy about the fact that his brother keeps dying, regardless of whether Jake returns afterward.

Jake, meanwhile, has shown a troubling tendency to regard the successful resurrections as evidence that he is fine.

C.O.R.E. wishes to clarify that these are not equivalent conclusions.

A person surviving repeated fatal accidents does not make the accidents acceptable.

Nor does it make the person responsible for supervising a six-year-old child any less responsible for preventing them.

The current situation has been summarized by one particularly exhausted residential observer:

"He's not trying to test his immortality. He's just really, really bad at walking."

## 2. INCIDENT SERIES A — THE STAIRCASE

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE stairwell  
**Incident Type:** Accidental fatal fall  
**Outcome:** Resurrection confirmed  
**Witnesses:** Tom Akana and nearby residents

The first post-acquisition incident occurred when Jake fell down a staircase.

The precise circumstances remain disputed. Jake described the event as an accident. Tom described it as "the most preventable thing I have ever seen."

Available accounts indicate that Jake lost his footing and fell.

The impact was severe enough to result in death.

Tom reached him shortly afterward.

For several seconds, the stairwell was silent.

Then Tom spoke.

**Tom:** "Jake?"

No response.

**Tom:** "Jake, get up."

Nothing.

Tom hurried down the remaining steps.

**Tom:** "No. No, no, no. You're kidding."

He knelt beside his brother.

**Tom:** "Jake. Come on."

A resident approaching the stairwell stopped upon seeing the scene.

**Resident:** "What happened?"

**Tom:** "HE FELL DOWN THE STAIRS!"

**Resident:** "Is he—"

**Tom:** "I DON'T KNOW!"

White fire erupted around Jake.

Tom immediately recoiled, then leaned forward again as the flames began to consume the fallen boy's outline.

**Tom:** "Oh, you've got to be kidding me."

Jake reappeared through the white fire, coughing and rolling onto his side.

**Jake:** "OW!"

Tom stared at him.

**Jake:** "OW, OW, OW—"

**Tom:** "YOU FELL DOWN THE ENTIRE STAIRCASE!"

**Jake:** "I KNOW!"

**Tom:** "HOW?!"

**Jake:** "I MISSED A STEP!"

**Tom:** "THERE WERE MORE STEPS AFTER THAT!"

Jake blinked.

**Jake:** "...Oh."

Tom covered his face with both hands.

**Tom:** "I am going to develop a headache. I am a ghost. I am going to develop a headache."

Jake got to his feet, apparently restored.

**Jake:** "So, um... does this mean I can still go downstairs?"

Tom slowly lowered his hands.

**Tom:** "YOU ARE TAKING THE ELEVATOR."

**Jake:** "But—"

**Tom:** "THE ELEVATOR."

**Jake:** "Okay!"

Tom took Jake's hand and escorted him away from the stairwell.

The incident was subsequently recorded as the first confirmed accidental resurrection following the original shooting.

**C.O.R.E. NOTE:** The staircase remains structurally intact. The problem has been identified as the small resident attempting to descend it without adequate attention to his surroundings.

## 3. INCIDENT SERIES B — THE FLAT FLOOR

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE, Floor 1 Common Room  
**Incident Type:** Accidental fall while walking  
**Outcome:** Resurrection confirmed  
**Witnesses:** Clover, Tom Akana

The second incident was significantly less dramatic in its initial circumstances.

Jake was walking across the Common Room.

There were no stairs.

There was no hostile entity.

There was no apparent environmental hazard.

There was, however, Jake.

He tripped.

The resulting fall caused a fatal injury.

White fire manifested shortly afterward, and Jake revived.

Clover and Tom were both present.

The following exchange was recovered from the incident record.

**Clover:** "...Jake?"

**Tom:** "No."

**Clover:** "I haven't said anything yet."

**Tom:** "I KNOW WHAT YOU'RE ABOUT TO SAY."

The white fire flared.

Jake returned, rolled onto his back, and stared at the ceiling.

**Jake:** "..."

**Clover:** "Jake?"

**Jake:** "I think the floor attacked me."

Tom turned toward him.

**Tom:** "THE FLOOR DID NOT ATTACK YOU."

**Jake:** "I FELL!"

**Tom:** "YOU WERE WALKING IN A STRAIGHT LINE!"

**Jake:** "I KNOW!"

**Clover:** "Did you trip over anything?"

Jake considered the question.

**Jake:** "No."

Clover looked at the floor.

Then at Jake.

Then at Tom.

**Clover:** "He tripped over nothing."

Tom stood motionless.

**Tom:** "I'm going to need a moment."

**Jake:** "But I'm okay!"

**Tom:** "THAT IS NOT THE POINT!"

**Jake:** "Then what is the point?"

**Tom:** "THE POINT IS THAT YOU SHOULD NOT BE DYING WHILE WALKING!"

Jake thought about this.

**Jake:** "...That seems fair."

Clover crouched beside Jake and checked him over before helping him sit up.

**Clover:** "Can you please try to watch where you're going?"

**Jake:** "I was watching!"

**Clover:** "What were you watching?"

Jake paused.

**Jake:** "I don't remember."

Clover looked at Tom.

Tom looked at Clover.

Neither spoke for several seconds.

**Tom:** "We're going to have to supervise him."

**Clover:** "We already do."

**Tom:** "More."

**Clover:** "How much more?"

**Tom:** "I DON'T KNOW. WE MAY NEED TO INVENT A NEW AMOUNT."

Jake raised his hand.

**Jake:** "Can I have cake?"

**Tom and Clover:** "NO!"

**Jake:** "...Worth asking."

## 4. INCIDENT SERIES C — THE RUNNING EXPERIMENT THAT WASN'T

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE residential corridor  
**Incident Type:** Accidental fatal fall  
**Outcome:** Resurrection confirmed  
**Witnesses:** Tom Akana, Clover

This incident occurred when Jake began running down a residential corridor.

C.O.R.E. emphasizes that Jake was not attempting to test his resurrection capability. No deliberate self-injury was identified.

He was simply running.

He tripped.

He fell.

The result was fatal.

White fire followed.

Tom, who had been attempting to persuade Jake to slow down, witnessed the entire sequence.

**Tom:** "Jake, stop running."

**Jake:** "WHY?"

**Tom:** "BECAUSE YOU'RE GOING TO FALL!"

**Jake:** "I'M FINE!"

Jake fell.

There was a brief silence.

**Tom:** "...I hate being right."

Clover arrived moments later.

**Clover:** "Again?"

**Tom:** "Again."

**Clover:** "How many times is that now?"

**Tom:** "I HAVE STOPPED COUNTING."

The white fire subsided.

Jake sat up.

**Jake:** "Hi!"

Tom stared at him with the exhausted expression of someone whose brother had just demonstrated that warnings about gravity were apparently optional.

**Tom:** "Hi."

**Jake:** "You're not going to yell at me?"

**Tom:** "I am deciding whether yelling will accomplish anything."

**Jake:** "Oh."

**Clover:** "Jake, why were you running?"

**Jake:** "I wanted to get there faster."

**Clover:** "Where?"

Jake opened his mouth.

He stopped.

He looked down the corridor.

Then back at Clover.

**Jake:** "...I forgot."

Tom slowly turned toward the wall.

**Tom:** "I need to lie down."

**Jake:** "Can ghosts lie down?"

**Tom:** "I'M GOING TO FIND OUT."

Clover took Jake by the hand before he could attempt another sprint.

**Clover:** "Walking."

**Jake:** "Walking."

**Clover:** "Slowly."

**Jake:** "Slowly."

**Tom:** "And preferably without dying."

**Jake:** "I'll try!"

**Tom:** "THAT IS NOT A REASSURING ANSWER!"

## 5. CONSOLIDATED OBSERVATION: THE RESURRECTION PATTERN

The incidents collectively establish a consistent pattern.

|Observation|Current Finding|
|---|---|
|Initial fatal injury|Gunshot wound to the neck|
|Subsequent incidents|Accidental falls and mishaps|
|Visible manifestation|White fire|
|Recovery|Jake returns to life|
|Apparent residual injury|No obvious lasting impairment observed immediately after the documented events|
|Deliberate testing|None identified|
|Primary concern|Repeated preventable accidents|
|Most distressed observer|Tom Akana|

The recurring appearance of white fire, followed by Jake's return to life, strongly supports the conclusion that Phoenix-derived Transcendence is responsible for the repeated resurrection events.

However, the incidents do not establish the ability's absolute limits.

C.O.R.E. cannot conclude that Jake will recover from every conceivable cause of death, that the process is instantaneous under all circumstances, or that he cannot experience lasting consequences.

The fact that several accidents have ended in successful resurrections is evidence of a repeatable phenomenon, not proof that the phenomenon is infallible.

**The ability is repeatable. The accidents are preventable. These facts must not be confused.**

## 6. TOM AKANA — WELFARE ASSESSMENT

Tom's distress has increased as the accidents have continued.

The original shooting frightened him because he believed he might lose Jake. The later incidents have not reassured him in the way C.O.R.E. initially hoped they might.

Instead, Tom has been forced to watch Jake repeatedly die in situations that should never have become fatal.

His frustration is not directed solely at the existence of the ability. It is directed at the circumstances that keep requiring it.

### Follow-Up Interview

**C.O.R.E. Psychologist:** "Tom, do you understand that Jake appears capable of resurrecting himself repeatedly?"

**Tom:** "Yes."

**Psychologist:** "Does knowing that help?"

**Tom:** "No."

**Psychologist:** "Why not?"

**Tom:** "Because I still have to watch him die."

The psychologist paused.

**Psychologist:** "Even though he comes back?"

**Tom:** "YES. EVEN THOUGH HE COMES BACK."

Tom folded his arms.

**Tom:** "Do you know what it's like to see your brother fall down a staircase and stop moving?"

**Psychologist:** "I can't claim to know exactly what that feels like."

**Tom:** "Good. Because it is horrible."

**Psychologist:** "And then he gets up again."

**Tom:** "After catching fire!"

**Psychologist:** "Right."

**Tom:** "And then he asks for cake."

The psychologist took a moment before responding.

**Psychologist:** "That does sound difficult."

**Tom:** "IT IS DIFFICULT!"

**Psychologist:** "What would help?"

Tom's expression changed.

His voice became quieter.

**Tom:** "I want him to stop getting hurt."

**Psychologist:** "We'll work on that."

**Tom:** "And I want people to stop acting like it doesn't matter because he comes back."

**Psychologist:** "That is a reasonable request."

Tom looked down.

**Tom:** "He's my brother."

**Psychologist:** "I know."

**Tom:** "I don't care how many times he can come back. I don't want him to die."

_Interview concluded._

### Assessment

Tom's repeated distress is consistent with his attachment to Jake and his experience of witnessing the incidents.

His frustration should not be dismissed as excessive simply because Jake subsequently recovers.

C.O.R.E. recommends that personnel focus on preventing further accidents rather than repeatedly asking Tom to accept the resurrections as normal.

Tom is not required to become comfortable with Jake's deaths.

## 7. RESIDENTIAL SAFETY REVIEW

Following the repeated incidents, C.O.R.E. has authorized a review of SAFEHOUSE's ordinary residential safety practices.

The objective is not to restrict Jake's movement unnecessarily. It is to reduce the chance that ordinary childhood mishaps become fatal incidents.

### Immediate Recommendations

**Staircases:** Jake is to be reminded to use handrails and take his time. The elevator should be available when appropriate.

**Corridors:** Running indoors should be discouraged, particularly in occupied residential areas.

**Walking surfaces:** Common areas should be kept clear of avoidable obstructions.

**Supervision:** Personnel and responsible residents should intervene when Jake's behavior presents an obvious risk.

**Tom's welfare:** Tom must not be made solely responsible for preventing Jake's accidents. He is Jake's brother, not a full-time safety officer.

**Clover's welfare:** Clover's assistance is appreciated, but she must not be expected to manage every situation without support.

**Jake's dignity:** Staff should explain safety precautions in a manner appropriate to his age. Humiliating him or treating him as a nuisance is prohibited.

**Resurrection documentation:** Any further naturally occurring event should be recorded without encouraging Jake to reproduce it.

## 8. THE CAKE PROBLEM

C.O.R.E. would ordinarily consider this section unnecessary.

Unfortunately, the repeated incident records have made it relevant.

Following multiple resurrections, Jake has consistently demonstrated an ability to return to ordinary conversation with remarkable speed.

His first concerns frequently involve whether he is allowed to resume his previous activity, whether Tom is still angry, and whether food is available.

Cake has been mentioned on several occasions.

The following exchange occurred after the third documented accidental resurrection.

**Jake:** "So, can I have cake now?"

**Tom:** "You JUST DIED."

**Jake:** "And came back!"

**Tom:** "THAT IS NOT A REASON TO HAVE CAKE!"

**Jake:** "It's a reason to celebrate!"

**Tom:** "IT IS A REASON TO SIT DOWN AND STOP MOVING!"

**Jake:** "Can I sit down with cake?"

Clover, who had been listening from the nearby sofa, covered her face.

**Clover:** "He's going to keep asking until someone gives him cake."

**Tom:** "THEN WE MUST REMAIN STRONG."

**Jake:** "I can hear you."

**Tom:** "GOOD."

A pause.

**Jake:** "Please?"

Tom looked at Clover.

Clover looked at Tom.

Neither wanted to reward Jake for treating his repeated accidents as an opportunity to negotiate for dessert.

Neither wanted to deny him a small comfort after a frightening experience, either.

**Clover:** "After you've rested."

**Jake:** "YES!"

**Tom:** "Clover!"

**Clover:** "He needs to rest, Tom. Cake can wait until afterward."

Tom sighed.

**Tom:** "Fine. But he's not allowed to run to the kitchen."

**Jake:** "I'll walk!"

**Tom:** "SLOWLY."

**Jake:** "SLOWLY!"

**C.O.R.E. observation:** The cake was served after Jake rested. Jake did not run to the kitchen.

This was recorded as the most successful safety outcome of the day.

## 9. FINAL DETERMINATION

Jake Akana has now demonstrated repeated resurrection following multiple accidental deaths.

The consistency of the white-fire phenomenon and his subsequent recovery supports the conclusion that Phoenix-derived Transcendence is active and capable of recurring resurrection.

The repeated incidents also demonstrate that Jake's possession of this ability has not eliminated his vulnerability to accidents occurring in the first place.

His clumsiness, impulsiveness, and tendency to become distracted remain relevant safety concerns.

Tom's distress is substantial and must be taken seriously. Clover's assistance has been valuable, but neither individual should bear sole responsibility for preventing further incidents.

C.O.R.E. therefore establishes the following conclusions:

1. Jake's resurrection capability is repeatable under the circumstances observed.
    
2. The ultimate limits of the capability remain unknown.
    
3. The deaths documented in this report were accidental, not deliberate demonstrations.
    
4. Future accidents must be prevented wherever reasonably possible.
    
5. Jake's ability to resurrect does not diminish the seriousness of the injuries or the distress experienced by those who witness them.
    
6. Tom must not be expected to accept repeated deaths as an ordinary feature of his relationship with Jake.
    

**Final assessment:** Jake can apparently come back from death. C.O.R.E.'s priority is to ensure that he does not have to keep proving it.

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The first time Jake died, everyone was frightened.

The second time, everyone was frightened again.

By the third time, the staff had begun documenting the white fire, Tom had begun questioning every staircase in SAFEHOUSE, and Clover had apparently accepted that supervising Jake required an entirely new category of patience.

Jake, for his part, wanted cake.

We must be careful not to let repetition turn something terrible into something trivial.

To Jake, the accidents may seem like brief interruptions. He wakes up, discovers that he is fine, and wants to return to whatever he was doing.

To Tom, each incident is another moment in which his brother disappears before his eyes.

Both experiences are real.

Jake deserves to grow up without being treated as a walking disaster. Tom deserves to love his brother without having to watch him die over something as ordinary as missing a step.

And Clover deserves considerably more credit for keeping the peace than the incident logs currently provide.

The solution is not to teach Jake that death does not matter.

The solution is to help him understand that being able to return does not mean he should stop taking care of himself.

**Resurrection may give Jake another chance. It does not mean he should have to spend his childhood using those chances to survive the staircase.**

C.O.R.E. recommends that the next successful demonstration of Jake's resurrection be the one that never needs to happen.

And, for the love of everything in this facility, someone please hold his hand when he uses the stairs.
