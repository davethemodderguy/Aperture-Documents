# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A particle accelerator project previously placed on indefinite hold has been completed.

The project had remained stalled for approximately:

> **FIVE YEARS.**

The original project was considered technically viable but had repeatedly encountered engineering, calibration, and resource problems.

It was eventually shelved.

The project remained untouched for years.

Until Jake found it.

Jake has now completed it.

This would already be notable under ordinary circumstances.

It is considerably more notable given Jake's current medical condition.

Jake remains:

- extremely sickly;
    
- chronically exhausted;
    
- physically weak;
    
- unable to speak;
    
- severely low-energy;
    
- and visibly sluggish.
    

Despite this, he independently reviewed the incomplete project, identified the remaining problems, and completed the work.

---

# 1. PROJECT HISTORY

The accelerator was originally initiated approximately five years ago.

Its intended purpose was to provide a controlled platform for high-energy particle research within Aperture's anomalous research programs.

The project was never abandoned due to a lack of scientific value.

It simply stopped progressing.

Repeated development issues accumulated.

Personnel were reassigned.

Other projects received priority.

Several components became obsolete.

Documentation became fragmented.

Eventually, the accelerator was marked:

> **ON HOLD — INDEFINITE**

The project remained in that state for five years.

---

# 2. JAKE'S INVOLVEMENT

Jake discovered the project during one of his infrequent movements through the Research, Development & Engineering complex.

He apparently became interested after noticing the unfinished documentation.

There was initially no indication that he intended to complete it.

He simply began reading.

Then he returned the following day.

Then again.

And again.

Jake's activity remained extremely limited.

He did not spend long periods standing.

He frequently worked while seated.

At several points, he reportedly fell asleep beside the documentation.

He would wake up.

Read another section.

Make a correction.

Rest again.

---

# 3. THE FIVE-YEAR STALL

The original project documentation contained numerous unresolved problems.

Jake identified several of them without assistance.

His notes were unusually concise.

For example:

> **“This doesn't match the later calibration.”**

Another:

> **“Wrong assumption.”**

And, beside an especially old engineering note:

> **“Why did nobody change this?”**

No answer has been located.

---

# 4. NERİ'S OBSERVATION

Neri encountered Jake in the research area.

He was sitting at a workstation.

His head was tilted slightly to one side.

His glow was barely visible.

The teddy bear was resting against his chest.

Neri looked at the screen.

Then at Jake.

> **NERI:**  
> “What are you doing?”

Jake pointed toward the accelerator documentation.

Neri looked at it.

Then looked back at him.

> **NERI:**  
> “That project?”

Jake nodded.

Neri looked at the documentation again.

Five seconds passed.

> **NERI:**  
> “It's been stalled for five years.”

Jake nodded.

Neri looked at him.

Then at the documentation.

Then back at him.

> **NERI:**  
> “You're sick.”

Jake nodded.

> **NERI:**  
> “And you're working on that.”

Jake nodded again.

Neri stared at him.

Then quietly pulled a chair closer.

She sat down.

---

# 5. NEKO'S DISCOVERY

Neko initially assumed someone had accidentally modified the project files.

He opened the archived directory.

The project status still read:

> **ON HOLD — INDEFINITE**

Then he noticed the recent modifications.

The timestamps were current.

The changes were extensive.

Neko reviewed them.

Then reviewed them again.

Then checked the author information.

**JAKE.**

Neko reportedly remained silent for approximately twenty seconds.

He then opened the engineering logs.

The five-year-old project was no longer stalled.

---

# 6. ENGINEERING REVIEW

Neko conducted a full review before allowing the project to proceed.

The results were unexpected.

Jake had not simply filled in missing documentation.

He had:

- reconciled conflicting specifications;
    
- corrected outdated assumptions;
    
- reorganized portions of the control architecture;
    
- identified several inconsistencies in the original calculations;
    
- updated obsolete interface requirements;
    
- restored missing project dependencies;
    
- and completed the remaining engineering documentation.
    

No unauthorized external systems were accessed.

No restricted information was copied.

No security controls were bypassed.

The project was completed entirely within approved Aperture infrastructure.

---

# 7. THE FINAL PROBLEM

One major problem remained.

The accelerator's original design contained a persistent synchronization issue.

Previous researchers had attempted multiple solutions.

None had been accepted.

Jake examined the problem for several minutes.

He then wrote a single note.

> **“You're synchronizing the wrong reference.”**

He changed the reference model.

The problem disappeared.

Neko stared at the result.

> **NEKO:**  
> “...That's it?”

Jake nodded.

Neko looked at him.

> **NEKO:**  
> “You've been sitting here for three days.”

Jake shrugged.

Then rested his head against the back of the chair.

---

# 8. MEDICAL INTERRUPTION

Jake's work was temporarily suspended when medical personnel discovered that he had fallen asleep at the workstation.

He was not injured.

He was simply exhausted.

The screen was still displaying the accelerator control architecture.

His teddy bear was tucked beneath one arm.

His head was tilted against the chair.

His glow was barely visible.

Medical personnel woke him gently.

Jake looked at the screen.

Then at them.

He pointed toward the project.

They told him:

> “It can wait.”

Jake reluctantly nodded.

He was taken to rest.

---

# 9. COMPLETION

The final project review was completed after Jake returned.

The accelerator documentation was marked:

> **ENGINEERING COMPLETE**

The five-year-old project status was changed from:

> **ON HOLD — INDEFINITE**

to:

> **READY FOR CONTROLLED TESTING**

The physical accelerator itself remains subject to the standard C.O.R.E. testing and safety process.

Completion of the engineering project does **not** authorize operation.

---

# 10. JAKE'S RESPONSE

When Neko informed Jake that the project had passed engineering review, Jake simply looked at him.

Neko said:

> **NEKO:**  
> “You finished it.”

Jake nodded.

> **NEKO:**  
> “The entire project.”

Jake nodded again.

Neko smiled.

> **NEKO:**  
> “That's impressive.”

Jake stared at him.

Then slowly leaned sideways.

Neko caught him before he slipped from the chair.

Jake had fallen asleep.

---

# 11. WELFARE CONSIDERATION

C.O.R.E. is explicitly documenting this event because the achievement must **not** be interpreted as evidence that Jake has recovered.

He has not.

Jake's ability to perform complex intellectual work does not mean that his physical condition is stable.

He can still:

- become exhausted rapidly;
    
- fall asleep without warning;
    
- require assistance moving;
    
- become too weak to continue;
    
- and fail to request help.
    

Personnel are therefore prohibited from using the accelerator project as justification for increasing Jake's workload.

The conclusion:

> **“Jake can work.”**

does not mean:

> **“Jake should work continuously.”**

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

There is something almost absurd about the situation.

Jake spent five weeks outside in a blizzard.

He survived on canned tomato soup.

He came back unable to speak.

His glow almost gone.

His teddy bear's heartbeat barely audible.

His head permanently tilted slightly to one side.

He is exhausted almost constantly.

He barely moves.

He needs people to watch him because he will not ask for help.

And somehow—

he looked at a particle accelerator that had defeated an engineering team for five years and decided:

> **“I'll fix it.”**

He did.

Then he fell asleep.

Neko has recommended that Jake be prohibited from touching the project again until he has recovered sufficiently.

Jake has not agreed.

He simply pointed at the completed documentation.

Then pointed at himself.

Then at the cafeteria.

Neko understood.

Jake wanted food.

The accelerator could wait.

For once,

**Jake could too.**
