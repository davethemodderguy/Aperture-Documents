# 1. OVERVIEW

The Tachyon Industries Merch Store has developed an unexpected customer base.

Personnel who do **not** own Tachyon Industries Limited Merchandise have nevertheless become regular visitors.

Some visit to inspect the display wall.

Some visit to ask questions.

Some visit because Nora is operating the counter.

Some visit simply because they like looking at rare equipment.

And a surprisingly large number purchase:

> **USER MANUALS**

This has become the store's most consistently successful product category.

---

# 2. THE DISPLAY WALL

The five functioning display platforms remain the primary attraction:

- **Night Stalker**
    
- **Sleight of Hand**
    
- **Ceberus**
    
- **Deadeye**
    
- **FSR Deadbolt**
    

Personnel frequently stop in front of the display wall despite having no ownership authorization for any of the displayed merchandise.

A common conversation is:

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> “Can I look?”

> **NORA:**  
> “Sure.”

The employee examines the display.

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> “I don't own any of these.”

> **NORA:**  
> “Okay.”

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> “Can I still look?”

> **NORA:**  
> “That's what the window is for.”

The employee remains for another fifteen minutes.

---

# 3. THE MANUAL PROBLEM

Tachyon Industries originally produced user manuals primarily for owners.

They were expected to explain:

- platform characteristics;
    
- controls;
    
- compatible configurations;
    
- approved accessories;
    
- maintenance information;
    
- ammunition compatibility;
    
- safety warnings;
    
- configuration references;
    
- and general operating information.
    

The manuals were not expected to become popular among people who did not own the corresponding merchandise.

They did.

---

# 4. BEST-SELLING PRODUCT

Current sales data indicates that **Tachyon Industries User Manuals** consistently rank among the store's best-selling items.

This is particularly strange because many purchasers do not own the merchandise described in the manual.

Some do not even intend to acquire it.

When asked why they purchased a manual, responses included:

> **“It's interesting.”**

> **“I like reading technical documentation.”**

> **“The diagrams are cool.”**

> **“I want to know how it works.”**

> **“I don't own one, but maybe someday.”**

> **“Nora said there were five versions.”**

And, most commonly:

> **“Have you seen how thick the Deadbolt manual is?”**

---

# 5. THE DEADLOCK MANUAL

The **FSR Deadbolt User Manual** has become particularly popular.

The manual covers the Deadbolt platform and its officially supported configurations.

Personnel who do not own an FSR Deadbolt have nevertheless purchased copies.

One employee was asked why.

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> “I don't have a Deadbolt.”

> **STORE STAFF:**  
> “Then why did you buy the manual?”

The employee held up the book.

> **EMPLOYEE:**  
> “Because it's the Deadbolt.”

No further explanation was provided.

---

# 6. NORA'S OBSERVATION

Nora appears to understand the phenomenon better than most adults.

When asked why people buy manuals without owning the merchandise:

> **NORA:**  
> “They like it.”

> **STAFF:**  
> “The manuals?”

Nora nodded.

> **NORA:**  
> “People like books.”

She then returned to her seat.

C.O.R.E. considers this explanation annoyingly plausible.

---

# 7. MANUALS AS COLLECTIBLES

A secondary market has effectively developed inside SAFEHOUSE.

Some personnel now collect Tachyon Industries manuals as documentation of the Limited Merchandise program.

Collections have been observed containing:

- Night Stalker manual;
    
- Sleight of Hand manual;
    
- Ceberus manual;
    
- Deadeye manual;
    
- FSR Deadbolt manual;
    
- accessory catalogs;
    
- configuration supplements;
    
- limited-edition documentation.
    

Several collectors own the manuals despite owning **none** of the associated equipment.

One collector reportedly stated:

> **“I'm collecting the documentation because I can't collect the guns.”**

C.O.R.E. considers this a reasonable explanation.

# 9. THE "I DON'T OWN ONE" CUSTOMER

This has become common enough that the store has developed an unofficial customer category.

> **NON-OWNER ENTHUSIAST**

Definition:

> Personnel who possess no Tachyon Industries Limited Merchandise but demonstrate significant interest in its documentation, accessories, display pieces, or history.

They may purchase:

- manuals;
    
- catalogs;
    
- cleaning kits where authorized;
    
- display accessories;
    
- Tachyon Industries merchandise cases;
    
- commemorative materials;
    
- compatible non-weapon accessories.
    

They may also simply stand in front of the display wall.

For extended periods.

# 12. STORE ECONOMICS

The unexpected popularity of manuals has created a peculiar retail situation.

The store's rarest products remain extremely difficult to obtain.

The manuals, meanwhile, are comparatively accessible.

As a result:

> **Actual Tachyon equipment:**  
> Extremely limited.

> **Tachyon documentation:**  
> Everywhere.

One employee summarized the situation as:

> **“I can't own the Deadbolt, but I can own the book explaining the Deadbolt.”**

The statement was subsequently added to the store's unofficial marketing board.

---

# 13. CURRENT CUSTOMER PATTERN

The typical visitor now follows one of three paths.

### PATH A — OWNER

Enters → checks accessories → purchases compatible equipment → leaves.

### PATH B — ENTHUSIAST

Enters → stares at display wall → asks seventeen questions → buys manual → leaves.

### PATH C — NORA VISITOR

Enters → talks to Nora → gets distracted → looks at merchandise → buys manual → stays another thirty minutes.

Path C is currently the most common.

# C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

The Tachyon Merch Store was created to provide accessories for people who owned Tachyon Industries Limited Merchandise.

It has instead become a small museum, retail counter, technical library, and informal gathering point.

The strangest part is the manuals.

People who don't own a Night Stalker buy the Night Stalker manual.

People who don't own a Ceberus buy the Ceberus manual.

People who don't own a Sleight of Hand buy the Sleight of Hand manual.

People who certainly do not own one of the **two FSR Deadbolts** still buy the Deadbolt manual.

When asked why, the answer is remarkably consistent:

> **“It's interesting.”**

Apparently, Tachyon Industries accidentally discovered that people will collect the documentation for something they cannot own.

Nora has begun recommending manuals to visitors.

Her current sales pitch is:

> **“You don't need the gun. You can read the book.”**

Sales have increased.

Neri has not been informed that Nora has become a successful salesperson.

C.O.R.E. recommends keeping it that way.
