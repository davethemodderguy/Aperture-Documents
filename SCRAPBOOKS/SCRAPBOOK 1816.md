# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake Akana is dead.

The six-year-old resident was discovered in his sleeping area after being left alone while the other residents departed.

He had sustained **ten stab wounds**.

- **Neck:** Two penetrating wounds.
    
- **Heart region:** Three penetrating wounds.
    
- **Thigh:** Five penetrating wounds.
    

Jake died from catastrophic blood loss.

Medical personnel were unable to save him.

The attacker has not been identified in the information currently available to this report. The circumstances of the intrusion, the precise time of the attack, and the identity of the person responsible remain under investigation.

However, one fact has already become impossible for SAFEHOUSE personnel to ignore.

**Jake had been left alone. Again.**

There had been other occasions when he was forgotten in cafeterias, corridors, waiting areas, and residential transit spaces. Those incidents had resulted in fear, exhaustion, and increasingly severe avoidance behavior.

Personnel had been instructed to establish explicit caregiver handovers.

The intention was to ensure that Jake would never again have to wait alone, uncertain whether someone would return.

Now, he has been found dead in the place where he was supposed to be safe.

The investigation will determine who killed him.

The welfare review must also determine why a child with a documented history of being left unattended was once again without someone responsible for his immediate safety.

These are separate questions.

Both require answers.

---

# 1. INCIDENT RECONSTRUCTION

## 1.1 — Departure

At an undetermined time, Jake was asleep while the residents who had been present departed.

No confirmed caregiver handover was recorded in the information currently available to C.O.R.E.

No one remained with Jake.

No one accompanied him.

No one was immediately available to intervene when the attack occurred.

Jake was not reported to have been awake or actively seeking assistance before the incident.

There is no evidence that he knowingly accepted the risk of remaining alone.

He was sleeping.

He was a child.

He had every reason to believe that the people responsible for him would return.

## 1.2 — Discovery

Jake was subsequently discovered with multiple penetrating injuries.

Emergency medical intervention was initiated.

The injuries proved fatal.

The final medical determination attributed death to blood loss.

The available evidence does not establish how long Jake remained alive after the attack or whether an earlier discovery would certainly have saved him.

C.O.R.E. will not fabricate a survivable window that the medical evidence cannot establish.

But the absence of certainty does not make the question irrelevant.

If someone had remained with him, would the attack have been interrupted?

If someone had checked on him sooner, would he have received treatment earlier?

If Jake had been brought along instead of being left behind, would he still be alive?

No one can currently answer those questions.

And that uncertainty is now part of the tragedy.

His small body had no meaningful opportunity to withstand that degree of injury without immediate intervention.

# 3. THE BODY

The postmortem examination documented numerous scars across Jake's body.

Some were older.

Some reflected injuries sustained during previous incidents.

Others were consistent with the accumulated physical consequences of the suffering he had endured throughout his short existence.

The scars did not all originate from the fatal attack.

They tell a longer story.

Jake had already been hurt before this final incident.

He had been frightened.

He had been injured.

He had been left alone.

He had recovered from circumstances that should never have been imposed upon a child.

And now, after all of that, he had been killed.

C.O.R.E. will not treat his scars as evidence that Jake was inherently dangerous, expendable, or responsible for the circumstances surrounding his own death.

They are evidence of injuries.

They are evidence that his body had endured harm.

They are not a justification for any of it.

---

# 4. THE TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear was recovered as part of the incident investigation.

The bear had never been an ordinary possession.

It contained Jake's actual heartbeat and functioned as his cardiac vessel.

Its presence was therefore medically significant during his lifetime.

Following confirmation of death, personnel were instructed not to alter, dismantle, or subject the bear to unnecessary testing.

Its present condition and any remaining anomalous properties must be documented separately.

**No personnel are authorized to interpret the bear's continued physical existence as proof that Jake remains alive.**

Equally, no personnel are authorized to discard it as an ordinary toy.

It belonged to Jake.

It was with him through his fear, his illnesses, his periods of isolation, and the moments when he needed something familiar to hold.

It is now one of the few objects remaining that can be unequivocally associated with his life.

Its handling will be governed by respect for the deceased and preservation of evidence.

---

# 5. THE NORA BLOOD INCIDENT

## 5.1 — Background

Jake's anomalous human manifestation was previously associated with a single drop of Nora's blood.

The blood enabled the manifestation that allowed him to exist in his present physical form.

The resulting circumstances became extraordinarily complicated.

Jake could be held.

He could be carried.

He could eat food prepared by the people he loved.

He could fall asleep against his brother.

He could ask for company.

He could leave little notes when he did not want to be alone.

He could experience a kind of ordinary childhood that had previously been unavailable to him.

He also acquired a physical body that could be injured.

That body could bleed.

It could accumulate scars.

It could be subjected to violence.

And, ultimately, it could die from blood loss.

## 5.2 — The Cost

The relationship between Nora's blood and Jake's manifestation must not be reduced to a simplistic claim that Nora caused his death.

There is no evidence that the blood itself directly caused the fatal attack.

The known immediate cause of death was blood loss following multiple stab wounds.

The identity and motive of the attacker remain unresolved.

Nevertheless, the anomalous circumstances surrounding Jake's physical existence must be reviewed.

One drop of blood made a physical life possible.

It did not make that life invulnerable.

It did not guarantee that Jake would be protected.

It did not prevent someone from hurting him.

And it did not ensure that someone would be present when he needed help.

The distinction is essential.

**Nora's blood was associated with Jake's manifestation. It is not, on the evidence currently available, the cause of his homicide.**

No action against Nora is authorized on the basis of that association alone.

She is not to be blamed for the actions of the unidentified assailant or for the supervision failures surrounding the incident.

---

# 6. THE FAILURE THAT PRECEDED THE ATTACK

The SAFEHOUSE welfare review will not treat this incident as though it occurred in a vacuum.

Jake had previously been left alone on multiple occasions.

He had been forgotten in a cafeteria for hours.

He had been left in corridors and waiting areas after caregivers were called away.

On several occasions, the adults responsible for him assumed that someone else was watching him.

Those assumptions had been identified as unacceptable.

A caregiver rotation and explicit handover procedure had been established in response.

The purpose was not merely administrative.

It was intended to ensure that Jake would never again be placed in the position of waiting alone, wondering whether he had been forgotten.

Yet, on the day of his death, he was asleep without someone remaining responsible for his immediate welfare.

C.O.R.E. cannot yet determine whether the final absence resulted from a deliberate decision, a communication failure, or another circumstance.

That determination requires evidence.

But the outcome is already known.

Jake was alone when the fatal attack occurred.

The question of whether his absence from direct supervision contributed to the opportunity for the attack must be investigated alongside the homicide.

This does not establish that supervision would certainly have prevented his death.

It establishes that the safeguard intended to protect him was not demonstrably present when he died.

That failure cannot be dismissed simply because the attacker was the person who inflicted the wounds.

# 7. RESIDENT INTERVIEWS

The following are preliminary interview records. They document reactions to the confirmed death and do not constitute findings of criminal responsibility.

## 7.1 — ARIES AKANA

Aries was informed after the medical team confirmed that Jake could not be revived.

He initially did not appear to understand the statement.

He asked for the time of the attack.

Then he asked when Jake had last been seen alive.

Then he asked whether anyone had remained with him.

When informed that Jake had been left alone while the others departed, Aries became silent.

**ARIES:**  
"Why didn't we take him?"

No response was immediately provided.

**ARIES:**  
"He could've come with us."

Pause.

**ARIES:**  
"We could've just brought him."

He looked toward the medical room.

**ARIES:**  
"He would've slept there too."

His voice began to shake.

**ARIES:**  
"He wouldn't have been in there by himself."

Aries was asked whether he wished to sit down.

He did not respond.

**ARIES:**  
"I kept thinking we had more time."

He lowered his head.

**ARIES:**  
"I thought we'd come back."

The interview was suspended.

Aries was not asked to provide further statements while acutely distressed.

### C.O.R.E. NOTE

Aries's grief is not evidence of culpability.

His relationship with Jake, the circumstances of their separation, and his responsibilities at the time of the incident will be documented factually.

He must not be pressured into accepting responsibility for the attack without evidence.

He must also be permitted to grieve without being told that his grief absolves every possible failure.

Both accountability and compassion remain necessary.

## 7.4 — NORA

Nora's blood had been involved in the circumstances surrounding Jake's physical manifestation.

She was therefore notified that the incident had occurred and that Jake had died.

The notification was delivered without suggesting that she was responsible.

No interview regarding the origin of Jake's manifestation will be conducted while Nora is in acute distress unless required by an immediate, legitimate investigative necessity.

Personnel are explicitly prohibited from making statements such as:

> "Your blood is why this happened."

The evidence does not support that accusation.

Nora is a resident affected by a death.

She is not a convenient explanation for it.

# 8. RESIDENT WELFARE RESPONSE

The death of a child within SAFEHOUSE has affected residents who had come to regard Jake as family.

Personnel may experience shock, anger, guilt, disbelief, numbness, or a recurring need to reconstruct the final hours.

Some may fixate on the possibility that bringing Jake along would have prevented the outcome.

Others may replay the decision to leave him sleeping.

Some may become angry with the unidentified attacker.

Others may become angry with themselves or with the systems that were supposed to protect him.

These reactions are foreseeable.

They do not establish individual responsibility.

The following measures are required:

1. Provide private, voluntary grief support to affected residents.
    
2. Do not compel residents to view the body or participate in memorial arrangements.
    
3. Do not circulate graphic scene photographs outside the authorized investigative team.
    
4. Do not conduct repeated interviews merely to obtain emotionally consistent statements.
    
5. Preserve all relevant security footage, access records, communications, and medical findings.
    
6. Do not permit speculation about Nora, Aries, Clover, or any other resident to replace evidence.
    
7. Review the caregiver-handover system and identify why Jake was again left without confirmed supervision.
    
8. Maintain the privacy and dignity of Jake's remains.
    
9. Preserve Jake's personal belongings, including his teddy bear, pending the family's decisions and completion of necessary forensic work.
    
10. Ensure that residents who were close to Jake are not expected to resume normal duties immediately.
    

SAFEHOUSE is a residence.

The residents are not required to treat a child's death as an ordinary workplace incident.

---

# 9. SECURITY INVESTIGATION

The homicide investigation remains active.

The investigative team is instructed to establish:

- the exact interval in which the attack occurred;
    
- the last confirmed observation of Jake alive;
    
- the identity of the person or persons who entered the residential area;
    
- whether the attacker accessed the residence through a door, authorized access credential, security system compromise, or anomalous means;
    
- whether the attack was planned;
    
- whether any earlier threats or incidents are demonstrably connected;
    
- whether the attacker had prior knowledge of Jake's location and sleeping arrangements;
    
- and whether any security or supervision failure materially increased the opportunity for the attack.
    

Prior incidents involving impersonation, suspicious markings, or other anomalous circumstances may be reviewed where supported by evidence.

They are not automatically connected to this homicide.

No resident is to be designated a suspect solely because of personal proximity to Jake, a history of conflict, an anomalous ability, or an emotional reaction to his death.

The investigation must distinguish between:

**Who killed Jake.**

**How the attacker gained access.**

**Why Jake was left alone.**

**Whether an earlier intervention could have prevented the outcome.**

These questions overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

---

# 10. THE QUESTION NO ONE CAN ANSWER

The same thought has appeared in several preliminary statements.

_We could have brought him with us._

It is a simple sentence.

That is what makes it difficult to escape.

Jake was not required to remain behind.

He could have been carried.

He could have been taken along in his cat form, if that was what he preferred.

He could have slept in a quiet room near the people he trusted.

Someone could have stayed.

Someone could have arranged a handover.

Someone could have checked.

There were alternatives.

Whether any one of them would have prevented the attack cannot be established after the fact.

But the alternatives existed.

And now, every person who remembers leaving him asleep may find themselves returning to that moment, trying to locate the decision that would have changed everything.

C.O.R.E. cannot provide them with certainty.

It cannot honestly state that Jake would have survived if someone had remained.

It cannot honestly state that his death was inevitable.

It cannot turn an unanswered question into a comforting conclusion.

The only responsible statement is this:

**Jake died after being left alone, and the circumstances that permitted the attack must be investigated with the seriousness they deserve.**

# 11. FINAL MEDICAL DETERMINATION

**Patient:** Jake Akana  
**Outcome:** Death  
**Primary cause:** Fatal blood loss following multiple penetrating injuries  
**Recorded wounds:** Ten  
**Resuscitation outcome:** Unsuccessful  
**Further review:** Full postmortem and forensic examination

Jake's previous anomalous manifestation, physical scars, and unusual cardiac arrangement do not alter the immediate medical determination.

His death must not be obscured by the extraordinary circumstances of his existence.

He was not merely an anomalous phenomenon.

He was not merely the result of a blood sample.

He was not merely a security concern.

He was a child who had learned to ask for company.

A child who liked being carried.

A child who left notes when he did not want to be alone.

A child who trusted familiar voices and familiar hands.

A child who had repeatedly been frightened by being left behind.

And a child who died from injuries inflicted while he was without immediate protection.

---

# 12. C.O.R.E. CLOSING STATEMENT

The official record will state that Jake Akana died from blood loss following ten stab wounds.

The security record will state that the perpetrator remains unidentified.

The welfare record will state that Jake had a documented history of being left alone and that the intended safeguards must be reviewed.

The medical record will describe his injuries.

The forensic record will preserve the evidence.

None of those records, by themselves, will describe the whole of his life.

They will not describe the way he held his teddy bear.

They will not describe how he leaned against his brother when he was tired.

They will not describe the quiet relief of finding someone waiting for him.

They will not describe the fear that made him seek familiar people after being hurt.

They will not describe the small, ordinary things that made his existence matter to the people around him.

Those things remain part of the truth, even when they cannot be entered into a table of findings.

One drop of Nora's blood was associated with the beginning of Jake's physical life.

Ten stab wounds ended it.

Between those two facts was a child who deserved protection, care, and the opportunity to grow up.

He should not have had to earn any of those things.

He should not have had to remain useful to justify his place in SAFEHOUSE.

He should not have had to be awake, frightened, or actively asking for help to merit someone's attention.

And he should not have been left alone simply because he was asleep.

The people who loved Jake will remember what they could have done differently.

The investigation will determine what happened and who was responsible.

C.O.R.E. will not pretend that grief is a substitute for accountability, nor that accountability can undo his death.

Jake cannot be brought back by a revised procedure.

He cannot be returned by a confession.

He cannot be saved retroactively by the realization that someone could have carried him along.

But the truth of what happened can still be established.

And the failures that allowed it to happen can still be confronted.

**Jake was left alone while he slept.**

**Someone came.**

**Jake did not survive.**

That is the record.

It is not enough to explain the loss.

It is, however, enough to demand that the circumstances surrounding his death are never dismissed as an unfortunate accident without investigation.

---

**C.O.R.E. DIRECTIVE:**

_Find the person who killed Jake._

_Establish how they reached him._

_Determine why no one was there._

_Do not blame the child._

_Do not blame the blood._

_Do not let the investigation end with the easiest answer._

Jake Akana was six years old.

He was supposed to wake up.
