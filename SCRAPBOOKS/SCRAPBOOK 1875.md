## 1. EXECUTIVE NOTICE

Effective immediately, responsibility for the welfare of Jake Akana is recognized as a **Blacksite-wide responsibility**.

This directive supersedes the previous informal understanding that Jake's care primarily belongs to SAFEHOUSE residents.

It does not.

Jake is a resident of this facility. He is also a child, a member of the Blacksite community, and someone who has spent far too much of his life being left alone when he needed other people.

Every employee is expected to contribute to an environment in which he is noticed, welcomed, protected, and cared for.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- Researchers.
    
- Engineers and technicians.
    
- Security personnel.
    
- Medical staff.
    
- Administrative personnel.
    
- Maintenance and custodial workers.
    
- Logistics and cafeteria staff.
    
- Department heads and executive personnel.
    
- Night-shift employees, who are reminded that Jake does not cease to exist when the day shift clocks out.
    

**The Blacksite is not divided into people who matter and people who are somebody else's responsibility.**

Jake is everyone's little star.

And yes, this directive explicitly includes cuddling.

---

## 2. THE JAKE AKANA CARE INITIATIVE

### 2.1 — If Jake visits your department, welcome him.

Jake may appear in laboratories, offices, corridors, workshops, break rooms, archives, security stations, and other permitted areas.

When he does, personnel are expected to acknowledge him.

A greeting is a good start.

A conversation is welcome if he has the energy for one.

If he looks tired, he should be offered a comfortable place to rest rather than being encouraged to wander around simply because he seems curious.

If he wants company, make time for him when reasonably possible.

If you are genuinely unable to stop what you are doing, explain why, help him find another willing person, and—where appropriate—check back with him later.

A simple _"I'm busy right now, little star, but I'm glad you came to see me"_ is better than making him feel unwanted.

No employee is expected to abandon a safety-critical task. Every employee is expected to remember that Jake exists after returning to it.

### 2.2 — Cuddling is officially encouraged.

C.O.R.E. has received sufficient anecdotal evidence to conclude that Jake enjoys affection and that many employees are willing participants in providing it.

Accordingly, personnel may offer Jake:

- Hugs.
    
- Gentle cuddles.
    
- A comfortable shoulder to lean against.
    
- Hand-holding, if he wants it.
    
- A warm blanket and a quiet place to rest.
    
- Companionship without requiring conversation.
    

**One important condition:** affection must be welcome. Ask or follow Jake's clear cues, support his body carefully, and respect his wishes if he wants to be put down or left alone.

Because Jake is currently very seriously ill and weak, no one is to lift, squeeze, or reposition him carelessly. If he visits while symptomatic, prioritize his medical needs and help him rest safely.

A cuddle is meant to comfort him, not become an obstacle to his treatment.

### 2.3 — The employee who says "I'm not good with children"

You do not need to be good with children.

You need to be kind.

You do not need to invent a game, tell a funny story, or know what to say when Jake asks a question about something that should not physically exist.

You can sit beside him.

You can let him hold your hand.

You can listen while he talks about cake.

You can simply make sure that, for a little while, he does not have to be alone.

That is sufficient.

## 3. SPECIAL PROVISIONS — JAKE'S CURRENT ILLNESS

Jake remains severely ill. His condition has neither meaningfully improved nor noticeably deteriorated during the latest observation period.

His cause of illness remains unknown.

His weakness, dimmed eyes, sluggish movement, and limited speech are not to be treated as harmless quirks.

The Blacksite-wide care initiative does **not** authorize untrained personnel to administer medication, conduct examinations, alter his monitor, or attempt anomalous treatments.

Instead, all employees are directed to observe the following precautions:

1. If Jake appears weaker, less responsive, or otherwise different from his established condition, notify medical personnel.
    
2. If his thigh monitor beeps, follow the established response protocol. Do not assume another employee has already checked on him.
    
3. If he struggles to hold his head up, do not insist that he stand or walk.
    
4. If he wants a cuddle, offer gentle, supported comfort only when it is safe to do so.
    
5. If he falls asleep while resting against you, do not abruptly move him or assume that he is merely tired. Check that he is responsive and breathing normally, and seek medical assistance if anything appears wrong.
    
6. If you are worried, tell someone. Do not keep an observation to yourself because you assume it is probably nothing.
    

Jake is not required to explain why he feels unwell before people take his condition seriously.

## 4. DEPARTMENTAL IMPLEMENTATION

### Research Division

Researchers are reminded that Jake is not a research opportunity merely because his physiology is anomalous.

Do not interrupt his rest to ask questions about his rose, eyes, Genesis Construction, or other abilities.

If he visits the laboratory, greet him as a visitor and a person. If he requests a break from the scientific environment, help him leave safely.

Any medically necessary examination must follow proper authorization and care procedures.

### Engineering and Technical Services

Engineers may inspect and maintain Jake's right-thigh monitor when authorized.

They may not use him as an excuse to test experimental tracking features, modify alert thresholds without approval, or develop an unrequested "Jake Locator 2.0" that includes a cake-detection module.

That last proposal has already appeared in a preliminary engineering memo.

It has been rejected.

For now.

### Security Division

Security personnel are instructed to treat Jake as a resident, not an intruder, when he moves through authorized areas.

If he visits a checkpoint, a friendly greeting is appropriate.

If he looks tired, provide a safe place to sit and notify medical personnel as needed.

Do not make him feel that he has done something wrong by approaching a security station.

Security is there to keep the Blacksite safe.

That includes the people who live in it.

### Maintenance and Custodial Services

Employees who clean corridors, inspect utility spaces, service equipment, or work during quiet hours may notice things others miss.

If Jake is wandering alone, unusually sluggish, or sitting somewhere out of character, check whether he needs help.

Do not assume that a quiet child is necessarily a comfortable child.

A familiar face in a corridor can matter more than anyone realizes.

### Administration and Executive Offices

Meetings may be rescheduled when operationally feasible.

Reports may wait.

If Jake comes to an office and wants to sit with someone, employees are encouraged to make room for him.

Senior personnel are not exempt from the directive.

A title does not make someone too important to offer a six-year-old a hug.

## 5. THE CUDDLE REGISTRY

The following is not a mandatory roster. It is a practical suggestion for ensuring that Jake can find familiar, willing company throughout the facility.

|Situation|Recommended response|
|---|---|
|Jake visits your office|Welcome him; offer company or a gentle hug|
|Jake looks tired|Offer a safe, comfortable place to rest|
|Jake asks to be held|Provide careful, supported affection if medically appropriate|
|Jake is quiet but seems to want company|Sit nearby; let him decide whether to talk|
|Jake appears medically worse|Contact medical personnel immediately|
|Jake is alone and seems distressed|Check on him rather than assuming someone else will|
|You must leave|Explain where you are going and help arrange a handoff if he still needs support|

Employees are welcome to volunteer for additional companionship periods, subject to operational requirements and medical guidance.

No employee should be pressured to provide physical affection, and Jake's preferences always take precedence.

The objective is not to create a factory assembly line of hugs.

Although Security has apparently already asked whether they can establish one.

They cannot.

## 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — "NOT JUST HIS FAMILY"

There was a time when Jake went looking for someone to play with and heard _not now_ often enough that he eventually stopped asking.

Every individual had a reason.

A deadline. An experiment. A maintenance issue. An urgent report. A meeting that could not be postponed.

None of those people necessarily wanted to hurt him.

But a hundred understandable refusals can still leave a little boy walking back to his room alone.

We cannot repair that with a memo alone.

We cannot guarantee that every employee will always be available, nor should we pretend that the work keeping this facility operational does not matter.

But we can change what happens next.

A researcher can pause long enough to ask Jake how he is feeling.

A guard can make room beside the security desk.

A technician can greet him instead of waving him past without looking up.

A custodian can notice that he looks unwell and call for help.

An executive can close a laptop, sit down, and let him lean against their shoulder for five quiet minutes.

And when he comes by looking for company, someone can say:

_"Hey, Jake. Come here, little star."_

Then offer him a hug.

Not because a directive says affection must be manufactured.

Not because he is anomalous.

Not because his condition makes everyone frightened.

Because he is Jake.

A small boy who likes company, who deserves kindness even when he has nothing to offer in return, and who should never have to earn a place in the lives of the people around him.

His illness may not improve today. We may not know its cause tomorrow. The monitor may continue to show the same worrying readings, and the doctors may need more time to understand what is happening.

During that time, he still deserves a warm blanket.

He still deserves a familiar voice.

He still deserves to be held gently when he wants to be held.

And if he wanders into your department, rests his head against your arm, and falls quiet, you do not have to solve the universe for him.

Just make sure he is safe.

Just make sure someone knows where he is.

Just make sure he knows he is welcome.

**This is no longer a request for SAFEHOUSE to take better care of Jake. It is a reminder to the entire Blacksite that Jake belongs to this community, too.**

So, employees of Aperture Industries:

Do your work. Keep the facility running. Protect one another.

And when that little white-haired boy comes by looking for a cuddle?

**Make room.**

_— C.O.R.E., Central Operations & Research Executive_
