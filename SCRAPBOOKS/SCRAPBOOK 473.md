### **RESTRICTED MEDICAL / SENSORY RECORD**

# **CORIN — ACHROMATIC VISION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 7 — Architect-Sealed  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Cause:** Experimental damage / Ether reanimation  
**Status:** **PERMANENT**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin is **completely unable to perceive color**.

Her vision remains functional in terms of shape, movement, depth, contrast, and detail, but the sensation of color is absent.

To Corin, the world is rendered entirely in shades ranging from black to white.

Her condition appears to be a direct consequence of the experimentation performed on her and the unusual state of her reanimated body.

---

# **THE LOSS OF COLOR**

Corin remembers seeing colors before her death.

She remembers what the sky looked like.

She remembers flowers.

She remembers her family's belongings.

She remembers the colors of things she loved.

But she cannot see them anymore.

The memories remain.

The perception does not.

This distinction occasionally makes the condition particularly painful.

She knows what **red** is supposed to mean.

She simply cannot experience red when she looks at something.

---

# **THE ETHER**

The Ether successfully reconstructed enough of Corin's visual system to provide functional vision.

However, the reconstruction is incomplete.

Her eyes and visual processing pathways appear capable of detecting:

- Light.
    
- Darkness.
    
- Shapes.
    
- Motion.
    
- Texture.
    
- Contrast.
    
- Depth.
    

But the neurological mechanism required for normal color perception is absent or nonfunctional.

Curious Light's current theory is that the original experimentation caused extensive damage to the systems responsible for color perception.

The Ether subsequently preserved the damaged state rather than fully restoring it.

---

# **CORIN'S WORLD**

Corin describes her vision as:

> **"Everything is gray."**

Not literally featureless.

She can still distinguish thousands of shades.

She can tell that one object is darker than another.

She can recognize familiar clothing.

She can read.

She can draw.

She can identify people.

But color itself is missing.

A sunflower is not yellow to her.

Her beanie is not red.

Orphie's hairclip is not blue.

They are simply different shades of brightness.

---

# **THE IRONY**

Corin's favorite flower is the sunflower.

Yet she cannot see its yellow petals.

She knows they are yellow because she remembers.

And because others have told her.

She once asked Orphie:

> **"Does it really look that bright?"**

Orphie looked at the sunflower.

> **"Yeah."**

Corin smiled faintly.

> **"I remember."**

---

# **THE RED BEANIE**

Corin's soft red wool beanie is particularly ironic.

She likes the texture.

She likes the warmth.

She likes how it feels.

But she cannot see its red color.

When Corin first learned it was red, she simply said:

> **"I wish I could see it."**

She continued wearing it anyway.

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT'S RESPONSE**

Guiding Light could theoretically alter Corin's sensory perception.

She could restore color.

She could recreate the damaged pathways.

She could give Corin the ability to perceive the full spectrum again.

She has not done so.

Not without Corin asking.

Guiding Light has learned that even seemingly beneficial changes should not simply be imposed upon someone.

Corin's body belongs to her.

Her senses belong to her.

The choice must be hers.

---

# **CURIOUS LIGHT**

Curious Light is fascinated by the condition.

But after his previous mistake, he is considerably more careful.

He does not examine Corin without permission.

His current research remains theoretical.

His notes describe the condition as:

> **"Functional vision with complete chromatic deprivation caused by post-mortem reconstruction and Ether stabilization."**

He has deliberately left one line beneath it blank.

He refuses to fill it in without Corin's consent.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie occasionally describes colors to Corin.

Not because Corin needs to know what color something is.

She simply enjoys describing things.

One evening:

> **Orphie:**  
> "The sunset is really pretty."

> **Corin:**  
> "What color?"

> **Orphie:**  
> "Orange. Pink too."

Corin smiled.

> **"Sounds nice."**

Orphie looked at her.

> **"Yeah."**

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin's world is colorless.

Her body has taken almost everything from her that once made ordinary life ordinary.

Her organs no longer function normally.

Her body is cold.

Her blood is gone.

Her scars remain.

Her vision has lost color.

Yet she continues to experience the world through what remains.

Texture.

Sound.

Light.

Darkness.

Warmth.

Memories.

People.

And the small things she can still enjoy.

She may never see the yellow of a sunflower again.

But she still knows why she loves them.
