### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / PERSONNEL CARE DIVISION**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — MISCOMMUNICATION & REFUSAL OF CARE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – Internal Personnel  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** 6  
**Species:** Full-Body Rabbit Thiren  
**Status:** Active — Medical Supervision Required

---

# **BACKGROUND**

Following Alice's twenty-five-hour exterior exposure and subsequent illness, Corin attempted to speak with her privately.

The conversation was intended to reassure Alice.

It did not.

Corin was exhausted, frightened, and increasingly distressed by Alice's continued belief that she was responsible for the burden placed upon everyone around her.

In an attempt to encourage Alice to stop blaming herself, Corin used a phrase that was poorly chosen.

The exact wording was:

> **"Alice, you can't keep making everyone worry about you."**

Corin intended this to mean:

> _"You are making us worry because we love you, and you need to let us help you."_

Alice understood something entirely different.

---

# **ALICE'S INTERPRETATION**

Alice became very quiet.

Her ears lowered.

She looked toward her hands.

After several seconds, she quietly asked:

> "...Alice make everyone worry?"

Corin immediately attempted to clarify.

> "That's not what I meant."

Alice shook her head.

> "...It's okay."

Corin continued:

> "Alice, listen—"

Alice interrupted softly:

> "...Alice make everyone tired too?"

Corin realized the mistake.

Before she could properly explain, Alice had already withdrawn.

She quietly said:

> "...Sorry."

The conversation ended shortly afterward.

---

# **FOLLOWING DAY**

The next morning, medical personnel attempted to provide Alice with breakfast.

Alice refused.

She pushed the food away.

> "...Don't need."

Water was offered.

Alice shook her head.

> "...Someone else can have it."

Medication was then offered according to her treatment schedule.

Alice again refused.

> "...Alice don't want make everyone worry."

Personnel immediately recognized that the previous conversation had been misinterpreted.

Alice had connected **receiving care** with **causing worry**.

She had therefore reached a dangerous conclusion:

> **If Alice stops needing things, nobody has to worry about Alice.**

---

# **PROGRESSIVE REFUSAL**

Throughout the day, Alice continued refusing:

- Food.
    
- Water.
    
- Prescribed medicine.
    
- Snacks.
    
- Nutritional drinks.
    
- Additional blankets.
    
- Assistance.
    

She did not become angry.

She did not throw anything.

She did not protest.

She simply refused.

When asked why, her response remained consistent:

> "...Alice is okay."

Medical personnel informed her that she was still recovering from illness.

Alice answered:

> "...Alice can wait."

She then looked at her hands.

> "...Alice don't want make everyone tired."

---

# **MEDICAL RESPONSE**

The refusal of fluids and medication was treated as a medical concern.

Alice is already physically weak and was recovering from prolonged exposure.

Extended refusal of hydration and prescribed treatment could significantly worsen her condition.

Medical personnel therefore initiated a calm intervention.

They did not shame Alice.

They did not threaten her.

They did not tell her that she was being difficult.

Instead, they explained:

> "Taking medicine isn't making us tired."

Alice did not respond.

> "Drinking water isn't bothering us."

Silence.

> "Eating isn't bothering us."

Alice finally looked up.

> "...Really?"

The nurse answered:

> "Really."

---

# **CORIN'S REALIZATION**

Corin returned after being informed of Alice's refusal.

She immediately understood what had happened.

Corin sat beside Alice.

She did not begin with an apology.

She began with a correction.

> "Alice."

Alice looked at her.

> "When I said you make everyone worry, I said it wrong."

Alice's ears lowered again.

Corin continued:

> "I didn't mean that you are a problem."

Silence.

> "I meant that we worry because we love you."

Alice stared at her.

> "...But Alice make you tired."

Corin shook her head.

> "Being tired doesn't mean I don't want you here."

Alice remained silent.

Corin continued:

> "And taking care of you isn't something you need to apologize for."

Alice's eyes began to water.

She looked away.

> "...Oh."

---

# **THE MEDICINE**

The medicine was offered again.

Alice looked at it for several seconds.

> "...This make everyone worry?"

Corin answered:

> "No."

> "It helps you get better."

Alice hesitated.

> "...And nobody tired?"

Corin shook her head.

> "Nobody is tired because you took medicine."

Alice slowly accepted it.

Medical personnel remained nearby to ensure that she received it safely.

---

# **FOOD AND WATER**

A small meal was offered afterward.

Alice did not immediately eat.

Instead, she looked at Corin.

> "...Can Alice have this?"

Corin replied:

> "Yes."

> "...Really?"

> "Really."

Alice took a small bite.

Then another.

Water was placed beside her.

After a moment, Alice drank.

The medical team documented the response.

---

# **COMMUNICATION GUIDANCE**

C.O.R.E. has issued additional communication guidance for personnel interacting with Alice.

Avoid ambiguous statements such as:

> "You're making everyone worry."

> "You're making everyone tired."

> "You need to stop doing this to us."

> "Everyone has enough to deal with."

Alice may interpret these literally and self-blamingly.

Preferred language:

> **"We're worried because we care about you."**

> **"You are allowed to need help."**

> **"Taking care of you is not a burden."**

> **"You don't have to handle this alone."**

> **"You are not in trouble."**

The distinction is critical.

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin did not intend to hurt Alice.

She was scared.

She was tired.

She wanted Alice to understand that people were worried because they cared.

But Alice heard something else.

She heard:

> **"Alice is making everyone tired."**

And because Alice already believed she was worthless, that single misunderstanding was enough to make her stop accepting the things keeping her alive and recovering.

Food became something she was not supposed to need.

Water became something she should give to someone else.

Medicine became something she should refuse.

Care became something she believed she had to earn.

Thankfully, the misunderstanding was discovered before Alice's condition deteriorated further.

Corin later remained beside her while Alice finished her meal.

Alice was quiet for most of it.

Near the end, she whispered:

> "...Corin?"

> "Yeah?"

> "...Alice can still stay?"

Corin answered immediately.

> **"Always."**

Alice looked down at her food.

Then quietly:

> "...Okay."

And for the first time that day, Alice ate without apologizing for it.
