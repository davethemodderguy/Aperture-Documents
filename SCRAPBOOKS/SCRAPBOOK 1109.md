# 2. CHALLENGE PARAMETERS

The challenge takes place within the designated outdoor competition zone surrounding the Blacksite.

Participants must remain outside for the duration of the challenge.

The last eligible participant remaining in the competition wins.

### Participants may:

- move around the designated area;
    
- rest;
    
- sleep;
    
- eat;
    
- drink;
    
- cooperate with other participants;
    
- construct temporary shelters;
    
- change locations;
    
- assist other participants;
    
- use ordinary permitted equipment;
    
- leave voluntarily.
    

### Participants may NOT:

- return indoors;
    
- receive prohibited outside assistance;
    
- deliberately sabotage another participant's basic survival;
    
- leave the designated competition boundary;
    
- claim victory before the challenge concludes.
    

A participant who voluntarily returns indoors is considered:

> **ELIMINATED.**

---

# 3. IMPORTANT CLARIFICATION

This is **not** a combat exercise.

Weapons are prohibited from the competition.

The challenge is endurance.

Not marksmanship.

Not CQB.

Not tactical superiority.

Not killing.

Simply:

> **Who can remain outside the longest?**

Neri has explicitly stated:

> **"I don't want anyone getting hurt."**

---

# 4. WEEK 1 CONDITIONS

The first week began under relatively ordinary conditions.

The weather was initially manageable.

Temperatures remained within normal operating limits.

There was no anomalous environmental event.

No hostile entities were introduced.

No hidden enemies appeared.

No surprise combat exercise occurred.

The participants initially believed the challenge would therefore be easy.

This assessment lasted approximately four hours.

---

# 5. PARTICIPANT BEHAVIOR — DAY 1

The first several hours were characterized by confidence.

Several MSAC Operators established positions near the outer perimeter.

Others immediately began constructing makeshift shelters.

One participant reportedly stated:

> "This is the easiest Night Stalker challenge yet."

C.O.R.E. Archivist Note:

> **Incorrect.**

Neri was observed watching from inside the Blacksite.

She was drinking coffee.

She appeared amused.

---

# 6. DAY 2

The first eliminations occurred during the second day.

Several participants voluntarily withdrew after discovering that remaining outside continuously was considerably more difficult than anticipated.

The primary issue was not physical exhaustion.

It was boredom.

MSAC personnel are highly trained for hostile environments.

They are considerably less trained for sitting outside doing absolutely nothing for several days.

One operator wrote:

> "I would rather clear a hostile compound."

Another responded:

> "Don't give her ideas."

---

# 7. DAY 3

The remaining participants began organizing themselves.

Several established a shared camp.

Others remained independent.

This produced an unexpected development.

Participants began assisting one another.

Food was shared.

Shelter materials were exchanged.

One operator repaired another participant's sleeping area.

Another voluntarily remained awake while another slept.

C.O.R.E. noted the behavior.

Neri noted it as well.

---

# 8. NERI'S OBSERVATION

Neri was asked whether cooperation violated the spirit of a "last man standing" challenge.

She shook her head.

When asked whether she expected participants to compete individually, she shook her head again.

She then wrote:

> **"Protect your friends."**

The message was filed under:

**RELEVANT.**

---

# 9. DAY 4–5

Fatigue began accumulating.

The challenge stopped being amusing.

Operators who had initially treated the event as a competition began treating it as an endurance exercise.

Sleep schedules became irregular.

Food became increasingly important.

Participants became increasingly protective of their limited personal belongings.

Several operators began referring to their shelters as:

> **"Home."**

This was followed by an argument over who had the best shelter.

The argument ended when another operator pointed out that everyone was sleeping outside.

The argument resumed anyway.

---

# 10. DAY 6

One participant withdrew.

He had remained outside for approximately six days.

Before returning indoors, he looked toward the Blacksite and stated:

> "I have fought things from other dimensions."

Pause.

> "Why is sleeping outside harder?"

No answer was provided.

---

# 11. DAY 7 — END OF WEEK 1

At the end of the first week, the remaining participants were still outside.

No winner had been declared.

The challenge remains active.

Neri appeared at the perimeter briefly.

She looked over the remaining camps.

She then placed several boxes of supplies nearby.

She did not speak.

An operator asked:

> "Are those for us?"

Neri nodded.

Another asked:

> "Are we allowed to use them?"

Neri nodded again.

A third asked:

> "What's in them?"

Neri walked away.

---

# 12. WEEK 1 ASSESSMENT

The first week has demonstrated that the challenge is considerably different from previous Night Stalker competitions.

There is no obvious technical skill to master.

There is no opponent to defeat.

There is no target to eliminate.

The challenge instead tests:

- endurance;
    
- patience;
    
- adaptability;
    
- resource management;
    
- cooperation;
    
- willingness to continue;
    
- and, perhaps most importantly, the ability to function when there is nothing to do except wait.
    

Several participants have already demonstrated strong cooperation.

C.O.R.E. considers this favorable.
