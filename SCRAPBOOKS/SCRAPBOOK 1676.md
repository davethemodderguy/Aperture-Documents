# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SAFEHOUSE has received a new resident.

Her name is:

> **Clover.**

The name was not originally assigned to her by T.O.P.S.

It was given to her by other personnel after her distinctive eyes became difficult to describe without eventually resorting to the same comparison.

Her pupils are shaped like **four-leaf clovers.**

Her hair is green.

Her eyes are green.

And, somewhat ironically, she has experienced enough misfortune in her life that the visual symbolism has become almost cruel.

Clover is twenty-six years old.

She previously worked as an accountant for **T.O.P.S.**

She later transferred to the **Krampus Compliance Authority**, where she remains affiliated.

Following recent circumstances, Clover has been approved for residence within SAFEHOUSE.

Initial welfare assessments indicate:

- stable physical condition;
    
- functional cognitive capacity;
    
- strong numerical and organizational skills;
    
- considerable artistic interest;
    
- occasional clumsiness;
    
- generally cooperative behavior;
    
- and a persistent tendency to underestimate how much other people care about her.
    

She also appears to possess an unusually complicated relationship with the concept of **luck.**

---

# 1. EARLY CHILDHOOD

Clover's early childhood was reportedly happy.

Her parents cared for her.

She was well fed.

She had a home.

She had toys.

She drew frequently.

Most importantly, she appeared to be extraordinarily lucky.

Small things consistently went in her favor.

She would find lost objects.

She would avoid accidents by coincidence.

She would win small games without appearing to understand how.

Broken objects somehow failed to injure her.

Coincidences repeatedly worked in her favor.

Her parents initially considered this charming.

Clover herself simply considered it normal.

She did not know that she was unusual.

She was a child.

---

# 2. THE ACCIDENT

Clover was six years old when everything changed.

The exact circumstances remain partially restricted.

What is known is sufficient.

An accident occurred.

Her father was killed.

Clover survived.

She was physically unharmed.

Her father did not.

The event fundamentally changed her mother's perception of her.

Instead of seeing Clover as a lucky child, her mother began to believe the opposite.

She became convinced that Clover's presence caused **bad luck.**

The distinction between coincidence and causation disappeared.

Clover was blamed.

Not because she intended anything.

Not because she understood what had happened.

Not because she possessed any malicious ability.

Simply because she was there.

---

# 3. ABANDONMENT

Clover was eventually thrown out of her home.

She was six years old.

She was left on the streets with little more than the belongings she could carry.

There is no indication that she understood why.

The available records contain several references to her repeatedly asking whether she had done something wrong.

She apparently believed that if she could determine what she had done, she could fix it.

She could not.

She was a child being punished for surviving an accident.

---

# 4. T.O.P.S. INTERVENTION

Clover was eventually discovered by an unidentified T.O.P.S. scientist.

His identity remains unavailable in surviving records.

The scientist took responsibility for her.

He provided:

- food;
    
- shelter;
    
- clothing;
    
- education;
    
- medical care;
    
- and eventually access to T.O.P.S. facilities.
    

He also appears to have encouraged her drawing.

Clover reportedly became particularly interested in diagrams, accounting records, laboratory notes, and illustrations.

She would draw on anything available.

Sometimes paper.

Sometimes notebooks.

Occasionally documents she was specifically told not to draw on.

This habit has survived into adulthood.

---

# 5. SECOND LOSS

When Clover was barely ten years old, the scientist who had rescued her died in a laboratory accident.

This became the second major loss of her childhood.

The circumstances were unrelated to Clover's previous accident.

Nevertheless, Clover reportedly interpreted it differently.

Her existing belief became:

> **"People who stay near me die."**

This belief was not supported by evidence.

It was supported by grief.

That distinction appears to have mattered very little to a ten-year-old child.

T.O.P.S. continued caring for her afterward.

Clover eventually remained within the organization.

---

# 6. T.O.P.S. ACCOUNTING

Clover eventually became a T.O.P.S. accountant.

The appointment initially appeared unusual.

It was not.

She was exceptionally good with numbers.

Her ability to identify discrepancies was described as almost unreasonable.

She could detect accounting errors that other personnel had missed for weeks.

She remembered figures easily.

She noticed patterns.

She could reconstruct incomplete financial records.

She was also reportedly very good at determining when somebody had accidentally — or intentionally — misreported something.

Clover's professional reputation eventually became:

> **"If Clover says the numbers are wrong, the numbers are wrong."**

She was rarely mistaken.

---

# 7. TRANSFER TO KCA

Clover later transferred from T.O.P.S. to the **Krampus Compliance Authority.**

The reasons for the transfer are partly personal and partly professional.

KCA provided her with a different environment.

She remained involved in financial and administrative work while gaining greater exposure to organizational compliance.

Her transition was considered successful.

She became particularly useful when examining:

- expenditures;
    
- resource allocation;
    
- discrepancies;
    
- procurement records;
    
- unusual transactions;
    
- and documentation surrounding anomalous incidents.
    

Her accounting background has made her unexpectedly valuable to KCA.

---

# 8. PERSONALITY

Clover is generally pleasant.

She is not particularly loud.

She can become animated when discussing something she enjoys.

She enjoys drawing.

She frequently carries a notebook.

The contents vary.

Some pages contain ordinary sketches.

Others contain people.

Some contain buildings.

Some contain abstract shapes.

Some contain extremely detailed financial diagrams that should probably not be considered art.

When asked why she drew an accounting flowchart in the middle of a landscape:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "It looked empty."

No further explanation was provided.

---

# 9. CLUMSINESS

Despite her professional competence, Clover is occasionally clumsy.

This is particularly noticeable when she is distracted.

She has been observed:

- walking into furniture;
    
- dropping pens;
    
- tripping over objects that were not particularly difficult to see;
    
- knocking over drinks;
    
- forgetting where she placed her notebook;
    
- and occasionally attempting to pick something up while simultaneously dropping something else.
    

The irony has not escaped personnel.

Clover herself is less amused.

When informed that her clumsiness is inconsistent with her supposed luck:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Luck isn't the same thing as coordination."

This statement has been accepted as scientifically reasonable.

---

# 10. THE LUCK QUESTION

C.O.R.E. has not classified Clover as inherently lucky or unlucky.

There is insufficient evidence.

Her childhood contains a statistically unusual number of fortunate coincidences.

It also contains an unusually severe number of tragedies.

Neither category can currently be demonstrated to originate from Clover.

Personnel are therefore instructed **not** to tell her:

> "You're bad luck."

Personnel should also avoid repeatedly telling her:

> "You're incredibly lucky."

Both statements may cause unnecessary distress.

Clover's relationship with luck is complicated enough without other people assigning meaning to it.

---

# 11. FIRST SAFEHOUSE ARRIVAL

Clover arrived at SAFEHOUSE carrying:

- one suitcase;
    
- one KCA identification card;
    
- several notebooks;
    
- drawing supplies;
    
- and an accounting folder she apparently refused to leave behind.
    

She stood inside the entrance for approximately twelve seconds.

She looked around.

Then looked down at her suitcase.

Then back at the apartment complex.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "This is... bigger than I expected."

The resident who greeted her reportedly replied:

> **"You'll get used to it."**

Clover nodded.

She then attempted to move her suitcase.

The suitcase caught on the doorway.

Clover pulled harder.

The suitcase tipped over.

Several pencils rolled across the floor.

She stared at them.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Good start."

---

# 12. NERI

Neri encountered Clover later that day.

Clover was sitting at a table drawing.

Neri stopped beside her.

She looked at the drawing.

It depicted a small green plant.

Neri pointed at it.

Clover looked up.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "It's a four-leaf clover."

Neri examined it.

Then looked at Clover's eyes.

Clover noticed.

She smiled faintly.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Yeah."

Neri pointed toward her own eyes.

Then toward the drawing.

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I get that a lot."

Neri sat down.

Neither spoke afterward.

They simply continued drawing.

---

# 13. BAO

Bao introduced herself shortly afterward.

Clover immediately recognized her KCA affiliation.

The conversation was brief.

> **BAO:**  
> "If you need anything, tell me."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I'll be fine."

Bao gave her a look.

Clover paused.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...I'll tell you."

Bao smiled.

Clover returned to her drawing.

---

# 14. JANE DOE

Jane was more direct.

She looked at Clover's eyes.

Then her hair.

Then the four-leaf pupils again.

> **JANE:**  
> "So you're Clover."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Yes."

> **JANE:**  
> "Do you actually have good luck?"

Clover considered the question.

Long pause.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I killed my father when I was six."

Jane's expression immediately changed.

Clover looked down.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "So... I don't know."

Jane did not make another joke.

She simply pulled out a chair.

> **JANE:**  
> "Fair answer."

Clover sat down.

---

# 15. DRAWING HABIT

Clover's drawings appear to serve several purposes.

They may be recreational.

They may be emotional processing.

They may simply be something she enjoys.

Personnel should not automatically interpret every drawing as psychological evidence.

One recurring subject is a four-leaf clover.

Another is a family.

Several drawings depict an older male figure standing beside a small child.

The identity of the man has not been confirmed.

Clover has never explicitly stated who he is.

She does not need to.

---

# 16. CURRENT RESIDENT STATUS

Clover is now considered a SAFEHOUSE resident.

Her KCA affiliation remains active.

She retains permission to conduct KCA-related administrative work remotely when necessary.

She is not required to participate in resident activities.

She is not required to socialize.

She is not required to explain her past.

She is not required to prove that she is safe to be around.

Most importantly:

> **Clover is not responsible for every unfortunate event that occurs around her.**

This point has been emphasized to relevant personnel.

---

# C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Clover's history is unusually cruel for someone whose defining characteristic was once described as **luck.**

She had a happy childhood.

Then an accident took her father.

Her mother blamed her.

She was abandoned.

A stranger rescued her.

That stranger died.

She remained with T.O.P.S.

She built a career.

She transferred to KCA.

And now she has arrived at SAFEHOUSE.

She is twenty-six years old.

She draws.

She works with numbers.

She occasionally trips over furniture.

She sometimes drops things.

She still seems surprised when people offer to help her.

She has spent most of her life trying to determine whether she brings fortune or disaster wherever she goes.

C.O.R.E. recommends that nobody attempt to answer that question for her.

Because perhaps the most important thing Clover needs to learn is simpler:

> **Not everything that happens around you is your fault.**

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Clover's first evening in SAFEHOUSE was uneventful.

She sat at the kitchen table.

She drew quietly.

Someone brought her tea.

Someone else left a plate of food beside her.

She thanked them.

A pencil rolled off the table.

Clover reached for it.

She missed.

The pencil rolled farther.

She stood.

Took two steps.

Tripped over her own suitcase.

Caught herself against the table.

Silence.

Clover looked around.

Nobody laughed.

Neri quietly pushed the pencil toward her.

Clover picked it up.

She sat back down.

Then continued drawing.

For perhaps the first time in a very long time, an accident happened around Clover...

...and nobody blamed her for it.
