# **HEAVEN'S BLACKSITE**

## **ABILITY DEMONSTRATION RECORD**

### **SUBJECT: NORMA LOCKWOOD – AERIAL MOBILITY & FEATHER EFFECT**

**Classification:** LEVEL 3 – Internal Training Archive

---

## **PURPOSE**

Following requests from the Blacksite Tactical Training Division, Senior Manager **Norma Lockwood** agreed to demonstrate the practical capabilities of her wings under controlled conditions.

Attendance included members of SAFEHOUSE, several researchers, and flight-capable personnel.

---

## **FLIGHT DEMONSTRATION**

Norma stood in the center of the training field.

Without any dramatic buildup, she quietly removed her jacket.

Her folded wings slowly unfurled from behind her back.

The training field fell silent.

The wings were immaculate, each feather neatly aligned despite normally remaining hidden beneath her clothing.

She flexed them once.

A gentle gust swept across the field.

Then—

She was airborne.

Not by jumping.

Not by using Ether propulsion.

Simply by taking flight.

---

## **OBSERVED PERFORMANCE**

Norma displayed remarkable control throughout the exercise.

Recorded maneuvers included:

- Instant vertical takeoff.
    
- Silent hovering.
    
- High-speed banking turns.
    
- Precision dives.
    
- Rapid deceleration without instability.
    
- Sustained inverted flight.
    
- Tight figure-eight patterns.
    
- Controlled barrel rolls.
    
- Stationary hovering accurate to within a few centimeters.
    

Researchers noted there was no wasted movement.

Every adjustment of her wings appeared deliberate and efficient.

One flight instructor summarized the demonstration as:

> "She doesn't fight the air."

> "She works with it."

---

As someone accustomed to exceptional mobility, Taiga watched every movement with complete concentration.

When Norma completed a near-vertical recovery from a dive, Taiga quietly muttered:

Coming from Taiga, it was high praise.

---

He later requested access to the flight recordings for further study.

---

Endmin crossed her arms, carefully observing every maneuver.

After the demonstration concluded, she simply nodded.

> "Combat-capable."

After a brief pause, she added:

---

## **FEATHER PHENOMENON**

As Norma landed, several small feathers naturally drifted free from her wings.

Initially, researchers assumed they were ordinary molted feathers.

They were not.

One feather floated gently toward a volunteer standing within the marked test area.

The moment it brushed against his shoulder—

He froze.

Completely.

He remained conscious and aware but was unable to voluntarily move until Norma stepped forward and carefully removed the feather.

Movement immediately returned.

Subsequent testing confirmed the effect.

---

## **ABILITY DESIGNATION**

**Designation:** _Stillfeather_ (Temporary Classification)

### **Observed Properties**

Feathers naturally shed by Norma retain a residual Ether effect for a limited period.

Upon direct contact, they can temporarily immobilize a target.

Current observations indicate:

- The effect is non-lethal.
    
- Consciousness is retained.
    
- Breathing remains unaffected.
    
- The immobilization ends when the Ether within the feather dissipates or the feather is removed.
    
- Duration varies depending on the feather's remaining Ether and the target's resistance.
    

Researchers emphasized that this effect appears to be an unintended byproduct of Norma's Ether abilities rather than something she consciously activates with every feather she loses.

## **STATUS**

Norma's flight capability has been certified as one of the most refined aerial skill sets currently documented within the Blacksite.

Personnel are reminded that any naturally shed feathers should be treated as anomalous objects until collected by their owner or the research team, as accidental contact may result in temporary immobilization.

Following the demonstration, Taiga, Mart, and Endmin all independently requested copies of the flight recordings for tactical analysis—an uncommon acknowledgment of just how impressive Norma's aerial control had been.
