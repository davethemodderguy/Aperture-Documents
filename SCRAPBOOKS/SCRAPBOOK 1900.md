---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Mia's unexpected return, C.O.R.E. has observed a substantial improvement in Sora's general emotional presentation.

The change extends beyond the initial reunion.

Sora appears happier during ordinary daily activities, more willing to remain in shared spaces, and noticeably more comfortable in the company of others.

Most notably, Sora, Mia, and Jake have developed a consistent three-person social group.

Personnel can now frequently find all three children together.

They eat together, spend time in the common areas, accompany one another between activities, and frequently remain close even when they are not actively interacting.

Their presence together has become a familiar sight on Floor 7.

The most significant observation is that Sora no longer appears to be merely enduring his surroundings.

He is beginning to enjoy living in them.

---

## 2. OBSERVED BEHAVIORAL CHANGES

Before Mia's return, Sora's social world was relatively small.

Jake was his primary companion. Bao provided maternal support, and other residents were gradually becoming familiar to him.

Although Sora had begun showing signs of trust, his overall behavior remained cautious.

Since Mia's return, personnel have recorded several changes.

|Previous behavior|Current observation|
|---|---|
|Frequently remained close to Jake alone|Usually accompanied by both Jake and Mia|
|Limited spontaneous expressions|Smiles more frequently|
|Often stayed within familiar areas|More willing to accompany the other children|
|Relied on his whiteboard when necessary|Continues using it, but appears more comfortable communicating through gestures and expressions as well|
|Occasionally appeared withdrawn|More consistently engaged during group activities|

These observations do not establish that Sora's previous difficulties have disappeared.

He remains nonverbal, shy, and vulnerable to fear.

However, the overall trend is positive.

---

## 3. THE THREE-PERSON GROUP

Personnel have informally begun referring to the children as the _Floor 7 Trio_.

The designation is not an official organizational title.

It simply describes an increasingly consistent observation.

Wherever one of the three children is found, the other two are frequently nearby.

### Jake

Jake appears delighted by Mia's return.

He has readily accepted her presence and seems comfortable sharing his time with both siblings.

His interactions with Sora remain affectionate, and he has developed a friendly relationship with Mia.

He is often the most energetic member of the group.

### Sora

Sora's change is the most noticeable.

He appears considerably more relaxed when Mia is present.

He frequently stays beside her, much as he previously stayed beside Jake.

His smiles are no longer an exceptionally rare occurrence.

He also appears more willing to initiate small interactions, whether through gestures, written messages, or simply approaching the other two children.

### Mia

Mia has settled into the group naturally.

She continues to regard Sora as her older brother despite their identical age, and she has accepted Jake as part of their little circle.

She does not appear troubled by the fact that Sora and Jake already had an established friendship before her arrival.

Instead, she participates in their activities and spends time with both of them.

## 4. SELECTED OBSERVATIONS

### Observation A – Breakfast

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Main Kitchen  
**Time:** 08:16

All three children were present at breakfast.

Jake was talking enthusiastically about an activity planned for later that day.

Mia listened while eating.

Sora sat between them.

At one point, Jake said something that caused Mia to laugh.

Sora looked between them and began smiling.

Mia noticed.

**MIA:** What's funny?

Sora picked up his whiteboard.

**SORA – WHITEBOARD:**  
_You two._

**JAKE:** What about us?

Sora erased the board and wrote again.

**SORA – WHITEBOARD:**  
_Nothing._

Mia laughed.

Jake looked mildly offended.

**JAKE:** Hey!

Sora continued smiling.

Personnel noted that Sora remained engaged throughout breakfast rather than finishing his meal and immediately withdrawing.

### Observation B – The Common Room

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7 Common Area  
**Time:** 14:42

Jake was playing with a small toy while Mia sat nearby.

Sora was resting against Mia's shoulder.

Neither child appeared particularly interested in initiating conversation.

They simply remained together.

After several minutes, Jake moved closer and joined them.

No structured activity was taking place.

The three children remained in the same position for approximately twenty minutes.

A staff member later described the scene as:

_"Three children doing absolutely nothing, and somehow having a wonderful time."_

C.O.R.E. considers this a positive observation.

Companionship does not require constant conversation or entertainment.

### Observation C – The Missing Third Member

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Elevator Entrance  
**Time:** 17:05

Jake and Mia were observed walking toward the common area.

Sora was not with them.

Mia stopped.

**MIA:** Where's Sora?

**JAKE:** I thought he was with you!

Both children turned around and returned to Floor 7.

They found Sora near his den.

He was holding his whiteboard.

**SORA – WHITEBOARD:**  
_Where going?_

**MIA:** We were looking for you!

Sora tilted his head.

**JAKE:** You can't just disappear on us!

Sora wrote:

**SORA – WHITEBOARD:**  
_I live here._

Mia laughed.

Jake crossed his arms.

**JAKE:** That's not the point!

Sora smiled and followed them.

Personnel noted that the children had begun actively noticing one another's absence.

This appears to be a reciprocal attachment rather than a relationship in which Sora alone seeks the company of the other two.

## 5. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

Mia's return appears to have provided Sora with something that previous support measures could not fully provide: the presence of someone from his earliest family life.

Jake helped him build a new connection after his arrival.

Bao provided maternal care.

SAFEHOUSE offered stability.

But Mia represents continuity with the life Sora lost.

She remembers the same grandmother, the same home, and the same childhood experiences.

She is not merely someone who understands his past through an explanation.

She lived it with him.

Her presence may therefore provide a unique sense of familiarity and belonging.

C.O.R.E. cautions against treating Mia as a therapeutic instrument or assigning her responsibility for Sora's recovery.

She is also a child with her own history and needs.

The improvement in Sora's mood is welcome, but Mia must be allowed to enjoy her new home without being expected to maintain his happiness.

The same applies to Jake.

Neither child should be made responsible for keeping Sora emotionally stable.

Their friendship is valuable precisely because it is allowed to be a friendship.

---

## 6. CURRENT WELFARE RECOMMENDATIONS

- Continue allowing the three children to spend time together naturally.
    
- Avoid unnecessary separation during recreational activities.
    
- Preserve individual time and personal space for each child.
    
- Maintain access to Sora's whiteboard and Mia's personal belongings.
    
- Continue monitoring Sora's emotional condition without treating every quiet period as a setback.
    
- Ensure that Jake and Mia receive the same consideration and support as Sora.
    
- Encourage ordinary childhood activities rather than structuring every interaction around their traumatic histories.
    

C.O.R.E. also recommends retaining the informal designation _Floor 7 Trio_ in welfare observations.

It is a convenient description of a genuine social pattern.

## 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE – THREE LITTLE SHADOWS

There used to be a little boy who spent most of his time hiding.

He had lost his family.

He had lost his voice.

He had lost the person he loved most.

Then Jake found him.

They became friends. Sora began following him around, and eventually SAFEHOUSE became a place where he could sleep without fearing every approaching footstep.

Then Mia came back.

And now?

Well, now it is difficult to find any of the three without finding the other two.

Jake is there.

Sora is there.

Mia is there.

Sometimes they're playing. Sometimes they're eating. Sometimes they're arguing over something completely unimportant.

And sometimes they're just sitting together.

Sora smiles more often now.

Not because someone asks him to.

Not because someone reminds him that he should be happy.

He simply has his sister beside him.

He has Jake beside him.

He has people he can expect to see when he wakes up.

There is something particularly reassuring about seeing Sora walk through SAFEHOUSE with Mia on one side and Jake on the other.

For a child who once spent so much time alone, he now seems to have trouble going anywhere without company.

And perhaps that's all right.

We should still give him room to be alone whenever he wants it.

But for now, the sight of three little children wandering around Floor 7 together has become one of the most pleasant things our personnel get to witness.

**Sora spent so long missing someone. Now, when he looks beside him, she's there.**

And Jake is there too.

— **C.O.R.E. Residential Welfare Division**
