### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / PERSONNEL & MEDICAL LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — HEMOLACRIA**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Age:** Approximately 5 years old  
**Status:** **CRITICAL / PERMANENT OCULAR DAMAGE**

---

# **OBSERVATION**

A new abnormality has been documented.

Whenever Alice cries, the tears from her **left eye** become mixed with blood.

The phenomenon is consistent enough to be classified as:

> **Hemolacria.**

Her right eye remains permanently blind and severely damaged.

The left eye is currently her only functional eye.

It is now also producing blood during periods of significant emotional distress.

---

# **FIRST OBSERVED INCIDENT**

Alice had been crying quietly.

Corin noticed a dark red streak running down Alice's cheek.

Initially, she assumed Alice had injured herself.

She wiped the fluid away.

More appeared.

Corin froze.

> **"Alice..."**

Alice looked up.

> **"Hm?"**

Corin carefully wiped beneath her left eye again.

The fluid was unmistakably blood.

Alice touched her cheek.

> **"Why is Alice crying red?"**

Corin had no immediate answer.

---

# **MEDICAL EXAMINATION**

The medical department conducted an immediate examination of Alice's remaining functional eye.

The eye itself remains capable of vision.

However, the surrounding ocular structures are severely stressed.

The exact mechanism producing the bloody tears remains under investigation.

Given Alice's current systemic condition, medical personnel suspect that her extreme physiological instability contributes to the phenomenon.

Further observation is required.

---

# **EMOTIONAL TRIGGER**

The bleeding appears most frequently during intense crying.

This creates another dangerous feedback loop.

Alice becomes distressed.

↓

Alice cries.

↓

Blood appears from her remaining eye.

↓

Someone becomes frightened.

↓

Alice notices their reaction.

↓

Alice believes she has caused another problem.

↓

Her distress increases.

The SAFEHOUSE has therefore been instructed to remain calm whenever the phenomenon occurs.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Alice does not seem frightened by the blood itself.

She is more concerned about everyone else's reaction.

When Belle saw the blood for the first time, she immediately became visibly upset.

Alice noticed.

She stopped crying.

She wiped her cheek.

Then she smiled.

> **"Don't worry."**

Belle stared at her.

> **"Alice..."**

> **"Alice okay."**

She wasn't.

She simply didn't want Belle to be scared.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin reacted differently.

She did not panic in front of Alice.

She gently cleaned Alice's face.

> **"It's okay."**

Alice asked:

> **"Did Alice hurt you?"**

Corin shook her head.

> **"No."**

> **"Did Alice make you sad?"**

> **"No."**

Alice looked confused.

> **"Then why you look sad?"**

Corin paused.

> **"...Because I love you."**

Alice didn't understand.

But she stopped trying to make Corin feel better.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle has begun carrying a small clean cloth specifically for Alice.

She doesn't make a big deal about it.

If Alice cries, Belle quietly hands it over.

Sometimes Alice forgets what it is for.

Belle simply explains:

> **"For your eye."**

Alice nods.

> **"Thank you."**

---

# **LUMI**

Lumi has expressed concern that Alice's tendency to suppress emotional distress may now be physically dangerous.

Alice already tries to stop herself from crying because she believes crying makes everyone worry.

The presence of blood makes that belief stronger.

Medical personnel have therefore emphasized:

> **Alice must not be encouraged to suppress crying.**

The underlying cause must be treated and monitored.

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie has ordered continuous monitoring of Alice's remaining functional eye.

The right eye is permanently blind.

The left eye is now effectively Alice's primary visual organ.

Any deterioration must be detected immediately.

The possibility of further damage is considered unacceptable.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice has only one functioning eye.

And now, when she cries, even that eye cries blood.

The sight is frightening.

Alice knows this.

So she tries not to cry.

Not because the blood scares her.

Because she sees everyone else becoming afraid.

And once again, her first instinct is to protect everyone around her instead of herself.

She wipes the blood from her cheek.

She forces a tiny smile.

And says:

> **"Alice okay."**

Nobody believes her.

But nobody tells her that she's making them worry.

They simply stay beside her.
