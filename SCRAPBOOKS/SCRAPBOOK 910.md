## 1. Overview

Neri's abnormal hematological condition is believed to be a direct consequence of the experimentation and prolonged physical abuse inflicted upon her during childhood.

The condition is not consistent with ordinary anemia.

Neri's blood possesses an anomalous instability when removed from her body. Under certain environmental conditions, the sample does not remain liquid. Instead, it progressively **vaporizes**, beginning extremely slowly and accelerating when exposed to conditions that would normally be harmless to conventional blood.

The phenomenon is reproducible.

It is also deeply concerning because the same underlying instability appears to occur **inside Neri's circulatory system**, albeit at a much slower rate.

---

# 2. Initial Laboratory Observation

During a routine medical investigation, a researcher collected a small blood sample from Neri and transferred it into a sterile vial.

The sample appeared normal immediately after collection.

No unusual coloration, viscosity, temperature, or cellular behavior was initially observed.

The researcher subsequently transferred a small quantity onto a conventional laboratory Petri dish for examination.

### RESULT:

**The blood began to vaporize almost immediately.**

At first, the effect was subtle.

The edges of the sample appeared to thin and recede without any visible liquid displacement. Within seconds, measurable portions of the sample had simply ceased to exist.

There was no conventional evaporation pattern.

There was no heating.

There was no residue consistent with coagulation.

The sample was effectively **disintegrating into the surrounding environment.**

---

## 3. Carbon Dioxide Interaction

Further observation revealed that atmospheric conditions significantly influence the rate of decomposition.

In particular, exposure to **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)** causes the process to begin gradually even when the blood is otherwise undisturbed.

At low exposure, the reaction is extremely slow.

The sample may remain apparently stable for a considerable period before small portions begin disappearing.

Once initiated, however, the process does not appear to reverse.

- fight,

At that point:

**pallor → weakness → loss of consciousness → cardiovascular collapse**

can occur.
