### RESIDENT WELFARE & MEDICAL DIVISION

**SUBJECT:** Jake  
**TIME:** 00:00  
**INCIDENT:** Post-Nightmare Withdrawal

---

# 1. INCIDENT SUMMARY

At approximately **00:00**, Jake woke from a nightmare.

He did not leave his room.

He did not request assistance.

He did not go to the cafeteria.

He did not seek out Bao, Neri, Nora, Aeterna, or any other resident.

He simply got out of bed, walked to the corner of his living room, and curled himself into the space between the wall and the old mattress.

He cried.

He remained there.

---

# 2. JAKE'S ROOM

Jake's room remains intentionally simple.

The room contains:

- one bedroom;
    
- one bathroom;
    
- one living room;
    
- one old mattress;
    
- several plushies;
    
- a small collection of personal belongings;
    
- and very little else.
    

The remaining available space is largely unused.

Jake has never requested additional furniture.

He does not use a nightlight.

This is unnecessary.

His fractured halo provides sufficient illumination.

Even when Jake is asleep, the two fragments retain a faint azure glow.

Tonight, however, Jake did something slightly different.

---

# 3. THE HALO FRAGMENT

Following the nightmare, Jake sat upright in bed.

He looked around the dark room.

For several seconds, he did nothing.

Then he reached upward.

One of the two fragments of his fractured halo drifted down toward his hand.

Jake picked it up.

The fragment remained suspended just above his palm, emitting a weak blue-white light.

He carried it with him.

Not because he was afraid of the dark.

He has lived with darkness for a very long time.

He simply wanted to see where he was going.

Jake walked from the bedroom into the living room.

He placed the fragment beside the mattress.

It continued glowing.

He sat down.

Then curled up.

---

# 4. OBSERVATION

No alarm was triggered.

No medical emergency was detected.

Jake's physical condition appeared stable.

His stellar core remained active.

His teddy bear's heartbeat was present.

His respiration was normal.

The distress was emotional.

The room remained silent except for Jake's crying.

He did not scream.

He did not call for anyone.

He did not attempt to wake another resident.

He simply cried into one of the plushies he had pulled against himself.

---

# 5. THE NIGHTMARE

Jake did not immediately describe what he had dreamed.

No complete account was obtained.

However, fragments of speech were recorded while he was crying.

Most were barely audible.

> **Jake:** “...Mom...”

Pause.

> **Jake:** “I waited.”

Several seconds passed.

> **Jake:** “I waited.”

Jake tightened his grip around the plushie.

> **Jake:** “Why didn't you come back?”

He stopped speaking after that.

---

# 6. THE EMPTY ROOM

The most notable aspect of the incident was not Jake's crying.

It was what he did afterward.

He did not move toward the door.

He did not ask whether anyone was coming.

He did not check the hallway.

He did not look for footsteps.

He did not ask:

> “Are you coming back?”

Previously, Jake would have done exactly that.

Tonight, he remained in the corner.

The behavior appears consistent with his more recent withdrawal patterns.

He was not necessarily expecting someone to come.

He was simply hurting.

---

# 7. 00:17

At approximately **00:17**, the halo fragment remained beside Jake.

Jake had stopped crying loudly.

He was still producing occasional quiet sobs.

One hand remained around the plushie.

The other was resting against the mattress.

His teddy bear was pressed against his chest.

Its heartbeat remained steady.

Jake whispered:

> **Jake:** “I'm okay.”

There was no one in the room to hear him.

He said it anyway.

---

# 8. 00:31

Jake eventually lay down on the mattress.

He did not return to the bedroom.

He stayed in the living room.

The room contained almost nothing except the mattress, plushies, and the faint blue light of his halo fragment.

He pulled the blanket over himself.

The fragment remained nearby.

Jake closed his eyes.

A few minutes later, he opened them again.

He looked toward the door.

Nothing happened.

He looked away.

---

# 9. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Jake's response does not currently indicate a medical emergency.

However, the incident is consistent with his established nightmare pattern.

His nightmares remain strongly associated with:

- abandonment;
    
- waiting;
    
- isolation;
    
- his mother's disappearance;
    
- being left behind;
    
- his death;
    
- and the belief that nobody is coming back.
    

The absence of food-seeking behavior is also notable.

Jake normally enjoys food and has developed a particularly strong fondness for cake and other sweets.

Tonight, he did not appear interested.

He did not leave the room.

He did not ask anyone for anything.

He simply wanted to be somewhere quiet.

---

# 10. PERSONNEL GUIDANCE

Personnel should not immediately enter Jake's room simply because he is crying.

Jake's room is his private space.

If he requests company, provide it.

If he approaches someone, respond.

If a genuine medical concern develops, intervene.

Otherwise:

**Knock.**

Wait.

Allow Jake to decide whether he wants someone inside.

A person being alone after a nightmare is not automatically a crisis.

For Jake, however, prolonged isolation should still be observed carefully.

There is a significant difference between:

> **“I want to be alone.”**

and:

> **“I don't think anyone will come.”**

Personnel should avoid assuming which one applies.

---

# 11. MORNING FOLLOW-UP

At approximately **05:00**, the halo fragment was still beside Jake.

Jake had fallen asleep again.

He remained curled around the plushie.

His teddy bear was against his chest.

The fragment continued emitting a faint blue glow.

No nightlight had been installed.

None was necessary.

The little star had brought his own.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake did not ask for anyone tonight.

He did not ask for food.

He did not ask to be carried.

He did not ask Bao to stay.

He did not ask Neri to sit with him.

He did not call for Nora.

He simply woke up frightened and went somewhere quiet.

Then he picked up a piece of his broken halo and used it as a little lamp.

There is something strangely sad about that.

A child sitting alone in an almost-empty room, surrounded by plushies, holding part of his own halo just so he can see where he is.

He has spent enough of his life waiting in the dark.

Tonight, at least, he brought himself a little light.
