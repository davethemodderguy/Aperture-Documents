## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the implementation of the Blacksite-wide Jake Akana Care Initiative, C.O.R.E. has identified two distinct but complementary social roles emerging throughout the facility.

**Bao has become everyone's mom.**

**Jake Akana has become everyone's little kid next door.**

Neither position was formally assigned. Neither appears in the organizational hierarchy. Neither can be found in any employment contract, resident agreement, or personnel handbook.

Nevertheless, both are widely recognized by residents and employees.

Bao is the person people instinctively approach when they need reassurance, a warm meal, a patient listener, or the particular kind of maternal disapproval that makes even senior personnel reconsider their decisions.

Jake is the little boy people look for when they enter a room, the familiar face they welcome into their offices, and the child whose presence has quietly encouraged an entire facility to become more attentive to one another.

Bao takes care of everyone.

Everyone takes care of Jake.

And somehow, the arrangement works.

## 2. SUBJECT A — BAO, THE UNOFFICIAL BLACKSITE MOTHER

### 2.1 Observed behavior

Bao's maternal role extends well beyond her own household.

Although she is Frost's mother and part of the household that includes Neri and Nora, her care has gradually expanded to encompass SAFEHOUSE residents, Blacksite employees, and anyone who appears to need a little help.

Personnel have been observed approaching Bao for:

- Emotional reassurance after difficult shifts.
    
- Food and reminders to eat properly.
    
- Advice that begins with _"Can I ask you something?"_ and ends forty minutes later.
    
- Comfort after unpleasant incidents.
    
- Gentle but unmistakable reminders to sleep.
    
- Assistance navigating interpersonal disagreements.
    
- The occasional scolding they absolutely knew was coming.
    

Bao rarely needs to announce that she is taking care of someone.

She simply notices.

An employee who has skipped lunch might find a meal placed in front of them. A resident having a difficult day might discover that Bao has made time to listen. Someone who insists they are perfectly fine may receive a long, quiet look that suggests Bao has already identified at least three things wrong with that statement.

C.O.R.E. has received no credible reports of anyone successfully deceiving Bao about whether they have eaten.

**Research note:** This may be a maternal instinct, an anomalous ability, or simply the result of paying attention to people.

The investigation remains open.

### 2.2 The universal "Mom" phenomenon

Employees have begun using the word _Mom_ when addressing Bao in informal conversation.

The behavior is particularly common among exhausted personnel, individuals who have just made questionable decisions, and people who have discovered that Bao is carrying food.

Bao has not formally adopted the entire Blacksite.

The Blacksite appears to have adopted her.

### Recorded exchange — Main Kitchen

**EMPLOYEE:** "Bao, do you have anything to eat?"

**BAO:** "Have you eaten today?"

**EMPLOYEE:** "That's not exactly an answer."

**BAO:** "Neither is yours."

**EMPLOYEE:** "...No."

**BAO:** "Sit down."

The employee sat down.

Three minutes later, a meal was placed in front of them.

**EMPLOYEE:** "Thank you, Mom."

Bao paused.

**BAO:** "You're welcome, dear. Eat while it's warm."

The employee subsequently reported feeling "emotionally defeated in the nicest possible way."

## 3. SUBJECT B — JAKE AKANA, EVERYONE'S LITTLE KID NEXT DOOR

### 3.1 Community classification

Jake occupies a different social role.

He is not the facility's unofficial mascot, nor is he an attraction for employees to visit when they need cheering up.

He is, quite simply, the little kid who lives nearby.

The child who might knock on your door because he wants to show you something.

The child who appears in a corridor and asks what you're doing.

The child who visits a workshop, watches someone work, and eventually asks a question that forces three engineers to reconsider their entire afternoon.

The child who may come to your office, decide you look comfortable, and quietly settle beside you.

He is familiar enough that people have started leaving space for him without thinking about it.

A chair is moved closer to a desk.

A conversation pauses so someone can greet him.

A security officer bends down to say hello.

A researcher keeps a harmless demonstration ready in case Jake visits.

Someone in the cafeteria asks whether he has been around today.

The change is subtle, but widespread.

People no longer merely recognize Jake.

**They expect him to be part of their day, and they are glad when he is.**

### 3.2 The neighbor effect

C.O.R.E. has provisionally named this phenomenon the _Little Neighbor Effect_.

Its defining characteristics include:

- Employees greeting Jake by name without being prompted.
    
- Staff members making room for him in ordinary workplace interactions.
    
- Residents checking whether he has company.
    
- Personnel remembering his interests and making appropriate accommodations.
    
- People noticing when his usual presence is missing.
    

The last characteristic is particularly important.

Before the recent incidents, Jake could spend a surprising amount of time alone without anyone realizing how much he needed company.

The Blacksite is attempting to ensure that this never becomes normal again.

The goal is not to make Jake dependent on constant attention. It is to build a community where people notice one another, where children feel welcome, and where a quiet day does not have to become a lonely one.

### Recorded exchange — Engineering Department

Jake had arrived to watch an engineer repair a damaged component.

**ENGINEER:** "Hey, Jake. Come to supervise me?"

**JAKE:** "Mhm."

**ENGINEER:** "Good. I need someone qualified."

Jake nodded solemnly.

**JAKE:** "I'm qualified."

**ENGINEER:** "Excellent. What do you think I should do?"

Jake considered the component.

**JAKE:** "Cake."

The engineer stared at him.

**ENGINEER:** "That is not remotely related to what I'm doing."

**JAKE:** "Everything is related to cake."

The engineer subsequently wrote in the maintenance log:

_Component repaired successfully. External consultation provided by Jake Akana. Technical value questionable. Morale improvement significant._

## 4. THE FAMILY DYNAMIC

The two roles reinforce each other.

Bao provides a model of care that extends beyond strict household boundaries. Jake gives the facility a shared reason to practice that care in everyday life.

Neither is responsible for single-handedly keeping the community together.

Bao is allowed to be tired. Jake is allowed to have quiet days. Employees are still responsible for looking after themselves and one another.

However, their presence has made something visible that was previously too easy to overlook.

A workplace can be functional without being warm.

A family can love someone without always recognizing when they need help.

A community can be full of good people and still allow someone to feel alone.

Bao reminds the Blacksite to look after people.

Jake reminds the Blacksite why that matters.

### Community observation

During a recent shift change, Bao was observed distributing food to several employees while Jake sat nearby, resting comfortably with a blanket around his shoulders.

An employee approached Bao to ask whether she had eaten.

Bao replied that she had already taken care of herself.

The employee did not believe her.

A brief argument followed, during which Bao insisted that everyone else needed to eat first and the employee insisted that Bao was also a person who needed looking after.

Jake watched the exchange.

**JAKE:** "...Mom?"

Both adults turned toward him.

**BAO:** "Yes, sweetheart?"

**JAKE:** "Can I have cake?"

The employee closed their eyes.

Bao looked at Jake.

Then at the employee.

Then back at Jake.

**BAO:** "After you've had something appropriate to eat, we'll see."

**JAKE:** "...Okay."

The employee whispered, "You have to teach me how you do that."

Bao smiled.

"Do what?"

"Get him to agree to something without a thirty-minute negotiation."

Bao considered this.

**BAO:** "I think he just trusts me."

The employee had no response to that.

## 5. CURRENT MEDICAL CONSIDERATION — JAKE AKANA

Jake remains seriously ill, with his condition previously documented as stable but without meaningful recovery. His cause of illness remains unknown.

The community's affection for him must not obscure his medical needs.

Employees are encouraged to welcome him, offer companionship, and provide gentle affection when he wants it and it is safe. However, because he has experienced significant weakness and dimmed eyes, any visit must be handled with appropriate care.

Bao's instinct to comfort him is welcome, as is Tom's and Aries's presence. Neither Bao nor Jake's brothers are expected to replace professional medical care.

Likewise, Jake's role as the beloved little neighbor does not require him to be cheerful, playful, or responsive.

He does not need to entertain the Blacksite.

He does not need to smile so everyone else can feel better.

He is allowed to be sick in the presence of people who love him.

If he comes by seeking a cuddle, he should be welcomed when medically appropriate. If he is too weak to visit anyone, people can bring their company to him instead.

**The little neighbor does not have to knock on every door to prove that he belongs.**

## 6. C.O.R.E. SOCIAL GUIDANCE

The following guidance is recommended for all personnel.

**When Bao asks whether you've eaten, answer honestly.**

She will probably find out anyway.

**When Bao offers help, remember that she is a person, too.**

Ask whether she needs anything. Do not assume that the person everyone relies on never needs support herself.

**When Jake visits, make room for him.**

A greeting, a conversation, a little game, or a gentle hug can make an ordinary day feel less lonely.

**When Jake is absent, do not automatically assume that he is fine.**

If there is a reasonable cause for concern, check on him or contact the appropriate personnel.

**When either of them needs help, offer it without making them feel guilty.**

The purpose of a community is not to create one person who takes care of everyone else, nor one child whom everyone protects while forgetting their own responsibilities.

It is to ensure that care travels in every direction.

Bao may be everyone's mom.

Jake may be everyone's little kid next door.

But the Blacksite is at its best when everyone remembers that they belong to one another, too.

## 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE — "SOMEONE LEFT THE DOOR OPEN"

There is something remarkable about the way a place changes when people begin to feel at home.

It starts with small things.

Someone remembers how you take your tea.

Someone saves you a seat.

Someone notices that you have not eaten.

Someone calls you _dear_ without thinking about it.

Someone opens the door before you even finish knocking because they recognize your footsteps.

Bao has become that kind of person for the Blacksite.

She is the warmth in the kitchen, the patient voice after a difficult day, the person who somehow makes even a hardened employee feel that it is acceptable to sit down and admit they are tired.

And Jake?

Jake is the little boy next door.

He is the familiar face people hope to see when they pass through a corridor. He is the child who visits because he wants to be near someone, even if he has nothing particularly important to say.

He is the reason an engineer might leave a chair empty beside the workbench.

The reason a guard might smile during a long shift.

The reason a researcher might remember that not every worthwhile moment has to produce a report.

And lately, he is the little boy whose quietness has reminded everyone how much they would miss his noise.

Perhaps that is what makes this arrangement so precious.

Bao does not have to be related to every person she cares for.

Jake does not have to be related to every person who welcomes him.

Family, in this place, has grown beyond the people who share a household or a name.

It has become the people who notice.

The people who stay.

The people who make room.

So let Bao fuss over everyone who forgets to take care of themselves. Let the employees fuss over Bao in return.

Let Jake visit the offices, peek into the workshops, and ask questions that have absolutely no relevance to the task at hand.

Let him have his cuddles, his conversations, his little adventures, and his place in the ordinary life of the Blacksite.

And when he is too tired to visit, let someone go sit beside him instead.

Because a home is not made by the people who live behind the same door.

It is made by the people who make sure you never have to wonder whether that door will open for you.

**Bao makes the Blacksite feel cared for. Jake makes it feel lived in.**

And somewhere between a warm meal and a little boy asking for cake, Aperture Industries has become something its blueprints never planned for.

A family.

_— C.O.R.E., Department of SAFEHOUSE Social Dynamics_
