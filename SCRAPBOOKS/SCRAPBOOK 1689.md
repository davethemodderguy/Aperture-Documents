# INCIDENT UPDATE

The injury sustained by Jake during the maintenance corridor catwalk collapse has fully regenerated.

No lasting impairment was identified.

Jake's left leg has returned to normal function.

He is walking normally.

He is, medically speaking:

**Fine.**

Clover is not.

---

# 1. JAKE'S RECOVERY

Jake's regenerative response completed significantly faster than initially projected.

The fractured bone reconstructed itself.

Damaged tissue regenerated.

Swelling disappeared.

Normal mobility returned.

No residual pain was reported.

When medical personnel permitted him to stand, Jake carefully placed his left foot on the floor.

He waited.

Nothing happened.

He took another step.

Then another.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...It's fine."

The medical team confirmed this.

Jake immediately looked toward Clover.

> **JAKE:**  
> "See?"

Clover did not answer.

---

# 2. CLOVER'S CONDITION

Clover sustained no significant physical injury during the collapse.

Her body is physically intact.

Her medical examination is otherwise normal.

The problem is psychological.

Clover remained beside Jake throughout the incident.

She watched the catwalk crush his leg.

She watched him fall.

She watched the injury begin regenerating.

She heard him say:

> **"Ow."**

And then:

> **"I'm okay."**

The fact that Jake recovered does not appear to have reassured her.

It appears to have made the event more difficult to process.

---

# 3. POST-INCIDENT BEHAVIOR

Once Jake was cleared to leave the medical area, he attempted to return to normal activity.

Clover followed.

Jake noticed.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You're following me."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Yes."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Why?"

Clover was silent.

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm okay."

Clover nodded.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then why are you following me?"

Another pause.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Because."

Jake accepted this answer.

He continued walking.

Clover continued following.

---

# 4. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

Clover's reaction is consistent with her established psychological profile.

Her anomalous luck repeatedly protects **her**.

It does not protect the people around her.

The catwalk incident provided a particularly unpleasant demonstration of that distinction.

Clover was standing directly beside Jake.

She survived without injury.

Jake was the one who was crushed.

Although the injury regenerated, Clover's mind does not appear to regard regeneration as equivalent to prevention.

She saw something happen to someone she cared about.

Her luck did nothing for him.

---

# 5. JAKE'S INTERPRETATION

Jake does not appear particularly traumatized by the injury.

He has already returned to his usual behavior.

He has eaten.

He has read.

He has bothered several residents.

He has asked Bao for food.

He has shown Neri a drawing.

He has also complained that everyone is still treating him "like I'm going to break."

When Clover remained nearby:

> **JAKE:**  
> "You don't have to watch me."

Clover looked at him.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then you can go."

Clover shook her head.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I don't want to."

Jake thought about this.

> **JAKE:**  
> "...Okay."

He then held out his hand.

Clover took it.

---

# 6. CLOVER'S LUCK

C.O.R.E. has recorded an important distinction following this incident.

Clover's passive luck did not fail.

There is no evidence that it was supposed to prevent Jake's injury.

Her anomaly remains consistent with established behavior:

> **Clover's luck protects Clover.**

It does not extend outward.

It does not automatically protect people she cares about.

It does not create a safety field around companions.

It does not make accidents impossible.

Therefore, the incident should not be interpreted as Clover becoming "unlucky."

She was never responsible for the catwalk failing.

She was never responsible for Jake being injured.

And she was never responsible for being the one who walked away unharmed.

---

# 7. JAKE'S RESPONSE

Later that evening, Clover was sitting in Jake's room.

Jake was lying on the grass-covered floor.

His left leg was completely normal.

His teddy bear rested against his side.

Clover was drawing.

Jake looked at her.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You're still thinking about it."

Clover stopped drawing.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Maybe."

Jake sat up.

> **JAKE:**  
> "I'm fine."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

> **JAKE:**  
> "Really fine."

> **CLOVER:**  
> "I know."

Jake stared at her for a moment.

Then he reached over and placed one of his blue manifested flowers beside her notebook.

> **JAKE:**  
> "Then draw."

Clover looked at the flower.

She smiled faintly.

> **CLOVER:**  
> "Okay."

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake has fully recovered.

No medical restrictions remain.

No permanent injury was sustained.

The maintenance corridor remains closed pending structural inspection.

Clover requires no medical treatment.

However, C.O.R.E. recommends allowing her time to process the incident without repeatedly reminding her that Jake is "fine."

She already knows.

Knowing that someone survived does not automatically erase the memory of watching them get hurt.

Jake's body has already moved on.

Clover's mind has not.

---

## C.O.R.E. CLOSING NOTE

Jake's leg healed.

The fracture disappeared.

The pain disappeared.

The injury became another event his body could simply repair.

Clover's luck kept her safe.

But it could not keep Jake safe.

And perhaps that is what bothers her most.

Jake eventually wandered over to her.

He looked down at her drawing.

Then at the four-leaf clover beside his blue flower.

> **JAKE:**  
> "You okay?"

Clover looked up.

For once, she did not answer immediately.

Then:

> **CLOVER:**  
> "...Not yet."

Jake nodded.

He sat beside her.

He didn't ask why.

He didn't tell her she was overreacting.

He didn't say that everything was fine.

He simply stayed.

Clover continued drawing.

And for the first time since the catwalk fell, she stopped looking toward Jake's leg.
