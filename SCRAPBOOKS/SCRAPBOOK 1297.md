# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has resumed limited verbal communication.

His emotional condition, however, remains severely affected by the words he overheard regarding his mother.

The statement:

> **“A problematic and accidental child.”**

has become deeply embedded within Jake's emotional state.

Jake rarely discusses it directly.

He does not need to.

Personnel have identified a considerably more reliable indicator of Jake's current emotions:

> **His teddy bear.**

The bear is not merely a comfort object.

It contains Jake's heart.

Its heartbeat and manifested behavior appear to respond directly to Jake's emotional condition.

Consequently:

> **To understand Jake, observe the bear.**

---

# 1. THE WORDS

Jake has not forgotten what he heard.

He has not argued against it.

He has not asked whether the Archivists were telling the truth.

He has not asked what his mother meant.

Instead, the words appear to have settled into his thoughts.

They recur without being spoken.

The phrase:

> **“Accidental child.”**

appears particularly damaging.

Jake had spent decades believing that his mother had loved him.

The teddy bear was part of that belief.

His mother gave it to him.

He held it when he was afraid.

He carried it when he was alone.

He associated it with comfort.

Now the memory has become complicated.

The same object that once represented:

> **“Mom loved me.”**

now exists alongside:

> **“Mom didn't want me.”**

Jake has not found a way to reconcile the two.

---

# 2. JAKE'S SILENCE

Jake speaks again.

But there are subjects he avoids.

His mother is one of them.

If her name is mentioned, Jake frequently becomes quiet.

If someone asks whether he remembers her, he may simply look away.

If asked whether he wants to talk about her:

> **Jake:**  
> “No.”

That is often the end of the conversation.

Personnel are instructed to respect it.

---

# 3. THE BEAR AS AN EMOTIONAL INDICATOR

The teddy bear's behavior provides information that Jake does not verbally communicate.

Its heartbeat is directly linked to Jake.

More importantly, its posture and manifestation behavior change with him.

The bear may:

- sit normally against Jake's back;
    
- tighten its manifested grip around his shoulders;
    
- shift closer to his body;
    
- become completely still;
    
- move around to Jake's front;
    
- be pulled tightly against his chest;
    
- or, during severe distress, appear to cling to him.
    

These changes consistently correspond with Jake's emotional state.

C.O.R.E. therefore considers the bear a secondary emotional observation system.

Informally:

> **The bear tells us what Jake won't.**

---

# 4. CALM

When Jake is relatively calm, the bear remains on his back.

Its arms rest loosely around his shoulders.

The heartbeat is slow and steady.

> **thump.**

> **thump.**

Jake walks normally.

He may speak.

He may ask questions.

He may observe other residents.

This is currently considered Jake's most stable state.

---

# 5. CONTENTMENT

Jake's happiness remains uncommon.

When it occurs, however, the bear changes.

Its heartbeat becomes slightly lighter and faster.

Jake may occasionally move it around to his front.

He may hug it.

The bear's head rests against Jake's chest.

Jake has been observed quietly saying:

> **“He's okay.”**

When asked who he was referring to, Jake pointed at the bear.

He did not elaborate.

---

# 6. FEAR

Fear produces a much more obvious response.

The bear immediately tightens around Jake.

Jake tends to pull his shoulders inward.

The bear's heartbeat accelerates.

> **thump-thump.**

> **thump-thump.**

Jake becomes less verbal.

If the fear persists, he may bring the bear around to his front and hold it tightly.

The behavior resembles a child seeking comfort.

In this case, however, the child is literally holding his own heart.

---

# 7. SADNESS

Sadness produces the opposite pattern.

The bear becomes extremely still.

Its heartbeat slows.

Jake often sits down.

He may stare at nothing.

He may hold the bear without moving.

There is frequently no visible crying at first.

The bear simply becomes quiet.

Personnel have learned not to mistake this for emotional stability.

It is often the opposite.

---

# 8. SEVERE DISTRESS

During severe emotional distress, the bear's heartbeat becomes irregular.

Not medically unstable.

Anomalously irregular.

The rhythm may briefly become:

> **thump...**

> **thump-thump.**

> **...thump.**

Jake generally becomes completely silent during these episodes.

He may hold the bear against his chest with both arms.

His halo fragments dim.

He may remain in that position for several minutes.

This is considered a high-priority welfare indicator.

---

# 9. WHEN HIS MOTHER IS MENTIONED

The most consistent trigger is his mother.

During one observation, an unrelated conversation included the phrase:

> “When Jake was with his mother—”

Jake stopped walking.

The bear immediately tightened around his shoulders.

Its heartbeat increased.

Jake's expression changed.

> **Jake:**  
> “Don't.”

The person stopped.

Jake continued walking.

The bear remained tightly attached to him for several minutes afterward.

No further discussion occurred.

---

# 10. NORA'S UNDERSTANDING

Nora has become particularly skilled at interpreting the bear.

She does not treat its behavior as a replacement for asking Jake what he wants.

She simply recognizes the signs.

One evening:

The bear's heartbeat became faster.

Nora looked at Jake.

> **Nora:**  
> “Bad memory?”

Jake nodded.

She did not ask which one.

She simply walked beside him.

After several minutes, the heartbeat slowed.

---

# 11. NERI'S OBSERVATION

Neri has also recognized the pattern.

She once watched Jake sitting quietly with the bear against his chest.

The bear was almost completely still.

Neri wrote:

> **“Sad?”**

Jake nodded.

She wrote:

> **“Want company?”**

Jake nodded again.

Neri sat beside him.

No conversation followed.

The bear's heartbeat gradually became steadier.

---

# 12. AETERNA

Aeterna considers the bear's emotional responsiveness significant.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “It is difficult for him to hide.”

Nora looked at the bear.

> **Nora:**  
> “He doesn't have to say anything.”

Aeterna nodded.

> **Aeterna:**  
> “Exactly.”

Jake remained nearby.

The bear's heartbeat continued.

> **thump.**

---

# 13. C.O.R.E. GUIDANCE

Personnel should not treat the teddy bear as a diagnostic instrument in the conventional medical sense.

Its responses should be interpreted alongside Jake's behavior.

However, personnel are encouraged to pay attention to it.

Especially when Jake himself appears outwardly calm.

Jake has demonstrated a tendency to minimize his own distress verbally.

The bear is considerably less capable of doing so.

It responds.

It tightens.

It beats faster.

It becomes still.

It clings.

Jake may say:

> **“I'm okay.”**

while the bear says:

> **thump-thump-thump-thump.**

Personnel should notice the discrepancy.

---

# 14. THE HEART OF THE MATTER

There is an uncomfortable irony in Jake's current condition.

His heart was once something nobody could see.

Now everyone can hear it.

And it reacts to things Jake refuses to talk about.

His mother's words hurt him deeply.

He does not need to repeat them.

He does not need to explain them.

His teddy bear already carries the evidence.

Every time Jake remembers.

Every time he becomes afraid.

Every time he wonders why his mother did not want him.

Every time the old belief that she loved him collides with what he overheard—

The bear responds.

---

# FINAL OBSERVATION

At 22:18, Jake was sitting on the floor beside Nora.

His teddy bear was on his back.

Its arms were loosely around his shoulders.

> **thump.**

Jake was speaking quietly about something unrelated.

He mentioned the cafeteria.

He asked whether Alice would let him ride her scooter.

He asked Nora whether Frost was awake.

For several minutes, he sounded almost normal.

Then someone walked past and mentioned Jake's childhood.

Jake stopped.

The bear tightened around him.

> **thump-thump.**

Jake looked down.

His voice became quiet.

> **Jake:**  
> “I don't want to talk about Mom.”

Nora nodded.

> **Nora:**  
> “Okay.”

Nothing more was said.

Jake leaned backward slightly.

The bear remained against him.

Its heartbeat gradually slowed.

> **thump.**

> **thump.**

Jake never needed to explain how badly those words had hurt him.

The bear already knew.

And, perhaps more importantly,

**the bear makes sure everyone else can know, too.**
