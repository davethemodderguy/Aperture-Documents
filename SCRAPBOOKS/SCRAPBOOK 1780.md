# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The feline situation has entered a new phase.

**Endmin Merryweather has returned to work.**

**Jake has returned to his private residence.**

Both remain in feline form.

Endmin's return to work was approved following a period of rest and a review of her immediate workload. Additional safeguards are being implemented to prevent her from immediately returning to the overworking habits that preceded her transformation.

Jake, meanwhile, has decided that he would rather spend his time in his own room.

His room already contains a cat bed.

He eats ordinary food.

He has access to water, his usual belongings, and his teddy bear.

He is comfortable.

He is also increasingly willing to express his displeasure through his teeth.

This has prompted three separate reminders to SAFEHOUSE personnel.

1. Jake is a person, not a pet.
    
2. Jake does not require canned cat food simply because he currently looks like a cat.
    
3. **Jerry needs to stop giving Jake canned cat food.**
    

The third reminder was necessary.

Apparently, the first two were not enough.

# 1. ENDMIN MERRYWEATHER — RETURN TO DUTY

Endmin formally resumed her duties as Director of the Central Execution Department after her period of rest.

Her return was not immediate.

Belle reviewed her working arrangements before authorizing her return, and the department confirmed that temporary delegations had been established for duties that did not require Endmin's personal involvement.

The following conditions remain in effect:

- Endmin is expected to take scheduled breaks.
    
- Routine tasks are to be delegated when appropriate.
    
- Non-urgent work is not to be carried into her personal resting hours.
    
- Departmental personnel are not to treat her recent transformation as evidence that her workload concerns have somehow resolved themselves.
    
- Any further signs of significant exhaustion must be addressed rather than ignored.
    

Endmin has accepted these arrangements.

Mostly.

At 09:15, she was observed reviewing a report.

At 09:42, she was observed reviewing another report.

At 10:03, she was observed attempting to review a third report during her scheduled break.

Belle intervened.

**BELLE:** "Endmin."

**ENDMIN:** "Meow."

**BELLE:** "Break."

Endmin looked at the report.

Then at her mother.

**ENDMIN:** "Meow."

**BELLE:** "The report will still exist in fifteen minutes."

Endmin's ears lowered slightly.

She put the report down.

Belle nodded.

**BELLE:** "Good."

She left.

Endmin waited approximately six seconds before resting her head on the desk.

C.O.R.E. considers this an acceptable compromise.

For now.

# 2. JAKE HAS RETURNED TO HIS ROOM

Jake has elected to leave the shared feline resting area and return to his private SAFEHOUSE residence.

This was his own decision.

He was not asked to leave, and no one attempted to persuade him to remain in the company of the other residents.

His room was already equipped with a cat bed from his previous feline period.

The bed remains available.

Jake appears to remember it.

Upon entering his room, he walked directly to the bed, circled it twice, and settled into it.

His teddy bear was placed within easy reach.

Jake tucked his paws underneath himself.

He closed his eyes.

Approximately ten minutes later, a welfare officer checked on him.

Jake opened one eye.

The officer stopped at the doorway.

**WELFARE OFFICER:** "Everything all right?"

Jake blinked.

**JAKE:** "Meow."

**WELFARE OFFICER:** "Need anything?"

Jake closed his eye again.

The officer interpreted this as a preference not to continue the conversation.

The door was left at its usual safe position.

Jake went back to sleep.

**ASSESSMENT:** Jake appears content with his current accommodation.

There is no requirement for him to spend his day in a communal area simply because several residents happen to enjoy having a cat around.

He may socialize when he wants to.

He may also spend an entire afternoon in his room.

Both are acceptable.

# 3. FOOD CLARIFICATION — JAKE EATS NORMAL FOOD

A recurring misunderstanding has been identified among SAFEHOUSE personnel.

Jake is currently a cat.

Jake is not therefore required to eat commercially prepared cat food.

His current feline form has not invalidated his established dietary needs.

He continues to eat ordinary food appropriate to his needs and current medical condition.

His meals are prepared and monitored accordingly.

There is no medical justification for replacing his normal meals with canned cat food merely because he has four paws, pointed ears, and a tail.

This should not have required a formal notice.

Unfortunately, Jerry exists.

---

# 4. JERRY, THE CANNED CAT FOOD INCIDENT

The first incident occurred at approximately 11:20.

Jerry entered Jake's residence carrying a bowl.

The bowl contained canned cat food.

Jake was lying in his cat bed.

He raised his head.

He sniffed the air.

He looked at the bowl.

Then he looked at Jerry.

**JERRY:** "Lunch!"

Jake stared.

**JERRY:** "I thought you might like something appropriate for a cat."

Jake's ears flattened.

Jerry placed the bowl on the floor.

**JERRY:** "It's chicken flavor."

Jake got up.

Jerry smiled.

Jake approached the bowl.

He sniffed it once.

Then he looked back at Jerry.

**JAKE:** "No."

Jerry froze.

**JERRY:** "...You can talk?"

Jake stared at him.

**JAKE:** "Yes."

**JERRY:** "Oh."

A pause.

**JERRY:** "Well, you still might like it."

Jake turned around and returned to his cat bed.

Jerry remained beside the untouched bowl.

The incident should have ended there.

It did not.

## SECOND INCIDENT

At 13:05, Jerry returned.

He was carrying a different can.

The label indicated fish flavor.

Jake was grooming one paw.

Jerry set the can on the nearby table.

**JERRY:** "What about this one?"

Jake stopped grooming.

He looked at Jerry.

His tail began moving from side to side.

**JAKE:** "Jerry."

**JERRY:** "Yes?"

**JAKE:** "Stop."

**JERRY:** "I'm just trying to—"

Jake walked over and bit Jerry's hand.

Not hard enough to cause significant injury.

Hard enough to communicate a very clear objection.

Jerry yelped.

Jake released him immediately and returned to his bed.

Jerry stared at his hand.

Then at Jake.

**JERRY:** "YOU BIT ME!"

Jake tucked his paws underneath himself.

**JAKE:** "Meow."

The incident was recorded as the first confirmed instance of Jake using a bite to express irritation during the current feline period.

The bowl was removed.

The fish-flavored can was confiscated from Jerry's possession for the remainder of the day.

The cafeteria was informed that Jake's normal meal arrangements remained unchanged.

# 5. FORMAL NOTICE TO JERRY

**TO:** Jerry  
**FROM:** C.O.R.E. Resident Welfare Division  
**SUBJECT:** Unauthorized Dietary Substitution — Jake

Jerry is hereby instructed to discontinue offering Jake canned cat food as a substitute for his ordinary meals.

This instruction applies regardless of flavor.

Chicken is not an exception.

Fish is not an exception.

Any future flavor Jerry discovers is also not an exception.

Jake's current appearance does not constitute dietary consent.

His willingness to sniff a bowl does not constitute dietary consent.

His refusal to eat a bowl does not mean Jerry should purchase another bowl.

And biting Jerry after repeated unwanted offerings should not have been necessary to establish this boundary.

If Jake requires a dietary adjustment, it will be determined through appropriate medical guidance.

Until then:

**Feed Jake his normal food.**

Jerry has acknowledged the notice.

When asked whether he understood, he responded:

**JERRY:** "I understand."

When asked whether he would stop bringing canned cat food, he hesitated.

**JERRY:** "...Even the expensive kind?"

**C.O.R.E. WELFARE OFFICER:** "Especially the expensive kind."

**NOTICE STATUS:** ACKNOWLEDGED.

**COMPLIANCE STATUS:** UNDER OBSERVATION.

# 6. THE BITING PROBLEM

The canned food incident was not isolated.

Jake has recently become more willing to bite when annoyed.

Observed triggers include:

- Repeated unwanted handling.
    
- Being disturbed while sleeping.
    
- Someone placing an object directly in front of his face.
    
- Unwanted attempts to pick him up.
    
- Persistent attempts to make him play.
    
- People ignoring his first refusal.
    
- Jerry bringing canned cat food into his room.
    

The biting appears to function as a more immediate physical boundary than Jake's usual verbal or nonverbal responses.

In some cases, he gives warning signs first.

His ears flatten.

His tail begins flicking sharply.

He turns his head away.

He stops engaging.

He may issue a short verbal refusal.

If these signs are ignored, he may bite.

Personnel are instructed to respond to the earlier signs rather than waiting for the bite.

Jake is not required to tolerate unwanted contact simply because he is currently small enough to fit in someone's arms.

He is also not required to remain endlessly patient when someone ignores his refusal.

However, biting can injure people and should not become the default method of communication. Personnel should give him space, respect his warnings, and seek medical assessment for any bite that breaks the skin.

Jake should not be punished, cornered, or forcibly restrained merely for communicating that he has had enough.

The goal is to prevent situations from escalating.

Not to teach him that nobody will listen until he stops expressing discomfort altogether.

---

# 7. A SHORT GUIDE TO INTERACTING WITH JAKE

**If Jake approaches you:** Let him decide whether he wants contact.

**If Jake rubs against your hand:** Gentle petting may be welcome. Do not assume that he wants to be picked up.

**If Jake lies in his bed:** Leave him alone unless there is a genuine welfare concern.

**If Jake turns away:** Stop trying to get his attention.

**If Jake's ears flatten and his tail starts flicking:** Give him space.

**If Jake says no:** That is a complete answer.

**If Jake bites:** Withdraw calmly, attend to any injury, and review what happened. Do not retaliate.

**If you are Jerry:** Stop bringing him canned cat food.

This final instruction has been highlighted because previous communications apparently failed to achieve the desired outcome.

# 9. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Endmin's return to work and Jake's return to his room demonstrate that the residents are making different choices about how they wish to spend their time during the ongoing transformations.

Endmin wants to resume her role, provided her workload is managed more sustainably.

Jake wants his own room, his cat bed, his teddy bear, and his normal food.

Neither preference is inherently problematic.

The principal concern is ensuring that Endmin's return does not become another cycle of excessive work, and that Jake's feline form does not encourage others to disregard his autonomy.

A cat-shaped director remains a director.

A cat-shaped child remains a child.

Neither owes anyone constant availability.

Neither exists to entertain the residents.

And neither should have to bite someone to make a simple refusal count.
