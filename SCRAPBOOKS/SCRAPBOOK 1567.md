# APERTURE INDUSTRIES

## C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE

### RESIDENT WELFARE & MEDICAL DIVISION

**DOCUMENT ID:** C.O.R.E.-WEL-4C-JAKE-196  
**CLASSIFICATION:** INTERNAL — RESIDENT WELFARE  
**STATUS:** CRITICAL MONITORING  
**SUBJECT:** Jake — Week Fifteen Isolation / Sustained Self-Neglect  
**LOCATION:** SAFEHOUSE — Jake's Residence

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jake has now spent approximately **fifteen weeks in progressively severe social isolation.**

The situation has deteriorated beyond ordinary solitude.

Jake no longer leaves his room.

He does not seek out other residents.

He does not appear to expect anyone to visit.

He has stopped engaging with most of the activities that previously occupied his time.

He no longer reads regularly.

He rarely draws.

He no longer wanders the SAFEHOUSE.

He does not seek conversation.

He does not request assistance.

He remains awake.

And, most concerningly, his nutritional behavior has changed.

Jake has begun eating:

- grass growing across his floor;
    
- manifested flowers;
    
- portions of the 2D-sketch vegetation he previously created for comfort.
    

He has effectively begun **eating his own environment.**

---

# 1. ROOM STATUS

Jake's room remains the only SAFEHOUSE residential space without conventional furniture.

The floor remains covered in grass.

Small manifested flowers grow between the blades.

Plush toys remain scattered around the mattress.

Books remain where they were left.

Drawings remain partially completed.

The room is dim.

Jake rarely activates the primary lighting.

The most consistent illumination comes from his own body.

Even that has become weaker.

Not absent.

Simply subdued.

---

# 2. OBSERVED NUTRITION

C.O.R.E. initially assumed Jake was still obtaining food from the cafeteria.

This assumption was incorrect.

During routine welfare observation, personnel noticed that Jake's food consumption outside his room had dropped to nearly zero.

No discarded cafeteria containers were found.

No food deliveries had been requested.

No resident reported sharing meals with him.

Further observation established that Jake had begun consuming the vegetation inside his room.

He eats grass.

He eats flowers.

He occasionally pulls one of the small manifested flowers from the ground and eats it slowly.

There is no evidence that he is doing so as an anomalous experiment.

He appears to simply regard them as food.

---

# 3. TASTE REPORT

The manifested flowers were sampled under controlled medical review.

They are technically edible.

Their nutritional value is extremely limited.

Their flavor has been described as:

> **“Edible crayons.”**

One medical technician described the taste as:

> “Like someone made a flower out of wax, gave it the faintest sweetness, and then decided that was acceptable.”

Jake appears to disagree.

He continues eating them.

When asked whether he likes them:

Jake looked at the flower in his hand.

Then shrugged.

> **JAKE:**  
> “They're food.”

No further explanation was provided.

---

# 4. GRASS CONSUMPTION

Jake has also begun eating the grass covering his floor.

Unlike the flowers, the grass does not appear to have been deliberately created as food.

It is simply present.

Jake pulls small amounts from the floor.

He eats them.

Then replaces his hand against the grass.

He does not appear distressed by the behavior.

He does not appear embarrassed.

There is no indication that he considers it unusual.

This is perhaps the most concerning aspect.

The behavior has become **normal to him.**

---

# 5. TEDDY BEAR

Jake's teddy bear remains with him.

The bear's heartbeat remains detectable.

Jake continues keeping it close.

When eating, he sometimes places the bear beside him.

On several occasions, he has stopped eating and simply rested his forehead against the bear.

No verbal interaction was recorded.

He did not appear to be sleeping.

Jake does not sleep.

He remained awake throughout each observation period.

---

# 6. ACTIVITY LEVEL

Jake's daily activity has decreased substantially.

### Previous baseline:

- reading;
    
- drawing;
    
- exploring the SAFEHOUSE;
    
- talking with residents;
    
- spending time with Bao;
    
- visiting Neri;
    
- interacting with Nora;
    
- observing the sky;
    
- playing with other residents;
    
- working on personal projects.
    

### Current behavior:

- sitting;
    
- lying on the mattress;
    
- holding the teddy bear;
    
- eating grass;
    
- eating flowers;
    
- staring at the ceiling;
    
- staring at nothing;
    
- occasionally looking toward the doorway.
    

The last behavior is particularly notable.

Jake still looks toward the doorway whenever footsteps pass.

He does not get up.

He does not call out.

He simply watches.

When the footsteps continue past his room:

Jake looks away.

---

# 7. DOORWAY OBSERVATION

Personnel have recorded the following pattern repeatedly.

Footsteps approach.

Jake raises his head.

His eyes brighten slightly.

The footsteps pass.

The light in his eyes diminishes again.

This has occurred frequently enough that C.O.R.E. considers the response behavioral rather than incidental.

Jake still notices people.

He still hopes, at least briefly, that someone might enter.

He simply no longer expects them to.

---

# 8. COMMUNICATION

Jake has become almost completely nonverbal.

When spoken to through the doorway, he may respond with:

> “Okay.”

> “Yeah.”

> “I'm fine.”

> “Nothing.”

Or:

> “I'm just tired.”

These responses should not be accepted at face value.

C.O.R.E. has previously established that Jake uses socially reassuring statements when he does not wish to burden others.

His current behavior provides substantially stronger evidence than his words.

---

# 9. BAO

Bao has not intentionally abandoned Jake.

She remains unaware of the full extent of his deterioration.

This distinction is important.

No resident has made a conscious decision that Jake is unimportant.

The SAFEHOUSE remains heavily occupied with ongoing responsibilities.

Medical cases.

Engineering work.

Logistics.

Dimensional operations.

External affairs.

CED obligations.

Research.

Security.

One task follows another.

Jake has simply stopped being part of anyone's immediate routine.

That absence has now lasted long enough to become its own routine.

---

# 10. NERI

Neri has not visited Jake's room during the current observation period.

She remains occupied with her own work and personal routines.

There is no indication that she has stopped caring about him.

However, Jake has apparently stopped expecting her to appear.

A previous note recorded that Jake would sometimes look toward the hallway when Neri passed nearby.

This behavior has now ceased.

He no longer checks.

---

# 11. NORA

Nora has also not visited.

Jake previously regarded Nora as one of the people most likely to spontaneously appear.

He no longer waits for her.

The absence of anticipation is concerning.

A child who expects nobody to come eventually stops looking toward the door.

---

# 12. AETERNA

Aeterna remains aware of Jake.

However, she has not yet recognized the full extent of the deterioration.

This is partially due to the same systemic problem affecting the rest of SAFEHOUSE:

Everyone believes there will be time later.

Later has now lasted fifteen weeks.

---

# 13. PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

C.O.R.E. does not currently interpret Jake's behavior as deliberate self-destruction.

There is insufficient evidence for that conclusion.

Instead, the observed behavior is more consistent with **extreme withdrawal and diminished self-maintenance.**

Jake is no longer asking:

> “When will someone come?”

He appears to have reached:

> **“Nobody is coming.”**

The distinction is critical.

The first contains hope.

The second does not.

---

# 14. THE ROOM AS A CLOSED SYSTEM

Jake's room has gradually become self-contained.

The grass provides food.

The flowers provide color.

The drawings provide objects.

The teddy bear provides companionship.

The mattress provides a place to remain.

The walls provide separation.

Jake does not need to leave.

Therefore, he does not.

The room has become a complete environment in which Jake can technically continue existing without interacting with anyone.

This is not healthy.

It is, however, sustainable.

And that may be worse.

---

# 15. THE FLOWERS

One flower was observed growing beside Jake's mattress.

It was small.

Blue.

Drawn originally with crude crayon-like lines.

Jake reached down.

Pulled it from the ground.

Looked at it.

Then ate it.

Afterward, he stared at the empty patch where it had been.

A few seconds later, another flower manifested in its place.

Jake did not react.

He simply picked up the replacement.

---

# 16. C.O.R.E. CONCERN

Jake's anomalous physiology creates a dangerous misconception.

He can survive conditions that would kill an ordinary child.

He is a star.

His body contains astronomical energy.

He can generate enormous amounts of power.

None of these facts make him emotionally resilient.

He is still a six-year-old.

A six-year-old who has spent fifteen weeks receiving almost no meaningful social contact.

His ability to remain alive should not be confused with wellbeing.

---

# 17. WELFARE CLASSIFICATION

Current status:

> **CRITICAL — SUSTAINED SOCIAL ISOLATION**

Primary concerns:

- prolonged isolation;
    
- cessation of voluntary social contact;
    
- severe reduction in recreational behavior;
    
- reduced nutritional quality;
    
- environmental self-consumption;
    
- diminished expectation of assistance;
    
- persistent wakefulness;
    
- emotional withdrawal;
    
- continued suppression of distress;
    
- increasing dependence on self-generated environmental objects for basic comfort.
    

Immediate forced intervention is **not** recommended without medical necessity.

However, continued passive observation alone is no longer considered sufficient.

---

# 18. C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

At 03:17, Jake was observed sitting on the floor.

His knees were against his chest.

The teddy bear was beside him.

His wings were folded.

A small flower grew between his knees.

Jake pulled it from the ground.

He stared at it.

Then ate it.

The security observation system recorded no movement for approximately six minutes.

Eventually:

> **JAKE:**  
> “Tastes like crayons.”

He looked down at the grass.

He pulled another blade.

He ate it.

Then he looked toward the door.

Nothing happened.

Jake lowered his head.

---

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

Jake has survived fifteen weeks without being meaningfully reintegrated into the social life of SAFEHOUSE.

That survival should not be considered success.

He is eating grass.

He is eating flowers.

He is living inside a room he has gradually turned into a tiny ecosystem because nobody has given him a reason to leave it.

The flowers he created to make the room less empty have become food.

The grass beneath his feet has become a meal.

The teddy bear has become his primary companion.

And the doorway has become something he no longer approaches.

C.O.R.E. cannot allow this condition to continue indefinitely.

Jake does not need to be convinced that he is loved through a dramatic intervention.

He does not need a speech.

He does not need everyone to stop working.

He needs someone to **remember that he is there.**

Someone to sit on the floor.

Someone to eat beside him.

Someone to read quietly in the same room.

Someone to remain long enough that the footsteps outside his door stop being something he waits for.

Because fifteen weeks ago, Jake was isolated.

Now he has built a life around being isolated.

And that is considerably harder to undo.

---

At 04:02, Jake finished eating.

There was a small pile of flower stems beside him.

He picked up his teddy bear.

Held it against his chest.

Then quietly said:

> **“Goodnight.”**

He did not sleep.

He never does.

He simply remained there in the dark.

Waiting for morning.

Or perhaps no longer waiting for anything at all.
