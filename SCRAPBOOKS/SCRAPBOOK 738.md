### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE-WILDLIFE / ANOMALOUS OBSERVATION LOG**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — MEMORY DEGRADATION**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — C.O.R.E. EYES ONLY  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **SEVERE MEMORY LOSS / TRANSPARENCY PROGRESSION**

---

# **OBSERVATION**

Alice has forgotten too much.

At first, the forgotten things were small.

A conversation.

A meal.

A place she had visited.

A drawing she had made.

Now it is becoming different.

Alice has begun forgetting things that once seemed permanent.

---

# **WHAT ALICE REMEMBERS**

Alice still knows a few things.

She knows she is a bunny.

She knows she lives in the Treehouse.

She knows she likes being around people.

She knows she has a scooter.

She knows she likes drawing.

She knows she is loved.

Sometimes.

---

# **WHAT SHE HAS FORGOTTEN**

Alice has forgotten parts of her relationships.

She has forgotten things people have done for her.

She has forgotten why certain objects are important.

She sometimes looks at her journal and doesn't recognize her own handwriting.

She has forgotten some of the names she worked so hard to preserve.

The most painful incident occurred when she opened the journal to a page about Corin.

Alice stared at the drawing.

She recognized the face.

But not the name.

She slowly wrote:

> **"Who?"**

Corin had to leave the room for several minutes.

---

# **THE JOURNAL**

The journal is no longer simply a memory aid.

It has become a record of Alice's life.

Every page tells her something she once knew.

Who her friends are.

Who loves her.

Where she lives.

Who built her scooter.

Who carries her when she gets tired.

Who sits beside her when she cries.

Alice reads the pages repeatedly.

Sometimes she remembers.

Sometimes she doesn't.

Sometimes she reads the same page twice within an hour.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Alice has noticed the gaps.

She knows something is wrong.

She wrote:

> **"Alice forgetting."**

Then:

> **"Alice trying remember."**

Then, after a long pause:

> **"Why Alice forget?"**

Nobody had a good answer.

---

# **TRANSPARENCY**

The physical progression continues alongside the memory loss.

Alice is visibly more transparent than she was when the phenomenon began.

Her body remains tangible.

Her voice remains absent.

Her drawings remain beautiful.

But increasingly, light passes through her.

Sometimes someone has to look twice to realize she is standing nearby.

---

# **THE WORST MOMENT**

Alice was sitting with Belle.

Belle was holding a carrot.

Alice looked at her.

She smiled.

Then asked:

> **"Belle?"**

Belle froze.

Alice had forgotten her name.

Belle smiled anyway.

> "Yeah."

Alice looked relieved.

> **"Friend?"**

Belle nodded.

> "Best friend."

Alice wrote it down immediately.

> **"Belle friend."**

She underlined it.

Then hugged Belle.

Belle hugged her back.

---

# **CORIN'S RESPONSE**

Corin has stopped asking Alice whether she remembers her.

Instead, she tells Alice.

Every day.

> "I'm Corin."

> "I'm your Big Sis."

> "I found you."

> "You live with us."

> "You are loved."

Sometimes Alice remembers.

Sometimes she simply writes it down.

Either way, Corin repeats it.

---

# **THE RULE**

Nobody is allowed to become frustrated when Alice forgets.

Not when she asks the same question twice.

Not when she forgets someone's name.

Not when she forgets something she learned yesterday.

Not when she forgets something that happened an hour ago.

The burden of remembering cannot be placed on Alice.

**She is already carrying enough.**

---

# **CURRENT CONCERN**

Alice has not yet forgotten herself.

She still recognizes the name **Alice**.

She still understands that the little white rabbit in the journal is supposed to be her.

But the distance between:

> **"Alice is forgetting things."**

and

> **"Alice doesn't remember being Alice."**

is becoming increasingly frightening.

Nobody will allow that line to be crossed willingly.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

Alice spent so much time writing:

> **DO NOT FORGET**

Now everyone else understands why.

The journal was never really for Alice alone.

It is for everyone.

So that when Alice forgets—

someone else can remember for her.
