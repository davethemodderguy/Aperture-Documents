# **APERTURE INDUSTRIES**

## **C.O.R.E. – CENTRAL OPERATIONS & RESEARCH EXECUTIVE**

### **PROJECT SAFEHOUSE – CARE STRATEGY UPDATE**

## **SUBJECT: CORIN LOCKWOOD – SUPPORT THROUGH PRESENCE**

**Classification:** LEVEL 7 – Medical & Internal Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

After extensive consultation with Medical Services, Psychological Services, and Corin herself, Project SAFEHOUSE adjusted how residents interact with Corin during periods of emotional distress.

The objective is not to ignore her suffering.

Nor is it to repeatedly challenge or "fix" it.

Staff observed that frequent attempts to persuade Corin out of her painful thoughts often left her more exhausted and withdrawn.

As a result, the emphasis shifted.

From trying to solve her pain...

To making sure she never has to endure it alone.

---

# **REVISED APPROACH**

Residents no longer respond to every expression of sadness with reassurance or debate.

Instead, they focus on quiet companionship.

If Corin wants to talk...

Someone listens.

If she wants silence...

Someone sits nearby.

If she wants to read...

Someone reads with her.

If she chooses to sleep beneath a tree...

Someone checks that she has blankets, food, and water, then gives her the space she requested.

The goal is to respect Corin's choices while ensuring she remains safe and connected to the people who care about her.

---

# **MART**

Mart summarized the approach simply.

> "Not every problem has a conversation."

> "Sometimes it just needs company."

He often spends evenings reading near Corin without saying a word.

Neither considers the silence awkward.

---

# **BELLE**

Belle no longer asks,

> "Are you okay?"

She already knows the answer is complicated.

Instead, she asks,

> "Tea?"

Sometimes Corin accepts.

Sometimes she doesn't.

Belle never takes either answer personally.

---

# **KENT**

Kent still makes coffee every evening.

He quietly prepares a mug of warm milk beside it.

He never announces who it is for.

Some nights the mug is still there in the morning.

Most nights...

It isn't.

---

# **CINDER**

Cinder stopped trying to convince Corin that she was wrong about herself.

Instead, she treats Corin exactly as she always has.

She hands over paperwork.

Complains about budgets.

Asks for calculations.

Laughs at dry jokes.

Corin is reminded, through ordinary routine, that she still belongs.

---

# **LUMI**

One afternoon, Lumi asked Medical Services,

> "Should I keep telling her everything will be alright?"

The attending psychologist gently shook their head.

> "Tell her you'll stay."

That evening, Lumi found Corin sitting outside with a book.

She sat beside her.

No speeches.

No advice.

After several minutes, Corin quietly asked,

> "...You're not going to try to cheer me up?"

Lumi smiled.

> "Only if you ask."

Another few minutes passed.

Corin leaned against her mother's shoulder.

Neither spoke again until sunset.

---

# **MEDICAL ASSESSMENT**

Psychological Services emphasize that this approach is **not** based on believing Corin's pain no longer matters.

Rather, it reflects an understanding that chronic trauma cannot always be eased through words alone.

The most helpful support is often consistent presence, respect for her boundaries, and gentle encouragement to remain connected with others.

---

# **CURRENT STATUS**

Corin's pain has not disappeared.

Everyone in SAFEHOUSE knows that.

They also know they cannot erase fifteen years of trauma.

So they stopped trying to force healing to happen on a timetable.

Instead, they chose something quieter.

To leave a seat beside them.

To save her a cup of tea.

To read in silence.

To walk with her when she asks.

And to remind Corin, not with speeches but with ordinary daily life, that she never has to face that pain completely alone.

**End of Project SAFEHOUSE Care Strategy Update**
