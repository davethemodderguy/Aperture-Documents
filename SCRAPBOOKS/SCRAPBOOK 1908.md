## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following Sora's prolonged withdrawal into his den, SAFEHOUSE welfare personnel have begun incorporating occasional, gentle head pats into their welfare visits.

The measure is deliberately simple.

When approaching Sora, a familiar and trusted person may gently pat his head, provided he appears comfortable with the contact. This is not to be performed as an obligation, reward, behavioral intervention, or attempt to coax him out of his den.

The intention is to offer a small, consistent reminder of affection without requiring him to communicate.

Sora remains mute. His original whiteboard has been destroyed, and he continues to refuse the replacement.

Consequently, staff must pay particular attention to his body language.

A head pat must stop immediately if he recoils, stiffens, pulls away, or otherwise appears uncomfortable.

**The objective is not to make Sora respond. It is to let him receive affection without having to ask for it.**

---

## 2. IMPLEMENTATION

Head pats are permitted during ordinary welfare visits, provided the following conditions are met:

- The person approaching Sora is familiar to him.
    
- Their movements are slow and visible.
    
- They do not reach into his den unexpectedly.
    
- Contact is brief and gentle.
    
- His response is observed rather than assumed.
    
- No one insists on continuing if he does not appear receptive.
    

Staff are specifically advised against surrounding him or taking turns patting his head.

This is not a group activity.

One person, occasionally, is sufficient.

Mia and Jake may offer affection naturally, but neither child is expected to visit on a schedule or provide emotional support every day.

## 3. OBSERVATION LOG

### 3.1 Jake — Morning Visit

Jake approached Sora's den in the morning.

Sora was sitting inside, facing the wall.

**JAKE:** Hi, Sora.

No response.

Jake sat near the entrance.

**JAKE:** I'm going to give you a head pat, okay?

He waited briefly before gently reaching forward.

His hand rested on Sora's head for a moment, carefully brushing his hair.

Sora did not move away.

Jake smiled.

**JAKE:** There.

He gave him one more gentle pat.

**JAKE:** Good morning.

Sora remained silent.

Jake did not ask him to come outside. He simply sat nearby for a little while before leaving.

### 3.2 Mia — Afternoon Visit

Mia found Sora sitting beside the white wool scarf.

She approached slowly.

**MIA:** Can I?

Sora looked toward her.

He did not visibly recoil or withdraw.

Mia gently patted his head.

**MIA:** There you go.

She carefully smoothed a few stray hairs.

**MIA:** We made that scarf for you, remember?

Sora looked at it.

Mia did not ask him to wear it.

She gave him another gentle pat before leaving him to rest.

### 3.3 Welfare Staff — Evening Check

A familiar welfare worker approached during the evening check.

Sora was awake.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Hello, little one.

The worker crouched near the entrance.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Just checking on you.

After allowing Sora time to notice their presence, the worker gently patted his head.

Sora remained still.

The worker withdrew their hand.

**STAFF MEMBER:** Rest well.

No further interaction was attempted.

---

## 4. RESPONSE AND ASSESSMENT

Sora has not demonstrated a significant change in his overall social behavior.

He continues to spend most of his time inside his den.

However, occasional head pats have been tolerated during the observed interactions.

C.O.R.E. does not interpret this as evidence of recovery, nor does the absence of resistance automatically indicate consent.

Staff must remain attentive to nonverbal signs of discomfort.

The measure may provide familiar physical reassurance, but it cannot replace communication access, psychological support, medical care, or the restoration of a stable daily routine.

**Additional observation:** Sora's neck injuries remain a consideration. Staff must avoid touching or applying pressure to his neck. Head pats must remain gentle and confined to the top of his head.

---

## 5. PERSONNEL GUIDANCE

The following instruction is now included in Floor 7 welfare guidance:

**Give Sora a gentle head pat occasionally, when he appears receptive.**

Do not make a ceremony out of it.

Do not announce that he is improving because he tolerated one.

Do not expect him to smile.

Do not ask him to reciprocate.

And, most importantly, do not use affection as a substitute for listening to his needs.

Sometimes, the most appropriate interaction may consist of entering his field of view, gently patting his head, and leaving him in peace.

---

## 6. CLOSING NOTE — A LITTLE AFFECTION

Jake used to have Sora following him everywhere.

Now, Jake has to visit Sora's den.

He still brings things. Sometimes toys, sometimes food, sometimes nothing at all.

Mia visits, too.

And occasionally, one of them gives Sora a little head pat.

There is no dramatic response.

No sudden smile. No excited wagging. No immediate return to the common room.

Sometimes Sora simply sits there.

But he is not being asked to do anything.

He does not have to write a message. He does not have to explain why he is sad. He does not have to pretend that everything is fine.

For a little while, someone is simply being kind to him.

Perhaps that is all he can manage right now.

And that is enough.

**He does not need to earn affection by coming out of his den.**

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
