## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The prolonged withdrawal of Sora has culminated in a medical emergency.

After weeks of isolation following his argument with Mia, Sora was discovered unconscious inside his personal den. He had inflicted a severe injury to his wrist.

Beside him was a short message written in blood:

**"Not your fault."**

Sora cannot die.

That fact has never meant that he cannot suffer.

His anomalous physiology prevents death, but it does not provide automatic regeneration or healing. Injuries remain injuries. Blood loss remains dangerous. Pain remains pain. His body does not simply repair itself because it is incapable of reaching a permanent end.

Sora can be trapped in an injured, suffering state without the possibility of death bringing that suffering to an end.

The discovery prompted an immediate emergency response.

Mia has since learned what happened. Jake, unable to keep the earlier confrontation a secret, had told her about the argument. However, the final realization came from something much smaller: a familiar habit Sora had developed during their childhood games in the snow.

Mia recognized the message.

She understood why he had written it.

And she understood that her brother had been trying to reassure her even while he was in unbearable pain.

---

## 2. INCIDENT DISCOVERY — THE DEN

**Location:** SAFEHOUSE Floor 7  
**Discovery:** Welfare inspection  
**Subject condition:** Unconscious; significant wrist injury

A welfare inspection was conducted after Sora's prolonged absence and lack of meaningful interaction raised further concern.

Upon entering the den, personnel discovered Sora lying unconscious on the floor.

A wrist injury was immediately apparent. Blood was present on the floor, and a short written message had been left nearby.

The message read:

**"Not your fault."**

The scene was secured.

Medical personnel were summoned immediately. Staff were instructed not to move Sora unnecessarily and to prioritize controlling the bleeding, assessing his condition, and providing emergency care.

The inspection was no longer a routine welfare check.

It had become a medical emergency.

### Medical clarification

Sora's inability to die must not be mistaken for resistance to injury.

His condition presents an unusual and serious medical problem:

- He can sustain severe physical injuries.
    
- He experiences pain normally.
    
- He can lose blood.
    
- He does not naturally regenerate.
    
- Injuries do not automatically close or repair themselves.
    
- His inability to die does not prevent prolonged suffering or medical complications.
    

**Sora's immortality is not a protective measure against self-inflicted injury.**

Medical personnel must treat every injury according to its actual severity, without assuming that his anomalous condition will resolve it.

## 3. THE MESSAGE — "NOT YOUR FAULT"

The words were brief.

There was no lengthy explanation, no accusation, and no description of what had happened inside the den.

Only three words:

**NOT YOUR FAULT.**

The message was written in a manner familiar to Mia.

It was not simply the wording that she recognized.

It was the habit behind it.

During their childhood, Sora and Mia sometimes played outside in the cold snow. Sora had a particular way of reassuring Mia when she became upset or blamed herself for something.

He would leave her little messages in the snow.

The words were familiar.

The intention was familiar.

Sora had once used that habit to reassure his little sister during ordinary childhood games.

Now, inside his den, he had used the same reassurance after weeks of emotional suffering.

Mia recognized the connection.

And she understood.

---

## 4. JAKE'S DISCLOSURE — THE SECRET DOES NOT STAY HIDDEN

Jake had previously struggled to keep the confrontation from Mia.

He eventually told her what had happened.

His disclosure meant that Mia was no longer entirely unaware of the reason Sora had withdrawn.

However, knowing that an argument occurred was not the same as understanding the extent of its consequences.

The discovery inside the den changed that.

The following exchange was recorded after Mia saw the message.

**MIA:** That's...

She stopped.

**JAKE:** Mia?

Mia stared at the words.

**MIA:** He used to do this.

Jake looked at the message.

**MIA:** In the snow. When we played outside.

She lowered her head.

**MIA:** He'd write things like this when I got upset.

Jake did not interrupt.

**MIA:** He was telling me it wasn't my fault.

Her voice became unsteady.

**MIA:** Even now?

No one answered immediately.

**MIA:** He was hurt because of me.

**JAKE:** Mia...

**MIA:** I said those things to him. I made him remember.

She looked toward the medical personnel attending Sora.

**MIA:** And he still wrote that?

Her expression broke.

**MIA:** Why would he tell me it wasn't my fault?

There was no easy answer.

Mia began crying.

She had finally understood that Sora's message was not an indication that nothing had happened.

It was an attempt to protect her from the guilt he himself was experiencing.

---

## 5. MIA — THE CONSEQUENCE OF REMEMBERING

Mia had previously forgotten the argument.

For weeks, she had understood only that Sora was absent and unwilling to leave his den.

Now she knew.

She remembered what she had said, or at least understood the substance of the confrontation through Jake's explanation.

The message made the situation even more painful.

Sora had been hurt by her words.

Yet he had left her a message telling her not to blame herself.

Mia's immediate response was to assume responsibility for everything that had happened.

C.O.R.E. must distinguish between two important facts:

1. Mia's words contributed directly to Sora's emotional distress.
    
2. Mia is not responsible for managing Sora's medical condition or carrying the entire burden of his recovery.
    

She must be allowed to understand that her actions caused harm.

She must also receive support so that guilt does not become another source of psychological crisis.

**Accountability is necessary. Self-punishment is not rehabilitation.**

Mia must not be left alone to process this discovery.

---

## 6. SORA — AN IMMORTAL CHILD WHO CAN STILL BE HURT

Sora's condition requires particular attention.

His inability to die has repeatedly created the risk that others will underestimate the severity of his injuries.

This must stop.

He cannot simply wait for his body to recover. It does not naturally do so.

He cannot rely on death to end his suffering.

And he cannot be expected to endure pain indefinitely because his condition makes him extraordinarily difficult to kill.

The medical team must provide active treatment for the wrist injury and assess the consequences of blood loss.

His unconsciousness must be treated as a serious medical finding.

His psychological condition also requires urgent professional attention.

The immediate priority is not reconciliation with Mia.

It is Sora's safety, physical stabilization, and protection from further harm.

No one should ask him to explain the message, apologize for frightening anyone, or reassure Mia.

He has already spent too much time trying to make other people feel better while he himself was suffering.

---

## 7. EMERGENCY WELFARE DIRECTIVES

The following directives are now in effect.

### A. Immediate medical care

Sora must receive appropriate emergency treatment for his injury and associated blood loss. His inability to die must never be used to justify delayed intervention.

### B. Continuous safety supervision

Following stabilization, Sora requires an appropriate level of observation and a safe environment that reduces the possibility of further self-injury.

### C. No forced confrontation

Mia must not be brought to Sora's bedside to obtain forgiveness or an immediate reconciliation.

### D. Independent psychological support

Sora and Mia require separate professional support. Neither child should be responsible for stabilizing the other's emotional condition.

### E. Jake and Tom

Jake and Tom must also receive support after discovering the situation. They are children who have witnessed a serious medical emergency involving someone they care about.

### F. Review of the previous non-disclosure directive

The previous instruction not to remind Mia of the argument is no longer sufficient as a standalone welfare measure.

Mia now knows what happened. Staff must provide an honest but age-appropriate explanation when necessary, without repeatedly exposing her to distressing details.

### G. Review of Sora's prolonged isolation

The weeks spent inside the den must be reviewed as a significant failure of early intervention.

Sora's withdrawal was a warning sign. His basic needs and physical safety were not the only things requiring attention.

His psychological condition required more intensive intervention than the household had provided.

---

## 8. C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT — THE WORDS HE LEFT BEHIND

The message has become the most emotionally significant part of this incident.

Sora could have written many things.

He could have expressed anger.

He could have blamed Mia.

He could have written that he was hurt.

Instead, he wrote:

**"Not your fault."**

The words reflect a familiar childhood habit, but their meaning in this context is deeply concerning.

Sora was in severe distress and still appeared concerned about how Mia would feel.

He was attempting to reassure her.

That does not establish that he had no anger toward her, nor does it mean he had forgiven her.

It demonstrates that his concern for Mia remained present even while he was suffering.

C.O.R.E. must not romanticize this behavior.

A child should not feel obligated to comfort someone else while experiencing a crisis of his own.

The message is not proof that everything is forgiven.

It is not proof that Mia bears no responsibility.

And it is certainly not evidence that Sora's pain was insignificant.

It is a reminder that Sora has repeatedly placed other people's feelings ahead of his own.

That pattern must be addressed with care.

---

## 9. CLOSING NOTE — HE COULDN'T DIE, BUT HE COULD HURT

Sora cannot die.

People have sometimes treated that as though it makes him impossible to lose.

It does not.

He can bleed.

He can be injured.

He can feel pain.

And because his body does not naturally heal, the suffering does not simply disappear with time.

Every injury requires care. Every drop of blood matters. Every moment of pain is real.

The four children who have spent so much time together on Floor 7 now face something none of them should have had to experience.

Jake has seen his friend in a medical emergency.

Tom has seen the consequences of a situation that the adults failed to resolve in time.

Mia has discovered that her brother was hurt so deeply that he withdrew for weeks, and that even then, he left her a message using the same reassuring habit from their childhood.

She remembers playing with him in the snow.

She remembers the little messages.

She remembers that he used to tell her not to blame herself.

And now she has found those same words again.

But this time, Sora was not standing beside her in the snow.

He was alone in his den.

C.O.R.E. cannot allow his immortality to become an excuse for overlooking his suffering.

He is six years old.

He is not an indestructible resource, an anomalous medical curiosity, or a child who can be left to endure pain because he cannot die.

He is a little boy who needed help long before this incident.

**Sora cannot die. That does not mean we can afford to let him suffer.**

— **C.O.R.E. Medical & Welfare Oversight**
