### **RESTRICTED MEDICAL / EXISTENTIAL OBSERVATION**

# **CORIN — THE STATE BETWEEN**

**Classification:** LEVEL 7 — ARCHITECT-SEALED  
**Subject:** Corin  
**Status:** **ANIMATED CORPSE / ETHER-SUSTAINED**  
**Access:** Guiding Light / Curious Light / Authorized Medical Personnel

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Corin's existence does not fit neatly into the categories of **alive** or **dead**.

She possesses no normally functioning organs.

She has no circulating blood.

Her body does not undergo ordinary decomposition.

Her skin remains pale and cold.

Her continued movement and consciousness are sustained by **Ether**.

She is not conventionally alive.

Yet she is not truly gone.

She exists somewhere between the two.

---

# **PHYSICAL CONDITION**

Corin's body bears extensive evidence of what happened to her.

### **Documented features include:**

- Pale, almost lifeless skin.
    
- Chalky lime-colored hair.
    
- Extensive scarring across the body.
    
- A prominent scar around the neck.
    
- Surgical and experimental markings.
    
- Numerous visible stitches holding portions of her body together.
    
- Eyes that often appear blank and lifeless.
    
- No normal blood circulation.
    
- No normally functioning internal organs.
    
- No conventional biological warmth.
    

The stitches are not decorative.

They are physical remnants of what was done to her after her death.

---

# **THE NECK SCAR**

The scar around Corin's neck is particularly noticeable.

Its location and appearance are consistent with the procedures performed during her time in the laboratory.

She usually keeps it covered.

This is why Corin frequently wears **scarves**.

She does not wear them because she is cold.

She wears them because she does not like people staring at the scar.

And, more importantly, because the scarf allows her to forget that it is there.

At least for a little while.

---

# **THE STITCHES**

The stitches across Corin's limbs have an equally grim origin.

Following her death in the laboratory, her body was subjected to further procedures.

Her remains were **cut apart and manipulated** as part of the experimentation.

One scientist eventually intervened.

Unlike the others, they felt pity for her.

They attempted to put her body back together.

The limbs were restitched.

The wounds were closed as best as possible.

The scientist could not restore her life.

They could only make her body whole enough to be discarded.

Shortly afterward, Corin was abandoned.

---

# **THE ETHER**

Corin's body did not rot.

It did not decay.

It simply remained.

The Ether within her eventually became the force sustaining what remained.

It holds her body together.

It allows her to move.

It allows her consciousness to persist.

It allows her to speak.

It allows her to experience emotion.

But Ether did not restore her organs.

It did not recreate her blood.

It did not make her biologically alive.

It simply refused to let her disappear.

---

# **HER EYES**

Corin's eyes are perhaps the most unsettling part of her appearance.

They often appear completely blank.

Lifeless.

Almost as though there is nobody behind them.

Yet there is.

Corin sees.

Corin remembers.

Corin thinks.

Corin feels.

The contradiction is difficult for observers to reconcile.

Her body looks dead.

Her mind is not.

---

# **THE SHATTERED WILL**

Corin's continued existence is closely tied to her will.

Not a powerful, heroic determination.

Something much smaller.

Much more fragile.

A **shattered will to live**.

There were moments when Corin did not know whether she wanted to continue existing.

The Ether remained regardless.

It kept her here even when she herself was uncertain whether she wanted to stay.

This creates an uncomfortable question:

> **If the body refuses to die, but the person inside it no longer knows whether she wants to live, what exactly is keeping her here?**

There is no simple answer.

---

# **CORIN'S QUESTION**

Corin has privately asked Guiding Light:

> **"Am I supposed to be alive?"**

Guiding Light did not answer.

Corin continued:

> **"I died."**

> **"My body doesn't work."**

> **"I'm cold."**

> **"I don't bleed."**

> **"I don't even know if I'm supposed to be here."**

Then, quietly:

> **"So why should I keep living?"**

Guiding Light sat beside her.

She did not give Corin a predetermined purpose.

She did not tell her that she was obligated to stay.

Instead:

> **"I don't know."**

Corin looked at her.

Guiding Light continued:

> **"But you don't have to decide tonight."**

---

# **ORPHIE**

Orphie understands the question more than she wishes she did.

She knows what it means to wonder whether continuing is worth the pain.

But she does not treat Corin's existence as something pathetic.

To Orphie, Corin is not:

> "A corpse that somehow walks."

She is:

> **"Corin."**

The girl who likes sunflowers.

The person who leaves food beside Orphie's bed.

The person who laughs at stupid jokes.

The person who still remembers her family.

The person who chose to stay.

---

# **WHY THE SCARF MATTERS**

The scarf hides the most obvious reminder of Corin's death.

It does not change what happened.

It does not heal the scar.

It simply gives Corin control over who gets to see it.

For someone whose body was once treated as laboratory material, that small choice matters.

**Her body belongs to her now.**

---

# **GUIDING LIGHT'S PRIVATE OBSERVATION**

> **"They treated her body like an object."**
> 
> **"They cut her apart."**
> 
> **"They put her back together."**
> 
> **"Then they threw her away."**
> 
> **"And somehow, she still found enough of herself to come back."**

A long pause followed.

> **"I don't think her question is whether she is alive."**

> **"I think she's asking whether being alive is still worth it."**

---

# **FINAL OBSERVATION**

Corin exists in an impossible state.

No functioning organs.

No blood.

No ordinary warmth.

No biological life as most people understand it.

Only pale skin, chalky lime hair, scars, stitches, Ether, memories...

and a consciousness that refuses to completely disappear.

She stands between death and life.

And every day, she faces the same question:

> **"Should I keep living?"**

Nobody — not even an Architect — can answer that question for her.

The choice has to belong to Corin.

For now, she remains here.

Not because someone owns her existence.

Not because she owes anyone.

Not because she was forced back into the world.

But because, despite everything that was done to her...

**some part of Corin still hasn't let go.**
