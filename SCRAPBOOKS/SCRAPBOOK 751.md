### **CORE-SAFEHOUSE / WILDLIFE & PERSONNEL CARE**

# **ALICE LOCKWOOD — UNINTENTIONAL OVERSIGHT LOG**

**Classification:** LEVEL 10 — SAFEHOUSE PERSONNEL  
**Subject:** Alice Lockwood  
**Status:** **PRESENT / STABLE / HIGH SUPERVISION REQUIRED**

---

# **OVERVIEW**

Alice is loved by everyone in the SAFEHOUSE.

This has never been questioned.

Unfortunately, being loved does not make someone impossible to forget.

Alice is exceptionally quiet.

She rarely complains.

She rarely calls for help.

She can disappear into the background of a busy room without anyone noticing.

As a result, a recurring problem has emerged:

**People unintentionally forget where Alice is.**

Not for hours.

Usually only minutes.

But with Alice's condition, even minutes can matter.

---

# **INCIDENT 01 — THE WALK**

Alice went outside with Corin.

They walked around the Treehouse perimeter.

Alice rode her scooter for part of the way.

Eventually, Corin returned inside.

She spoke with Orphie.

They discussed something unrelated.

Several minutes passed.

Corin suddenly stopped.

> "...Where's Alice?"

Everyone froze.

Alice was still outside.

She had stopped near a tree and was quietly drawing in her notebook.

When Corin found her, Alice looked up.

> **"Big sis forgot Alice."**

Corin immediately apologized.

Alice simply shrugged.

She wasn't angry.

That somehow made it worse.

---

# **INCIDENT 02 — THE DOOR**

Alice entered a room behind someone.

The door closed.

Alice remained on the other side.

She was too quiet to be noticed.

Nobody realized she was there until approximately ten minutes later.

When the door opened again, Alice was sitting on the floor.

Her hat was slightly crooked.

She looked tired.

> **"Alice stuck."**

The personnel involved apologized repeatedly.

Alice wrote:

> **"Is okay."**

It wasn't really okay.

---

# **INCIDENT 03 — THE ELEVATOR**

Alice followed a group into an elevator.

Everyone exited.

The doors closed.

Alice remained inside.

She didn't press the emergency button.

She simply sat down.

When the elevator returned, Neko found her.

He stared.

Alice looked up.

> **"Neko."**

> "Why are you in here?"

Alice pointed at the closed doors.

> **"Door win."**

Neko immediately added another reminder to the SAFEHOUSE security system.

---

# **INCIDENT 04 — THE KITCHEN**

Corin was cooking.

Belle was talking.

Star was explaining something.

Bao was preparing ingredients.

Everyone was busy.

Alice sat quietly at the table.

Nobody noticed when she became tired and rested her head against the table.

Approximately twenty minutes later, Corin looked over.

> "...Alice?"

Alice lifted her head.

> **"Hi."**

Corin nearly had a heart attack.

Alice had been there the entire time.

---

# **INCIDENT 05 — THE SCOOTER**

Alice went for a short ride.

Everyone assumed she was with someone else.

She wasn't.

She eventually returned to the SAFEHOUSE alone.

Nobody had realized she had left.

Neko checked the scooter tracker afterward.

The route was harmless.

The conclusion wasn't.

Alice could leave without anyone noticing.

---

# **WHY IT HAPPENS**

There is no malice involved.

Nobody is neglecting Alice intentionally.

Quite the opposite.

The SAFEHOUSE contains many people.

People are constantly moving.

Working.

Talking.

Cooking.

Repairing things.

Going on missions.

Returning from missions.

Alice is quiet.

She doesn't demand attention.

She doesn't complain when uncomfortable.

She doesn't yell when she needs help.

And because everyone knows Alice is loved and protected, people sometimes unconsciously assume:

> **"Someone else is watching her."**

Sometimes nobody is.

---

# **ALICE'S RESPONSE**

Alice rarely becomes angry.

When she is found, she usually says:

> **"Alice okay."**

Sometimes:

> **"Alice wait."**

Sometimes:

> **"Alice don't want bother."**

That last sentence is the most concerning.

---

# **NEW SAFEHOUSE PROTOCOL**

A new system has been implemented.

Not because Alice is considered a burden.

Because everyone has realized that **good intentions are not a supervision system.**

Whenever Alice leaves a designated area:

**Someone must explicitly acknowledge that they are responsible for her.**

Not:

> "She's probably with Corin."

Not:

> "Someone's watching her."

Not:

> "She'll come back."

A specific person.

A specific responsibility.

And before leaving the area:

> **"Alice is with me."**

---

# **DOOR POLICY**

All doors frequently used by Alice now have occupancy sensors.

If Alice is detected inside a room when the door closes, an alert is generated.

The alert is intentionally gentle.

No sirens.

Just:

> **ALICE INSIDE**

Neko refused to make it louder.

> "She doesn't need to feel like she's in trouble."

---

# **SCOOTER POLICY**

Alice's scooter remains tracked.

Its tracker now provides an additional notification if Alice travels outside her expected route without a designated companion.

The system does not prevent her from riding.

It simply makes sure someone knows where she is.

---

# **CORIN**

Corin has taken the incidents personally.

Not because anyone blames her.

Nobody does.

But Alice's history makes being forgotten particularly painful.

Corin remembers the years Alice spent afraid of being unwanted.

She remembers the sentence in the notebook.

> **"No one loves me."**

She knows that's not true.

But she also knows that accidentally leaving Alice alone can make that old fear feel real.

So Corin has become more careful.

Before leaving a room, she checks.

Before closing a door, she checks.

Before returning from a walk, she checks.

And when Alice is beside her, Corin says:

> "I've got you."

Alice usually nods.

---

# **ALICE'S JOURNAL**

Alice eventually wrote about the incidents.

Her handwriting was small.

> **"Sometimes everyone forget Alice."**

> **"They don't mean it."**

> **"Alice know they love me."**

Then:

> **"Alice just quiet."**

She drew herself sitting beside a closed door.

The drawing was not sad.

That somehow made it sadder.

---

# **FINAL NOTE**

The SAFEHOUSE has learned an uncomfortable lesson.

Alice doesn't need less love.

She needs more **awareness.**

She is not loud enough to demand attention.

She won't always say when she is cold.

She won't always say when she is tired.

She won't always say when she's hungry.

She won't always say when she's been left behind.

So the responsibility cannot be placed on Alice.

The adults around her have to remember.

Not because Alice is helpless.

Because she is small.

Because she is fragile.

Because she has already spent too much of her life believing she shouldn't inconvenience anyone.

And because forgetting someone accidentally doesn't make the consequences accidental.
