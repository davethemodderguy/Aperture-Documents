## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Additional information concerning Sora's history has revealed the circumstances behind his profound fear of human beings, his difficulty accepting assistance, and his tendency to avoid becoming an inconvenience to others.

Before arriving at Aperture, Sora had a family.

It consisted of an elderly woman who owned a farm in the suburbs of Texas, and two Thiren children under her care: Sora and his younger sister, a Cat Thiren.

The elderly woman was reportedly kind. Over time, the three developed a close relationship. Sora and his sister regarded her as a grandmother.

Their circumstances changed following her death.

The elderly woman's son staged her death following a heart attack witnessed by both children. He subsequently inherited the farm and began dismantling it. The destruction of their home was accompanied by escalating abuse, psychological torment, and physical torture directed at both siblings.

The sister eventually died as a consequence of the abuse.

The son subsequently targeted Sora. Following a severe physical confrontation in which he was badly beaten, the perpetrator fled. Sora remained behind, having lost both his grandmother and his younger sister.

He survived alone for years before eventually reaching the Blacksite.

**Sora's fear of humans is therefore not an unexplained behavioral characteristic. It is rooted in repeated, catastrophic experiences of betrayal, violence, and loss at the hands of a human being.**

---

## 2. THE FAMILY BEFORE THE INCIDENT

The farm was located in the suburban outskirts of Texas.

Its owner was an elderly woman who treated Sora and his younger sister with kindness. The siblings grew close to her, and she became a grandmother figure to them.

For the children, the farm was more than a place to live.

It was home.

They had each other, and they had someone who cared for them. Their lives were not necessarily without hardship, but they possessed something that would later become almost entirely absent from Sora's existence: a reliable source of affection and safety.

The younger sibling was a Cat Thiren.

Her name remains unknown.

Sora cannot remember it.

This is a particularly significant detail. He remembers that he had a little sister. He remembers that she was there. He remembers losing her.

But the name itself is beyond his recollection.

C.O.R.E. will not attempt to assign her a replacement name without Sora's consent.

---

## 3. THE FARM INCIDENT

The elderly woman suffered a heart attack and died.

According to the information received, her son staged the circumstances surrounding her death. Both Sora and his sister witnessed the incident.

Following the woman's death, ownership of the farm passed to her son.

His subsequent actions transformed the children's home into an environment of destruction.

He began tearing the farm apart, dismantling the property and destroying the surroundings that had previously represented security to the siblings.

The destruction was not merely structural.

Both children were subjected to psychological trauma and physical torture.

Their living conditions deteriorated severely. They were left outside, without adequate shelter or reliable food. They were forced to defend themselves against predators and survive through whatever they could find.

Their diet reportedly consisted of grass and animal corpses.

These were not isolated inconveniences. They were the conditions under which two young children were forced to survive after losing the only adult who had cared for them.

The abuse eventually resulted in the younger sister's death.

The perpetrator then turned his attention toward Sora.

At some point, the son was beaten badly enough to flee permanently. He did not return for years.

Sora survived, but survival did not restore what had been taken from him.

---

## 4. THE SEQUENCE OF LOSS

C.O.R.E. identifies three major stages in Sora's history.

**First loss – His grandmother.**

The elderly woman represented kindness, stability, and familial affection. Her death, witnessed by both children, was followed by the destruction of the home she had provided.

**Second loss – His younger sister.**

The only other person who shared his childhood circumstances was subjected to the same abuse. She eventually died.

Sora cannot remember her name, but the loss remains part of his history.

**Third loss – Everything familiar.**

The farm was destroyed. His grandmother was gone. His sister was gone. The person responsible had disappeared, leaving Sora without any meaningful sense of security or continuity.

He spent years surviving without the family he once had.

By the time he reached Aperture, he had already experienced the loss of nearly everything that had once made him feel safe.

---

## 5. PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT

This history provides substantial context for Sora's behavior at the Blacksite.

His fear of human contact is consistent with his experiences.

He once had a close and loving relationship with a human caretaker. That relationship ended through death, and the caretaker's son subsequently became the source of prolonged abuse and the destruction of his family.

It is important to recognize the distinction:

**Sora did not learn that every human being was cruel. He learned that someone who had access to his home, his family, and his trust could destroy all three.**

The distinction may be intellectually simple, but it is not necessarily emotionally accessible to him.

His reluctance to accept food, his avoidance of medical assistance, his attempts to remain unnoticed, and his apparent unwillingness to inconvenience others must be considered in this context.

His behavior should not be interpreted as ingratitude or deliberate noncompliance.

It may instead reflect a survival pattern developed in an environment where dependence was dangerous.

His sister's death also introduces another dimension to his current attachment to Jake.

Sora has lost someone he loved before. His close attachment to Jake may represent something profoundly important to him, although C.O.R.E. will not presume to define that relationship on his behalf.

His disappearance following the incident on Floor 7 must therefore be treated with particular care. The possibility that a seemingly harmless human interaction triggered an overwhelming fear response is consistent with the history now available.

This does not establish that Sora no longer trusts Jake.

It establishes that his fear may be strong enough to prevent him from reaching someone he otherwise desperately wants to be near.

## 6. PERSONNEL DIRECTIVES

Effective immediately:

1. Personnel are prohibited from deliberately startling, cornering, chasing, or otherwise frightening Sora.
    
2. No employee is to interpret his fear as an invitation to force social interaction.
    
3. Any recovered information concerning his younger sister must be preserved without alteration.
    
4. Personnel must not pressure Sora to recall his sister's name.
    
5. Any attempt to recover his memories must be conducted with his informed participation and appropriate medical oversight.
    
6. Jake must not be made responsible for locating, treating, or emotionally stabilizing Sora.
    
7. The search for Sora must continue with minimal disturbance to SAFEHOUSE residents.
    
8. Sora's Den is to remain available to him, without reassignment or removal of its contents.
    

Personnel are also reminded that the fact Sora cannot die does not make his suffering inconsequential. His injuries, hunger, fear, and psychological distress remain genuine.

## 7. C.O.R.E. CLOSING REMARK

There is a particular cruelty in what happened to Sora.

He knew what kindness looked like.

He had a grandmother. He had a little sister. He had a home where the three of them were close enough to become a family.

Then someone took it away.

He lost the woman he called grandmother. He lost the little sister whose name he can no longer remember. He lost the farm, the life they shared, and eventually even the ability to feel safe around the people who might have helped him.

For years, he survived on things no child should ever have to eat, in conditions no child should ever have to endure.

And when he finally reached us, he still tried not to inconvenience anyone.

We should consider what that means.

Sora has spent much of his life learning that the world can take everything from him. We cannot undo that history, and we cannot promise that he will never be frightened again.

But we can make one promise.

**Nobody at this facility will be permitted to turn his home into a place he has to survive.**

His sister's name may be lost to him.

His grandmother is gone.

But Sora is not required to lose another person simply because he is afraid to reach out.

And if he ever comes back to his little den on Floor 7, he should find it exactly where he left it.

Not because we expect him to return.

Because he deserves to have somewhere that remains his, even when he cannot bring himself to come home.

— **C.O.R.E. Welfare Oversight**
