# INCIDENT SUMMARY

Phoenix was awake late at night.

This was not considered unusual.

It was, however, considered unfortunate.

Phoenix's established sleep disorder prevents her from reliably initiating sleep at night.

She may become exhausted.

She may become physically incapable of remaining productive.

She may even fall asleep involuntarily during the day.

But when she actually wants to sleep at night:

> **Her body frequently refuses.**

Ether Regression Syndrome has made this considerably worse.

---

# 1. 02:47 — KITCHEN

Phoenix was found sitting alone in the SAFEHOUSE kitchen.

The lights were dimmed.

A cup of water sat beside her.

She was not working.

This was unusual.

Phoenix was simply sitting there.

Her eyes were visibly tired.

She had one hand pressed against her forehead.

A resident entering the kitchen noticed her.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “You're still awake?”

Phoenix nodded.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Yeah.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “Can't sleep?”

Phoenix shook her head.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “No.”

The resident looked at the clock.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “It's almost three.”

Phoenix looked at the clock.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “I know.”

---

# 2. PHYSICAL CONDITION

Phoenix appeared exhausted.

Not energetic.

Not productive.

Not alert by choice.

Simply awake.

Her posture was poor.

Her movements were slow.

Her eyes were visibly strained.

She occasionally rubbed her arms and shoulders as though attempting to relieve persistent discomfort.

When asked whether she was in pain:

Phoenix paused.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “A little.”

The resident waited.

Phoenix corrected herself.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “More than a little.”

---

# 3. SHE DID NOT WANT TO WORK

The resident noticed that Phoenix's medical bag was nowhere nearby.

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “You're not working?”

Phoenix shook her head.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “No.”

> **RESIDENT:**  
> “Good.”

Phoenix looked at them.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “I wasn't going to.”

This was considered a positive development.

Phoenix has historically responded to exhaustion by continuing to work until her body forcibly stops her.

On this occasion, she had apparently decided to simply remain awake.

# 4. JAKE

At approximately 03:02, Jake appeared in the doorway.

He was carrying his teddy bear.

He looked at Phoenix.

Phoenix looked at him.

Neither spoke initially.

Jake walked over.

> **Jake:**  
> “You're awake.”

Phoenix nodded.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “So are you.”

Jake shrugged.

> **Jake:**  
> “I woke up.”

Phoenix looked at his teddy bear.

The heartbeat was faintly audible in the quiet kitchen.

She smiled slightly.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Bad dream?”

Jake shook his head.

> **Jake:**  
> “No.”

Pause.

> **Jake:**  
> “I just wanted water.”

Phoenix nodded.

> **Phoenix:**  
> “Okay.”

Jake got his water.

He then sat at the table.

Phoenix remained where she was.

---

# C.O.R.E. OBSERVATION

Phoenix's late-night wakefulness is not necessarily an emergency.

However, prolonged inability to sleep remains medically significant given:

- Ether Regression Syndrome;
    
- narcolepsy;
    
- chronic fatigue;
    
- repeated Transcendense events;
    
- persistent pain;
    
- and her already severely deteriorated psychological state.
    

The primary concern is not that Phoenix is awake at 03:00.

The concern is that she may spend another night awake while her body desperately needs rest.

# C.O.R.E. ASSESSMENT

Phoenix did not work.

She did not attempt to treat anyone.

She did not force herself to be productive.

She did not pretend that she was fine.

She simply sat awake in the kitchen.

Then Jake found her.

And stayed.

No medical breakthrough.

No miraculous cure.

Just two exhausted residents sitting together in the middle of the night.

Sometimes that is what welfare looks like.

Not fixing the problem.

Just making sure someone doesn't have to experience it completely alone.
